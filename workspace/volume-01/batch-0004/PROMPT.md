This is Act IV, the public dry line, and it ends the volume. **The batch cliff is Chapter 48, and it is a half-lit public forecast hall**: a piece of glass pinned to a board, a column of figures being argued over by two people who were not in the room, a woman at a copy desk two rows back with a hand that does not shake, and a poor ward's tower with a dark window above the low end of the city. The storm is over; nothing in this batch is a weather event.

Hard limits for this batch, all of which are already fixed and all of which are enforced in the cards:

- **Zero bindings. Zero System panels.** All four of Volume 01's bindings are spent and the batch's last panel was Chapter 29. **No claim may be entered in Chapters 36–48, and no chapter may produce a panel.** If a chapter seems to need one, the chapter is wrong.
- **No advancement.** Marek stays a Reader. He must not follow a sequence across two towers and must not attempt a Conductor's action.
- **Nothing is reopened.** The Chapter 21 compound branch and the Chapter 29 corridor branch are both closed and both stay closed. **Reopening either is a continuity failure.**
- **The trial claim's cost remains unprovable.** Chapter 40 gets as close to it as the series ever gets and **still produces no document, no witness, no name and no paper that could be produced in a room.** The near-miss is the point. No state file may describe how it could be proved.
- **No deaths.** The costs in this batch are an office, a post, a column, a board, a page, two carriers, a wall of glass in a frame, and three years.
- **Marek's hearing loss is permanent, is never repaired, and is never explained to anybody.** About a third of the fine range is gone on the left with a high thin ringing on that side. It is not the Volume 12 sequence burn. **It is not the ground of the censure, nobody in the building knows about it, and no character hears it explained.**
- **The Lower Sill culvert's failure and the south-bank landslide stay in two separate columns.** The dry line is not built out of the landslide. **The culvert is not rebuilt. The front returns in three years, and three years is a Friday and not a prophecy.**
- **Tovan's demotion and the reclassification happened on the third of the month, in Chapter 35, and are not re-staged.** The inquiry is not about whether the entry was changed. The entry is not reopened. **Tovan is a copy clerk in every chapter of this batch and is never consulted about anything that matters. Her copy does not become known in this volume.**
- **The corridor claim stands** and is not reopened. It is true, it is stamped, it is in the ledger, and it is listed in a column in Chapter 44.
- **Do not name the Meridian, the Compact, the First Compact, or the Crown Basin sheet.** Crown Basin stays offstage and the volume does not go inside. **The Ninth Ward Record may be named in Chapter 47 as a citation and may not be recovered, quoted, or described from the inside.** The routing fragment is Chapter 47. **Ione Sere's one lie in this volume is Chapter 47** and neither may appear earlier in this batch.
- **The hand that cut the index is not identified in this volume.** That is Volume 05. **Do not point at Rook, do not point at Anja, and do not give the reader a solution the outline has not earned.**
- **Ottine Vask's name appears in Chapter 47 and nowhere earlier.** She does not appear, speak, be mourned in a scene, or be suspected.
- **Rook appears throughout and is never caught lying, never monologues, and never loses an argument by being wrong about the weather.** He chairs the review of his own authorization, declares the interest in four minutes, does the fairest available job, and is the reason the method still feels reasonable at the end. **The reader should be more afraid of him in Chapter 48 than in Chapter 35.**
- **Malle and Venn stay separate.** Malle answers for a field classification in Chapter 42 and is not overturned. **Venn's reclassification problem — readings that were never acted on are not compellable, because an observation is not a promise — was named in one exchange in Chapter 35 and must not be named or explained again.**
- **The Ninth Ward frame is still unreadable at Chapter 48 and the reason is the ward's poverty.** 4486 was paid for and never collected; the crown became Marek's private plate. **No character in Chapters 36–48 learns that Marek destroyed the paid-for pane**, and the frame's condition is a beat, not a scene.
- **Marek's cuff still holds two readings and a filing mark, in three blocks of space.** A mark is not a reading. Do not write "three readings."
- **The standing figures, all fixed in prose:** four hundred and forty people in Low Spindle's lowest four blocks; two hundred and twenty on the Hearthward terraces, of whom a hundred and sixty went up before midnight and sixty-one came out of the market; forty-one children counted outside the Sump Lane school; the Hearthward vaults hold ninety drawers of which the lower six are ruined; Tarin has landed on Hearthward a hundred and fifty times; the Council's technical cell has a hundred and eighty people in it; a hundred and forty thousand people in the basin. **Do not reuse one figure for two unrelated quantities.** The Ferrygate figures are separate and are these: six names on the rain-garden rota and generally five turning up, two names on the shelter rota, eleven carts and two barrows booked by the market factor, four chartered barges on the grain window. The Kestrel figures are separate: four licensed carriers, two insurers, one grain factor, eleven crews on the Line's name. The south-bank convoy is **nine**.
- **The relationship stays professional.** Competence, argument, a censure, and a document handed over in a hall. **No attraction beat, no rescue romance, no jealousy, and no romance in the final chapter.** Volume 03 owns the first real trust milestone and this is not it.
- **No new final enemy, no new magic rule, no new faction.** **The Quiet Ledger is not yet a thing and Sable Orin is not in it and must not be written as being in it.**
- **The dry line, the spent-turn column, the shed's public sign, the hand-lettered board at the freight apron, and the First Dry Line are staged in this batch, in that order, and nowhere else in the series before it.** The board is a load list and not a memorial and it carries no name and no seal; the column prints what closed and cannot print who closed it; **the phrase *dry line* enters from a member of the public in Chapter 46 and not from anybody in the building.**
