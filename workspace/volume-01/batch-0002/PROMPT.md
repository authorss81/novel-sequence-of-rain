Hard limits for this batch, all of which are already fixed and all of which are enforced in the cards:

- **Marek stays a Reader.** No advancement, and he must not follow a sequence across two towers.
- **Exactly one binding: the Nacre Gate trial claim in Chapter 21.** Chapters 11–20 spend nothing. The Chapter 11 watch is a free read. Sable Orin's Chapter 14 reading is published and **nobody acts on it** — that refusal is the chapter's event, and it is the inverse of Chapter 8.
- **One System panel in the batch**, in Chapter 21, at the moment of certification. No second panel, no escape clause.
- **No deaths.** Costs are hours, wages, a market's month, and two old people in a wet room for one night.
- **The trial claim's cost is unprovable to everyone but Marek.** No document may be produced. If a reader could hold paper proving what the claim cost, the volume's argument collapses.
- **Do not name the Meridian, the Compact, Rook, Ione Sere, Ottine Vask, the Ninth Ward Record, or the Crown Basin sheet.** Chapter 18 yields a date on a piece of glass and nothing else. The routing fragment is Chapter 47 and Ione's one lie in this volume is Chapter 47.
- **Do not stage the Lower Sill culvert's failure, the convoy that never leaves, the dry line, the spent-turn column, the Kestrel corridor claim, or Marek's hearing loss.** Those are Chapters 22–35 or later. The convoy is loaded and does not move. Chapter 16 is not the Oren chapter; Chapter 34 is, and there is no reconciliation in Chapter 16.
- **Sable Orin's Chapter 8 number stays permanently unidentified.** Do not add a clue, a callback, a suspicion, or a state-file note about what it closed.
- **Marek's cuff holds two readings and a filing mark, in three blocks of space.** A mark is not a reading. Do not write "three readings." Exact contents are in `state/continuity.md`.
- **Never state an absolute hours-to-arrival.** The Office's published figure to the front is the only clock the prose may quote, and it slides downward: fifty hours on day seven, twenty at ten o'clock on the night of day eight, nine at 10:00 on day nine, six at noon, one at 17:00. The single fixed anchor is that the collision reached the basin at about 18:00 on day nine. Do not re-derive an arrival time from a chapter clock; write *the front is on the ridge*, *tomorrow evening*, *the last day of it*. The same rule governs any new outline card: state a clock time and a day, never an hours-to-arrival.
- **Marek never touched the tower on the morning of day eight.** The tower keeper lifted both plates out at 06:10 on Marek's word from the Thursday evening, wrapped them, and set the sailcloth at the foot of the ladder; Marek was seven miles off at Hearthward with a lamp in the outfall throat at that minute. Both plate movements and the throat are at 06:10 and they are seven miles apart. If a Chapter 11–21 scene needs either plate, it is in the tube in his coat.
- **Chapter 5 planted the gap on the bench, at about 11:20 on day seven, and put it down to the lamp.** That is the only earlier look at the dead stretch in the moisture trace, and Marek does not recognise it: he describes it in his own plain words, as *a quarter of an inch of glass with nothing on it* where the line *started behind itself*, and he decides it is the lamp. **Chapter 7 is where it becomes a moral fact, and Chapter 7 is where the technical vocabulary lives** — the smear, the dropout, the hard edge, the shallow slope. Do not let a Chapter 11–21 scene restate the Chapter 5 plant, and do not give any character the Chapter 7 reading before Chapter 7 has happened.
- **The Ninth Ward pane: Marek did not darken a working notice.** The frame has held a bubbled temporary since a fortnight before the opening; 4486 was paid for, never collected, and sat in Pell's shop; Marek cut his plate out of *that*. The two Chapter 8 dates — *unreadable for a fortnight* and the routing slip's *out since the sixth* — are a deliberate collision and are **not to be resolved in prose**. The frame is still unreadable at Chapter 48 and the reason is the ward's poverty.
- **The Anwen Sarr meeting is at twenty to eleven on day seven**, not eleven o'clock. Anwen arrives at five past ten and the public hall is already filling by eleven; the rota is therefore known before the hall. The garden crew is **four men and a foreman**.
- **Sable Orin's numbers name a corridor and a time and no place.** Neris says the rule out loud in Chapter 8 — *I know they're numbers with a time on them and no place, and no name, and no seal* — and it is what makes them uncompellable. Translating a corridor into a street is a warden's own work and is done on the page. Do not let Sable name a district, a street, a school, or a block.
- **The standing figures, all fixed in prose:** four hundred and forty people in Low Spindle's lowest four blocks; two hundred and twenty people on the Hearthward terraces, of whom a hundred and sixty went up the terraces before midnight and sixty-one came out of the market; forty-one children counted outside the Sump Lane school, being the forty who walk up from the low blocks plus one carried out of a house with no downstairs; the Hearthward vaults hold ninety drawers of which the lower six are the ruined ones; Tarin has landed on Hearthward a hundred and fifty times; the Council's technical cell has a hundred and eighty people in it. - **Pane 4486 was never installed.** The Ninth Ward's frame has held a bubbled temporary since a fortnight before the opening; 4486 was paid for, never collected, and sat in Pell's shop.
