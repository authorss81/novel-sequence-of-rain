Hard limits for this batch, all of which are already fixed and all of which are enforced in the cards:

- **Marek stays a Reader.** No advancement. He must not follow a sequence across two towers and must not attempt a Conductor's action.
- **Exactly one binding: the Kestrel corridor claim in Chapter 29.** Chapters 22–28 spend nothing. Chapter 22's nine hours is the tail of the Chapter 21 compound branch and is not a new spend.
- **One System panel in the entire batch**, in Chapter 29, at the moment of binding. No second panel, no escape clause.
- **No deaths.** The costs are a corridor claim, a crewman's wrist, four minutes of hearing, a culvert, a season of drainage, hours of standing water in ground-floor rooms, a shed of timber, a career, and a clerk's post. **Do not add one.** The twelve deaths of Volume 12 must not have a rehearsal, and Volume 01 contains none.
- **Marek's hearing loss happens in Chapter 31** and is caused by four minutes in cold moving water after a child in Low Spindle. It is a physical injury, not a binding, and it is not the Volume 12 sequence burn. It is never repaired. It must not be foreshadowed as a symptom in Chapters 22–30, and it **changes nothing whatever about the record** — that sentence belongs at the end of Chapter 31.
- **The Lower Sill culvert's failure is the spend; the south-bank landslide at 05:00 is storm damage.** The public account attributes them separately and correctly. Do not fold them together.
- **Nobody ordered the convoy to stop, and nobody says it will not go.** It is loaded at the south-bank shed with shoring timber and a replacement dewatering pump, its crew is nine, and its window is a **reach-level (low-water) window about forty minutes wide on the morning of the thirtieth**. - **Do not stage the dry line, the spent-turn column, the shed's public sign, the bellweather board, the First Dry Line, or any public marker.** Those are Chapters 36–48. Chapter 35 closes a ledger and opens a copy desk and nothing else.
- **Do not name the Meridian, the Compact, Ione Sere, Ottine Vask, the Ninth Ward Record, or the Crown Basin sheet.** The routing fragment is Chapter 47 and Ione's one lie in this volume is Chapter 47. Neither may appear in Chapters 22–35.

He is the reason the volume's method feels reasonable, and the reader should be more afraid of him at the end than at the start.

If a Chapters 22–35 scene needs a plate, the private one is in the tube in his coat; the tower's own is in the ninth ward feed seat; and the two are **not independent evidence**.
