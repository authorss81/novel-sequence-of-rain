**Chapter range:** 1–10
**Per-chapter word range:** 2,200–3,200 for ordinary chapters; longer only where a complete scene requires it, and shorter only for a compact reversal. A ten-chapter batch of ordinary scenes runs 24,000–31,000 words.
**Batch midpoint:** Chapter 5

## Batch goal

Get Marek Vale off the repair bench and onto a watch rotation, having spent his first branch in public and concealed it in private. The batch ends with the published figure at one hour and the collision itself at about 18:00, the Bellweather watch staffed, and Marek holding the only reading of the collision's order plus three pencil notes in his coat cuff.

The batch must teach four rules without a lecture: a read is free and a binding spends; a conditional forecast binds the moment its condition is accepted and triggered; a binding closes the still-available turn that would have prevented its stated outcome, and a human act of preparation is such a turn; and the Office has a professional, kindly, well-dressed way of making a spent possibility look like an unattached observation.

## Starting condition

Marek repairs sequence glass in the Weatherglass maintenance shop. He has never written a claim into the ward ledger, never heard the witness bell sound for a ward, and never made a forecast another person had to act on. The Bellweather Front is out past three days and closing, and the Office's instruments are already disagreeing with each other about it by six hours. Nothing is wrong yet. Everything is about to be.

**Clock convention for this batch — corrected at the Batch 0001 review.** Each card states a clock time and a day only. A cycle is six hours; a watch bell is the hour. Weather phases are the *leading rain*, the *second belt*, and the *collision* — never *turn*, which stays the technical term for a change in a sequence.

**Do not quote an absolute hours-to-arrival anywhere, in the cards or in the prose.** This batch originally carried an hours-before-the-front column and the prose followed it; the repair pass stripped every instance out of Chapters 1–10 because the figures could not be made consistent with a single arrival hour. The reason the number moves is now a fact the reader owns, and it is the batch's own subject. The **only** fixed anchor is the **collision reaching the basin at about 18:00 on day nine**. The Office's published figure is the sole clock the prose may quote, and it slides downward across the batch: fifty hours on day seven, twenty on the night of day eight, nine at 10:00 on day nine, six at noon, one at 17:00.

## Starting relationships

- **Marek / Tarin:** not spoken to in two years. Hearthward is unmentioned by both and present in every room.
- **Marek / Malle:** solid, protective, and about to need something from him that he has never been asked for.
- **Marek / Pell:** the only friendship that does not require him to be right. Pell's question this batch: *who did you ask to carry this?*
- **Marek / Tovan:** strangers on adjacent benches, and by the end of Chapter 4 the beginning of a working trust neither has named.
- **Marek / Anwen:** bad terms, inherited from the rooftop that was not in the forecast.

## Required character changes

- **Marek** goes from a man who repairs instruments to a man who has spent a branch, knows exactly what it cost, and chooses to say nothing about it for two more days. His flaw is not discovered by a reader noticing; it is committed by him, deliberately, in Chapter 7, and that is the batch's real turning point.
- **Tarin** goes from a captain who publishes her own uncertainty as a competitive position to a captain who has watched the accurate version fail anyway. She learns the cost is not a matter of intent.
- **Tovan** goes from a copy clerk who believes accuracy is a property of handwriting to a clerk who has watched a claim enter the world through her pencil. Her fear arrives early, on purpose.
- **Anwen** gets a second piece of evidence that Marek's forecasts are correct and incomplete, two years after the first, and this time it is on her own terraces.
- **Neris** gets the first line of a document that will outlast everyone who tells her not to write it.
- **Malle** gets the first of Marek's falsifiable accounts, and the first of several that are technically responsive and false.

## Required power changes

None. **Marek remains a Reader for the whole batch and must not follow a sequence across two towers.** What changes is his strain: the inner-ear pressure and the loss of fine hearing that follow a binding, and the worse symptom of being unable to stop seeing a movement that has already stopped. He ends the batch with a strained instrument of a mind, three pencil notes in a cuff, and a permanent debt to the poorest ward in the city that he has not yet been told about.

## Required world reveals

1. The Office's instruments can measure the Bellweather collision. Only a bench trace shows its **order**.
2. The Office has already sold a Bellweather window to the Cloud Exchange under a secrecy clause, so the Office has a commercial stake in one answer.
3. A binding happens when a claim has a place, a time or trigger, an effect, and a **witness** — a pencil, a bell, a voice, an act.
4. A conditional forecast binds the moment its condition is accepted and triggered, which makes a condition something people agree to rather than something a forecaster gets to have.
5. A binding closes the still-available turn that would have prevented its stated outcome, and a crew that would have gone out is such a turn. The claim can be entirely true while the preparation that would have made it survivable is the thing that closed.
6. Caution is free. A ward that closes a school on an observation without turning it into a claim spends nothing, and the Office credits nobody for it.
7. The Office's defence against inconvenient evidence is reclassification: an unattached observation is not a promise, and an observation nobody acted on cannot be compelled to mean anything later.
8. A public claim touching a water system or a market must be entered in the **emergency ledger** inside one cycle. The Office knows this rule and does not yet have any reason to care about it.

## Canon guardrails for this batch

- **No branch closes because Marek notices something.** Chapters 1, 3, 7, and 10 contain reads, private traces, and pencil notes. All of them are free. Say so in the text at least once, in Marek's own reasoning, and let the relief of it be visible.
- **Two bindings happen in this batch.** The Hearthward conditional claim is entered in Chapter 5 and triggered in Chapter 6. Sable's reading in Chapter 8 becomes a binding only because Neris acts on it, and what it spends is small, deniable, and never identified — not in prose, and not in the state files.
- **The Hearthward claim is entirely true, and that is the point.** Its stated effect includes the lower row draining clear. The lower row does drain clear. What nobody in the claim said is that a full outfall on the Hearthward terraces takes nine hours instead of three, and that six ground-floor rooms on the lowest row sit below the surcharge line the whole way. The garden crew's Thursday visit was the still-available turn that would have shortened that run, and closing it is what the claim did.
- **Marek's own contribution to the damage is narrowed certainty, not hidden magic.** He claimed a physical system as a certainty without knowing the ward's drainage, and he did not tell Anwen Sarr the difference between what his instruments answered and what her district would experience. That is his canon flaw performed as a job, and it is worse than a mysterious side effect because there is nothing to blame but him.
- **The Marek–Tarin relationship stays professional.** Competence, argument, and one shared scare. No attraction beat, no rescue, no jealousy.
- **No deaths in this batch.** The Hearthward ruin is six ground-floor rooms, a season's seed stock, and a night's work.
- **One System panel in the entire batch**, in Chapter 5, immediately before the entry. There is no escape clause and no second panel. A notice is tied to a consequential binding choice, and Chapter 5 is the only chapter in this batch where one is being made.
- **Do not name the Meridian Cut, the Ninth Ward Record, Cassian Rook's Cut order, or Ione Sere's part** in this batch. The oldest reference to the Ninth Ward tower in these ten chapters is a filing mark with a piece cut out of the citation beside it, and the reader is not told what the piece covered.
- **The twelve-year trace in the Ninth Ward tower's sequence plate is Batch 0002's discovery, not this batch's.** Chapter 10 ends on paper: a signature he recognises, a filing mark, and a hand-cut gap. Chapter 18 is where the plate itself gives up its date.
- **No new final enemy, no new magic rule, no new faction.** The four new minor names in the volume outline — Feth Raal, Ilva Sorn, Nessa Tarrow, and Ivo Slate — are supporting texture and none is the subject of a chapter.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass maintenance shop, 21:40, day four. Cold shop, paraffin and hot metal, three benches and one bad lamp.

**Immediate goal:** Fit a replacement **sequence plate** to the Ninth Ward tower's cracked feed before Malle's fourteen-hundred inspection, and clear the backlog: a bent aneroid, a jammed rain gauge, and Feth Raal's barometer, which he will do last and not charge for. The tower's public **signal pane** is cracked too, but that is the tower crew's job on a different budget, and Marek has never once got anyone to move on it.

**Resistance:** The plate he pulls out is a sequence plate, and his bench rig renders it in an order that matches nothing in the calibration set — Gather and Break arriving where Weight should sit, Witness arriving where Release should sit, the whole sequence shuffled like a hand of cards put down wrong. It is not a fault. It is a live trace, and it is on a plate the Office wrote off four years ago.

**Information revealed:** The Office's own instruments have the front at sixty-eight to seventy-four hours and cannot agree on it by six hours. Marek's cracked plate shows why: the three air masses are arriving inside one cycle, so the six movements are going to reach Veyra out of order. The genre promise, in the first chapter, delivered through a piece of broken glass on a workbench.

**Action or decision:** He reads it twice, ruins the first plate because he was not braced for it, and cuts a second. He does not write the reading into the chart room's register. He writes the time, the plate number, and the tower it came from on the inside of his left cuff in pencil, and says out loud, to the bench and to nobody, that if he is wrong he will have to say it out loud to somebody.

**Emotional change:** Frightened and elated in the same breath, and then immediately ashamed of the elation, because a man who is excited about a catastrophe is a man who has already decided the catastrophe is about something else. The first breath of the flaw.

**Power or resource change:** A read. No spend. Cost is a headache, the ruined plate, and a bent needle left in his hand. He ends the night with four hours of sleep and a decision to go and look at the Ninth Ward feed himself.

**Continuity fact:** The Ninth Ward tower's sequence feed renders the Six Movements out of order. The Office's instruments can measure the collision and cannot see its order. Marek keeps a private note in his cuff, outside the register, which is the first thing he has ever done off the record.

**Ending type:** Discovery.

**Next-chapter pull:** The old plate carries a serial from the T-4 glass bundle Pell Ardan cut for the tower — the bundle that put a signal pane in the public frame and a spare plate in the feed. Both have since cracked. In the morning, the tower crew are coming to collect the permanent replacement out of Pell's shop, and the yards are going to find out what the Office has already sold them.

### Chapter 0002

**POV:** Tarin Senn.

**Location/time:** Kestrel Yards, 06:00, day five. Working yards: mooring crews, a lift under repair, three route boards, and a great deal of shouted arithmetic.

**Immediate goal:** Get a launch decision made for the *Petrel* on the strength of a window she believes in, and keep the Kestrel Line's uncertainty note on the yard board where nobody can quietly take it down.

**Resistance:** Maro Hesk's licensed agent Ivo Slate produces an Exchange window for the same passage at half the price, on the strength of an Office forecast Marek has never seen and the yard board has never been shown. Tarin's own route officer, Ilva Sorn, makes the argument that is arithmetic and true: the Line cannot keep publishing uncertainty and keep the charter book, and there are eleven crews in this yard who take work on the strength of it.

**Information revealed:** The Office sold a Bellweather window to the Exchange under a standard secrecy clause four days ago, before the front was confirmed. Tarin learns this from a board being changed under her, not from a person. **This is craft, not sequence-reading:** Tarin flies on local instruments, the behaviour of her own glass over nine years, and a pattern she has personally flown twice. Her crew's glass gives her a *different timing* from the Office's published figure. She cannot see the order of a sequence and does not claim to. Nessa Tarrow, on the night watch, copies the Office's window to the board and then, at Tarin's instruction, copies Tarin's counter-reading underneath it with both times on the sheet — the first time anyone has held two versions of the same notice.

**Action or decision:** She refuses Slate's window, flies the *Petrel* on local glass and her own judgment, and loses a charter worth a month. She posts the Office's window and her own reading side by side on the board.

**Emotional change:** The specific humiliation of being right in public at her own expense. Underneath it, a question she does not yet have the shape to ask: the Office was not wrong, it was early, and somebody is still buying.

**Power or resource change:** No weather power used. Cost is a charter, a strained friendship with Ilva Sorn, and a yard board that now carries a public record of its captain disagreeing with the Weather Office.

**Continuity fact:** The Cloud Exchange bought a Bellweather window from the Office under a secrecy clause. Tarin's independent instrument reading puts the collision's timing well ahead of the Office's published figure. Tarin and Marek have not spoken in two years and neither mentions Hearthward in this chapter, though it is in the room.

**Ending type:** Reversal — the danger is not only that the Office is wrong, but that being right is a thing someone can sell.

**Next-chapter pull:** Slate does not argue. He thanks her, notes the time, and files his own agency report citing the yard board as evidence that the Office's window was understated at time of sale. She has just been invoiced for her competence.

### Chapter 0003

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass, 10:00 to 16:00, day six; then Pell's glassworks in the Kestrel Yards workshop district.

**Immediate goal:** Get the collision reading in front of the watch board with his name on it, and be believed without having to be senior.

**Resistance:** He has no standing. He is a maintenance hand with a bench trace and no instrument chain behind it, and the procedure for wanting a watch-board slot is to file through Malle, whose day is a solid block of watch assignments. What he actually needs is a second, independent trace on good glass — and the only good glass in reach is a fresh bundle Pell finished at dawn and that the tower crew are coming to collect this morning: the permanent replacement for the Ninth Ward tower's cracked signal pane, a job the Office deferred for two years because nobody would move on it.

**Information revealed:** The Ninth Ward has been running a temporary pane with an air bubble in the middle of it, which is why its public notice has been unreadable for a fortnight and why the poorest ward in the city has been living off handwritten copies. That is the entire reason the tower's glass is in a glassblower's shop instead of in a tower.

**Action or decision:** He cuts his private plate out of the tower's pane, on Pell's terms — Pell keeps the money coming, Marek does the tower's glasswork in exchange, and Marek carries the whole conversation into the tower office himself, which puts him in the Ninth Ward tower two days later whether the tower invites him or not. In the corridor outside the watch room, Malle stops him for thirty seconds and asks the question he has never been asked before: *if you are right, what would I see that would prove it.* Marek gives him a falsifiable answer that is entirely true and answers nothing. It is the first of several.

**Emotional change:** The specific, unresentful shame of having spent a poor ward's public warning on a private certainty, and the first small cold satisfaction of having been asked, for the first time in his career, to say what would prove him right.

**Power or resource change:** Read only, no spend. Resource change: one excellent private plate he is not allowed to publish, and a standing debt to Pell and to the Ninth Ward tower that is now physical rather than abstract.

**Continuity fact:** The Ninth Ward's public signal glass has been unreadable for a fortnight and stays that way to Chapter 48. **It is not dark because of Marek** — the frame has held a bubbled temporary since a fortnight before the opening, and 4486 was never installed. Marek's private sequence plate is built out of the tower's permanent pane, and the tower now has a physical reason to see him. Malle has asked for a falsifiable account, and received a true one that is not an account.

**Ending type:** Failure, honestly labelled. The goal does not happen; the proof exists and belongs to him alone and is unusable.

**Next-chapter pull:** The watch board's day clerk is Tovan Iles, and she has never taken a claim from a maintenance hand. She is also the only person in the building who copies a claim into the ward ledger and strikes the bell, which means the only way to get a reading in front of the city is to hand it to the one woman whose whole job is to make it official.

### Chapter 0004

**POV:** Tovan Iles.

**Location/time:** The ward ledger bench, Weatherglass ground floor, 09:15, day seven. A long room, a numbered bench, a bell, a rack of copy sheets, and nine ward heads on the wall.

**Immediate goal:** Clear the bench before the watch bell, and do not put her hand on anything that belongs to a ward she does not understand. She is one examination from a glass-tender's post, which is a real job with a real wage, and the ward heads are looking at her.

**Resistance:** Marek arrives with a reading that has no claim in it — an order of movements, no place, no time, no effect. The bench rules have no drawer for that. If she writes it up it becomes an unattached observation, a category the Office can reclassify at will and which nobody has to answer for. If she does not, it disappears into the day's noise, which is what happens to most readings.

**Information revealed:** What a claim is, as a job rather than a doctrine. Place, time or trigger, effect, and a witness — and for a public claim the witness is her pencil, the entry sheet, and the bell she strikes for that ward's head. A claim becomes real not when he is right but when the bell sounds and somebody is sent to act on it. She also explains, correctly and without malice, why the Office is frightened of readings nobody acted on: an observation is not a promise, and an observation nobody acted on cannot be made to mean anything later.

**Action or decision:** She files the reading as an unattached observation, because that is the honest drawer, and stamps it. Then she names the emergency-ledger rule to him in passing — a public claim that touches a water system, a route, a hospital, a school, or a market has to be entered in the emergency ledger inside one cycle, and the Office knows it, and nobody has ever yet had to use it on a bench this quiet. Off the record, with her hand not on the bell, she asks the question the whole novel is going to keep asking: if a reading was never turned into anything anybody acted on, can it be said to have predicted anything at all? He answers badly. He says a reading that was never acted on is a reading that was never tested, and that this is not the same as a wrong one. She hears the evasion and lets it stand, because she is twenty-seven and has not yet learned to push.

**Emotional change:** Terror of a specific and reasonable kind — the fear of being the person whose handwriting turns out to matter. And the first faint, unexamined disappointment at a competent adult choosing a safe sentence.

**Power or resource change:** No weather power. Her authority grows by one entry of real weight in the day's rack, and Marek's standing with her becomes real in a way neither will name.

**Continuity fact:** The ward ledger's witness chain is pencil, entry sheet, and the bell struck for that ward's head. A reading with no place, time, or effect is an unattached observation, and unattached observations are reclassifiable. This is the machine that will bury the Bellweather entry, shown to the reader three chapters before it is used on Marek.

**Ending type:** Discovery, and the batch's rule lesson. Nobody in this chapter learns anything about the weather and the reader learns everything about power.

**Next-chapter pull:** Anwen Sarr's assembly comes in at the far end of the bench asking what the Office will publish for Hearthward, and a conditional claim is the only shape that will get them anything before the front. Marek has an order of movements, a district that needs its water held, and no instrument chain. Tovan has a bell she has never struck for a weather claim.

### Chapter 0005

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The ward ledger bench, then the public forecast hall, 11:00, day seven. The batch's structural turn.

**Immediate goal:** Get Hearthward's stored water through the front's leading rain without the terraces going under, in a form that will be acted on inside six hours.

**Resistance:** An unconditional claim is not available to him and he knows it. What is available is the conditional shape, and the condition is the ward's to accept rather than his. What he does not have is any instrument chain on Hearthward at all — he has never been to the terraces, has never seen the outfall, and cannot tell Anwen Sarr what a full drain on that ground does over nine hours. The claim has to be written anyway, because the rain is coming, and the honest thing and the useful thing are the same shape here and only here. His narrowing is not a matter of hiding a cost he knows about. It is a matter of not knowing a cost well enough to ask about it, which is worse, and which he will not be able to tell apart from virtue for another twenty chapters.

**Information revealed:** A conditional forecast binds when its condition is accepted and triggered, not when it is written. Everything before that is a proposition. Everything after it is a spend with his name at the top.

**Action or decision:** He drafts it tight. Place, time, effect, listed alternative, expiry, named sponsor — and the effect is the whole of the claim's reach, including the part he has never seen: *the lower row drains clear.* Tovan enters it, names the emergency-ledger entry on the sheet, and strikes the bell for the yards' ward head and for Hearthward, which is the sound that makes it real. In the hall nobody applauds, because a bell for a ward is an ordinary thing that happens about nine times a week.

**Panel:** one, short, immediately before the entry.

```text
CLAIM: the spill gate holds shut — stored water to the third watch, upper and middle rows dry, lower row drains clear.
ONE POSSIBLE TURN WILL CLOSE.
```

**Emotional change:** Terror, immediately followed by the specific, unfamiliar relief of a problem that is now somebody else's problem to act on. He has never felt that relief before and he does not examine it.

**Power or resource change:** The first binding of the batch. Cost: inner-ear pressure, a metallic taste that lasts four hours, and the beginning of an inability to look away from a movement that has already stopped. He does not know yet that anything closed.

**Continuity fact:** The Hearthward conditional claim is witnessed, entered, bell-struck, entered in the emergency ledger, expiring at the third watch, and true. The ward ledgers record it as his. The claim's stated effect includes the lower row draining clear, and the lower row does drain clear.

**Ending type:** Decision, taken almost correctly by a man who does not know enough. The batch turns here.

**Next-chapter pull:** For the leading rain, the terraces get exactly what he promised them. The rain comes, the gate holds, the stored water is intact, the upper and middle rows are dry — and the lower row's drains, cleared on Tuesday, run at capacity for nine hours instead of three, with six ground-floor rooms sitting below the surcharge line the whole way.

### Chapter 0006

**POV:** Tarin Senn.

**Location/time:** Hearthward terraces, 23:00, day seven. The front's leading rain. Kestrel mooring crews on the grass above the market, fifty or sixty people in a wet market, and a shed at the bottom of the lower row where the rain-garden crew keep their tools.

**Immediate goal:** Get the *Wren* and two other ships onto the grass above the Hearthward market, tied down in a crosswind coming round, and keep fifty market people out of the way while she does it.

**Resistance:** The claim held. The rain came. The water came. The lower row's drains filled at 23:40 and took until 08:00 to run clear, which is three times as long as it takes when the drains are clear, and six ground-floor rooms on the lowest row sit below the surcharge line for the whole of it. The seed stock in the vaults' lowest drawers is ruined. Nobody dies. It reads, to everyone standing in it, as weather.

**Information revealed:** The cost of the conditional claim, seen by a witness who does not know she is seeing it. Tarin has landed on Hearthward a hundred and fifty times and knows the ground: she watched the drains being cleared on the previous Thursday because she was standing on the grass above them, and she knows that a clear drain on the third night of rain is a Thursday problem that has quietly moved itself to next Tuesday. She cannot get from there to *a claim closed a possibility*, so she does the only useful thing available and asks a question out loud.

**Action or decision:** She secures the ships, counts sixty-one people out of the market, and then asks Marek — who has been on the terrace with the ward glass since eight the previous evening — who stood the garden crew down, and when. He does not know. He says he does not know, which is true, and he does not say the rest of it, which is not.

**Emotional change:** Hers is the beginning of real anger, and it is not yet pointed at a person. His is a small, extremely precise shame that arrives with the sentence he chose not to say, and he recognises it immediately and puts it away, which is the part that should worry the reader.

**Power or resource change:** No weather power used by anyone. Physical cost: six ground-floor rooms, a season's seed stock, and a market that will open late for a month.

**Continuity fact:** The flood on Hearthward's lower row was caused by a branch that closed, not by a wrong forecast. The claim was true and the lower row did drain clear. The drains were cleared on the Tuesday and were not cleared again. Tarin and Marek have now had a real conversation for the first time in two years, and it consisted of one question and one half-answer.

**Ending type:** External climax. The batch's most expensive hour, and the reader has no idea what caused it.

**Next-chapter pull:** In the morning, the terrace foreman tells a glassblower that he has no memory of the night he did not work, and that he has a clear, specific feeling of relief about it. He is not lying. That is worse.

### Chapter 0007

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass, 08:30, day eight. His bench, then a corridor, then the floor outside Malle's office. Cold, bright, much too quiet.

**Immediate goal:** Work out whether the Hearthward flood was a coincidence, a bad drain, or something he did, without writing any of it down where a clerk could find it.

**Resistance:** The traces. On his private plate the Release movement of the second turn does not recover at the moment the physics says it must; it recovers about forty minutes late, and in the gap there is a stretch of nothing, which is the shape a closed possibility leaves when you look at a sequence closely enough. He can see it and he cannot prove it, because the only other plate that shows the same gap is the one he cut himself, three nights ago, at his own bench, with no provenance, in a ward whose public glass is dark and whose clerk has no reason to believe the maintenance hand who ruined it. Inner-ear pressure. A taste like a coin. Fine sound gone from one side and the loss not yet explained.

**Information revealed:** The mechanism, at the level of a working man rather than a doctrine. Hearthward's water held. The garden crew's clearing of the lower drains had not happened, and could not now happen, because the branch in which it happened was the branch in which the lower row had needed it — and a claim's success closes the turn that would have prevented it, whether that turn is a weather outcome or four people with shovels and a two-day window. His binding worked. It saved two hundred and twenty people on those terraces a wet night and it closed a door with people behind it.

**Action or decision:** He reconstructs it alone, at his bench, with a pencil and no register. Then he makes the decision that is the real content of this volume's first ten chapters: he does not file it. He writes the times on the inside of his left cuff, where the plate number already is. He goes to Malle and gives him a falsifiable account that is technically responsive and describes the shuffled order, the arrival times, and the exhausted instruments — and leaves out the gap. Malle asks the one question that would have caught it, which is what the claim had displaced, and Marek answers around it, and Malle lets it go because he has a front to staff. The reader knows exactly what just happened and nobody in the building does.

**Emotional change:** The moment his self-image and his behaviour come apart, with no one watching. He tells himself he is protecting the ward's water and his own standing, and he is not yet the kind of man who would ask which.

**Power or resource change:** Read and reconstruction. No binding, no spend, no panel. Strain worsens permanently: he can no longer stop seeing a movement that has already stopped, and he does not yet know that this is a symptom rather than a gift.

**Continuity fact:** Marek knows the Hearthward claim spent the garden crew's clearing and has told no one. The record contains no evidence, because the second plate is his own and unlogged and the cuff note is in his coat. Malle has now asked twice what would prove Marek right and what a claim displaces, and has been given a true answer and a circle.

**Ending type:** Moral climax and low point. Cost, and the deliberate choice to pay it quietly.

### Chapter 0008

**POV:** Neris Kade.

**Location/time:** The Ninth Ward, Low Spindle, 16:00, day eight. Shelters, pumps, a school, a ward assembly with a bad chair, and the worst-maintained instruments in the city.

**Immediate goal:** Get one usable warning into the lowest four blocks of the Ninth Ward, find out who to shout at if it is wrong, and get the ward's school emptied before the second belt arrives.

**Resistance:** The notice she has is technically accurate and practically useless. It names a window and an effect and says nothing about when the pumps should run, when the school should empty, or what to do with the ninety people on the ground floors who will not move. Her ward's own tower has no readable public glass, and when she sends a runner to the Weatherglass to ask why, she is told the pane is out for replacement and the replacement has been delayed. **Power or resource change:** No weather power. She acquires an independent reading source and an obligation to it, and accepts in writing and against her own judgement the duty of maintaining a public list of omissions, which will be expensive.

**Continuity fact:** Neris Kade maintains a list of what the notices do not say. Sable Orin has published an unsigned reading that Neris acted on, which made it a binding, and the Office filed it as an unattached observation. The Ninth Ward's public glass is out and delayed, and a clerk has told Neris why. The mechanism of a binding, witnessed at its smallest and least consequential, in the district with the least equipment and the most need.

**Ending type:** Discovery, and the turn toward consequence.

**Next-chapter pull:** Anwen Sarr has read Marek's claim on the ward board the way everyone else did, and she has read the glass twice. She is coming to the Weatherglass, and she is bringing the foreman who does not remember the night he did not work.

### Chapter 0009

### Chapter 00010

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass, 17:00, day nine, one hour before the front. The batch's aftermath and hook.

**Immediate goal:** Read the roster, understand what is being asked of him, and find out why the Ninth Ward tower's chart provenance line has been trimmed.

**Resistance:** The arithmetic. The yards want the corridor, the low wards want rain, and the same western draw cannot do both — and the Exchange's purchased window is admissible in the room because four licensed carriers have already paid against it, so refusing it is a commercial problem as well as a scientific one. Nessa Tarrow is instructed to copy the official notice and not the counter-reading, and does so without much thought, and a reader who has been paying attention knows exactly what that costs. Sable publishes a verified reservation of the corridor turn — that the yards must be under sail by 04:00 to clear the return lane — and Lior Venn reclassifies it in an afternoon, correctly and without heat, as an unattached observation: it was never lodged, so it is not compellable, and it cannot be produced later. Marek's private plate agrees with Sable. It is the first corroboration he has ever had, and it has already been filed away. Then, at the end of the day, doing the only job he is still trusted with — repairing chart glass — he finds that the Ninth Ward chart's upper margin has been trimmed, and that the trim is old and the paper around it is not.

**Information revealed:** The city's first working system for not knowing, in three rooms and one sentence. And then a piece of paper. The provenance line for the Ninth Ward tower's chart carries a **lodger's name in a hand Marek knows** — his mother's — a filing mark, and a sequence chart reference. Beside the signature there is a gap where an index reference used to be, cut out by hand, close enough to the cut edge to show the knife was steady. The filing mark says a witnessed claim was lodged against the Ninth Ward. The citation says somebody went back and removed the way to find it.

**Action or decision:** He writes the time, the plate number, and the filing mark on the inside of his left cuff, where the other notes already are, in pencil, on a coat, and tells nobody. He has spent a week learning that written evidence in the wrong drawer is worse than no evidence, and he has an hour to decide which drawer is the wrong one.

**Emotional change:** Not despair. The specific, dangerous lift of a man who has found proof that the institution he distrusts is wrong, at the exact moment he has proved that distrusting it is not the same as being right, and whose mother's handwriting is on the wall in front of him for the first time in twelve years.

**Power or resource change:** No spend. The cuff now holds three readings and one filing mark. The front is an hour out, the watch is staffed, the Ninth Ward's public glass is dark, and Marek is on a roster with a claim he has not written and a collision nobody else can see the order of.

**Continuity fact:** The Ninth Ward chart margin carries a lodger's signature Marek recognises, a filing mark for a witnessed claim, and a hand-cut index reference. The reader is not told whose hand cut it, what the index covered, or why. Sable's corridor reservation has been reclassified as an unattached observation. Nessa Tarrow copied the official notice and not the counter-reading. The twelve-year trace in the tower's sequence plate is a Batch 0002 discovery and must not be read in this chapter. Marek has told nobody any of this.

**Ending type:** Earned hook. The batch's work is done and the next one is defined: Chapters 11–21 are the Kestrel Line cooperation, the open readings, a field trip into the Ninth Ward tower, a father in Low Spindle, and a trial claim that works.

**Next-chapter pull:** The watch begins. Marek has a handful of hours to work out how to make a claim true about a corridor that also has to be a place people can be rescued into, and he has three pencil notes on a cuff that are the only uncorroborated evidence in the city. The front arrives with an order of movements only he can read, and the room that will hear him has already agreed on its answer.
