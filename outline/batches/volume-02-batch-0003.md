## Batch goal

**A preparation cannot be priced by anybody who did not intend it, and a tide can, and the batch finds that out the expensive way.** The assessor has a ledger with one line in it that says the first claimant's figure is unknown and she expires at the end of the financial year, and she asks a ward for a figure and is given the two figures there are and an account of the thing between them, which is a wall. A market which has struck a line through twice in public cannot sell the thing behind it, and so it has to admit that a number exists, and the only number in this basin it owns was bought off a councillor in a coat for eleven shillings on the second of Sleetmonth. A twenty-nine-year-old underwriter goes down the hill road, reads the only book in the basin that anybody who lives here can check, and works out the whole of the compensation problem in about a page of arithmetic: **only a party who intended a preparation can ever be compensated for one, which means a ward, a yard, a lighterman and a salvage man, and not the two hundred and forty children, who are in somebody else's plan.** A reader pays a counter-reader eleven shillings and a shilling to go up a ladder he has not gone up, and holds a trace across two towers for the first time, and the cost is a metallic taste and a left ear that is quieter afterwards, and the thing that comes of it is that **the ninth ward can now say, in its own words and with a date on it, when its own reach is coming down — and a district's own hours are not a window and are not a notice and are the first notice the ward has had since the frame went dark.** A Director's second circular, four paragraphs, every word of it true, says that a level is not a sequence and that the Office will not cut or sell sequence glass to any party but a ward tower, and it is the most frightening document of the volume and it is signed by a man who has never lied. A salvage man who has been buying the same forty minutes for eleven years writes down, on ordinary paper, in an ordinary hand, exactly what he intended to do in a reach, and leaves it on a shed counter and sends it to nobody, and it is the only document in the volume that prices a preparation. A lighterman spends the last big spring tide on the sixth ward's channel to keep forty tons of stone off a berth that is not to be worked, and writes his cost in pencil on the back of a Council form, **and the tide does not come up, and the tide is the first thing anybody in this basin has ever spent that was not weather at all.**

## Starting condition

**The evening of Sunday the twentieth day of Drownmonth.** The season's band held and the basin is wet and there is no weather in this batch to speak of. A temporary compensation ledger has been open for two days on the ground floor of the Council's basin house. It is a bound book about two inches thick, it is temporary, it is provisional, **it expires at the end of the financial year**, and it has three entries: the assessor's nineteen words, a warden's fair hand, and a copy clerk's true copy in a tray.

**Every one of the three is an admission that nobody can produce a number.**

The Exange has struck a line through twice in public in one quarter, on the same reach, at the same price, and has paid three refunds, and has told nobody what it is going to do about it. A counter clerk said out loud on a step in the rain that a market cannot go on selling the thing behind a line it has struck through twice, and a partner put a shorter version of it on the wall in the firm's own hand, and nobody has done anything about it. **A board on the tannery side of Tannery Walk carries a reader's working with two named readers, two public corrections and an unresolved disagreement about a movement, and a line at the bottom saying nobody must do anything because of it.** A third object is in the Exchange's printed list at threepence a week and nobody has put the two together and Sable Orin must not. The ninth ward's line is in the register for the first time since the season of three hundred and fifty-five, written by a clerk of Hearthward and carried in off the street. **And the Exchange has a level instrument in a wall at the south end of the Bellweather freight apron, with a glass datum in it cut and seated by Marek Vale, and a market now owns the only measurement of a river in the city, and a Director has said in writing that the Office has never had one and will not build one and does not consider the absence a defect in its work.**

## Required world reveals

1. **A loss that is a period cannot be entered as a sum, and the Council's only instrument for a sum is a figure a claimant supplies, and the Council may not invent one.** The two shillings and sixpence stands and the ledger does not grow.

3. **A level is not a sequence, and a figure of depth however published is evidence of the publisher's belief and not of the water.** The Office says this in print in Chapter 77 and it is correct and it is the sentence that makes the Exchange's product a number.
4. **The sequence plate in a reader's hand and the sequence plate in a tower's seat are the same object and the Office's stock is the only stock there is, and a Director may lawfully decline to sell one.**
5. **A tide is arithmetic and needs no reader, and that is the only reason a claim about one can be certified on an evening's notice — and it is also the reason nobody in this basin has ever bound one, because a tide is not a possibility, it is a schedule, and a district that spends a schedule is spending the only thing it had.**
6. **A market which cannot sell a reach has to sell the record of the reach, and the record is worth what the reach is worth, and the only figure of that reach the firm owns was bought from a councillor in a coat.**
7. **A claim by a private trader in a ward breaches that trader's own contracts, and the ward's claim breached nobody's, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of this volume's argument about consent.**

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 00071 — A Year Is Not a Duration

**POV:** Sabin Orle.

**Location/time:** The Council's basin house, ground floor, Drownmonth the twentieth, a Sunday, from about ten.

**Immediate goal:** Get a second line into a book that expires at the end of the financial year, without inventing a number.

**Resistance:** The only parties who can produce a figure are the parties who intended a preparation, and this basin has three of those and none of them is in the building. **A loss that is a period cannot be entered as a sum.** The ward can be asked and she asks, in a letter of four lines that goes out on the Monday, and the answer comes back on the Wednesday in Neris Kade's working hand and it gives her the two figures there are and an account of what is between them.

**Information revealed:** *A year is four hundred and thirty mornings on which nine men did not go out to a flat because there was no water off it, and there is no column in the world for that, and if you build one I will fill it in and I will want it read out in a room.* **And the line that is the chapter: what is between the two figures is a wall, and a wall is a wall.** Also: **the Council may not invent a figure.** The assessor's own power is to adopt an interim figure where a claimant cannot be identified, and she has used it once, and using it twice for the same event would be an invention, and she will not do it, and she is frightened of how easily she could.

**Action or decision:** She rules that **the interim figure of two shillings and sixpence stands for the whole of the season and not for each event**, which caps the Council's exposure to a sum no larger than a district's day rate, and which is honest and cold and correct, and it means the ward is paid for a morning and not for a wall. And she does the second thing: she puts her own nineteen-word finding on the flyleaf into a second minute of her own, with its date, **because a finding that names a district's silence and is not copied anywhere is a finding that can be removed by whoever removes the page.**

**Power or resource change:** No spend, no panel, no claim. The ledger does not grow.

**Continuity fact:** The 2/6 arithmetic is **not** re-derived and must not be altered: 45d and 16d, added and halved, to the nearest penny downward. The four-thirty figure is a district's own and is the only new number in the chapter and it is not money.

**Ending type:** A ruling that pays a district less than it lost, correctly, by a woman who will not explain herself.

**Next-chapter pull:** A market that cannot price a reach has to say something, and the firm has an instrument and a rule against asking and a form with nothing in it, and the board is due at eleven on the Monday.

### Chapter 00072 — The Price Of A River

**POV:** Edda Tarn.

**Location/time:** The Exchange's counting floor, quotation hall and forward board, Drownmonth the twenty-first and twenty-second.

**Immediate goal:** Re-issue the Bellweather reach line for the autumn low on a board that has to be up at eleven, when the firm has struck that line through twice in public at one price.

**Resistance:** The refund rule publishes the price of a withdrawn window, so the firm has already published two prices for the same forty minutes and struck them. **A market that has struck a line through twice cannot sell the thing behind it, and the only lawful way out is to admit that a number exists.** The firm has an instrument — a copper float, a helical drum, a chain and a glass datum cut by a censured reader — and it has no use to price it against, because its own rule excludes nobody and the district that has a use will not answer.

**Information revealed:** **A market that cannot sell a reach has to sell the record of the reach.** The firm's answer is to publish the instrument's own log on the board: the level, the dates, the two struck lines, the three refunds, and *what the instrument cannot see*, printed in the firm's own hand. **And the sentence a nineteen-year-old clerk writes on the back of a form: a level is not a sequence, and if we print the level and call it a reading somebody will believe it, and then it is a lie with a date on it.**

**Action or decision:** She puts the record up. It is the first public measurement of a river in this basin. And she uses her own fourth line — *ask the districts, not for their schedules* — and the firm issues a request to nine districts that each may refuse, which is the first time the firm has ever asked a district anything at all, **and it costs a morning of a licensing clerk's time and it frightens Hesk, and Edda Tarn does not care.**

**Power or resource change:** No spend, no panel, no claim.

**Continuity fact:** The Exchange's list price of **threepence a week** for a supplied corrected reading stands and is **thirteen shillings a year, and two years of it is one pound six.** The board in the quotation hall and the free sheet at Mrs Bain's counter and the printed list are the **three places** the volume's resolution requires, and Ch 82 uses all three.

**Ending type:** A market printing a river for the first time, and being frightened of what it has printed.

**Next-chapter pull:** A record of a river on a board is a number nobody has checked, and the only check in the basin is a chain, a staff and a post in a shed, and a man of twenty-nine has been told to come and read the book.

### Chapter 00073 — What A Preparation Costs

**POV:** Tobias Mear.

**Location/time:** The south-bank shed and a wall by the canal, Drownmonth the twenty-third, a Wednesday. **Midpoint.**

**Immediate goal:** Work out what a reach is worth now that two lines have been struck through at one price and a Council has paid two shillings and sixpence.

**Resistance:** He comes to read Petrie's book, as he was told to, and reads four pages of a level book with the chain readings in a column for nine years, **and it settles nothing, because a level book has never once recorded a plan.** So he does the only thing available to him, which is arithmetic, on the back of a Council form, in pencil, over about four hours: the cost of a preparation is the men plus the gear plus **the cost of the thing you did not do**, and the second term is the whole of it and is not in anybody's book.

**Information revealed:** **Only a party who intended a preparation can ever be compensated for one.** A preparation is a plan and a plan has a name on it. In this basin that is four classes of party — a ward, a yard, a lighterman, a salvage man — **and the two hundred and forty children at the Sump Lane school are in four plans and are named in none of them, and neither is the woman on the ground floor of the third block.** He writes it down and it is eleven lines and it is the volume's thesis and it is not a speech, and it is on the back of a Council form in a hand that is not a clerk's.

**Action or decision:** He sends it to the assessor, in an envelope with nothing on it, for the second time in a month, **and this time he does it with a copy of the two lines' arithmetic in it, so the Council has a figure that is not a firm's and not a district's and is his.** And he writes at the top of his own page: *this is not a claim and not a complaint and I am not a district and I am not owed anything.*

**Power or resource change:** No spend, no panel, no claim.

**Continuity fact:** **Petrie's book settles a level and nothing else, and Petrie says so and does not know he is saying it.** The four figures stay where they are: **7/6, 3/9, 1/4, and 2/6 as an interim the Council adopted.** Mear does not touch any of them and does not derive a fifth.

**Ending type:** A man who prices things for a living finds the shape of the whole problem and finds it unpayable, and writes it down anyway.

**Next-chapter pull:** A Council minute that names a district's silence is now a public record, and the clerk who wrote the sentence that caused it is a clerk of another district, and the Board has written back.

### Chapter 00077 — Four Paragraphs

**POV:** Ilse Doff.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass maintenance shop on the ridge, Drownmonth the twenty-seventh, a Sunday.

**Immediate goal:** Work out what a circular from the Director changes about a shop that cuts glass.

**Resistance:** **Circular 15 is four paragraphs and every one of them is true.** A level is not a sequence. The Office has no instrument which looks at a river and will not build one and does not consider the absence a defect. **The Office will not cut, grind or supply a sequence plate for any party other than a ward tower, and the reason given is that a plate is an instrument of record and a plate in a private hand is a reading no public body can check.** And the fourth paragraph: where a party's figure has been used in another party's product, the Office has no duty and no instrument.

**Information revealed:** **A Director may lawfully close a trade in glass, and the closing takes a week and a signature, and nobody has to do anything dishonest to achieve it.** And the reason it is frightening is not the third paragraph: it is that the ninth ward's new hours, printed on a shutter by two readers with a date on them, are a level and a set of hours, and paragraph four declines to have anything to do with them, and the ward's public glass is still dark, so the shutter is still the only notice the district has.

### Chapter 00082 — The Number On The Board
