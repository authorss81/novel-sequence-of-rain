## Batch goal

A claim is not weather, so a claim is a breach, and somebody has to answer for a breach — and the party that has to answer is a Storm Warden with no money and a solicitor on the Nacre Gate. Meanwhile an instrument maker's form comes back from the Council's technical cell **with nothing in it**, and the firm that asked for it discovers that it cannot fill it in itself, and the question the register raises — *who stopped asking the ninth ward* — is put, in a room, by a person with a grudge and a memory, and the answer turns out to be a job rather than a conspiracy. **And Marek learns that the only way to hold a sequence across two towers is to stop being the only one, and that the woman who will hold the other end charges for it and attaches a condition, and the condition is better than his and is not consent.**

**The batch cliff is Chapter 70: a district's own figure at last, on a fair hand, in a book, and the person who has to sign it is the man who spent the corridor, and he is asked whether he will.**

## Starting condition

The second of Drownmonth. A ward has bound a true claim that stopped a reach window and cost itself a repair. The Exchange has paid its own buyer thirteen shillings and declined in writing to claim anything against the ward. A Council assessor has minuted a limitation on a reader. A copy clerk's box has two documents in it. A tower keeper has a fifth column nobody asked for. A counter-reader is on a bench with a fee and a term. **And the Exchange's clerk went to the technical cell four weeks ago for the standing schedule of what each ward is able to do about its water, and has not come back with it.**

Three documents in this city now carry the twenty-third and none of them can be used in a room:

1. **The Office's published column**, which says a window was quoted and not opened and gives no reason, because there is no instrument in the Office which looks at a reach.
2. **The entered claim sheet**, with the ward's own ninety words in capitals in the seventh box, on a rail for ninety days, in a room on the ground floor.
3. **Tovan Iles's second true copy**, in a locked box on the same floor, which is the only document in the basin that says *THE WARD IS CLOSING ITS OWN REPAIR WINDOW TO STOP A SECOND SALE OF IT. That is the cost and it is not money*, and which cannot be produced to anybody, including its author.

And one document that the ward's own negligence destroyed: **an eighteen-page quarterly return in a market file with a receipt for eleven shillings, and not in the register.**

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 00059 — The Form With Nothing In It

**POV:** Oren Lyle, nineteen, a clerk of the Cloud Exchange.

**Location/time:** The technical cell on the ground floor of the Council's basin house, Drownmonth the third, from about ten.

**Immediate goal:** Collect a form the firm has paid four pounds eleven for in fees and time, and bring it back in a condition that can be filed.

**Resistance:** The form is a **request for a schedule**, and a Council has no procedure for issuing one, and the clerk who handles it is a records officer in the basement who treats an unanswerable request as a category. **The form comes back with the heading filled in, the season filled in, and nothing under it.** The ninth ward's line is empty and the fourth's is empty and the sixth's has a run of nine. **And the same is true of a form the Exchange fills in itself**, which is the discovery of the chapter: the firm is as bad as the Council, because the firm has never asked a district what it can do either, because asking costs a relationship and not asking costs a client.

**Information revealed:** **A form that is filled in is a record and a form that is not is a silence, and the silence has a date.** A records officer names the year — the fiftieth — and names a man: *there was a fella wanted it done properly and then he went, and after that nobody's wanted it.* He is not hiding anything and is annoyed on the Council's behalf. He also says the thing that the volume is made of, without any idea: **that the schedule is a form a *district's own clerk* fills in, and that the district is not obliged to, and that nobody has ever written to a district saying it was.**

**Action or decision:** Lyle takes it back because he was told to bring it even if it is empty, **and he asks one question, which is out of scope and which is the whole of his character: he asks whether the Council has ever sent the form out.** It has not. It has never once. The form exists to be returned by a district that comes to it, and no district has come to it since the fiftieth, and the reason nobody has come is on page four of a form the ninth ward filled in once and never again.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.**

**Continuity fact:** The Exchange's standing instruction is *take the form they answer in, do not paraphrase it, and if they send us a form with nothing in it, I want that form with nothing in it, kept, dated, and brought to me.* **He brings it.** Mear's memorandum says *ask the technical cell what a ward can do about its water*; the two documents must not be confused and neither is a paraphrase of the other.

**Ending type:** A form, empty, dated, in a boy's satchel.

**Next-chapter pull:** Hesk has an empty form and a product that needs a full one, and a rule against asking, and the only party in the basin that has ever produced a schedule of a ward's water is a district that sold its own for eleven shillings.

### Chapter 00060 — A Partner's Mistake

**POV:** Maro Hesk.

**Location/time:** The Exchange's second floor, the fourth, from nine until dark; then a solicitor's room on the Nacre Gate in the evening.

**Immediate goal:** Fill a gap in his own product without breaking the one rule his firm is proud of, which is that it never asks a district anything.

**Resistance:** **The rule is not asked for. It is inherited and never examined, and this is the chapter in which a man who has built a career on a strength finds out it is a wall.** He cannot ask the ninth ward; if he asks, he is a party. **His four attempts each fail for a reason that is correct:** the Exchange's own act excludes its own commercial questions from a district's answers; a district's answer would be a document the firm then owns; a district's *schedule* is the thing he wants and a schedule is not an answer. **And then the lawyer tells him the thing that organises the rest of the volume: a schedule of a party's capabilities given to a counterparty without that party's knowledge is not evidence of the party. It is a list.** He is told, in a room on the Nacre Gate, by a solicitor of about fifty with a fee of a guinea, that he may have a document and may not use it, and that the only lawful route to a schedule of a ward is a **request the ward may refuse**, and the Exchange has never sent one because it has never been willing to be refused by a district it might want to do business with in eleven years.

**Information revealed:** **A refusal to ask is a commercial asset and it can be spent.** Also: the solicitor is **not** the ward's solicitor and cannot be, because the ward's man is on the Nacre Gate and has told the ward not to spend anything on it. **The Exchange could pay for the ward's lawyer and has not, and why it has not is the chapter's last line.**

**Action or decision:** **Hesk does the cheapest possible thing and it is the right thing, and he is the first person in the volume to do it: he writes to the ninth ward, through the district, in the ordinary way, asking for its schedule of available responses, and states on the face of the letter that the ward may refuse, that the Exchange will pay for the answer at the ordinary price, and that a refusal will be filed as a refusal and will not be held against the ward in any future transaction.** He signs it himself, in his own name, and does not have it put through a lawyer, and the reason is that a letter a district can refuse must look like it came from a man and not from a firm. **And the ward's answer comes back inside four days, and it is a refusal, and it is the best document in the volume so far.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.** What he spends is the firm's oldest and cheapest strength.

**Continuity fact:** **The Exchange is not a party to any district's preparations and is not required to price them** is the Charter line, and it is now a live problem and not a records curiosity. **Nobody merges that line with the Deputy for Records' hand on it and with the technical cell's register.**

**Ending type:** A firm asks, and is refused, in writing, at eleven shillings' postage.

**Next-chapter pull:** The refusal names the person it was written to and the reason, and the reason is that the ward has nothing to give anybody, because its own figures went to the Council two years ago in a return and are in a press downstairs and not in the register — and because a warden who has spent her ward's window on purpose cannot now be seen writing to the firm that sold it.

### Chapter 00061 — Six Catch Pits

**POV:** Neris Kade.

**Location/time:** Low Spindle and the Bellweather freight apron, Drownmonth the sixth, a Saturday, from seven in the morning until the light goes.

**Immediate goal:** Get the low ground ready for a back-cut that nobody has told her about, with a reach that is coming down whether anybody is ready or not.

**Resistance:** **The reach is coming down and the market has already sold the window.** Word comes into the district on the Friday from a chandler's counter, in a printed line, with a price on it, and the line says a reach window for the morning of the seventeenth at seven and six, refundable at three and nine, and the ward did not buy one and has been closed out. **And the reach is coming down anyway, because weather does not consult a price list.** A reach-level window is about nine usable days a year and there is one coming and no window on it, and the district's only asset in this is six catch pits, a barrow, two wardens, and a crew at the south-bank shed that has been on the books since the sixth of the Bellweather month.

**Information revealed:** **A reach window is not bought for a price; it is bought for a *tide*, and the tide is the ebb, and the ebb is a sequence fact, and no market sells a sequence.** The Exchange's four-hours-agreeableness is a level instrument and a set of hours. **What the district needs is not forty minutes of certain water. It is a bend, and a bend is a turn, and a turn is the thing the Office will not put on a wall and the Exchange cannot see and the reader in a coat with one ear will not commit to paper.** **And the district has just proved it can be trusted with its own reach, because it closed its own window, and the price of that trust is a year.**

**Action or decision:** **She does not buy a window and she does not claim anything.** She does the free thing, which is the whole of Volume 02's argument: she writes the ward's clearing rota, shelter rota and pump order onto one dated sheet, in her own hand, on the ordinary form, and sends it to the warden's council and to nobody else, and **it is a voluntary document and it is unpublishable and it is the first thing the ninth ward has produced for anybody in two years that nobody asked for.** She then goes down to the south bank and tells Petrie to have the flatbed's ground inspected and says the words *if the mark comes off the post we do not go,* and Petrie looks at her for about four seconds and says the thing that is the chapter's ending: **he does not ask why, and she does not say, and he has been waiting nine weeks for somebody to say anything at all about the thirtieth, and a woman from a district office has just told him to inspect the ground of a flat he has wanted to load for two months, and he is a shed foreman and not a fool.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.** Caution is free and uncounted and this is the batch's demonstration of it: a warden spends nothing and is credited with nothing and does the only useful thing in the chapter.

**Continuity fact:** **The load is still there** — about nine hundred pounds of timber in four iron bands, a four-hundred-and-ten-pound pump on a pallet, nine crew on the district's books, order 114, a painted board with no name and the sixth of the Bellweather month on it, and a door with a chain on it. **The wall is on its second consecutive deferred season and there is a front in two years and about eleven months, which is a Friday, and it is a table on a wall.** Ampow's wet notebook gains a line. **The ninth ward's shelter rota card is still in a drawer and the sheet she has never been able to write is still unwritten, and the chapter must not resolve it, and the sheet she writes in this chapter is a *clearing* rota and not a *shelter* rota, and the difference is the point.**

**Ending type:** A woman doing the free thing properly, and a shed foreman not asking a question.

**Next-chapter pull:** A firm has an empty form and a ward has a fresh sheet nobody asked for, and the only way the two get into the same room is if somebody in between is paid to carry them, and the man in that position is a nineteen-year-old clerk with a satchel and a firm instruction.

### Chapter 00062 — The Fifth Question

**POV:** Ivo Slate.

**Location/time:** The Exchange's second floor, Drownmonth the seventh, a Sunday, from eleven.

**Immediate goal:** Find out what an empty form actually means commercially, without asking, because asking is what the firm is for and he is the firm's man.

**Resistance:** **Slate is the man who has done every courteous thing in this volume and this is the chapter where his courtesy is shown to be a method and the method is shown to be a wall.** He has four conversations on one Sunday morning, with a barge operator, a chandler, a carrier's clerk and a man from the works, and he learns four things he did not know, and he learns them because he asks, and asking is his job, and **he notices halfway through the fourth conversation that he has asked four questions and none of them was about the weather, and that this is the first time in eleven years he has had a day in which that was true.**

**Information revealed:** The window market's real product is not the window. **It is the fact that four unrelated people already agree on what the basin is doing, and the window is the receipt.** **A firm that stops asking stops knowing anything on Sunday, and a firm that starts asking a district anything is a firm that can be refused by a district, and a firm that can be refused has to be able to afford it.** **And the second back-cut is on the seventeenth and four of the four men know it and one of them has a price for it and none of them has a level instrument.**

**Action or decision:** Slate writes two lines. The first is a memorandum to the firm: *the Sunday question is a question about people and not about glass, and we have stopped doing it, and the day we started paying for readings instead we stopped asking the only people who answer for free.* The second is a letter to the ninth ward, unsigned, drafted in his own words, asking the ward what its available responses to a season's water are, and offering the ordinary price. **Hesk signs it. It goes out on the Monday. And the ward refuses it inside four days, in a hand Neris Kade has used every day of her working life, and the refusal is the best document in the volume, because it is in the ward's own words and it names the reason and the reason is true and the reason is that the ward has nothing to give anybody and that a district which has just spent its own window on purpose is not going to be seen writing to the firm that sold it.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.**

**Continuity fact:** Slate files a report on everything and this is the first one that argues against his own employer. **He is not a dissident and is not a plot device; he is a man who noticed something on a Sunday and wrote it down, and the volume does not tell him what it is worth.**

**Ending type:** A firm asking a question it has not asked in eleven years, and being told no in the plainest possible language.

**Next-chapter pull:** The refusal is in Neris Kade's hand and it names her district's one asset and it says the asset is not available, and there is a version of the ward's answer in a press on the ground floor of the Council's own building, in a fair hand, four years old, and the ward is not the only one that has an answer nobody ever asked for.

### Chapter 00063 — The Carrier's Man

**POV:** Tobias Mear.

**Location/time:** The yards and the freight apron, Drownmonth the ninth, a Tuesday, from eight.

**Immediate goal:** Find out whether the forward board's second back-cut is going to be sold twice, before somebody buys it, on the strength of one memorandum in pencil and a form with nothing in it.

**Resistance:** **He is an underwriter and he is doing the work of a market operator and he is not authorised to.** His own memorandum says two prices for two objects cannot be compared, and the chapter makes him try, and the trying is the chapter. **The second back-cut on the seventeenth is the season's biggest and the only one the Exchange can quote with a level instrument, and it is going to sell, and the second sale will be to a man with a gang and a barge, and by this batch's end Mear knows that the second sale has already been made and it is to a firm and not to a man.** He is also the only person in the basin holding both halves of the problem — the firm will not ask a district, and the Council's register is empty — **and he has one of the two documents that Volume 03 needs and does not know it, and in this chapter he copies his own memorandum and sends it to the Council's assessor because he has worked out that the only use a number like his has is in a claim nobody can bring.** That is the chapter's turn and it is a turn toward the civic and away from the commercial, and he does it for the wrong reason and it is still the right thing.

**Information revealed:** **A price that is wrong by a factor of five and a half in the seller's favour is not a lie and is a hazard, and a market that prints it is a market that has published a measurement it has not checked, and the only people who can check it are the people it did not ask.** The clause he is reading aloud in this chapter is **Mear's own, and it is the clause the volume's compensation argument will be built on in Act III: *a price published without a schedule of the use is a price for the publisher's buyer and not for the thing.***

**Action or decision:** He buys a second instrument for the second back-cut, on the Exchange's money, and installs it **on the yard's own slip at Kestrel, on Tarin Senn's ground, with her permission and against the Line's fifth condition, and the Line publishes that the yard's instrument is the yard's own.** He is therefore, in his own firm, the second person to be refused a second reader and the first person to be refused an instrument and to accept the refusal's price.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.**

**Continuity fact:** **The window of the seventeenth of Drownmonth is quoted at 7/6 with a refund at 3/9 and has not been claimed and will not be.** The register's three wards with nothing under them. Mear's memorandum: *7/6 against 1/4, five and a half times, these are not the same object, publish the first, hold this.*

**Ending type:** A man who prices things for a living deciding to publish his own mistake to a Council, and getting a reply within a week.

**Next-chapter pull:** The assessor has the memorandum, and the memorandum is a number, and a number with nobody's schedule attached to it is the exact thing a review is for, and the review is called on the Wednesday.

### Chapter 00064 — Who Stopped Asking

**POV:** Sabin Orle.

**Location/time:** The Council's basin house and then the technical cell, Drownmonth the tenth, a Wednesday, from about ten. **Midpoint.**

**Immediate goal:** Decide what to do with a memorandum from a commercial underwriter that contains a price, a comparison the author says is invalid, and an instruction that it be held, and which has arrived in her building with no sponsor and no claim attached to it.

**Resistance:** **It is not evidence of anything and she says so in the first minute.** A price is not a claim; a claim is not a purchase; a purchase is not a preparation; a preparation is not a cost. **And then she does the thing that makes this the batch's midpoint: she sends for the Standing Response Register and finds that the ninth ward's line is empty and the fourth's is empty and the sixth's has a run of nine, and she asks the one question nobody has asked, which is not *what happened* but *who filled it in and who stopped*, and gets an answer in under a minute, from a records officer, who gives her a year and a name and says that the year a man left, nobody wrote to any district again, and that the form has never once been sent out.** **Nobody conspired. It is a job that stopped, and the shape of a job that stopped is a shape that a person can be shaped by, and that is the volume's argument and it is worse than a conspiracy because it is cheaper and because everybody in the room was paid to do their part and did it.** **And the second half of the chapter is the discovery that the register's silence is not only the ninth ward's: the Exchange has a form with nothing in it too, because the firm never asked either, and Orle writes two identical sentences about two institutions and does not editorialise.**

**Information revealed:** **A form that is filled in is a record and a form that is not is a silence, and the silence has a date and a reason and neither is written anywhere, and the same sentence is true of a Council and of a market, and the reader is now holding the shape of Volume 03 in a paragraph the assessor did not intend to write.**

**Action or decision:** **She does three things and all three are correct and one of them is against her own house.** (1) She rules the memorandum inadmissible as evidence of any cost and admissible as a document of a party's belief, and says both in one sentence. (2) **She minuted the empty register as a *finding of the Council's own record* and not as a finding about a ward** — nineteen words, in the same register, in the same hand, on the same day, and it is the first time a technical cell has reported on itself. (3) She writes to the three districts and asks them, in the plainest form the Council has ever used, what the district can do about its water, and states that a district may refuse and that a refusal will be minuted as a refusal. **And the fourth thing she does is in a corridor, to a reader, and it is two sentences long and neither of them is about the register: she tells him what she minuted on the twenty-first of Sleetmonth and that it was a limitation and not a finding, and she asks him whether he would give evidence about a form he has never seen, and he says he would, and she says *I know you would, that is why I am telling you now rather than asking you in a room.***

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.**

**Continuity fact:** **The register's silence is a technical-cell fact and is not a Records act and is not Lior Venn's problem and must never be merged with it.** The rules of a *keeper's book* and of a *form in a press* are two different instruments. **Anwen Sarr's clause four is not discussed and she is not in this chapter and has not asked for this.**

**Ending type:** A nineteen-word self-finding, and a letter to three districts that is the first thing the Council has sent out of that building in a generation.

**Next-chapter pull:** Three districts, one of which will refuse, one of which will answer, and one of which will answer with a book nobody has ever seen, and the answers will arrive at different times and in different hands, and the first one to arrive is a sailor's.

### Chapter 00065 — Two Channels

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** Kestrel Yards and a room over the Ninth Baths, Drownmonth the twelfth, a Friday, from six in the morning.

She says the warden's term stands and she is not moving it and he can pay or he can go without her. **He pays and publishes it, and it is wrong twice, and she catches both, and the board carries the two corrections in her hand with the hours on them, which is the yard's column of differences reproduced by a counter-reader for nothing, and nobody in this basin has ever thought to ask for it.**

Two ends held at once put a thing in the middle that is in neither, and he notices it on the second afternoon, and does not report it, and **this is his flaw, performed for the first time in the commercial register, and it is the same refusal as Chapter 7 and Chapter 18 and it is on the page and nobody catches it.** Sable Orin catches something else instead and does not know what she caught.

### Chapter 00066 — The Price Of Looking

**POV:** Edda Tarn.

**Location/time:** The Exchange's second floor and a chandler's counter, Drownmonth the thirteenth, a Saturday, from ten.

**Immediate goal:** Explain to a firm why a hand-painted board in a shut shop is a commercial threat, and do it without the firm losing its temper or its product.

**Resistance:** **The board is better than the sheet.** It is dated, it is timed, it is corrected in public by a counter-reader with a fee and a term, and it is in the district the sheet is delivered to, and it is *signed by two people who can be named.* The sheet is anonymous, uncorrected, and printed on the same stock every morning. **And the commercial threat is not the board; it is the correction line.** A sheet that is corrected in private is a claim; a sheet that is corrected in public is a record, and a record can be compared with a price, and a price can be refused.

**Information revealed:** **A market cannot sell certainty against a record that is cheaper to check than to buy, and the Exchange's own refund rule is the mechanism of its own defeat, because a firm that has published a price once cannot unpublish it and has to keep publishing it.** **And the second effect, which nobody saw coming: the district's free service becomes worth something to the firm.** There are four other wards with dark glass and no free service, and a firm that can supply a corrected, timed, signed reading to a ward for eleven shillings a week has a product nobody in the basin has thought of, **and the counter-reader has just given the product away for nothing, in a ward, once, on a shutter.**

**Action or decision:** Edda Tarn does the correct commercial thing and is told to do it, and it is the chapter's turn: **she prices the shutter.** Not by closing it and not by buying it and not by writing to a ward. She puts a price on a counter-reader's free published reading, in the firm's own list, at a rate that reflects what it is worth to a buyer, and it is a very small number, **and that number is the price of everything Sable Orin has done for two years, and it is in a list, in a fair hand, on a wall, and a nineteen-year-old clerk will see it on Monday morning.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.**

**Continuity fact:** **Sable Orin's fee is eleven shillings a read and a retainer, in coin, in advance, and it is in the firm's list at a rate that is not eleven shillings and the two figures are not the same and never will be.** Nothing in this chapter touches her consent problem, and **she does not know the shutter is being priced in this batch.**

**Ending type:** A small number on a wall, and a woman with a cloth and a counter who does not know what she is looking at.

**Next-chapter pull:** A clerk sees a number and asks who set it, and the answer is a licensed agent's memorandum, and a partner asks a question on a Sunday, and the question is the one the whole volume has been circling: *what is it worth to a person, and how would anybody find out.*

### Chapter 00067 — A Shed Is Not A Document

**POV:** Petrie.

**Location/time:** The Bellweather freight apron and the ninth ward, Drownmonth the fourteenth, a Sunday, daylight.

**Immediate goal:** Refuse, politely, to be a party to four different things at once, and keep a shed.

**Resistance:** **Four people in one week want a shed for four different purposes and the shed contains one banded stack and a pump and a painted board and a level book with a column in it that a warden has now filled in.** A water office clerk wants the timber for a reversion audit in April. A district crew wants the level book copied for a return. A man from a firm wants to know whether the board's date is a date. And a forward board's agent wants to buy the flat's forty minutes for a photograph. **Petrie is a shed foreman of about sixty-eight and has kept the shed about thirty years and has a rule, and the rule is that a shed keeps a load and a load is not a document and a document is not a shed**, and he applies it four times in a day and it is correct four times, and **the fourth time it is the chapter.** He refuses the photograph and explains why in one sentence: *because the day it's on a sheet of paper with a number on it, it's a cost, and the minute it's a cost there's a man somewhere who wanted it cheaper, and then somebody comes down this road with a form.* **Doak, the municipal apron man, backs him on that and is the only person in the chapter who is not surprised.**

**Information revealed:** **The photograph is the real threat and nobody in the volume has seen it yet.** A market has discovered that a reach window can be sold on the strength of a photograph of a load that is not going anywhere, and a photograph is cheaper than a survey and there are nineteen of them in this basin. **Petrie's rule is the oldest compensation logic in the city: a thing that is written down and priced is a thing somebody can be made to pay for, and a thing that is kept is not, and the difference is worth about four pounds of timber and about a year of a wall.**

**Action or decision:** He writes **one** thing down, in his own level book, on the Sunday, in the correct column, and it is the first entry in thirty years in the column headed *no work, reason*, and it is not the warden's, because the warden's is in a different book, and his says: *Reach asked for by three parties this week. Sold to none. No work, because nobody has anything I can sell.* And then he does the chapter's real work: **he asks a question. He has never asked one. In about seventy seconds, at about four in the afternoon, standing in his own doorway, he asks Neris Kade what happened on the thirtieth, because for the first time in nine weeks somebody from a district office has told him to inspect a flat.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.** **Petrie suspects nobody and has never suspected anybody and is not going to, and the answer he gets in the next chapter is going to be a true sentence in the only vocabulary the conversation has, and that vocabulary is the reason it is not evidence.** This is *it was just the water* spoken the other way round, **about a different morning, by a different man, to a different person, and the echo is deliberate and must not be mistaken for a repetition.**

**Continuity fact:** The board is unchanged: **the sixth of the Bellweather month, nine crew, order 114, the load is here, painted by the foreman, no name, no thirtieth, no seal.** The load is unchanged: **about nine hundred pounds of timber in four iron bands, a four-hundred-and-ten-pound pump on a pallet with a Union guarantee on the drum, a cover that has been on since the eleventh of the Bellweather month, and nine crew on the district's books.** A front in **two years and about eleven months**, which is a Friday, and it is a table on a wall and not a threat, and he says so whenever anybody asks.

**Ending type:** A shed foreman asks one question in seventy seconds, having never asked one in thirty years.

**Next-chapter pull:** The answer to Petrie's question is going to be a technical one and there is nobody in this basin who can give it, and the only person who is not forbidden to is a counter-reader who charges eleven shillings, and on the seventeenth there is a reach coming down and a man in a boardroom who has never been on a freight apron in his life.

### Chapter 00068 — The Second Back-Cut

**Information revealed:** **A market's second sale is the whole of what a market is and the whole of what a market cannot defend.** The photograph bought a barge a window that was sold to a salvage firm the same afternoon, and the salvage firm was not a party to the second sale and would not have been, and **the second sale is legal, and the first sale is legal, and the two of them together mean that a reach is not scarce and no one who is told about it believes that.** The refusal rule is the instrument: *a window is sold to a party that can show a use,* and a salvage firm can show a use, and so can a pump gang, and the rule is exactly the rule that put the ward out of the market in the first place, **and a rule that excludes a district excludes anybody with the same shape, and the Exchange has a great many of them and does not know it and neither does the Council.**

**Action or decision:** The claim is entered, the panel appears, the back-cut comes on the morning of the seventeenth and **the reach does not come down, and the salvage does not go in, and the ward's own crew does not go out, and the crew of eleven on the contractor's books is stood down for a day and a half and paid for a day and a half, and the timber stays in the shed for a second consecutive season, and the wall is shored a third time out of other timber or it is not shored, and the front is in two years and about nine months.** And **the second line struck through on that wall in a public room, in a fair hand, with a price in the margin, in the same quarter, is the moment the volume's resolution becomes structurally possible: a market that has struck a line through twice in public cannot go on selling the thing behind it, and the firm knows it, and Edda Tarn says it out loud to a room and is not thanked for it.**
