## Batch goal

Turn a man with a private plate into a man who has been asked to put the instrument in a room with other people in it. Chapters 11–21 are the eleven chapters in which the Bellweather Front is behind them or overhead, the collision has come and gone without killing anybody, and the Weather Office discovers that the method which produced a good result has removed options nobody was shown.

Marek begins this batch believing the problem is that nobody will make the call. He ends it having watched a Council-certified call made correctly, cheaply, and in public applause, and having named in a room of applauding people the thing the applause was made of. Nobody can prove a word of what he says. That is the point of the batch.

**The batch must end on the reversal, not on the storm.** The storm's destructive pass, the Lower Sill culvert's failure, the convoy that never leaves, and Oren's chapter are Chapters 22–35 and must not be anticipated here.

## Starting condition

Day ten. The collision passed over the basin at about 18:00 on day nine and Marek read the order of the six movements aloud on the ward floor of the ninth ward to a crew of strangers and was right. Nobody died. Six ground-floor rooms on Hearthward's lowest row were ruined on the Thursday, a season of seed stock is in a market hall, the market is shut for a month, a school in Low Spindle was emptied on a warden's own authority at a cost out of four hundred families' mouths, and forty people in that district moved in the night on a number printed in a drying room above a public bath.

Marek holds: one private sequence plate, a coat cuff with two readings and a filing mark on it, a bench sheet in a flat drawer, a chart margin upstairs with a piece cut out of it, an unfiled tag for a March dropout, and a two-year unpaid debt to the poorest tower in the city. He has told nobody any of it. He is on a roster as a reader. He has been given the Bellweather watch and has already worked it.

## Required character changes

- **Marek** goes from a man who has hidden one cost to a man who has watched somebody else publish one, correctly, in front of applauding people. His private conviction — that the problem is that nobody will make the call — is falsified in the room where the call is being made. He is right about the method and it costs him both audiences.
- **Tarin** goes from a captain who has paid for being right to a captain who is asked to make somebody else's claim true with her own hull. She has to decide whether the Line is an instrument, and she decides it in public.
- **Tovan** goes from a clerk who has seen one claim enter the world to a clerk who sees a Council-certified one enter the world with four signatures on it, and who discovers that the more official a thing is the harder it is to hold.
- **Anwen** refuses, and is right to, and the reader must be able to see that she is right in the same chapter that says so.
- **Neris** writes, or refuses to write, the fifth line.
- **Oren** says one true thing about a full pipe that solves part of Marek's problem and creates a worse one.

## Required power changes

**None. Marek remains a Reader for the whole batch.** He must not follow a sequence across two towers. What changes is that he is now, on the record, the only person in Veyra who can say which order six movements arrive in, and that this is a *job* rather than a private competence: he is named, rostered, and paid, and a man with a job is a man who can be asked what he knew and when.

Strain does not improve. The inner-ear pressure, the metallic taste, and the thin hearing in the left ear are all still there at the end of Chapter 21. **The permanent hearing loss is Chapter 31 and is caused by four minutes in cold moving water. It is not this batch and must not be foreshadowed as a symptom here.**

## Required world reveals

1. The collision arrives out of order and the order is knowable in advance by a bench trace and by nothing else. The city survives a bad night because of a maintenance hand's private reading, and no instrument in Veyra can confirm a word of it.
2. Caution still costs nothing and is still uncounted: a school closed on a warden's own authority, a market shut for a month, a morning of wages out of four hundred families' mouths, and a bell rung for a corridor.
3. The **Lower Sill culvert** is the oldest continuously deferred repair in the city. A nine-person municipal convoy is loaded with shoring timber and a replacement dewatering pump, and has been for two years, and the Office calls it deferred maintenance. **Set it up. Do not dramatize its failure.**
4. The **Weather Council can certify a trial claim** under the Charter with a named sponsor, a published expiry, a listed alternative, and one regional seat concurring. It is a good instrument. It works.
5. A binding closes coordinated independent preparation, not just weather. Crews, precursor summons, ferry fallbacks, and shelter rotas are all such turns.
6. **A certified claim's cost can be unprovable to everyone but the reader of a private plate.** Nobody can hold the evidence, which means nobody can argue, which means the method is free of consequence.
7. An assembly's refusal to concur is a correct institutional act and not an obstruction. The reader should be able to see this without being told.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 00011

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** Low Spindle, the ninth ward floor, day nine, from about half past five. The collision arrives at about six. Night, small hours, and day ten before the pumps are manned.

**Immediate goal:** Say the order of the six movements out loud, in plain words, to a crew of strangers on a stone floor, at the time it happens — and get it wrong if it is going to be wrong, out loud, in those words.

**Resistance:** Nobody on that floor has a reason to believe a maintenance hand. The published window is an hour and a half out and every warden in the district has a pump order built on it. The people who can see the floor are Neris Kade, who has a list of four lines and no authority, and a young copy from the ninth ward who writes everything down. And there is the ordinary resistance of a night: the water arrives in the order he said it would, and a district that has been told correctly still has to *act* correctly, and acting is where the night is actually won or lost.

**Information revealed:** The order is knowable in advance and only from a bench trace. The Office's six best instruments cannot time the front. What the Office published was an hour and a half out on the return lane and the city has no mechanism to correct a published number in flight.

**Action or decision:** He says the order. He says it as numbers, in the words Malle gave him, and he says *not sure* twice, out loud, when he is not sure, and the second time a man in a wet coat on the floor repeats it back to a crew and gets it slightly wrong, and Marek corrects him in front of nine people rather than letting it stand. He also does the thing he is worst at: he tells the floor the whole of it, including the part he cannot time.

**Emotional change:** Not elation. The specific exhaustion of a man who has been right in front of strangers for the first time in his life and finds that it changes nothing about how the room is organised. And underneath it, one moment of pure professional pleasure at a job done properly, which he is ashamed of and which he does not examine, exactly as he did not examine it in the hall on the Thursday.

**Power or resource change:** **A read. Nothing is spent.** Cost: his voice, his sleep, and the last of the private thing — the order is now in four hundred people's heads and in a copy clerk's notebook, and it cannot be got back.

**Continuity fact:** The collision's order is publicly known from a private plate with no chain behind it. The published window was an hour and a half out. Nobody died. Six ground-floor rooms on Hearthward's lowest row stay ruined and the market stays shut for a month. The ninth ward's public frame is still dark.

**Ending type:** Earned relief, immediately poisoned. He has done the job. The Office's own bulletin the next morning says nothing whatever about the low ground.

**Next-chapter pull:** The ninth ward woke up because a maintenance man said a number on a stone floor, and the city's record of that is a line in a warden's notebook and a line in a copy clerk's notebook. Both are in drawers.

### Chapter 00012

**POV:** Neris Kade.

**Location/time:** Low Spindle, the district office, day ten, from eight in the morning.

**Immediate goal:** Get the district's own record of the night written down while the people who were standing on the floor are still willing to say what they saw, and get it somewhere that does not depend on a warden's memory.

**Resistance:** Sergeant Ampow wants the school opened and the wages back. The four block captains want to know who is paying. And the Office has already published a bulletin that is technically accurate about the weather and says nothing whatever about what happened on the low ground — which is not malice, it is the ordinary output of a building that publishes weather.

**Information revealed:** The city has a document-shaped hole in it exactly where the low ground is. The Office's published number was an hour and a half out; the low ground moved on a number printed in a drying room; and the two documents are now in two drawers in two buildings, and neither is a record, and both are evidence.

**Action or decision:** She writes the night down. She starts the fifth line, and this time she writes it, and it is not what anybody expected: it is not about her own rota, it is about the fact that a district which cannot read its own notice has no way to be wrong on the record.

**Emotional change:** The move from private anger to a document she has committed to. And the first cold understanding that a list nobody has asked for is a thing she will now have to defend, and that defending it will cost her the easy version of herself.

**Power or resource change:** No spend. A warden spends nothing; this is the point and it is stated plainly. What she spends is standing.

**Continuity fact:** The Office's bulletin for the collision is accurate and silent about the low ground. Ampow wants the school reopened. The pump order holds. The fourth line of the list still names Marek.

**Ending type:** Decision. She sends something, and it is not the list.

**Next-chapter pull:** The first thing that goes out of that office is not a complaint about the notice. It is a request, addressed to Kestrel Yards, for a corridor turn, because a man on a stone floor has told her the air will come in an order and she has decided to act on the order rather than on the window.

### Chapter 00013

**POV:** Tarin Senn.

**Location/time:** Kestrel Yards, day eleven. Working yards, a lift finally with its new pin, and three boards with one sheet on each.

**Immediate goal:** Find out whether the Kestrel Line is going to be asked to put its hulls behind somebody else's claim, and get a price on the answer before anybody asks the question out loud.

**Resistance:** Ivo Slate with a licence and a courtesy, and **Maro Hesk** arriving in person for the first time: not a villain, a man with four carrier contracts and a grain factor and two insurers who all paid against a window that was an hour and a half out. Hesk's position is that the Office was paid to be right and was not, and that the Exchange's remedy is the only remedy anybody has. He is not lying about any of it.

**Information revealed:** The **Lower Sill culvert.** A concrete landmark with a cracked headwall, shored twice, failing again, and the oldest continuously deferred repair in the city. A nine-person municipal convoy has been loaded with shoring timber and a replacement dewatering pump for two years and has a window that keeps moving. Nobody has pulled it. The Office calls it deferred maintenance.

**Action or decision:** Tarin refuses to let the Line be described as a resource without a term attached, and writes one: if the Line's glass is used to make a claim true, the Line's reading goes on the board with the hours on it, whatever the Office's window says. Slate is courteous and agrees, which is worse.

**Emotional change:** The humiliation of Chapter 5 returns, inverted. She lost a month's overhead for being right in public. Now she is being asked to be wrong on purpose, for money that is already spent, and the terms she extracts are the only part of it that is hers.

**Power or resource change:** No spend. The convoy is loaded and does not move. **Do not stage the convoy failing, and do not let anybody say it will not go.**

**Continuity fact:** Maro Hesk appears in prose and is a broker with contracts, not a plot. Ivo Slate remains courteous and within his licence. The Lower Sill convoy is nine people, shoring timber, and a dewatering pump, loaded at the south-bank shed.

**Ending type:** Setup with a price attached.

**Next-chapter pull:** The only instrument in Veyra that can time the corridor is a maintenance hand's private plate, and the yard board now carries a term sheet written by a captain.

### Chapter 00014

**POV:** Sable Orin.

**Location/time:** The drying room above the Ninth Baths, and a records room, day twelve. A Friday-turned-ordinary day; the front has gone and the city is drying out.

**Immediate goal:** Put out a reading with an hour on it, on the strength of what she watched happen on the ninth ward floor, and find out whether anybody in Veyra will act on a number from a room above a public bath.

**Resistance:** The answer, at first, is no. A district clerk thanks her and files it. An assembly declines to move a rota. The Office's own bulletin is already out and is correct. And the people she published for last time have spent four hundred people-hours and a morning of wages, and the fourth line of somebody else's list is about a plate in a coat.

**Information revealed:** A reading that nobody acts on is not a lesser claim. It is not a claim at all, and it is not compellable, and the person who published it has learned that being right is a thing that happens to her alone in a room. The Volume 03 consent problem starts here and is not named.

**Action or decision:** She keeps the number up anyway, and this time she publishes the **basis** — the instrument, the place, the hour, and the fact that she cannot say why it is right — and watches a room full of competent people decline it politely for reasons that are all correct. She does not retract it. She writes down who declined and why, in a notebook, and starts a second notebook, which is the first sign of an archive.

**Emotional change:** Not defeat. Something colder and more useful: the recognition that she has been offering a gift with a hidden price and that the price was the consent of people who were not asked. She is not sorry. She is reorganising.

**Power or resource change:** **Nothing is spent. The number is not acted on, and the chapter must make that visible as a failure rather than as a near miss.** The fourth notebook is the Quiet Ledger's shape without its name: archivists, instrument makers, former clerks, ward technicians, keeping things for different reasons. **It is not a faction and Sable is not in it.**

**Continuity fact:** Sable is right and unread. Neris's Chapter 8 number stays permanently unidentified and is not referred to. Ivo Slate's agency report from Chapter 2 is still in the Exchange's hands.

**Ending type:** Cost, in the currency of credibility.

**Next-chapter pull:** A warden on the ninth ward asks the maintenance hand for a walk up the tower, and the maintenance hand has a docket that says he has to be there anyway.

### Chapter 00015

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Ninth Ward tower, Tannery Walk, day thirteen. A brick shaft, a ladder, a copper tank that sweats, and a feed seat on the second landing.

**Immediate goal:** Do the glasswork he owes, which is the only job anybody will give him and the only reason he has a warrant to be in the building.

**Resistance:** The tower is a poor building with a good keeper and an empty public frame, and everything Marek touches in it is a version of a thing he did. The dead public pane is not an accident. The keeper has been here eleven years and has a perfectly good memory and no reason to use it. And the feed plate on the second landing is the plate Marek put back at ten o'clock on Friday night in the dark, on an instruction, without writing a line in the book.

**Information revealed:** The Ninth Ward's public frame is dark for the rest of the volume and there is money-shaped reason why. The tower's feed is working again because Marek put it back, which means the one piece of evidence in the city that shows the collision's order is sitting in a public building in a locked room, correctly installed, and belongs to the Office.

**Action or decision:** He does the work. He does it well and slowly and for free, and he does the feed seat while he is there, which is a small private act of maintenance that also puts him alone in front of a plate that has been recording for five days.

**Emotional change:** Shame, held at arm's length, turned into something that can be carried: the discovery that doing the work properly is not the same as repairing what he broke, and that nobody is going to distinguish between the two for him.

**Power or resource change:** No spend. A read of a plate that is not his, in a room with no witness but a keeper, which he does not take.

**Continuity fact:** The tower's own feed plate is in its seat and has been since day eight at 22:00. The public frame is still a bubbled temporary. The keeper's name and the eleven years are established here and matter later.

**Ending type:** Quiet competence, and a decision to stay in the city a while longer.

**Next-chapter pull:** The keeper mentions, without being asked, that Oren Vale used to work that tower's water, and Marek has a reason to be two streets away that he has not admitted to anybody including himself.

### Chapter 00016

**POV:** Marek Vale. **Batch midpoint.**

**Location/time:** Low Spindle, day thirteen, a kitchen and a wet outfall, evening.

**Immediate goal:** Ask his father a technical question about a surcharged pipe, and get an answer he can use.

**Resistance:** Oren Vale, sixty-one, retired culvert and pump mechanic, blunt and tired and protective in ways that feel like refusal. He has not forgiven Anja. He has not repaired anything with his son in six years. And he is a better mechanic than the Weather Office, and he is going to say the thing about a pipe that is already full which solves Marek's Thursday and destroys his week.

**Information revealed:** Oren has been in the ninth ward's outfall. He says, unprompted, that a pipe which is already full will take a terrace's rain and give nothing back, and that when that happens the water is not standing in a surcharged pipe, it is standing in a full one, and there is a difference and nobody upstairs has ever known it. **Marek arrived already knowing this and has it minuted as an unexplained sequence fault.**

**Action or decision:** Marek does not tell his father about the plate, the fault, the claim, or the cuff. He asks one more question than is comfortable and gets a second true thing. He leaves without having said the thing he came to say, which is that he is the reason the ward's glass is out and that he is standing in his father's district on the same night as the ward he cannot read.

**Emotional change:** The midpoint is not a revelation about the past. It is the plain discovery that the man who can explain the mechanism has been in the room the whole time, two streets away, for twelve years, and that Marek's professional life has been organised to avoid a kitchen.

**Power or resource change:** No spend. No advancement. **He is still a Reader and cannot follow a sequence across two towers.** What he gains is a fact and a debt, and what he loses is the last private comfort available to him, which is that the people who understand his work are all in a building he works in.

**Continuity fact:** Oren does not know about the claim, the plate, the feed fault, or the cuff. **The Chapter 34 chapter is not this one: there is no reconciliation here, no culvert, and no convoy.** Oren has never forgiven Anja and does not mention her.

**Ending type:** Reversal of scale. The problem is not hidden in an institution. It is in a kitchen, and it has been in a kitchen for twelve years.

**Next-chapter pull:** If the Ferrygate district is going to be asked to host a Council-certified trial claim, somebody has to find out what the Ferrygate district actually does when it is left alone, and the answer is in a place nobody has looked, because nobody has ever asked.

### Chapter 00018

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Ninth Ward tower, day sixteen. The feed seat, the lamp, the plate he fitted.

**Immediate goal:** Read the tower's own plate, properly, over five days of recorded sequence, and find out whether the second turn's gap is a fault after all.

**Resistance:** It is not a gap this time, or not only. On the sixth day of the plate's run, in the Witness line, there is a movement that behaves like a Witness and is dated twelve years back. It is complete, and it is instrumented, and it arrived in the glass **from below** rather than out of the air, in a way that has nothing to do with the weather going past a tower on Tannery Walk.

**Information revealed:** **The twelve-year trace.** Evidence. It is not a method, it contains no formula, it predicts nothing, and it does not tell Marek what happened. It tells him that something was Witnessed against the ninth ward twelve years ago, by somebody whose hand he can recognise when he gets to the margin, and that the record of it is still in the wire under his own tower. **Nothing else.
