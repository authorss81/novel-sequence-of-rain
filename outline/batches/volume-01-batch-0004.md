## Batch goal

The method survived and the record did not, and now a room has to be told about both. This is the batch in which the city is shown that something was spent at the Lower Sill and is not shown who spent it; in which a man who has never stopped being right is dismissed from the field for being right out loud; in which a twenty-seven-year-old clerk is a copy clerk two rows back and has been for a fortnight; in which a retired mechanic's sheet of paper becomes a public exhibit without anyone knowing what it is; and in which a woman with a piece of paper cut out of a margin finally says, in a room, a thing that is not true.

**The batch cliff is Chapter 48, and it is a half-lit public forecast hall with a piece of glass pinned to a board, a column of figures being argued over by two people who were not in the room, a woman at a copy desk with a hand that does not shake, and a poor ward's tower with a dark window above the street.**

## Starting condition

Day thirty-three. The storm is over and there will not be another one inside the month. The Bellweather entry for the thirtieth is filed as a **maintenance failure** and the corridor claim stands in the ledger as a true claim with the Director's authorization and approval stamp on it. Tovan Iles has been on a copy desk since twenty past two on the afternoon of the third day and is not going anywhere. Marek Vale is off the field list, has a third of his fine hearing gone from the left ear and a permanent ringing on that side, has never told anybody, and has two true documents in a drawer in the maintenance shop, one of which is four hundred and eleven words containing the order the six movements arrived in on the ninth of the month and cannot be filed anywhere.

Three documents in this city now carry the thirtieth and none of them can be used in a room:

1. **Neris Kade's ruled page, seven lines**, stamped and dated in her own hand, in a locked district drawer. The seventh line is the load-bearing one: *the thirtieth's return does not say the reach levels came down, because there is no instrument in the Office that looks at a reach, and therefore nothing that was lost on the reach can be said to have been lost by anybody. A window the city cannot see is not a window the city loses.* It is not compellable.
2. **A true copy of the thirtieth's day sheet as it stood before the reclassification**, in Tovan Iles's own hand, dated, timed, initialled, in her own box. Nobody knows. It is worth nothing.
3. **A sheet in Oren Vale's inside pocket**: four columns of years, a figure against each, the word *front* against four of them in four different hands, and on the back, in his son's writing, *wall overdue, timber in the shed, pump on the pallet, board repainted, nine stood. No minute.*

And one document that nobody has asked for and that the whole volume has been walking toward: **the eleventh night of the month in the chart room on the ridge, a framed sheet of paper on the last frame of the north wall with an inch and a half of its upper margin taken off with a good blade and a steady hand.**

### Chapter 00036 — The List of Citations

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass, day thirty-four, from about nine in the morning. An ante-room off the Council's chamber, the outer office, the field office board.

**Immediate goal:** Read the summons properly, and work out who called it and why, before anybody in the building has an opinion about it in front of him.

**Resistance:** **A Bellweather review is called under an ordinary Charter power and Marek is cited.** It is not an inquiry into his claim and it is not an inquiry into the classification, and he has to read it three times to find that out. It is a review of *the method*, with a list of citations attached, and the list has eight names on it and his is the second. **The chair is Cassian Rook and he declares his own interest in the first four minutes of the hearing, in a room he chairs, in front of a clerk who writes it down, and then does the fairest available job and the reader has to sit through it.** Nobody has called the review as an attack. That is worse. It has been called because the Council's spring report on the method needs a section on what a claim costs, and the Office cannot write that section, and so the Office has asked a room to find out. Malle is cited. Venn is cited. Anwen Sarr is cited as the author of a standing requirement. Tovan Iles is cited as the entering clerk. **Rook, Anwen Sarr, and the yard are all in the list, and the two districts that prepared are not, and Malle cannot get them added in the twenty minutes he has.**

**Information revealed:** The review's terms of reference, in the dry language the volume has been using: *to establish whether the effect of a certified or authorised claim on the preparations of the districts affected can be demonstrated from the record; and if it cannot, to report that it cannot.* **The second half is the sentence that does the work and the reader should notice it before anybody in the room does.**

**Action or decision:** Marek does the only thing available to him, which is to go and ask the eight cited people what they are each going to be asked, one at a time, in a corridor, without telling any of them what the others said.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend. A read is offered and refused on both sides again, and this time it is refused before it is offered.**

**Continuity fact:** The Bellweather watch was stood down on the eleventh. The long watch covering the body is on its sixth day and its reader is **Aldis Prynne**, and Prynne is **not** on the citation list, and Marek notices.

**Ending type:** A trap assembled in daylight, again, and this time by a man who did not build it.

**Next-chapter pull:** Anwen Sarr's clause four has been in the file since the eighteenth and is now the first thing on the agenda, and she is on the list, and she does not know that the second half of the terms of reference is a sentence about her.

### Chapter 00037 — The First Sentence

**POV:** Anwen Sarr.

**Location/time:** The Council chamber, day thirty-five, morning. The assembly's own file, a table in the room, eleven people in it.

**Immediate goal:** Keep clause four out of the hands of a room that will use it, and get it in there in a form that is not a veto and not a fiction.

**Resistance:** **Clause four is examined, by the woman who wrote it, in front of the clerk who wrote it down.** A Council assessor puts the two sentences of it side by side and asks the room which one it is. The first — no certified claim entered without its cost published at the point of entry — has been satisfied twice, on the twenty-second by the report and on the twenty-ninth by an entry that published everything the sponsor could name. The second — or the sponsor recording in writing that the cost cannot be made public and why — has been answered by nobody, and cannot be answered by anybody, because there is no way to write *why* when the honest answer is *because the mechanism does not report itself and the only instrument that would show it is in a lockable room in a ward that cannot read its own glass.* **Anwen is offered a soft landing and refuses it.** She is offered the standing requirement in a weaker form that the Council would honour, and she says no, and Kell Bray writes four clauses in one hand without lifting the pen, and the fourth is stronger than the first.

**Information revealed:** **The institution's answer to the volume's demand is not silence. It is a hearing, conducted properly, by a man who has to sit in it.** And the demand is defeated in the form the demander herself predicted in a corridor on the eighteenth, and she is right about that too, and being right about it is what costs her.

**Action or decision:** She makes one concession, and it is a real one, and it is the sentence that ends the argument: **she agrees that the cost of a claim may be published as what it closed without naming who chose it**, which is Chapter 44's column, and which she will spend the rest of her life defending and which is not what she asked for.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend.** An assembly's right to refuse remains real, cheap, and correct, and is not exercised here, and the chapter says so.

**Continuity fact:** **Anwen Sarr is not softened and is not bought off.** Ferren is in the room and says nothing, as at Chapter 20. The Hearthward market is still shut and the vault's lower six drawers are still ruined and nobody brings either up.

**Ending type:** A defeat that is a clause.

**Next-chapter pull:** The next citation is the Kestrel Line, and the Line's file is eight days of published errors and a column of differences, and it is the only document anybody has handed to that room yet.

### Chapter 00039 — The Same Answer

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Council chamber, day thirty-seven, from about eleven.

**Immediate goal:** Get through forty minutes of evidence without producing a document he cannot produce, and without lying.

**Resistance:** He gives the same answer he gave on the nineteenth. A claim holds its stated route by displacing the turn that most directly contradicts it, and what it displaced on the thirtieth was the ebb under the long band, and the ebb is the reach's bottom, and the convoy's window is on the reach's bottom, and there is no instrument in this city that looks at a reach. **He is asked which instrument showed him the bend, and he says a plate with no chain behind it in my coat, and the room writes it down, and Rook does not ask him where the plate came from, and Malle does not ask him either, and both of those silences are in the minutes as silences.** Then **Aldis Prynne is called.** He read the order on the ninth of the month in a certified run and got it right, and he did it from three places the Office itself published — the technical cell's file, the annex, and the ridge's own bulletin — and **he cannot say where any of them came from, because the Office's own record of the collision's order is a copy of a copy of a thing a man said out loud on a stone floor in the ninth ward, and the building has never been able to say that, and he has been in the building for thirty-one years.** Marek listens to a man he has never met describe his own reading back to him correctly, from records he cannot produce, and understands that the only corroboration he will ever have is a stranger's.

**Information revealed:** **A reader's attestation is a fact about the reader, and the Office has spent thirty-one years never asking a reader where a reading came from, and this is the first time the question has been nearly asked and both men in the room declined to ask it.** Nobody in that room is on Marek's side and neither is anyone against him.

**Action or decision:** He refuses, on the record, to have his name removed from the attestation, and gives as his reason a sentence that costs him: *if the reader's name comes off, the next one signs it alone.*

**Power or resource change:** **No spend.** He reads nothing in this chapter. He is asked to and does not, and says why, and the saying why is in the minutes.

**Continuity fact:** **Marek is dismissed from the field in Chapter 45 and this chapter is where the room decides it, and the reader should be able to see the decision being made by people who are each individually correct.**

**Ending type:** The same answer, and it is worse, and a stranger agrees with it.

### Chapter 00040 — Nobody Asked Me For A Copy

### Chapter 00041 — The Line About Herself

**POV:** Neris Kade.

**Location/time:** Low Spindle, day thirty-nine, the district office, the pump houses, a corridor, and a warden's council that night.

**Immediate goal:** Hand over seven lines to a Council without losing control of what happens to them, and without being asked a question in a room that she cannot answer.

**Resistance:** **She has to decide whether to send the page or take it herself, and the reasons for both are good, and taking it herself is a denial of a lawful request, and she knows it.** She sends it. ## Open questions after the batch

1. **Marek is off the field, censured, half deaf in one ear, and the only person who knows what the Bellweather claim cost.** What is left of him, and who is the first to take him seriously again — and is it a woman at a copy desk with a box nobody knows about?
2. **Two documents exist that nobody can use and nobody knows about: a stamped copy of a warden's seven-line page and a true copy of a day sheet.** Who asks first, and what is it worth by then?
3. **The twelve-year date is still only his, and he has now built a public column whose entire purpose is to print what a claim closed without naming who closed it.** How long can a reader be the only man in the city who can be shown a thing, and what happens the first time he wants to be believed about something else?
4. **A settlement index names a clerk who was dead before the record was lodged, and a woman who is not a liar has told him the renumbering story.** The witness chain was falsified. 8. **Rook's authorization stands, he chaired the review of his own authorization, and he is not a villain in a single line of this volume.** The reader should be more afraid of him now than at any point before.
