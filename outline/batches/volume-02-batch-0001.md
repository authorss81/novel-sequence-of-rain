## Batch goal

A censured man is employed by a market to cut its glass, and discovers that a market has built the instrument the Weather Office refused to build. A window is defined, priced, and published; a district finds its own quarterly return in a commercial schedule; a reach is measured for the first time in the city's history and given a number; and the ninth ward, in order to stop its weather being sold twice, gives up its own reach window in a witnessed claim that is entirely true, correctly certified, and costs the ward the thing it wanted.

**The batch cliff is Chapter 58: a register with a heading and nothing under it, and a note from a counter-reader who will do the work for a fee and one term, and the term is that the reading be public before the claim.**

## Starting condition

Seven weeks after the forty-eighth day of the Bellweather month. The method survived and the record is public and incomplete. Marek Vale is dismissed from field duty, censured, and still at a glass bench on the ridge. Tovan Iles is a copy clerk. The ninth ward's public glass is dark. The Lower Sill's timber and pump are in a shed under a painted board and a Bellweather of this order returns under four years. The Cloud Exchange sells forecast windows and has never published a price list.

## Clock convention for this batch

**The weather is ordinary and the calendar is civic.** Sleetmonth is the first month of the wet quarter and the season's rhythm is *rain, clear, rain, clear*. **The clearing is called a back-cut**, and the first back-cut of the season, and the largest, falls in the middle of Sleetmonth. A cycle is still six hours and a watch bell is still the hour, but **Volume 02 quotes ordinary clock hours freely** because there is no single front and no sliding hours-to-arrival figure; that rule was Volume 01's and it is not restated. **Day one of the batch is a Monday, the ninth of Sleetmonth.**

## Required world reveals

1. **A window is not a claim.** It names an operation and hours and asserts dependability, it spends nothing, guarantees nothing, and is not entered anywhere — so it is legally outside the Office and commercially inside everybody's life.
2. **The Office's refusal to measure a reach left the field open, and a firm took it.** The instrument is a float, a chain, a staff and a glass datum, and the glass datum is cut by the man the Office censured.
3. **A district's right to be asked is also a district's right to be resold.** A quarterly return sent in the ordinary course becomes a contract schedule in a market, and there is no form for withdrawing it.
4. **A price is a measurement, and a measurement of a preparation is only ever somebody's opinion of what the preparation was for.** The Exchange's reach price is four times the ward's and both are defensible.
5. **A claim is not weather, so a claim does not void a window contract — it breaches it**, and the breach falls on whoever entered it.
6. **A record of what was written down is not a record of what was available**, and the ninth ward's line in the Standing Response Register has been empty for longer than anyone looking at it has been alive.

### Chapter 00049 — Private Work

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The Weatherglass maintenance shop, Sleetmonth the ninth, a Monday, from about eight in the morning. The glass benches, the tube in his coat, the second drawer, the cuff.

**Immediate goal:** Find out what a man who is dismissed from field duty, censured, and off every watch list in the building may lawfully do with eight hours a day, and pay for a district he is behind on.

**Resistance:** **He may work a bench and nothing else.** He may not take a watch, may not be on a roster, may not attest for the Office, may not enter a claim, may not sign a reading, and may not leave the ridge on Office time. The maintenance foreman is kind about it in the way of a man who does not want the conversation. The order that carried his censure is a single line and every clause of it is true. And the Office's own rate for private glasswork is cheaper than his, so the work has to be taken outside, on his own hours, in cash, at a price he sets — which is the first time in eleven years he has set a price on anything.

**Information revealed:** A commission arrives at about half past nine from a man who introduces himself as a licensed agent and is extremely courteous about it. The work is a **sounding float, an iron chain, a drum, and a sealed glass cylinder to be cut, ground, annealed and seated as a datum.** It is not Office stock and it was not ordered by this office. Marek reads the instrument on the bench in about a page and knows what it is before he is told, because it is a tide gauge, and tide gauges are what the Office's own towers use and the Office's own towers do not use them for a river. **He is employed, for a fee, by the market, to fix the market's glass, and the Office's censure is the reason the work is on his bench.**

**Action or decision:** He takes the job. He says out loud, to nobody, that it is honest work and that the man is paying for it, and that he would rather be paid by a market than not paid, and that this is not a position anybody is going to be able to argue with him about later. **The cuff is full.** He writes the next block of pencil on the Exchange's own job docket, in the margin, in his own hand, because that is the paper in front of him, and the docket goes back to a counting floor in a building he has never been inside.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel, no claim.** A read is offered and refused: Ivo Slate asks him, in passing and in the wrong way, which tower a plate came from, and Marek answers him honestly and briefly and does not correct the shape of the question. **His hearing is used once** — he turns his head to the wrong side to take a figure and gets it wrong and takes it again.

**Continuity fact:** The private plate is in the tube in his coat. The tower's own plate is in the ninth ward feed seat. **Neither is used, and he has still not been up the ninth ward ladder.** The maintenance report of four hundred and eleven words is in the second drawer and cannot be filed anywhere and is not referred to.

**Ending type:** A job, and a place on a map forty yards from a painted board.

**Next-chapter pull:** The agent's address is a shed on the Bellweather freight apron, and the commission is for a reach gauge, and the Kestrel yards are three hundred yards from that shed, and the Line's board is where windows are being sold by somebody else already.

### Chapter 00050 — The Window Market

**POV:** Ilva Sorn.

**Location/time:** Kestrel Yards, Sleetmonth the tenth, a Tuesday, from about seven in the morning to the middle of the afternoon. The boards, the slate, the launch book, the yard office.

**Immediate goal:** Work out why a yard that publishes its own uncertainty is losing work to a product it has never heard of, and stop it without publishing anything that can be used against the Line.

**Resistance:** **A window market has set up on the yard's own ground.** A table with a cloth on it, outside a chandler's, with a printed price list under a weight, and a man taking money. A window is a named operation in a named set of hours, sold by the operation — *barge movement, four hours, on a stated reach* — and it is a different product from a forecast because it does not say what the weather will do; it says what your operation may rely on, and a refund rule hangs off it. **The Line's own published column of differences is the raw material.** A carrier has been buying the yard's honest hours off the board and reselling them as windows, and the yard cannot stop it, because the yard published them in public and the Exchange is not copying anything: it is reading.

**Information revealed:** The Exchange is quoting the Kestrel corridor. Not the Line's window — the Line's *corridor*, priced, to carriers who will pay for dependability they cannot get from an Office that publishes a band. And on the same list, on a second page, a line for a **reach** that no one at the yard has ever heard quoted. **The yard's four conditions were written for an Office.** They say: publish both hours, publish the difference, name the sponsor, no footnote. **None of them says anything about a price**, and a price is the one instrument that can take a published difference and make it into a product, and Tarin Senn says that out loud at her own boards in the afternoon and is outmanoeuvred by a piece of paper.

**Action or decision:** Ilva does the arithmetic and finds that the resold windows are being cut from the Line's own differences and that the yard is the supplier of record in a supply chain it has no clause about. She asks for the number of windows cut from the Line's column. She is given it, courteously, because it is a fact about the market and the Exchange is not concealing it. **She does not forbid it and cannot, and the yard's answer is to publish a fifth condition, which the Exchange accepts in writing in nine seconds, because the fifth condition costs the Exchange nothing and is the first thing in nine weeks that has cost it nothing.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel.** Craft and accounts, not reading. **Marek is not in this chapter.**

**Continuity fact:** The Line's column of differences is nineteen days long in the Bellweather month with eight days on which the third column is not empty. **It is now a growing public object and the batch does not renumber the Bellweather month.** The four conditions are the Ch 13 Exchange-facing set and the Ch 19 Office-facing set, restated a third time here against a market, and **the fifth condition is new and is Tarin's, not a paraphrase of the first four.**

**Ending type:** A product discovered being built out of somebody else's honesty.

**Next-chapter pull:** The second page of the list has a reach on it, and the reach is the Bellweather reach, and the yard's glass does not look at a reach, and there is one man in this basin who has just been paid to cut a datum for one.

### Chapter 00051 — The Ward's Only Document

**POV:** Neris Kade.

**Location/time:** Low Spindle, the ninth ward district office, Sleetmonth the eleventh, a Wednesday, from about two in the afternoon. The rain comes in at about four.

**Immediate goal:** Get the season's first rain through a ward that cannot read its own tower glass, without spending anything she cannot account for.

**Resistance:** The rain is ordinary. **That is the chapter's difficulty.** A first rain of the quarter is not a Bellweather, and there is no claim in it and no spend in it, and the ward handles it in about four hours with the clearing rota and one pump and two wardens, and nothing happens, and nothing is in any column. What the chapter is about is the **quarterly return**, which she got carried in four minutes in the Bellweather month, and which is now, at about three o'clock, being bought. **A warden in the warden's council has sold a copy of it to the Cloud Exchange, at the ordinary price for a quarterly return, under the ordinary terms, in the ordinary course.** It went out. It was correct. It was a courtesy of the ward to a Council that asked nicely, and the ward has no veto on what anybody does with a thing it told the truth about.

**Information revealed:** The Exchange's schedule of the ninth ward is now in a market file, and a schedule of a ward's clearing rota, shelter rota and pump order is a **statement of what the ward will do and will not do**, and a person buying one knows, for the first time, exactly which four hundred and forty people are not coming to the low ground on a given night and why, and what they will be doing instead. **The ward is the cheapest place in the basin to lose.** It has no money, no glass, and now no private information, and all three of those arrived in the same quarter.

**Action or decision:** She does the correct thing and it is not enough. She asks the warden's council to withdraw the return. She is told in writing, in four minutes, that a return already furnished cannot be recalled and that the council has no purchase in it, and that the Exchange paid for it and published it and there is no form. **She then does the thing that costs her, which is to go and stand in a yard and find out what the Exchange is going to do with it**, and she is refused politely at the counter and told that the schedule is a public document of a public body, which it is.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel.** A warden spends nothing. **What she spends is the belief that a district's own honesty is still its own.**

**Continuity fact:** The ward's **own** shelter rota, nine names on a card, in a drawer in Low Spindle, is still unwritten. **Her ruled page is still seven lines, is not renumbered, and has no eighth line.** The ninth ward tower's head still shows nothing at all. **The two Ferrygate residents are not on the page.**

**Ending type:** An ordinary rain handled perfectly, and a document handled irreversibly.

**Next-chapter pull:** The Exchange's counting floor is open to anybody with a reason to be on it, and a warden with a reason is the best kind of visitor, and the refund rule is on the wall.

### Chapter 00052 — The Counting Floor

**POV:** Maro Hesk.

**Location/time:** The Cloud Exchange, Sleetmonth the thirteenth, a Friday, from about ten in the morning until the bells. The counting floor, the two-sided books, the refund rule on the wall, the quotation hall.

**Immediate goal:** Work out why his best line of business has a hole in it and what a firm is supposed to do about a hole, before the hole gets bigger.

**Resistance:** **The reader is made to like him, and then to be afraid of him, in the same chapter, and both are earned.** He is forty-one and he did not create the pressure and he is not lying about anything. He has built a product that is legal because the Weather Office will not touch it: a **window** names an operation and a set of hours and asserts dependability, has no place, no trigger and no witness, and is not entered anywhere, and therefore spends nothing and guarantees nothing, and a firm that cannot guarantee a thing prices the gamble and refunds the difference. **He says plainly that the Office is right and that he is grateful, because if the Office certified windows then windows would cost a branch and there would be fewer of them, and that this is not a paradox he has found a way around, it is the market working.**

**Information revealed:** The **refund rule**, which is the volume's engine and is on a wall in this chapter: a quoted window that does not open is refunded at the quoted price, and a buyer who is not told what a window cost cannot know what he has lost, and so **the Exchange publishes the price of every window it withdraws.** There are four withdrawn lines in the Exchange's history. One of them is the **morning of the thirtieth of the Bellweather month, the Bellweather reach, and there is a price in the margin in his own hand.**

**It is the first number in the history of this city for what forty minutes at the bottom of the Bellweather reach was worth, and it is struck through because it was not delivered, and it is the only price anybody has ever had, and it was put there by a firm that wanted it not to be true.**

**Action or decision:** Hesk does not cover it and does not explain it and does not call it weather, and he sends for the reading that was behind the strike-through, because a forward board cannot be built without a price for a miss. The Office has no instrument that looks at a reach — it has said so itself, in a public hall, in print, in a column. **The Exchange has a gauge. The Exchange now has a hole where the Office's honest sentence is, and he cannot fill it and will not pretend to.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel.** **Hesk spends money, which is the first time in the series that money is a cost anybody can be shown.**

**Continuity fact:** **Nobody in this chapter is told why the window did not open.** Hesk has a price and no cause and does not speculate in front of his own clerks, and the reader is left with a number and a struck line and no explanation, exactly as the city is.

**Ending type:** A rule that is honest, working, and the mechanism of a concealment nobody chose.

**Next-chapter pull:** A price is a measurement, and a measurement needs a schedule of what the thing was going to be used for, and the Exchange has never needed one and now does, and the only body in the basin that keeps schedules of what a ward was going to do about its water is a Council technical cell that does not sell them.

### Chapter 00053 — What a Window Costs a Crew

**POV:** Tarin Senn.

**Location/time:** Kestrel Yards, Sleetmonth the sixteenth, a Monday, from six in the morning. The Line, the launch book, a forward contract on the table, a shed for the boom cable.

**Immediate goal:** Decide whether the Line will sell the basin a dependable hour it cannot promise, and pay the bills with a smaller job if it will not.

**Resistance:** The offer is good. **A forward window contract for the autumn low at the Bellweather reach, on a term, with a refund clause, and the money fixes the boom cable, the wrist's two months off, and the discount two carriers put on the yard's cover.** The boom cable is three hundred pounds and the yard is eleven hundred short of the quarter. She is not a person who refuses money out of principle; she is a person who has flown a hull inside somebody else's claim and come down four minutes and eleven seconds lighter in the hand than she went up. **Her own sentence from the yard a month ago does the work: a term sheet protects a yard's reputation and not a crew's body.** A window says an operation is dependable. A crew plans a flight around a dependable hour. **A yard that sells dependability is selling the right to be unlucky, and the buyer is not the one who is unlucky.**

**Information revealed:** The Line is **already inside somebody else's window, unnamed.** A resold difference from the yard's own column, cut into a four-hour window on a reach the Line has never flown, sold to a barge operator. The Line did not sell it and cannot un-sell it, and the operator's insurance is priced on a product made out of the yard's honesty. **Her four conditions, restated a third time and turned to face a market rather than an Office, and the restatement is the chapter's quiet centre.**

**Action or decision:** She refuses the forward contract and takes a smaller, duller charter to keep the yard solvent, **and the refusal is not rewarded and costs her money.** Then the Exchange offers, free, for the term of a contract, the loan of a reach instrument — a chain, a staff, a glass datum, serviced — **and she takes it**, because a yard that will not use a level gauge because it is undignified is a yard that will be told what the water is doing by a price list, and the Line's fifth condition is that the yard's own instruments are published, and this is the first time one of them is somebody else's.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel.** **Marek's hearing is used at the opening and is not explained:** she says a figure twice because he asks for it twice, and neither of them says why, and Tarin notices and does not ask, and the noticing is a beat and not a scene.

**Continuity fact:** **The Line's four conditions are the Ch 13 and Ch 19 sets. This is the third restatement and it is a different four again, with a fifth new condition, and no chapter may paraphrase the first two sets outside the yard.** No romance, no jealousy, no rescue. The word partnership is not said.

**Ending type:** A refusal that costs money, and an instrument accepted on bad terms because the alternative is worse.

**Next-chapter pull:** The instrument being offered is being cut and seated this week by a man on the Weatherglass ridge, and the Exchange is lending it, and a gauge is going onto the Bellweather reach whether the yard is contracted or not.

### Chapter 00054 — A Man Who Prices Honesty

**POV:** Tobias Mear.

**Location/time:** The south-bank freight apron and the Exchange's pricing desk, Sleetmonth the seventeenth, a Tuesday, a cold morning.

**Immediate goal:** Build a forward price list for reach windows, which means putting a number on forty minutes at the bottom of a bucket, with nothing to go on but a struck-through line and a visit.

**Resistance:** **A price needs a schedule of what the thing was going to be used for, and there are two incompatible uses and the market can only serve one.** He is twenty-nine and he is the younger man from the public hall in the Bellweather month who said the yard's honesty was now the yard's exposure, and he has been right about one thing and wrong about another and the chapter gives him both. He goes and looks at the reach. He finds Renn Sallow's shed, forty yards from Petrie's, and a stilling well cut into the apron wall, and a float, and a chain, and **a glass datum that a maintenance hand off the field has cut and seated for the Exchange and signed for**, and he reads the instrument well enough to know it is better than anything the Office owns, and he asks who cut it and is told the name of a man who is censured.

**Information revealed:** **The price.** The reach is about a fortnight a year and roughly nine usable days, and the window is the ebb under a long band, and the Exchange's buyer is a carrier who wants a lane and a barge and a flat. The ward's use is a pump and a timber stack and nine men. **The same forty minutes, two objects, and the market prices the one it can sell, so the number is about four times what a ward would say it is worth — and neither figure is a lie, and the difference is the whole of what the Exchange knows about its own buyers.** He finds a shed with a banded stack in it and a painted board and a foreman who tells him there is a front in three years and that it is not a threat, and **he does the arithmetic on the timber and the pump and realises that they have been sitting for nine weeks and the window that would have moved them is the one on the struck line.**

**Action or decision:** He does not go and find the foreman. He writes the number, and he writes it in the margin of the struck line in his own hand as a memorandum, **and he is scrupulous, and his scrupulousness is the reason a ward will later be offered four times its own figure by a man with a straight face.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel.** **The first time in this series that an accurate number is the mechanism of a wrong outcome, and no instrument is wrong and no person is lying.**

**Continuity fact:** **Petrie's painted board is up and unchanged and he does not know who this is.** The Order 114 tag and the Union guarantee on the pump drum are on the page. The shoring timber is still banded in four bands. **Nobody in this chapter is told why the window did not open.**

**Ending type:** A figure, correctly derived, that is wrong in a way only a buyer could see.

**Next-chapter pull:** A forward board needs a price list, and a price list must be displayed where a person waiting to buy will see it, and the Exchange has never displayed one, and the display is the moment the whole thing stops being a conversation.

### Chapter 00055 — The Forward Board

**POV:** Anwen Sarr.

**Location/time:** The Exchange's quotation hall, then Hearthward, Sleetmonth the eighteenth, a Wednesday, from about ten until dark.

**Immediate goal:** Get a district's own return out of a market's file, having watched a market put a price list on a wall where anybody can read it.

**Resistance:** **She cannot.** The Exchange's price list is public, and its schedules are the schedules of public bodies, and the ninth ward's clearing rota, shelter rota and pump order were furnished to a Council in the ordinary course and published by a firm, and there is **no form.** She is told so in four minutes by a courteous clerk who is correct, and by Kell Bray, who has looked for one all afternoon, and by the Exchange's own notice, which says, in the plainest language the volume has used: *a schedule of a public body's published practice is not the property of that body.* She asks for a district's right to withdraw and is told it does not exist and never has, and that the right to refuse a claim is a different right and is written down, and this one is not.

**Information revealed:** **A market of possible routes, printed, on a wall, in a public hall, with the Bellweather reach on it and the morning of the thirtieth struck through and a price in the margin.** Anwen Sarr stands in front of a number in a hand she does not know, in a city of a hundred and forty thousand people, and reads a figure for forty minutes at the bottom of a reach, and understands in about four seconds what it is, **because she gave away a name in a chamber in exchange for a column and the column printed what closed and not what it was worth, and the market has printed what it was worth and will not print what closed.** She is the one person in the basin who is qualified to say this and she has no standing to say it in this building, and the assembly clerk who is standing with her takes the words down anyway.

**Action or decision:** She does the thing that is hers to do and is not a consolation: **she makes the Exchange's line the agenda item of a standing requirement** — that where a public schedule is used in a forecast product, the district's own figure be printed beside it. It is the fourth clause again, in a worse room, with less to get, and Kell Bray drafts it in one hand without lifting the pen and it is refused within the hour by a firm that has never had to answer to an assembly and does not have to now.

**Continuity fact:** **Ferren is not in this chapter and Hearthward's market is open again and the season's seed is bought and paid for and the six lower drawers are not brought up.** Anwen Sarr is forty-six and is not softened and is not bought off and gets nothing.

**Ending type:** A defeat that is correct, in a worse room than last time.

### Chapter 00056 — Two Deliveries a Day

**POV:** Gant Ollis.

**Location/time:** The ninth ward tower, Sleetmonth the nineteenth, a Thursday, dawn to dusk. The ladder, the sill, the frame, the feed seat, the low ground below.

**Immediate goal:** Decide what to do about a printed sheet of weather arriving twice a day at a shop in his ward, free, from a firm with a counter in it.

**Resistance:** **The tower works.** The feed is healthy and the feed seat holds the plate Marek fitted and has held it since the eighth of the Bellweather month, and the keeper's own run sheets are in the tower's book and about a decade of Oren Vale's arithmetic is on a shelf, and **the public frame above his head has been unreadable since before the weather.** So the ward's only weather is what four women with pen and ink copy off a board at the Weatherglass, and now it is also what arrives twice a day on a carrier's tray at a tobacconist's counter, **printed, with a confidence on it, and a reason, and a place.** The Exchange's sheet is more legible than the Office's bulletin. It names the reach. It says what it is based on. It is free.

**Information revealed:** The Exchange is running a **free district notice service** in the wards whose glass is dark, because a district that cannot read its notice cannot buy a window, and a district that cannot buy a window is a district nobody has to compensate. **It is a commercial service and it is also, and this is the part Ollis cannot get past, better than the Office's.** He has never asked Marek about the plates in eleven years and he does not ask now, and he has a conversation with himself about it on a ladder at about two in the afternoon, in about four lines, and does not resolve it.

**Action or decision:** He accepts the delivery and reads it, because refusing it would be a thing he would have to explain to a district, and he does the thing that is his job, which is to time the two deliveries against his own run sheets and set them side by side in the tower's book — **a keeper's ordinary practice, a column of his own with a date at the head of every entry, correct, and the first independent check on a private weather product anybody in this basin has made.** He finds they agree. He records that they agree, in his own book, in a column nobody has asked for.

**Power or resource change:** **No spend, no panel.** A keeper reads a trace. **The tower's own plate is in its seat and Marek has still not been up the ladder, and the twelve-year date is in the substrate of a plate the keeper keeps in a locked room in a poor ward's tower, and Ollis has never looked at it and does not look at it.**

**Continuity fact:** **The frame is dark for money and the reason is the ward's poverty.** 4486 was paid for and never collected; its crown is a nine-inch plate in a tube in a coat. **No character in this chapter learns that.** The spare plate is cut, boxed, dated, and not fitted, on the shelf under the feed seat.

**Ending type:** A competent old man doing his job correctly and finding out that he has become a market's quality control.

**Next-chapter pull:** A free accurate weather service is on a district that cannot read its own glass, and a freeholder with two hundred yards of that district's lowest ground has bought a reach window, and the ward's assembly sits on Friday.

### Chapter 00057 — The Ward Sits

**POV:** Dessie Trask.

**Location/time:** The ninth ward assembly rooms, Sleetmonth the twentieth, a Friday, from six in the evening. The ward, its chair, its freeholder, its warden, its copy clerk, and a freeholder's man with an offer.

**Immediate goal:** Stop a freeholder taking two hundred yards of the ward's lowest ground out of the drains as salvage, without putting four hundred and forty people under water, and without the ward going bankrupt trying.

**Resistance:** **Garrick Dellow holds the freehold of two hundred yards and he is not a villain and offers, in good faith, a fair price.** He buys reach windows to strip copper and kerb when the water is off the low ground, and he has done it for years, and the spoil goes into the ward's own drains, and the ward's drains are the ward's flood problem, and the connection between the two has been true for as long as anyone has cared to write it down and has been in no column anywhere. **The Exchange has quoted the season's first back-cut and sold it twice: once to Dellow and once to nobody, because nobody is buying a reach for a pump.** The assembly has four hours and no money and two options, and both of them are real. The Exchange will withdraw the second sale if asked. The assembly cannot make it withdraw, and being right does not help, and the chapter shows the correct process failing correctly.

**Information revealed:** **A ward can refuse a claim and cannot refuse a schedule; a ward can refuse a contract and cannot refuse a contract's counterparty.** The assembly's own lever is the only one in the room: a concurring seat. A conditional claim that says *the reach does not come down to the quoted mark in the quoted hours* can be certified, has a listed alternative, and **voids the window physically without touching the contract** — and a claim is not weather, which is the fact the assembly is told by the assessor and does not understand and which Neris Kade understands immediately.

**Action or decision:** **The ward spends itself.** It enters a claim whose effect is that its own reach window does not open, so that a double sale of that window is worth nothing to either buyer. Neris Kade is the sponsor because a warden can sponsor a local claim for her ward. **The listed alternative is the price of the whole thing: if the Exchange withdraws the quotation before the entry bell, no claim binds and the ward keeps its window.** The ward is told, in writing, by a Council assessor who is legally required to say it and who hates saying it, that this is a true claim, that it satisfies every one of the six conditions, that it will be certified, and that **it will close a branch, and that the branch it closes is the ward's own repair window, and that the culvert will be unmaintained for a second consecutive season.**

**Power or resource change:** **No spend yet.** The panel does not appear until Chapter 58, at the moment of entry. The batch's only binding is entered in the next chapter.

**Continuity fact:** **Marek is asked to attest and says yes, and the chapter establishes on the page that he may: he is a reader, he is not on field duty, and an attestation outside the Office's service is not an attestation of this office.** He also says, once, in a corridor, in answer to a question nobody asked him, that he cannot hold a trace across two towers with one ear, and the assessor writes it down as a limitation and it is the volume's Tracer gate, identified and not entered.

**Ending type:** A ward voting to make itself poorer and safer, correctly, with the price read out loud before the vote.

**Next-chapter pull:** The Exchange will not withdraw, so the claim is entered on the twenty-first at a ward ledger bench by a copy clerk, and the back-cut comes on the twenty-third, and nobody in this city has a number for what it will cost except a firm that prices salvage.

### Chapter 00058 — The Reach Does Not Come Down

**POV:** Marek Vale.

**Location/time:** The ward ledger bench on the Sleetmonth twenty-first, a Saturday morning; the Bellweather reach and the ninth ward's low ground on the twenty-third, a Monday; the technical cell on the Monday afternoon.

**Immediate goal:** Get a true claim entered in a good hand with the cost printed in it, and then find out what the ward actually gave up, because a district cannot invoice against a number that describes somebody else's object.

**Resistance:** **The entry, and it is nearly botched by correct procedure.** Tovan Iles writes the claim as a copy clerk is trained to write it, and the cost line is not in the column because the column has six items and does not include a district's own assessment of its own loss, and her routine true copy — the one every copy clerk makes, in her own hand, dated, timed, initialled — preserves the whole sheet including the line the column does not want. She is not asked about it and she does not offer it and it goes in her own box with two other things, and **nobody knows.** The claim is entered at about ten, the entry bell is struck for the ninth ward's head, and the ninth ward's head does nothing, because the frame is dark, and this is a beat and not a scene, and the claim binds anyway because the bell is struck in a room with a witness in it and the glass on the wall is not the witness.

**The one System panel of the batch, at the moment of entry:**
```
CLAIM: the Bellweather reach does not come down to the quoted mark in the quoted hours; the ward's clearing rota is run and its first marked depth returned to the ward ledger.
ONE POSSIBLE TURN WILL CLOSE.
```

**Information revealed:** **The back-cut comes on the twenty-third and the reach does not come down.** The claim is entirely true. Dellow's men stand in a shed on an apron with a fortnight's work and a barge hired to a day, and the salvage does not go in, and the ward's crew does not go out, and the timber and the pump stay in the shed, and **the second window in nine weeks is gone and the wall goes into its third year of shoring from other timber or no shoring, and the Bellweather returns in two years and about eleven months instead of three.** The Exchange must strike a second line through in public, in the same column, on the same board, with a price in the margin, and it does so under its own refund rule and publishes it because the rule says it must.

**Action or decision:** **And the cost lands where nobody looked.** A claim is not weather. It does not void a window contract; **it breaches it**, and the breach falls on the ward, and Garrick Dellow's man serves a notice on the ninth ward assembly on the Monday morning requiring compensation for a window he bought and did not get, **and the ward has to be represented by a Storm Warden, and the ward has no money and Neris Kade is the Storm Warden.** The amount is stated. It is a number. It is the first time in this city that anybody has been handed a figure for a spend and a person to pay it, and it is wrong by about four times, in the payer's favour, and it is defensible.

**Ending type:** A true claim, correctly certified, publicly entered, entirely paid for, by the district that chose it, in a loss with no name on it and a price on it.

**Next-chapter pull and the volume's cliff:** The Exchange's refund arithmetic needs a **schedule of what the affected party had planned**, and the Exchange cannot get one, and the only instrument in the basin that records what a ward was able to do about its water is the **Standing Response Register** in the Council's technical cell, and Marek goes to look at the ninth ward's line because somebody has to, and **the line has a date against it and nothing under the date, and the last entry is older than anybody looking at it has been alive, and the clerk who produces it is not hiding anything and does not know what the page is.** A response that is not recorded was not available. And on the bench outside the technical cell, waiting, is a counter-reader who has quoted a fee and one term for the work the ward will need, **and the term is that the reading go on a board a warden is welcome to read, free, before the claim — which is a letter Neris Kade wrote in the Bellweather month, priced, and improved, and not solved.**
