## Central pressure

**A market has been invented for weather, and the product it sells is a possibility.**

In the Bellweather month the Weather Office certified a method whose costs cannot be documented. Nine weeks later a firm called the **Cloud Exchange** puts up a public price list, and the list does not sell forecasts. It sells **windows** — a named operation, a named set of hours, and a promise that the operation is dependable in them — and every window on the list is a claim-shaped assertion with a refund rule and no bell behind it.

The Office's answer, structurally, is that a window is not a claim. A window is a class of hours, not one; it names no place, no trigger, no witness, and it is not entered anywhere. A market can therefore sell dependability without spending anything, and it will do so honestly, and it will make money, and **it is the only party in the basin that is currently pricing what a claim costs.** That is the volume's engine and it is not a villainy: the Exchange built the instrument the Office refused to build.

**The Bellweather reach is the test case, because it is the one place the Office has no instrument and the Exchange has one.** A reach-level window is about a fortnight a year, of which roughly nine days are usable, and it is the ebb under a long band — the same mechanic that closed the Lower Sill convoy's forty minutes in Volume 01. The Office has never measured one. A float-and-chain instrument maker on the south-bank freight apron has been making tide gauges for forty years, and a market has now put one on the Bellweather reach, and the numbers are on a public board.

**The flood season then tests whether a city that can price a weather can also be told what it has lost.** The season's first back-cut comes in Sleetmonth, the Exchange has sold the reach window twice, and the ninth ward — the poorest ward in the city, four hundred and forty people on a low ground, a tower whose public glass has been dark for money since before the Bellweather month — discovers that the two people who bought its weather are not the Office and are not its own assembly either.

## Starting state

**Nine weeks after the forty-eighth day of the Bellweather month, and the method survived.**

- The corridor claim stands in the ledger as a true claim under a Director's authorization, and the public ledger has a column headed **CLAIM — DISPLACED TURNS**, with six items and no sponsor field, and a public table and a public hall know that something was spent at the Lower Sill and cannot see who spent it.
- **Marek Vale is dismissed from field duty and censured in the Office's own record.** He is not suspended from the glass benches. He may not be put on a watch, a roster, or a field list; he may not attest for the Office; he may not enter a claim as sponsor. He has about a third of his fine hearing gone in the left ear with a high thin ringing on that side, and he has never told anybody.
- **Tovan Iles is a copy clerk** in the west end of the records room, permanently, and the glass-tender's examination is in the spring and held by somebody else and she does not sit it. There is a true copy of the thirtieth's day sheet in her own box on the ground floor and nobody in the basin knows it exists.
- **Anwen Sarr's clause four is law-adjacent in practice:** a column headed *turns displaced*, which may name what closed and by no means who closed it. She gave the name away on the record and on purpose and has said in two hundred voices' hearing that she will defend it for the rest of her life.
- **The Lower Sill culvert is not rebuilt.** The timber and the pump are in a shed on the south-bank freight apron under a painted board, nine crew are still on the district's books, and a Bellweather of this order returns **under four years** on a water office's own wall.
- **Neris Kade got her warden's council to carry a quarterly return of the ward's clearing rota, shelter rota and pump order in four minutes.** It is the only such document in the ninth ward and it is now the only thing there anybody can buy.
- **Ninth Ward tower's public glass is still unreadable**, a bubbled temporary in a brass surround, and the reason is that nobody has money. A warden with a working feed in her tower has no way to publish anything to her own district.

## Starting power level

Marek Vale is a **Reader**. He can read a short local sequence and, on his own authority, bind nothing at all any more. His costs are inner-ear pressure half a tone up on the left, a metallic taste, and about a third of the lost fine range in one ear. In this volume those costs become professionally decisive: **a two-channel read is exactly what a reader with one good ear cannot do**, which is why the Tracer gate is a gate and not a promotion.

**What this volume spends, stated once.** **Batch 0001 spends one branch**, in Chapter 58, and it is spent by a district on purpose. Later batches add at most three more, and **none of them is Marek's own claim as sponsor**, because he cannot sponsor one. **No spent branch in this volume is reopened. Nothing in this volume is refunded. The Chapter 21 compound branch and the Chapter 29 branch both stay closed and both stay unprovable, and no chapter in this volume produces a document, a witness, a name or a paper that comes closer.**

## Working canon for this volume

Established or clarified by this outline, to be folded into `bible/world.md` and `bible/characters.md` at the volume audit. Items marked **[proposed]** are new detail.

1. **The calendar.** The Bellweather month is a long autumn month of at least forty-eight days. **Volume 02 opens seven weeks later, on the ninth of Sleetmonth, and no character has aged.** Sleetmonth is the first month of the wet quarter; the season's rhythm is *rain, clear, rain, clear*, and the clearing is called a **back-cut**. **The season's first back-cut, and the largest, comes in the middle of Sleetmonth** and is the one the Exchange has quoted. **[proposed]**
2. **A window is not a claim.** A window names an operation and a set of hours and asserts that the operation is dependable in them. It has no place, no trigger, no witness, and no entry anywhere, and **it therefore spends nothing and guarantees nothing.** The Office's position that a window is outside its authority is correct. **[proposed]**
3. **The reach gauge.** A copper float in a stilling well, an iron chain over a drum, a staff with marks cut in it, and a **sealed glass datum** cut and seated by hand so the float's travel is a linear record instead of a stretching chain. Made for forty years by tide gauges for the port, two breweries and a ferry landing. **The instrument existed. The Office never used one for a reach. The Exchange did.** **[proposed]**
4. **A force majeure clause does not cover a claim.** A claim is not weather. It is a witnessed civic act. **So a claim does not void a window contract, it breaches it**, and the breach falls on whoever entered it, and in Chapter 58 the party that has to answer for it is a Storm Warden. **[proposed]**
5. **The Standing Response Register.** A technical-cell record, one column per ward, one line per season, headed *available responses to a season's water*. **It is a record of what was written down, not of what was available**, and three wards have no entries at all for stretches of years, and the ninth ward's last entry is older than any of them. **This is the volume's new question and it is the cliff of Batch 0001.** **[proposed]**
6. **The compensation ledger is opened by arithmetic, not by reform.** A refund needs a price and a schedule of what the affected party had planned. The Exchange can supply the price and cannot supply the schedule. The Office can supply the schedule and has never supplied the price. **Neither party can open the ledger alone, and the ledger is therefore opened by accident in Act III.** **[proposed]**
7. **Sable Orin's terms.** She will do paid work on a condition, and the condition is the one Neris Kade wrote into a letter in the Bellweather month: *the reading goes on a board a warden is welcome to read, free, and she will not pass it to anybody who has to pay for it.* **She has learned the consent problem and is pricing it, and pricing it is not the same as solving it.** **[proposed]**
8. **Volume 02 contains no death caused by a claim.** The twelve deaths of Volume 12 must not have a rehearsal. A salvage man's bad back is not a death and does not become one. **[proposed]**
9. **The cuff is full.** Its third block has been full since the Bellweather month. Marek's next pencil goes on **the Exchange's own job docket**, because that is the paper in front of him, and Ivo Slate files a small agency report on everything. **[proposed]**

## Escalation sequence

### Act I — What a Window Is For (Chapters 49–58)

The censured man is employed by the market to fix its glass. A window is defined and priced. A district discovers its own quarterly return in a market schedule. The first back-cut is quoted, refused, and denied to a district on purpose. **The volume's only Batch 0001 spend is the ward's own reach window, given up by the ward, in writing, to stop a second sale of it.** The batch closes on a register with a heading and nothing under it.

### Act II — The Schedule of a Ward (Chapters 59–70)

The claim's refusal to be weather makes it a breach, and somebody has to answer for a breach. Assemblies discover that a district can refuse a claim and cannot refuse a schedule. **Marek identifies the Tracer gate — that a two-tower read needs a second reader and he has one ear — and asks Sable Orin for a second reader and is quoted a term instead of an answer.** A counter-reader begins to look like a supplier.

### Act III — The Price of Preparation (Chapters 71–82)

The compensation arithmetic. A refund price and a schedule of what the ward could have done, and the two do not meet. **Marek enters the Tracer stage the only way the gate allows: with a second reader anchoring the local end, at the cost of sequence bleed and a worse ear.** The Exchange cannot conceal the Bellweather dry line, because a market cannot sell a line it has struck through twice in public.

### Act IV — The Ledger (Chapters 83–96)

The temporary compensation ledger is opened, it is provisional, it pays one claimant and refuses another, and **the districts discover that a ledger which pays for documented losses is a ledger which only pays districts that document well.** The volume's promise is discharged; the veto question is not.

## Midpoint reversal — Chapter 57

For twenty chapters the volume's question has been *can a market sell a window honestly.* The answer on the page is **yes, and it has, and it is currently doing it, and the price is wrong by a factor of about four in the direction that flatters the seller.**

The Exchange's price for a reach window is not a lie. **It is a price for a different thing.** An underwriter's buyer cannot use a reach — a carrier wants a lane and a barge and forty minutes of a flat at the bottom of a bucket. A ward wants a flat for a pump and a timber stack and nine men. The same forty minutes, the same air, the same water, two entirely different objects, and the market priced the one it could sell. **A dry line is worth nothing and everything depending on who is looking at it, and by September there is a published number, and the published number is the Exchange's.**

Marek's reversal: for nine weeks he has believed the Office's sentence — *it is not accidental that a claim's cost cannot be documented; it is the design* — and he has believed that a cost nobody can price is a cost nobody can argue about and therefore a cost that is safe to keep inflicting. **He now has the opposite instrument and it is worse.** A price is a measurement, and a measurement is a claim about what a thing was worth, and there was no instrument in this basin for one and there is now one, and it belongs to a firm that sells salvage, and it is four times too high, and **a ward cannot invoice against a number that describes somebody else's object.**

And the ward's answer, in Chapter 57, is not a better number. It is to deny itself the window altogether.

## Volume climax — Chapters 78–88

The Exchange's forward board goes public with the Bellweather line struck through for a second consecutive quarter, in public, with a price, and the board is the market's own instrument and cannot be withdrawn without breaking its own refund rule. **An underwriter's letter goes to the Council, and an assessor opens a file, and the file is called a compensation ledger because the alternative word is a fraud.**

The climax is not a fight. **It is a hearing in which a market's price and a district's schedule are both admitted, neither is sufficient, and the thing that finally moves is a warden producing a document nobody asked for.** Three candidates for that document, and **only one of them is admissible and it is the worst of them:**

1. Neris Kade's seven-line page — admitted, not compellable, still.
2. Tovan Iles's private copy — nobody knows it exists and she is not asked.
3. **Garrick Dellow's salvage schedule** — an ordinary commercial document, in an ordinary hand, on ordinary paper, showing exactly what a man intended to do in a reach with forty minutes. **It is the only document in the volume that prices a preparation, and it is a salvage man's shopping list, and the compensation the ledger pays is measured against a stripper's plan.** That is the volume's last joke and it is not funny.

And the reversal that ends the volume: **the ledger pays the ward for the window it gave up, at the Exchange's price, because the Exchange's price is the only price in the basin, and the ward's own figure is refused for want of a record.** Anwen Sarr's clause four, again, defeating the person who wrote it, correctly, in a room, with a clerk.

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 96 the following are settled, in the record, on the page:

1. **The Exchange cannot profitably conceal the Bellweather dry line.** Not because anybody stopped it and not because it was exposed. **Because a market that has struck a line through twice in public cannot sell the thing behind it, and a market will not be the last party in a basin to admit a number exists.** The struck-through line is on a board in three places and the refund rule that put it there is the Exchange's own.
2. **A temporary compensation ledger exists**, opened by arithmetic, administered by a Council assessor, provisional, expiring at the end of the financial year, and **paying one claimant and refusing another in the same afternoon.** It is a ledger and not a fund, and it pays documented loss only.
3. **Local assemblies still have no real veto power, and this volume is the reason.** A district can refuse a claim. A district cannot refuse a price, cannot withdraw a return it has already sent, and cannot invoice against a number that describes somebody else's object. **The right to refuse is real, cheap, correct, and insufficient, and Volume 01's audience is owed that sentence.**
4. **A reach has a price, and the price is wrong, and the wrongness is the finding.** The Exchange's figure is four times the ward's and both are defensible and the difference is the whole of what the Exchange knows about its own buyers.
5. **Marek has lost the ability to act alone and gained the ability to hold a trace across two towers.** The Tracer stage is entered with a second reader anchoring the local end, and the cost is sequence bleed — he begins to read a movement that is not his — and a further loss in the left ear, and **the second reader is a woman who will not work for free and whose terms are better than his.**
6. **Tovan Iles is a copy clerk for the whole of this volume** and copies two claims whose costs are not in the column, and her box now holds three things, and the first person to ask her about any of them is not Tovan. **Her demotion is not overturned and is not a favour and nobody in the Office thanks her.**
7. **Sable Orin has begun to be paid for readings and has begun to attach conditions, and the conditions are better than anybody's and are still not consent.**

## New question for Volume 03

The **Standing Response Register** has a heading and nothing under it, and it has had nothing under it for the ninth ward for longer than anybody in the room has been alive, and the clerk who produces it is not hiding anything and does not know what it is. **A record of what was written down is not a record of what was available, and somebody's job was writing it down, and that person is a person who could be named.** Volume 02 inherits: *if the Office's own historical record of possible responses has whole districts missing out of it, and the missing is not an error but a shape, then who is the shape, and who benefits from a district that is not on the page — and why is the exchange of a good reading for a good price the easiest transaction in the basin?*

Volume 03's answer arrives as an archive rather than a market, which is the right shape: the buried record of forecasts that were officially never made is kept by a dispersed network of archivists and former clerks, and the first person to understand that disclosure without consent is another form of control is Sable Orin, in the worst possible moment for her to learn it.

## Canon guardrails for this volume

1. **A read is free. A window spends nothing and guarantees nothing.** The Exchange's product is a bet with a refund rule, and that is the volume's structural irony: **a guarantee requires spending a possibility, and the market has decided to price the gamble instead.** No window in this volume is a binding and no chapter may treat one as a claim.
2. **Marek is dismissed from field duty and may not sponsor a claim in this volume.** He may still read, may still work a bench, may still attest **outside the Office's service** for a private party, and the volume makes a point of establishing that all four of those are lawful. **Every spend in this volume is sponsored by somebody else**, and he is the instrument, the reader, or the man who cut the glass.
3. **One spend in Batch 0001, in Chapter 58, and it is a district's own and deliberate.** The displaced preparation must be named, physical, attributable, and printed in the column in the ordinary language of the district affected. **It is the ward's own reach window, and the ward chose to lose it, and nobody is refunded.**
4. **The Chapter 21 compound branch and the Chapter 29 branch stay closed and stay unprovable.** No chapter in this volume produces a document, a witness, a name or a paper that comes closer to proving either, and no state file may describe how anyone could.
5. **The hearing loss is permanent, is never repaired, and is never explained to anybody.** It is used physically — a whistle in the rain, a figure asked for twice, a bench worked from the wrong side — and **it is the mechanical reason the Tracer gate is a gate.**
6. **The Ninth Ward frame is unreadable until the last chapter of the volume and the reason is money.** 4486 was paid for and never collected; its crown is a private plate in a tube. **No character in Chapters 49–95 learns that Marek destroyed the paid-for pane.**
7. **Tovan's demotion is not overturned and is not a favour.** She is a copy clerk in every appearance in Volumes 02 through 10. **Her private copies are not compellable and nobody knows how many she has.**
8. **Lior Venn's problem is not named again.** It was named once, in Chapter 35 of Volume 01, in a corridor, in ninety seconds. The records pressure in this volume is carried by the **Standing Response Register in the technical cell**, which is a different institution from Records and a different mechanism, and the two must never be merged.
9. **Malle and Venn stay separate.** Malle does not obstruct this volume. He is a Deputy managing a method that has been recommended for continued trial and he is beginning to be frightened of what he is staffing, which is not an obstruction and is not a redemption.
10. **Rook is not the villain and is not the answer.** He is the reason a window is outside the Office's authority, and he is right, and it is the problem. **The reader should be more afraid of him at the end of this volume than at the start.**
11. **Anwen Sarr is not softened and is not bought off, and she loses correctly twice.** She gives away a name for a column in Volume 01 and a schedule in this one, and both times it is a real thing lost for a real thing gained.
12. **The relationship stays professional through Act I and Act II.** No attraction beat, no rescue, no jealousy. **The word partnership is said once, late, and it is said in a yard, and Volume 08 owns the deliberate choice.**
13. **Sable Orin is not in the Quiet Ledger and the Quiet Ledger is not yet a thing.** No chapter in Act I names either. **Her consent problem is live and is not solved by a speech.**
14. **No new final enemy, no new magic rule, no new faction, no padding.** A salvage dealer, an instrument maker, and a market are not a faction and not an enemy.

### Reconciliation decisions to lock at the start of Batch 0001

1. **The censure, the demotion, the column, the painted board, the routing fragment and the dark frame are all Volume 01 facts and are not re-staged.** Chapters 49–58 assume them and use them.
2. **The Exchange's Bellweather quotation line, struck through, is a new fact introduced in Chapter 52 and it is the only retrospectively new information about the thirtieth in this volume.** It says a window was quoted and not delivered. **It does not say why, and no character in this volume can find out why, and nobody is told that Marek spent it.** The Office has publicly said it has no instrument that looks at a reach; the Exchange has a number; the two facts sit side by side and nobody joins them.
3. **The private plate and the tower's own plate are in their Volume 01 positions.** The tower's own plate is in the ninth ward feed seat and Marek has still not been up the ladder. The private plate is in the tube. **Neither is used in Act I.**
4. **The cuff is full and stays full.** The Exchange's docket takes the next pencil, and Ivo Slate's agency report is the mechanism.
5. **No watch, no roster, no field list, no Office attestation, and no sponsorship.** Every one of those is checked on the page at least once, in a scene, by a person who is competent and has a reason.
