## Central pressure

**A buried archive contains forecasts that were officially never made, while Sable's readers distribute dangerous partial claims.**

Two pressures, in opposition, and neither of them is a villain.

**The first is an archive with no institution.** It is not a room and it is not a conspiracy. It is a dispersed set of ordinary books that nobody with power has ever asked for: a tower keeper's fifth column, a shed foreman's level book going back to 1888, a shop foreman's day book with a Director's circular copied into it entire, a sheet clerk's duty book in a house, a plasterer's day book in a yard, a ward office drawer with a label on it, and a nineteen-year-old's drawer under a glass weight. **The count is seven and it is fixed.** Every one of them was copied, kept, or put in a drawer by somebody who was not asked, and **there is no name for the whole of it, no form, no bell and no office.** The woman who holds the best of them refuses the naming in terms: *the moment you call it a thing it is a place, and a place is a thing that can be asked for.*

**The second is a reader's partial truth, distributed without consent.** Sable Orin's sheets name a corridor and an hour and no place, which means **a district cannot refuse them**, and she has printed for eleven people in eleven weeks and cannot account for four of her eleven sheets and has never asked a soul. One of them — a plasterer of the fourth ward — paid a shilling for a printed list of street names, chose the wrong Fen Row out of two by entirely correct reasoning, and painted a directive that stood a crew down six hours in a wet field and put a man of seventy-nine on a board and down four stairs in the dark. **The reading was right.** That is the point: the failure is not error. It is a true thing, published past the point where anybody could say no to it.

**And the two pressures are the same pressure.** A record nobody can be asked for and a claim nobody can refuse are the same defect seen from two ends, and the volume's argument is that **the cure for one is the disease of the other**: the moment somebody with authority builds a place where these books go, the books become producible, and producing them is a taking.

## Escalation sequence

**Act boundaries, and this volume is four batches of twelve: 97–108 Act I, 109–120 Act II, 121–132 the close of Act II, 133–144 Act III.** An earlier prompt labelled Batch 0003 as Act III; it is not, and the Batch 0003 and 0004 prompts must not inherit that.

### Act I — The archive's shape, and a district that refuses in writing (Chapters 97–108) — **PROSE, COMPLETE**

A Council minute asks in item four whose office receives a district's statement of what it can do about its water, **and it cannot be answered out of a book**, because there was never an addressed envelope and the Register's dates are the days the Register wrote them. A clerk writes nine letters, posts eight and holds the ninth, because a district is a person and has to be asked to her face. A nineteen-year-old begins a want-list nobody asked for. A plasterer invents a street for a reading that named a corridor, gets it wrong out of entirely correct reasoning, and costs a crew six hours. **A ward's public light burns for eight days and goes out, because the tin is a week old and there is no column for a lamp.** A chair refuses correctly to send her ward's own book to anybody and offers it to a district on request. **A fourth ward refuses the Council, a firm, a form and a list in one document, in its own name, on its own board, at its own cost, 8–1 with the one minuted.** A reader publishes that refusal without asking, is told in writing inside two hours that the practice is the same with the ends the other way round, **and does not take it down.** A sheet clerk of sixty-one comes up a hill on a bicycle and gives away one leaf out of nine hundred and takes the rest home in a parcel that was not tied, on terms she stated before she was asked.

**The batch spends nothing and no branch closes.** It ends on a refusal nobody withdrew and a name nobody will give.

### Act II — A missing convoy, and a consent-based archive argued rather than agreed (Chapters 109–132)

**The spine is a missing refugee convoy.** Somebody's list of about eleven people, or a boat's manifest, or a ward's transfer sheet, is out, and **the disappearance is a record problem and not a weather problem.** Invent the convoy, name it, date it, and let the batch discover that **the paper's absence is the only evidence anybody has that the people existed.** Nobody dies. **No Volume 01 chapter-shaped absence may be reused: Pere Die Fisk and the Ferrygate residents are closed.**

**Sable's readers, second wave.** The plasterer was the bad case. Act II needs a **good** case — a district that used a partial reading correctly and can say so — **and a cost case**, a second use of a reading that was right and was not attributable. Her fourth notebook carries both. **No second person may be named in the sheet-clerk shape. Hester Skeat is the only one.**

**The consent argument, argued and not settled.** The ninth ward answered the Council's form and the fourth ward refused it, and a Council technical cell now has two answers in nine days that contradict each other and reaches for a schedule. **Sabin Orle is staffing and frightened and obstructs nothing and is redeemed by nothing. Anwen Sarr is owed nothing and unsoftened and may appear once, correctly, and gets nothing.**

**A working infiltration.** A custodian, a press room, a post room, a rule book with a return nobody has to read, a basket emptied on a Friday, and the Council's own postwar day book. **Get inside it properly, by a person with a lawful reason and a small lie, and let the person who goes in be somebody the reader already has a reason to distrust.**

**Ione Sere's first partial confession, late and hard and partial.** She is the archivist and former custodian, forty-six, and she told one deliberate lie in Volume 01 Chapter 47. **What she brings is about the Council's own day book, the wire basket and the rule-book return. She may not be identified, dated, or placed in a way that answers who signed the Meridian Cut order or why Anja's route was sealed rather than closed. Batch 0003 finishes what she starts.**

**A reader's position exposed.** Sable is paid a shilling a week to be findable, the appointment is in a book in an office, and **she has not learned what the appointment has made her.** Act II may put the knife nearer the skin; **it may not cut it before the volume's own chosen moment, and it must not be Marek who tells her.**

### Act III — The false *perfect* forecast, and the first page of a consent-based public archive (Chapters 133–144)

**The resolution, and it is the volume's promise discharged.** A false *perfect* forecast is exposed **before it can close a community's last evacuation branch**, and **Sable Orin agrees to submit her records to a consent-based public archive.** The two are the same act seen from two ends: a forecast that is complete cannot be refused, and an archive that is complete cannot be withheld. **She agrees to a consent-based archive, which is not the same as a complete one, and that is the whole of her arc.**

**The climax is institutional and is not a fight.** Nobody learns to be a better reader. The thing that changes is that a community is asked, in public, on a page somebody has to sign, and the false forecast is stopped by an argument that a district is entitled to make.

## Midpoint reversal — Chapters 117–120, at the end of Batch 0002

For the first half of the volume the question is *can the buried record be produced.* The answer on the page is **yes, it can be read, and reading it is the problem.**

The records exist. Somebody kept them, competently, in the ordinary way, and they contain readings that were made and never entered anywhere. **And the reason nobody can produce them is not concealment.** It is that there is no office, no form and no index that would make a district clerk, a tower keeper or a plasterer a person who can be asked — and **the moment somebody with authority builds a place where these books go, the books become producible, and producing them is a taking.** The batch's discovery is that the missing convoy's paper is missing in exactly this way: not burned, not suppressed, **never indexed**, and its absence is the only proof the people existed.

So the volume's reversal: **the cure for an uncompellable record is a compellable one, and the volume's whole argument is that this trade is not worth making.** The nine uncompellable books survive Act I because nobody asked for them. **They are not safe because they are hidden. They are safe because seven people have never been asked.**

And the thing that makes it a reversal rather than a discovery: **Marek came into this volume believing that truth requires care in publication rather than exposure, and he leaves Act II understanding that the reason is not publication at all. It is that the instrument which would make the record safe to publish is the same instrument that would let somebody take it.**

## Volume climax — Chapters 139–144

A **perfect** forecast is circulating — one answer, no uncertainty, no place where a district can put its own observation — and it is false, and **it is about to close a community's last evacuation branch.** Being complete is what makes it binding-shaped; a forecast that cannot be wrong about anything is a forecast that has already spent the alternative in which that community chooses differently.

**It is exposed before it binds, and it is exposed by a district, not by a reader.** The reader can say that it is false. **The district is the only party that can say what it would have done instead, and that is what stops it.** The exposure is public, incomplete and deniable in exactly the way Volume 01's was, and the cost is permanent and is not refunded.

In the same stretch, and because the two are the same act, **Sable Orin agrees to submit her records to a consent-based public archive** — on terms she states before she is asked, in the register Hester Skeat used on a bench, and **she does not get a vote and does not want one.** Her fourth notebook goes in with her own clause under it and a district's written objection already in the book.

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 144 the following are settled, in the record, on the page:

1. **The false *perfect* forecast is stopped before it binds**, and no branch is spent doing it, **because a district was asked and answered in public and the alternative in which it chose differently was still open when the answer came.**
2. **A consent-based public archive exists**, and it is not complete, and Sable Orin's records are in it on terms, and **the seventh uncompellable books are still uncompellable and still nobody's.**
3. **The record of what was written down is no longer mistaken for a record of what was available**, and the Council's own day book has been read by somebody who had a lawful reason to read it.
4. **A refusal is a refusal only when somebody is obliged to hear it**, and the fourth ward's page and a reader's clause are both still on a wall in the same paint, neither withdrawn.
5. **Marek has lost the ability to treat precision as a private burden**, and he has not gained a stage, and the thing he has is worse and better than a stage: he can now say in public what he does not know and be believed about the part he does.
6. **The hearing is unrepaired, unmentioned and unexplained to anybody, and Sable Orin still has not learned it.**
7. **The bleed is still unreported, unreasoned and unresolved**, the cuff still has three blocks and no fourth, and he still intends to tell and has not.
