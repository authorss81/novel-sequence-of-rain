# Volume 01 Outline

**Volume:** 01
**Title:** *The Bellweather Forecast*
**Chapters:** 1–48
**Relationship scope:** Marek and Tarin's first forced operational partnership; professional respect only, no romance beat
**Power scope:** Reader throughout, with an unstable Tracer aptitude; no advancement is earned in this volume

---

## Central pressure

The **Bellweather Front** collides over Veyra: cold air off the Nacre Range, the warm lake draw, and a fast Ternway turn arriving inside one cycle, so the Six Movements reach Veyra out of their usual order. The front does not arrive until Chapter 29. Chapters 1–28 are the three days in which every faction in the city discovers what it wants a forecast to be.

The Weather Office is asked to produce **one safe answer for two incompatible goods**: keep the Kestrel airship corridor open for the yards, and keep the low wards from drowning. Those cannot both be true, because the western draw that opens a corridor is the same draw that has to ease for the exposed floodplain to be passable. Somebody will be given a claim, and somebody will pay for it in a possibility.

The volume's real antagonist in the first half is not a person. It is the **reflexive narrowing**: every adult in Veyra, from the Director down to a copy clerk, responds to uncertainty by reducing the number of things they are willing to say out loud. Marek is unusually good at it. That is the reason he gets the assignment.

## Starting state

Marek Vale, 32, repairs sequence glass in the Weatherglass maintenance shop. He is a competent instrument maker and a trained **Reader** who has bound perhaps a dozen short local claims in his life, all of them narrow and all of them his own business. He is not a Conductor, cannot follow a sequence across two towers without help, and has never made a claim that another person had to act on.

He is also the only person in the building who does not already know why the Office is frightened. The Office knows the six movements are going to arrive out of order. Nobody upstairs is willing to write that sentence down, because a written anomaly is a budget question, a Council question, and a career question. So the knowledge sits in the maintenance shop with a man who repairs glass for a living.

Three engines are already running before he knows any of it:

- **The Cloud Exchange has already bought the Bellweather window.** Maro Hesk paid for a narrow forecast under the Office's standard secrecy clause, which means the Office has a commercial interest in the answer being one answer and unarguable. Maro did not create the pressure. He profitably depends on it.
- **The Ninth Ward tower's public glass is unreadable.** It is the poor ward of the low eastern ground and it contains Low Spindle. Its signal pane cracked, the replacement was deferred for two years, and a bubbled temporary pane went up a fortnight ago that cannot carry a legible state. The ward has been living off handwritten copies.
- **Tovan Iles, 27, is the copy clerk on the ward ledger bench.** Her whole craft is the entry pencil and the bell, and she is about to learn what those two things do to the world.

## Starting relationships

- **Marek and Tarin Senn** have not spoken in two years. The T-3 Hearthward falling-out — a technically accurate forecast that omitted a rooftop landing option — is unresolved. He remembers that she was right. He remembers that she made him explain it to a room of angry passengers, and that he did not enjoy it, and that he has never worked out which of those two things is the part he is ashamed of.
- **Marek and Corrin Malle** are in a stable working arrangement of mutual professional respect. Malle protects the staff. Malle is also the man who will decide that a spend on his watch is a spend with his name on it, and he is already thinking about that before Marek is.
- **Marek and Anwen Sarr** are on bad terms from Hearthward. She has already learned once that his forecasts are correct and incomplete.
- **Marek and Oren Vale** have not repaired anything together in six years. Oren is in Low Spindle. Marek has a reason to be there in this volume that he cannot tell his father about.
- **Marek and Neris Kade** have met once, at a flooded door, where he was useful and she noticed. She has no opinion about him otherwise, which is exactly the state he needs to break.
- **Marek and Tovan Iles** are strangers on adjacent benches. She is one examination from a glass-tender's post and terrified of being the person whose handwriting turns out to matter.
- **Marek and Pell Ardan** are the oldest thing in his life. Pell worked beside him in the rain-maintenance apprenticeships, is not a reader, and is the one person who can tell Marek he is wrong without needing a reason from the Office.
- **Marek and Sable Orin** have met once, at a public meeting, and did not part well. She believes the Office's secret is its real weapon; he believes she is careless with other people's danger.
- **Ione Sere** knows more about Marek's mother than Marek does, and has decided, professionally and for her own reasons, that he should keep it that way for now.

## Starting power level

Marek is a **Reader**: he can see the likely order of a local sequence and bind one short claim, and he cannot safely follow a trace across two towers. His costs are inner-ear pressure, metallic taste, temporary loss of fine hearing, and a growing inability to ignore a sequence once he has seen it. In this volume those costs get worse in a specific, permanent way, described under Volume climax.

**What this volume spends, stated once.** Three witnessed bindings close branches in this volume: Marek's Hearthward conditional claim in Chapter 5, a Charter-certified trial claim on the Nacre Gate release in Chapter 21, and Marek's Kestrel corridor claim in Chapter 29. A fourth, much smaller binding happens in Chapter 8, when Sable Orin's unsigned reading becomes a claim because Neris Kade acts on it; nobody ever identifies what it closed, including in the state files. **All four are accurate. None is reopened. Nothing else in this volume spends anything.** Marek's own noticing, his private plates, and the twelve-year trace in Chapter 18 cost him sleep, hearing, and standing, and never a branch.

**Bellweather is a reach, not a ward.** It is the low ground the Ninth Ward drains into, cut by the Lower Sill culvert, with a freight apron and two pump houses at its edges. The storm's name and the culvert's dedication stone are the only things that still call it a place. Keep the ward line where the bible puts it — drawn on watershed boundaries above and across the reach — and keep the flood damage in the reach **and** in Low Spindle's lowest rows at the same time, because those rows drain into it. One event, two names for where it lands.

## Working canon for this volume

These are established or clarified by this outline and hold for Chapters 1–48. Items marked **[proposed]** are new detail rather than rules from the bible; they are collected here so batch writers and the volume audit have them in one place, and they should be folded into `bible/world.md` and `bible/characters.md` at the volume audit.

1. **Bellweather** is the low flood reach below the Ninth Ward's terraces and the destination of its drainage: the Lower Sill culvert, a freight apron, and two pump houses. The storm is named for the reach; the dedication stone on the culvert is the oldest surviving use of the name. **[proposed]**
2. **The Lower Sill culvert** is a Bellweather-era concrete landmark with a cracked headwall, shored twice, failing again. It was to be re-piped and shored in the night before the front by a nine-person municipal convoy carrying shoring timber and a replacement dewatering pump. The Office calls it deferred maintenance. It is the oldest continuously deferred repair in the city, and the Front returns in three years. **[proposed]**
3. **The Ninth Ward tower's glass chain, in order, and it is the spine of the volume's final image.** At the T-4 reconnection a crew installed a glass bundle: the **signal pane** went into the public frame and the **spare feed plate** went into the sequence feed. The feed plate cracked recently; in Chapter 1 Marek cuts and fits a correct replacement, which is an ordinary repair and corrects no bias. The public pane cracked later, a bubbled temporary pane went up a fortnight before the opening, and the permanent replacement — cut by Pell Ardan and collected the morning of Chapter 3 — becomes Marek's private sequence plate. The tower's public glass therefore stays dark from Chapter 3 to Chapter 48. **[proposed]**
4. **Terminology to add at the volume audit:** *signal pane* (the glazed public notice in a tower's frame, showing only a state) and *sequence plate* (the reader's working glass in a tower's feed, showing only a trace). Both are existing furniture, not new magic, and the distinction is the same one the bible already draws between signal glass and sequence glass. **[proposed]**
5. **The Bellweather collision** is visible in the traces from the moment the front's three air masses converge, roughly seventy hours before it reaches Veyra. The Office's instruments can measure it. Only a bench trace shows the **order**. **[proposed]**
6. **Ilva Sorn**, 34, is the Kestrel Line's route officer. She signs off every launch and every window. She believes published uncertainty costs them contracts they cannot afford to lose, and she is right about the arithmetic and wrong about what a contract is for. Ordinary, not a foil, not a reader. **[proposed]**
7. **Feth Raal** runs a barometer repair stall at the foot of the Weatherglass steps. Marek has re-glazed her dials for years and never charged her. A customer and a gossip conduit, not a plot device. **[proposed]**
8. **Nessa Tarrow** is the Kestrel Yards night watch. She copies official notices to the yard boards. In Chapter 2 she holds her first two versions of the same notice; in Chapter 20 she is told to hold only one. That difference is the whole book. **[proposed]**
9. **Ivo Slate** is the Cloud Exchange's licensed agent on the Kestrel window market. Courteous, correctly within the terms of his licence, and the reason a small yard's window disappears for a season. Named without a Cor- deliberately, so that Corrin Malle and Lior Venn are never confused with a third. **[proposed]**
10. **Ione Sere tells one deliberate lie in this volume.** In Chapter 47 she tells Marek, flatly and with total conviction, that routing entries from the pre-Cut years were renumbered during a ledger migration. It is not true. Her Volume 11 testimony must address this lie alongside both of her recorded acts; it compounds her guilt and is not a substitute for it. **[proposed]**
11. **Nacre Gate, the upper reservoir, and the Ferrygate district** are the setting for the Chapter 21 trial claim: the basin's northern water gate, the reservoir it holds, and the market district and ferry crossing it serves. **[proposed]**
12. **Volume 01 contains no deaths.** The costs are seed stock, a culvert, a season of drainage, hours of standing water, nine hours of two people trapped in a room that a vanished rota would have emptied, a career, and a clerk's post. This is deliberate: the twelve deaths of Volume 12 are a different kind of event and must not have a smaller rehearsal. Do not add one.
13. **Marek's permanent hearing loss** is real and never repaired. It is caused by four minutes in cold moving water in Chapter 31, and it costs him the fine hearing a sequence reader depends on. It is not the Volume 12 sequence burn and must not be written as a preview of it.
14. **Storm phases are not called turns.** *Turn* stays the technical term for a single change in a sequence. Weather phases in this volume are the **leading rain**, the **second belt**, and the **collision**, and the working clock is the six-hour **cycle** and its **watch bell**.

## Escalation sequence

### Act I — Plates and Pretence (Chapters 1–10)

Marek's ordinary competence, the first branch, and the discovery that the Office's instruments can see the danger while only a bench trace shows its order. The front is seventy hours out at Chapter 1. By Chapter 10 it is one hour out, the Bellweather watch is staffed, and the batch's last discovery is a filing mark with a piece cut out of the citation beside it.

### Act II — The Shared Instrument (Chapters 11–21)

Forced cooperation with the Kestrel Line, the Exchange's purchased window, Sable's open readings, the first field trip into the Ninth Ward tower, and Marek's father in Low Spindle. **The midpoint lands in Chapter 21.**

### Act III — The Bellweather Watch (Chapters 22–35)

The storm. The demand for one answer. The convoy that never leaves. See the Volume climax section for the exact chapter chain, because this is the part that must not be improvised.

### Act IV — The Public Dry Line (Chapters 36–48)

The inquiry, the censure, Tovan's demotion, the public hall, and the archive. The volume's promise is discharged and the larger mystery is handed over intact.

## Midpoint reversal — Chapter 21

Before the watch, the Weather Council certifies a **trial claim** on the Nacre Gate release as a controlled test of the single-answer method: a narrow claim, legally certified under the Charter with a named sponsor, a published expiry, a listed alternative, and one regional seat concurring. It is a good claim. It works. No casualties, the Ferrygate market opens on schedule, a Nacre Holds grain convoy makes its window, and the Council's minutes praise the method.

Marek reads the traces, and the trial claim spent a branch he can see as a gap. What it displaced was the coordinated independent preparation: the Ferrygate district's own rain-garden crews, its own ferry fallback, and a low-ground shelter rota that had been moving two elderly residents out of a ground-floor room before anyone had a reason to move them. The claim made the release definite, and every one of those fallbacks stopped being compatible with a plan that was not going to be needed.

The consequence arrives two days later as a small event that no notice covers: those two residents are in a ground-floor room for nine hours while water comes through a door that was going to be barricaded by a rota which no longer exists, and the district has to improvise. Nobody is hurt. Nobody ever returns to that flat. And no one in the room can produce evidence, because the evidence is a gap in a trace that Marek read on a plate he is not allowed to publish.

**What the reversal changes.** For twenty chapters Marek believes the problem is that nobody will make the call. After Chapter 21 he knows the call is made correctly, cheaply, and invisibly, and that the method's success is measured in options removed. He says so publicly, in the room where the method is being congratulated. He is right, and it costs him everything he had with both sides at once: the Office stops treating him as a useful instrument, and the assemblies stop treating him as a listener. Anwen Sarr's refusal in Chapter 20 was, by this point, the correct institutional result rather than an obstruction, and the reader should be able to see that it is correct.

The Bellweather Watch is then the moral trap of the volume: he knows exactly what a good result costs, and he goes and produces one.

## Volume climax — Chapters 27–35

The storm. The exact chain, because the Canon guardrails depend on it.

- **Ch 27 — the demand.** Rook's authorization requires one claim to cover the yards and the low wards. Tarin's Line, the route captains, and the Exchange have converged on a single corridor turn, and the Exchange's purchased window is admissible in the room because four licensed carriers have already paid against it.
- **Ch 28 — the alternatives that fail.** Marek offers a conditional claim with a listed alternative and a published expiry. The Council's own conditions require a positive routing effect at the yards inside the expiry window. It is a well-constructed claim and it is not authorized. He goes to Tarin, who has flown the dry line on local glass twice, and asks for a window. She gives it, and the Kestrel Line becomes the instrument that makes his claim true — which is the partnership this volume was built to produce.
- **Ch 29 — the claim.** Marek binds it: the Kestrel corridor remains passable through 04:00, entered on the ward ledger, witness bell struck, Director's stamp on it. It is a **conditional forecast**, and it binds when the Kestrel Line's arrival at the yards' mark is accepted. It is true. It saves the corridor.
- **Ch 30 — the air.** The passage. One avoidable injury to a crewman that the claim itself creates, because holding the turn also holds the crosswind that would normally have blown the mooring lines clear. Tarin takes the lift herself when it should have been a junior's. The relationship milestone is competence under shared risk, not rescue.
- **Ch 31 — the convoy.** The Lower Sill convoy is loaded and on the south-bank flat with a window of forty minutes, and the dry line never comes, because a claim that holds the corridor turn has fixed the turn. Nobody ordered the convoy to stop. There is no decision to point at. In the middle of this chapter Marek goes into four minutes of cold moving water after a child in Low Spindle and comes out with damaged hearing and a permanent ringing ear. It changes nothing about the record.
- **Ch 32 — the culvert.** The headwall fails in the peak rain. Water goes down the Bellweather reach and into Low Spindle's lowest rows at the same time, because those rows drain into the reach. The south-bank road is cut by a landslide at 05:00, which is storm damage and is attributed as storm damage in the public account; the missing repair is the spend.
- **Ch 33 — the absence.** The convoy shed, the timber, the pump, the nine crewmen who are still at the yard when everyone needs them. Counter-memory: the shed foreman remembers the whole night clearly and has no account of why they stood down. Neris's list of what the notice did not say becomes a document.
- **Ch 34 — Oren.** Marek goes to Low Spindle to be useful and ends up with his father, who knows what a nine-person convoy carrying shoring timber to a cracked headwall is worth. Oren does not know the cause. He knows the shape of the loss. This chapter also establishes the fire that keeps burning: the culvert is not rebuilt, and the front returns in three years.
- **Ch 35 — the burial.** Malle reclassifies the entry. Venn's reclassification problem is named in one exchange: readings that were never acted on are not compellable, because an observation is not a promise. Tovan Iles refuses to falsify the entry and is demoted to copy work. Rook's authorization stands. Not the method dies — the record does.

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 48 the following are settled, in the record, on the page:

1. **Marek's spend is public.** He forces the Bellweather traces into the public forecast hall in a form that is incomplete and deniable: the sequence, the turn, the corridor, the expiry, and a spent-turn column that names what closed without naming who chose it. The city can see that something was spent at the Lower Sill. The city cannot prove who spent it, and Marek is dismissed from the field and censured.
2. **Tovan Iles keeps the true entry.** She copies the ward ledger by hand, as a copy clerk is trained to do, and keeps the sheet in her own box. She is on a copy desk for the rest of the volume and for Volumes 2 through 10. She is not rewarded for it in this volume, and her demotion is never overturned as a favour to her.
3. **The first public dry line exists.** A physical and civic marker: the convoy shed kept as it was, a hand-lettered board at the Bellweather freight apron listing the load that never moved, and a column in the public ledger that readers learn to look for. The Bellweather dry line is the first thing in the city that people learn to read.
4. **A citation, not a document.** In the archive Marek finds a chart provenance line carrying a **lodger's name in a hand he knows** — his mother's — beside a filing mark and a routing reference. It proves three things and no more: the Ninth Ward Record was lodged as a witnessed claim, his mother signed it, and the index reference beside the signature was cut out of the citation trail by hand. The record itself is not there and will not be for ten more volumes.
5. **The cut index names Ottine Vask.** The remaining fragment reads as a routing entry: **VASK, O., R-9**, routing desk nine, in a clerk's hand. Ione Sere tells Marek that routing entries from the pre-Cut years were renumbered during a ledger migration. It is not true, and the reader is not told yet that it is not true.
6. **The unrepaired trace is physical, and it was proved in Chapter 18.** The Ninth Ward tower's sequence plate — the one Marek fitted — renders a Witness movement dated twelve years back, arriving from the buried vein rather than out of the glass. The Meridian is still carrying the record of a claim whose index was removed. It is not a new power and it reveals no method. It is evidence, and it is the strongest reason Marek has to keep going.
7. **The permanent costs stand.** Marek's hearing in one ear. Tovan's post. The Ninth Ward's public glass still dark, because nobody has money to replace a pane. Three years until the front returns, with the culvert unrebuilt and the compensation unspent.

## What this volume deliberately does not resolve

- It does not name who cut the index. That is Volume 05, and the hand is not Rook's.
- It does not give any content from the Ninth Ward Record, which is recovered in Volume 11.
- It does not find the Crown Basin sheet, which is Volume 12.
- It does not show the Meridian's interior. It shows the ground, the towers, the archive, and one dedication stone.
- It does not make Rook an enemy. He is competent, present, and helpful, and he is the reason the volume's method feels reasonable.
- It does not resolve the Warden's shoulder, the Commons, the Charter, the Exchange's business, or the No-Forecast movement's trial.
- It does not put Marek in a Conductor's action. He cannot alter a movement of a live sequence in this volume and must not try.

## New question for Volume 02

The cut index names a routing clerk who died two years before the Meridian Cut, which means the witness chain for the Ninth Ward Record was falsified, which means whoever removed the index was not hiding a decision but hiding the fact that the record could be checked. And the Ninth Ward tower is still carrying a twelve-year-old Witness movement that no current instrument can produce. So Volume 02 inherits: **if the Meridian kept the claim, and the paper trail was cut by someone who was still alive and still in the institution three years after the Cut, who is still in the institution, and what is the record still being used for?**

Volume 02's answer arrives commercially rather than historically, which is the right shape: the Cloud Exchange turns forecast windows into private property, Veyra discovers it cannot hide a dry line in a market that prices it, and the first compensation ledger in the city's history is opened by accident rather than by reform.

## Final chapter image

Chapter 48. The public forecast hall, late, half the lamps out because the hall has never been busier and the building was not designed for an argument. Marek at the board with the incomplete Bellweather trace pinned to it and the spent-turn column already being argued over by two people who were not in the room at 03:00. Tovan at a copy desk two rows back, copying something she is not permitted to keep, her hand steady and her jaw set. Above them, through the hall's high windows, the Ninth Ward tower's signal glass: dark, because the replacement pane is still a private sequence plate in a repairman's coat, and the temporary pane went back in cloudy because nobody has money to do it properly.

Rain arrives at the low end of the city, six hours after the front passed the point where a working culvert would have kept it out of the Bellweather reach, and the Ninth Ward stays dark.

## Canon guardrails for this volume

1. **A read is free.** Marek's private observations, the bench traces, the twelve-year trace, and the notes in his cuff cost fatigue, pain, sleep, and hearing. They spend nothing. No branch closes because he noticed something, thought about it, or was right.
2. **A binding closes the possibility that most directly contradicts it, and it closes preparations too.** The claim's stated outcome becomes dependable by displacing the still-available turn that would have prevented it. A still-open human act of preparation — a crew going out, a precursor summoning a crew, a warden turning up — is such a turn, because the claim's success makes the alternative look unnecessary and the network does not distinguish between an impossible outcome and an unmade preparation. This is the same mechanism as the Low Spindle gate claim in the ending, at the scale of six houses instead of twelve deaths. Write every spend in this volume so the displaced branch is named, physical, and attributable.
3. **Four bindings, four accurate claims, nothing reopened.** Chapter 5 and Chapter 29 are Marek's. Chapter 21 is certified under the Charter with a named sponsor. Chapter 8 is Sable's, bound by Neris's act, and what it closed is never identified, including in the state files.
4. **The Lower Sill culvert's failure is the spend. The south-bank landslide is storm damage.** The public account attributes them separately, and the volume's dry line is not built out of the landslide.
5. **No deaths.** See Working canon item 12.
6. **The Ninth Ward Record and the Crown Basin sheet are never mentioned together and never merged.** Volume 01's archive find is a signature, a filing mark, and a cut citation. It contains no forecast, no method, and no handwriting beyond a name.
7. **Ottine Vask is a name on a piece of paper.** She does not appear, speak, be mourned in a scene, or be suspected. Ione's lie about the ledger migration is a professional deflection, not a villain's lie.
8. **Rook's approval stamp is on the Bellweather notice** and he is present and effective from Chapter 27 on. He is never caught lying, never monologuing, and never loses an argument by being wrong about the weather.
9. **Marek's permanent hearing loss is not the sequence burn** and is not healed.
10. **The relationship stays professional.** No romantic beat, no saved-by-romance, no jealousy scene. Volume 03 owns the first real trust milestone.
11. **Malle and Venn stay separate.** Venn reclassifies readings nobody acted on. Malle decides what a live watch sends to the ledger, and the Bellweather spend is his to bury because it happened on his watch.
12. **The Midpoint's cost must remain unprovable to everyone but Marek.** If Chapter 21 produces a document anyone can hold, the volume's argument collapses into a mystery the reader can solve.
