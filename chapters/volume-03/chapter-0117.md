# Chapter 117 — A Place Is A Thing

The Council's technical cell posted a notice on the sixteenth of the month, on the board outside the basin house and in a hand to nine district offices, and it was a standing day and a printed head, and Wend Lyle read it in the counting floor on the Wednesday morning with a cup of water he did not drink.

*Fourth Thursday of each month. That a district state in writing what it is able to do about its water. Head printed, enclosed. Not obliged. A district which cannot answer may say so, and saying so is an answer.*

He read it twice. Then he went up to the second floor and got the form out of the drawer — all three of them, the way they always come out together, from under the glass weight, with no index over the top — and he put them on the desk side by side and did not look at them for about four minutes, and then he looked, and the reason he could not stop was that the notice on the board downstairs had told him exactly what it was and his own handwriting had told him, eleven days ago, in a column he had invented in his first month, that a thing the basin cannot produce is a different kind of thing from a thing that has been kept.

**He went through it in about forty minutes, at the desk, with the counting floor going on under him, and it is the only piece of work he has ever done that was not in a procedure, and he did not tell anybody he was doing it, and it took him the whole of a Wednesday and most of a Thursday.**

*A keeper's tower book. Fifth column, four times a day, eleven years, twelve agreements, shown and not given away, and the plate in the seat is in its seat and the spare is still boxed and dated spare, and no holder has ever been opened by anybody.*

*A shed foreman's level book. Nine hundred and about forty pages going back to 1888, and the water in every one of them and the plan in none, and one copy of it made properly by request, carried away under a tray weight by a boy of seventeen, and the copy is in a goods yard in a labelled drawer and the original is in a shed and neither of them has been asked for since.*

*A shop foreman's day book. Forty years of glass and a lehr log and a Director's circular in it entire with the seal and the date, copied out on a Sunday, and the foreman will let a person look at a page and will not let them take the book, and she was right and it is not a records rule.*

*A sheet clerk's duty book in a house. Two copies, a dry one in a dresser and a wet one under the stairs, and there is one of it in the world, and a woman of sixty-one is not on a list and there is not a list, and one page of it came out of the house on a Friday evening in a parcel that was not tied.*

*A plasterer's day book in a yard. Four columns and a day entered as work with no customer in the pay column, and it is the only one of these that a man can be produced out of and there is nothing in it a court could say was untrue.*

*A drawer in a ward office with a label on it that the chair wrote herself, about four hundred sheets in six or seven hands, and a standing offer on the district minute of the twenty-sixth of last month: on request, to a district, and not to a Council and not to a firm and not to a newspaper.*

*This one. Three Council forms in pencil in a nineteen-year-old's hand, dated and initialled, under a glass weight, on a middle desk, with no index over the top.*

**And then he wrote the line at the head of the third form, in the space where the head is, and the head is in his own handwriting and it says that this is not a list of things people have kept but a list of things the basin has been asked for and cannot produce.**

*And the other thing, which is the reason I am writing this and the reason I have not shown it to anybody: a notice on a board in the basin house with a standing day and a printed head on it is a place. And a place is a thing that can be asked for. And the moment there is a place, all seven of these are in it, and not one of the seven has anybody's name on it, and a printed head does not need a name to make a request, and a request does not need a reason.*

He read that back to himself twice and then he took the pen up and put a line under it, because he had a rule about rules and it is the only one he has, and the rule is that nothing goes on a form that he cannot show to a partner without his name coming off.

---

**So he did not report it, and that was the first thing he ever did in this building that was not a mistake, and the second thing he did was worse, and it took him four minutes.**

His foreman came up the stairs at about eleven on the Thursday with a request from a counting-floor clerk and stopped, as foremen do, at the desk with the drawer open.

"Where's the fourth ward's file copy?"

"In the drawer."

"Good. That's where a record goes." He took the request and looked at it. "Wend. That thing you brought back off the Council. The day book."

"That's a copy of eleven days of it."

"That's a copy," said the foreman. "In our office. In a drawer with a glass weight on it. **That's a record. If somebody comes and asks us in five years, we've got it, and we can say here's what we did on the tenth, and here's who was in the room, and that's a firm that's worth paying a broker's fee for.**" He went down the stairs and it was the kindest thing anybody has said to Wend Lyle in eleven months and it was in the second floor of a firm that a nineteen-year-old has been quietly ashamed of for eleven months.

"It isn't a record," said Wend Lyle, to an office with four desks in it.

"It is where a record goes," said the stairs, and he was already on them.

And a man of nineteen sat at a desk until half past twelve and understood the whole of the thing he has been spending two months on, and it took him about a minute and it is the first thing in his working life that he has understood rather than worked out.

*The seven of them are not unrecorded because nobody is careless. They are unrecorded because nobody has ever been asked. And a thing that has never been asked for is the only kind of record in this basin that a person can keep without becoming an officer, and the moment this Council has a standing day and a printed head, then there is an address, and the address is the basin house, and everything in this list can be delivered to a counter in four streets away by anybody who fills in one line of a form and pays two shillings at a counting floor.*

*And the two shillings is the cheapest thing on that notice. The fee is not what makes it work. The fee is what makes it legal.*

He put the three forms back under the glass weight, in the right order, and there was no index over the top, and he did not take them out again that evening.

**Before he locked the drawer he did the sensible thing, which is to find out whether the machine would in fact do what he had spent forty minutes working out, and it took him eleven minutes at the counter of the basin house on the Friday morning, and the answer was in a rule book he has now read twice.**

He asked what a request for a district's own papers looks like. He asked it the way a firm asks, which is as a broker, on behalf of a customer, with an interest in the subject-matter.

A request for a district's own papers requires a district. In writing. Signed by a person in the district who can be identified afterwards, on a form, with the ward's name at the head of it, and the request goes to the district and not to this office, and this office cannot make a district send anything, and has never tried, and is not going to.

And the whole of it, he worked out on the step, in about ninety seconds, on the Monday the seventeenth, is that a printed head and a standing day and two shillings at a counting floor are not a machine for taking things. They are a machine for asking, once a month, in a form, in the same words, whether anybody wants to hand anything over. And a district that fills it in has agreed to be asked, and a district that has agreed to be asked once has agreed to be asked for ever, and there is no box in that head that says any other thing.

*Sixth ward: a minute book, offered on request, to a district, and not to a Council. Eight days ago.*

*Fourth ward: a page on a board, a shilling a week refused in writing, a form put face down on a counter.*

*Ninth ward: a page in the post with a refusal written inside it about nine names.*

*And in a fortnight, nine printed heads.*

He looked at the step of the basin house for a while. And then he went back up the hill and did not tell a single living soul, and the seventh ward's head arrived on the Monday with his name on the envelope of it in a minute-taker's hand, and he put it in the drawer with the other three forms and the glass weight, and there is no index over the top.

And at about half past four he did the one thing he did all week, which was to add a row to his own column that no row of that column has ever held, and he wrote it in pencil and initialled it and dated it because it was a row of a firm's form, and the row reads:

*Held by us: none. Not held by us. Not held by anybody. Held by seven people who have not been asked.*

Which cannot be priced, and cannot be listed, and cannot be actioned, and is the only line in two months of his handwriting that a partner could not put a rate against, and he has not been able to say why he wrote it, and he wrote it about nine days after he wrote that it was held in a book in another building, and both of those lines are in the same drawer under the same weight, and one of them is true and one of them is not, and he has not taken either out.
