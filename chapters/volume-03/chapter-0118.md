# Chapter 118 — A Standing Day

Anwen Sarr came up to the basin house on the Tuesday morning of the eighteenth because the Hearthward seat is a Council seat, and a Council seat may send one person with a paper, and she came, and she is forty-six, and she chairs a rain-garden assembly, and she refused a certified claim in front of nine witnesses a season ago with a clause attached, and that clause is now the most quoted sentence in the building.

The clause says: that no claim be entered without the cost of that claim being published at the point of entry, or the sponsor recording in writing that the cost cannot be made public and why.

**Anwen Sarr was given four minutes. She used four minutes, she was correct, she got nothing, and she did not raise her voice, because she has never once raised her voice about a printed head in her life.**

"The head has six lines. It asks a district what it can do about its water and who can do it. It has no line for what the asking does."

"There is a fee at the top of it," said the minute-taker. "Two shillings."

"Then it has six lines and a price and nothing else, which is the same as a warden's pump order and the same as a form, and I am not going to argue with you about whether a form is a good instrument. I am going to argue about the fourth Thursday."

"The standing day is a day on which the districts are not in court."

"It is a day on which nine clerks are in their own offices with a piece of paper in front of them that says *the fourth Thursday* at the top of it. Do you know what a standing day does? I will tell you, because you are all technical men and nobody in this room has ever had to be on the receiving end of one. It does not compel anybody. It never has. It converts a decision into a habit, and a habit is the cheapest form of authority in the world, and the whole difference between a decision and a habit is that a district can withdraw a decision."

Nobody answered her, because there was nothing to answer.

"And there is a second thing and it is the one I actually want in the minute." She put her hand flat on the table. "This Council keeps a column. I have read it and I have quoted it in three districts and I am not going to stop quoting it. It is headed *turns displaced*, and it may name what closed, and by no means who closed it, and every claim certified in this basin has to fill it in or say why it cannot be filled in. That is the instrument's own word. Turn. And a form is a turn."

"Not a weather turn. A preparation. A thing a district was going to do and had not yet done, which was still open, and which a thing that was written on the seventh can now be shut by, because a district that has been asked has been asked, and a district that has answered has answered, and the fourth ward has answered nothing and will answer nothing and has said so on a board in its own name at its own cost, and there is not one box in your head that lets a district take an answer back out again."

Nothing was written into the minute except that an objection was received. There is no column for objections. The clause of the twenty-first is still law-adjacent in practice, and the DISPLACED TURNS column is now the mechanism that nine districts are arguing inside, and Anwen Sarr was given nothing, was owed nothing, and went down the hill at about half past ten in a raincoat.

---

**Corrin Malle was in a corridor at eleven with a roster, and said the one true sentence anybody in that building said that week, and it was to Sabin Orle, and he asked nothing for it.**

"You will get nine papers."

"I know."

"A month from now this Council will know more about the nine wards than it has known in a hundred and forty years, and every one of the nine will be wrong, because they are what nine clerks could write down in a quarter of an hour, and a wrong answer on a printed head from this Council is not a district's fault."

"I have it in pencil."

"Then you have it in the right place." He shifted the roster and did not go down the corridor for a moment. "Ma'am. I am going to be asked, in about two years, whether I thought this was a good idea, and I am going to have to say yes, because it is, and I shall have no way at all of being permitted to have been wrong about it. A schedule takes the decision out of a room and puts it in a diary, and then there is nobody to sue."

She wrote that down in pencil too, in the margin of her own copy, which is the thing she uses for the parts that will not go in the minute.

---

The Kestrel Line sent a paper as well, and it was four lines, and it lost, and Ilva Sorn brought it up herself, because the Line does not send a clerk to a thing like this for a living.

*That the standing day be read as a notice, and be attended by one person on behalf of the Line, and that the Line's objection be minuted. A board in a yard is a gate, and a gate is a court, and a court can be refused and a printed head cannot. We ask that the head carry a line reading: this return may be declined by the district, and the declining of it is not a failure to attend.*

**She was refused in ninety seconds by a man who had not heard of the Line and did not know what a yard was, and the objection went in the minute as an objection was received, and the head came off the press without the line on it, and the fee stayed at the top of it, where the copy clerk had put it.**

Ilva Sorn went down the steps and said one thing to the copy clerk in the corridor, which was that the clause had been put at the top of the head and nobody had ever done that before, and Tovan Iles said that it was her job, and Ilva Sorn said *I know it is*, and went out, and nobody thanked either of them.

And in the same corridor, four minutes later, a woman of thirty who captains an airship line out of Kestrel Yards said a thing to a man of thirty-two that was not about the standing day, and neither of them said anything else about it, and it is in here because it happened at a quarter to twelve on a Tuesday in the rain and neither of them had arranged for it to.

"Can a reader decline to be findable?" said Tarin Senn.

"There's no form for it."

"Then that is the answer, and it is the wrong one." She had her hand on the rail. "I have said what I came to say and I am going to go and say the other thing to somebody else."

She did not ask him what the question was for. She has never once asked him a question about himself in four months, and he has noticed, and he has not decided whether that is a courtesy.

"I know what it is about," said Marek Vale, to a corridor, to nobody.

"You do not, and I am not going to help you, and you may write that I said so." She was already going. "And when it happens it will not be me who tells her, and I would like it on the record that I said that first."

---

Then the Council's standing day went on the board and the nine returns went into the tray, and the board notice and the printed head are the same object, and the fourth Thursday of every month is printed at the head of it; and by that same afternoon the sixth ward had sent back the only answer in the basin that anybody in this Council will ever be proud of, which is one line, and it is the minute of the twenty-sixth of last month, copied out in full by a woman's own hand with nothing added and nothing removed:

*Book retained. Offered for the use of a district, on request. Not sent.*

Orle read it standing in the tray room at about four o'clock on the Tuesday, and then stood at the window for a while with her hands behind her back, and there was nothing in the room. The ninth ward's envelope was still on her desk unopened, because it is addressed to a person, and she is going to take it down to Low Spindle herself on the fourth Thursday, which is the standing day, and which is the first date in eleven years on which this Council has expected to hear from a district. The ninth ward is the only one she has any real hope about. The fourth ward is the only one she is frightened of, and she has not worked out why, and she has not asked anybody.

The second ward's return came in on the same afternoon, eight days after the head went out, and it is the answer she will keep, and it is four lines and every one of them is true.

*Second ward, district office. Return for the fourth Thursday.*

*We can put a pump on the low side and about nine men on a pump. We have a wall in its second season. We can empty two channels with a barrow and a boy. The reach gauge is a boy's own and is checked when he is at school and not before.*

*The person who can do it is the foreman of the works, whose name is on the order, and he is not in this ward on a Thursday, and we have written to you once before about a form and had four lines back.*

*We do not send the two shillings. We have sent it in the ordinary post and the clerk will tell you.*

It is in a fair hand and it took whoever wrote it about ten minutes and it is the only thing the Council has ever held about any district that is not a form, and **it is wrong in two places, and it is wrong because a ward that has never been asked what it can do does not know the answer, and cannot know the answer, and has no way of finding out that it does not know it, and the printed head has no column that says *this is a guess*.**

She entered it, and the entering clerk was a young man who had not been in the room, and there was no where-put column in the head, and the paper went into a tray, and the tray went onto a shelf, and there is a shelf in that room with a great many trays on it and nobody has ever counted them.
