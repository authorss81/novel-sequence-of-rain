# Chapter 107 — What We Are Not Obliged To Do

The shutter had been up about four hours when the first of the eleven people came into the fourth ward's office, and by one o'clock there had been nineteen, and two of them were from a firm, and one of them was the district council's chair, and the whole of the business took about nine minutes each and none of them were the same nine minutes.

Verna Sly kept the list. That was the first thing she did, and it is the thing she is for: she wrote down who had come and what they had wanted and in what order, in a column, with the hour, because in eleven years she has learned that the only difference between a district office and a queue at a counter is the column.

*Ninth. Man with a barrow, wants to know if the wall holds. Told the wall is not a reading and the reach is not a corridor and he is the eleventh person.*
*Twelfth. Lad off a boat, wants the hours. Told him it's not a notice and it's got two names on it and neither of them is in this ward.*
*Half one. Firm. A young man, very polite, a card, four questions, wants to know whether the district will permit the two readings to be printed weekly with the ward's name on it, and at what rate.*

"A shilling a week," said Verna Sly, without looking up, "and I have read what the ninth ward got for that, and the ninth ward is a ward with a dark tower and a wall in its third season and they took it in ninety seconds, and I am not saying a word about what a ward with money in it should do."

"Then it's refused."

"It's **not answered**, and I would be obliged if you'd sit down, because you have brought a form and I have a pencil and the difference between the two is the whole of my morning." She put the form face down on the counter. "I'll not sign a form that says *the district permits* on anything, because the minute I sign that, this ward has permitted something on a piece of board in a yard four miles off written by two men who cannot agree, and the next man who comes through that door with a card and a shilling a week will be able to produce my signature and my ward's name together, and there is no box in this form for that to be undone and I have looked."

---

**The council met at eight in the evening in the ward office with nine people in it and a smell of wet coats, and item three was the shutter and item four was a form from the Council with a box on it, and Verna Sly had drafted two documents that afternoon and read them both out.**

"This is the first one and it's short and it's a refusal, and I'll read it as drafted and then somebody will tell me where it's wrong, and I'm not defending it, I'm just going to read it."

*To the Weather Council. From the fourth ward.*

*The fourth ward declines to fill in the form of the twenty-first of this month.*

*The reason is not defiance. The form has two columns. The left-hand column asks what the district can do and the right-hand column asks who can do it, and the fourth ward can answer neither, and it is not going to guess, and it is going to say here which of the two it is worse to guess.*

*The fourth ward knows its own water. It has about nine hundred houses along that wall and a hundred and forty low doors on its own low side out of them, and it has a tide gauge in a master's shed and a wall and a pump and a market, and every one of those is known to somebody who lives here. The fourth ward has never in nine years been told, by any person, what it is able to do, and it has never once had a form on which to write it down.*

*And it will not fill one in now, because a form filled in on a wet Tuesday afternoon out of a chairman's memory is a document, and a document can be produced.*

Then the second paragraph, and it is the one the room argued about for eleven minutes:

***A district is not obliged to act on a reading it cannot check, and a reading that names a corridor and an hour and no place is not a reading. It is a piece of the work left out, and the ward that acts on it is doing the reading itself, badly, at speed, in the dark, and is then told by anybody who likes to come that it was given no notice at all.***

*The fourth ward will act on a reading that names a place, and an hour, and somebody it can go and ask. It has acted on those before, on a warden's own authority, with no bell and no claim, and it has been wrong twice and it does not regret either of them.*

*The fourth ward further declines to be entered in any list, return or index in respect of a reading, and further declines to have a reading of its own printed by anybody, and further declines to be asked this question again in a form.*

**Nobody could find the place in it where it was a boast, and the chair of the council, who had come to be against it, found two.**

He found the first one himself, which is to his credit.

"If we publish a refusal, we have to be able to get through the next one without anything. That's all it is. A refusal's fine until it's the only thing you've got."

"Then say that in writing," said Verna Sly, "and we'll put it on the wall with the rest, and the district can have its reasons and its cost in the same hand, which is better than having the reasons in one hand and being surprised by the cost in another."

**And the second one she had to be pulled out of him by a man of seventy-one in the back row who had not spoken.**

"You've said we won't be entered in any list. Do you know what that does? There's a *register* in this Council with the ninth ward's line empty since before I was born. And you're about to go on a book's list instead. You're all sat here talking about not being on a list."

Nobody had an answer, and Sly had one in about nine seconds and wrote it on the back of the form afterwards, and it is the sentence that decides the chapter:

**"Then the ward goes on a list on its own board, in its own words, with its own name at the top, and the list says it says no, and there is no form anywhere that can take that back out of it. That's the difference and that's the whole of the difference and I'd be prepared to put it in a minute if there was anybody in this city who was keeping one."**

---

**They carried it eight to one. The one was a woman who wanted the ward to be able to be found in a bad hour and said so, and it was minuted as a name and a reason, because that is what this ward does, and it is the only ward in this basin that has ever minuted a dissent with the dissenter's own words against the minute.**

Two amendments were added. One, that the refusal also answer the Council's minute item four, and the answer had to be a true one and it was on the shelf in the fourth ward's own file:

*There was no office and no person in this ward. A reading was written on the back of the form it arrived on, and the form went in the post to the Council, and the Council replied, in four lines, that it does not hold district papers. That reply went on the same round. The forms themselves were not held. They went into the wire basket in the corridor outside the press room, which is emptied on a Friday, and there is a return in the rule book for the basket with a line for each Friday and a man's signature against it, and the return does not require anybody to look at the basket or to know what was in it.*

*This ward's readings for nine years are therefore not in a book in this ward and are not in a book in the Council, and they are not recoverable, and this minute does not say they were destroyed in any way that anybody intended. A clerk of this ward wrote the day and the week in the ward file at the time, in his own hand, and nobody has ever read that line back, and it is in the drawer, and you may look at it if you come to the office and ask, which is exactly what nobody in the last nine years has done.*

And two, that the ward would answer the ninth ward's note.

---

**The ninth ward had sent four words to the offices of the other eight districts on the Wednesday afternoon, and this office got one of them at about two o'clock on the Thursday with a boy on a bicycle, and it was four words and it was not signed by a committee, it was signed with two initials.**

***WHO DECIDES. N. K.***

Verna Sly wrote the reply that afternoon and it is four lines and she read it twice and posted it in the ordinary post at twenty to five, at a cost of twopence, out of the district's money.

*To the warden of the ninth ward. From the fourth ward's office.*

*The district decides. That is the whole of our answer and it is not a form of words. The fourth ward has been asked three times in twenty years what it can do about its water, and has refused three times, and all three refusals were mine, and two of them are in the district book and one is a wall in a goods yard, and I would like the record to show that we have never once been asked it in a form that could be filled in.*

*The ward has also decided, eight to one, to put a refusal on its own board, in its own words, with its own name at the top, and to say that it does not recognise any reading that names a corridor and an hour and no place. The council sits at eight. The board will be up in the morning.*

*We do not think that is any use to you. We are putting it up because it is true and because a ward that has said no in a letter to a Council has said it to one clerk, and a ward that has said no on a board has said it to four hundred people.*

*And we are putting it up on a Thursday morning in the rain and we are not asking you to agree with it. We are the fourth ward and you are the ninth and we are not the same and we are not going to pretend to be.*

*— V. Sly, clerk, fourth ward.*

**On the Friday morning the thirtieth, the fourth ward's notice board — a painted board on the outside wall of the ward's own office in a goods yard off the north road, four feet by three, with nothing on it since the winter — carried a page of foolscap in four clips and a heading in a clerk's hand, and it went up at about ten past eight in the rain, and the district office had a queue on the step by half past nine.**

**FOURTH WARD. WHAT THE WARD DECLINES, AND WHY. The twenty-ninth day of Miremonth. The ward's own words. The ward's own board. Ask the clerk.**

And below the heading, in a fair small hand, the two paragraphs, and a third line at the foot which had been added at about half past eleven on the Thursday night, after the vote, by a clerk working alone:

*Copied out by hand, at the ward's cost, one sheet. Any person may come to the office and copy it. It may not be printed for sale.*

**The shilling a week a firm had offered was refused in writing at about three o'clock on the Thursday, and the young man with the card took the refusal away with him because that is what happens to a refusal, and he put it in a satchel, and it went up to a second floor, and a nineteen-year-old put it under a glass weight in a drawer on a middle desk without being asked and without initialling it, because it was not his, and because three forms of his own were in that drawer and a district's no was not going in on top of them unasked.**
