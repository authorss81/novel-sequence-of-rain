# Chapter 119 — The Friday I Signed

The sequence archive is on the second floor of the Weatherglass and it is a public reading room with a bench in it and a lamp at each end, and Marek Vale went up on the Thursday morning of the twentieth with a question that was entirely lawful and a reason that was not on the form, and the woman at the desk was forty-six and had been in that room for eleven years and had been in another room in another building before that, and she was not surprised to see him, and he was surprised to see her.

"You have asked me for a district's record eleven times in four months in nine different ways," said Ione Sere, "and I have said no nine times, and I am going to say no a tenth time in about four seconds if you sit down."

"Then say it a tenth time and I will go away."

"I will, and you will not, and I have watched you go away twice and come back with a different question." She squared a tray. "Ask it."

"Whether there is any run of ward plates in this archive. Not ward towers — the towers are in the towers. Ward instruments. A district's own glass, bought or cut, registered, run by whoever the district paid to run it."

"There are nine ward towers and a great many private glasses, and none of the private ones are registered with this Office because the Office does not supply them and will not cut, grind or anneal for a district, and that is in a Director's circular with a seal and a date on it and no form, and I have said that to a plasterer and to a shed foreman and to an assessor in the last six weeks, and I would be surprised if you had not heard it from one of them."

"I have heard it twice."

"Then you know the answer, and the answer is no, and there is a folio in the third press that says *none held*, and it is the only document in this building that I have ever been proud of." She did not look up. "What is it, then."

---

She let him have eleven minutes of it, and then she asked him one question, and the question was the reason he had come up the hill, and he had not known it was the reason.

"You are holding a piece of paper in a drawer that you did not write on." She said it flatly, the way a woman says a thing she has been waiting a fortnight to say. "You are the only man in this basin who has done that in my hearing, and I have been in these records for a long time, and it is the first interesting thing anybody has done in this room since the spring."

"I am not going to tell you whose it is."

"I did not ask, and I would not have taken it, and you have just told me." She put the tray down. "I am going to tell you something and then I am going to go back to the plates, and I am telling you because you have been in a records room twice this month and told the truth both times, and there is not many of those, and because it costs me nothing to say it to a man who cannot act on it."

---

"There is a rule book in the west end of the basin house," said Ione Sere, "and it is a book of rules, and it is the only object in that building's records that nobody has ever wanted to read, and I want you to have the whole of it in about four minutes, and then I am going back to my plates.

"There is a wire basket in a corridor outside a press room. It is emptied on a Friday. There is a return in the rule book for the basket, and the return is a day, and a week, and a line, and a man's signature against it.

"**And there is no column for what was in the basket, and there has never been one, and the return does not require anybody to look.**"

She turned a plate face down in a rack without looking at it, which is a thing you can only do with forty years of hands.

"And I signed it," she said. "For longer than I am going to tell you and longer than you are going to ask me. It was the only thing in that building I ever signed that nobody read, and I signed it every Friday, and I never once opened the basket, and I was careful about that, and being careful about it was a thing I was good at."

"That is not a confession and I want it understood that it is not one. A return is a receipt for transit. It says the basket went. It does not say what was in it and it was never printed with a place for it, and the person who wrote the rule did not forget the column, because there are four columns in that book and three of them are full every week. That is a design, and it is a good one, and it is the most carefully kept record in that building and it is carefully kept for the exact reason that it is worth nothing."

---

**"And here is the part that is a confession, and it is one sentence, and I have never said it out loud, and you are the second person in this basin who has been in a records room this month and told me the truth.**

"A clerk came, in a year I am not going to name, and asked me whether a sheet had gone in the basket on a Friday. And I knew that it had, because I had signed the return on that Friday and the return names the day and not the contents, and I told him so.

"And he asked me to look.

"And I did not look.

"And the reason is not that I thought there was nothing in it. The reason is that if I had looked then I would have had to write down what I found, and writing it down would have made it a record, and a record is a thing that can be asked for, and I had spent nine years signing a line that said I did not look. **So the not looking was the only thing I had, and I kept it, and he went away, and I never told anybody that I could have looked and did not.**"

The lamp at the end of the bench buzzed. Neither of them said anything for about a minute.

"You have said the same sentence three times this month," said Marek Vale, "in three other rooms, to three other people, and I do not think you know you have, and I am not going to tell you who."

"No," said Ione Sere. "**I would not expect me to be the only one. I would expect the people who are cleverest about it to be the ones who have never had to think about it, and you have a drawer with somebody's whole life in it and I have an archive with nine ward towers in it, and neither of us has ever been asked for either.**"

---

"Now. The day book," she said, "because you did not come up here for a plate and you have been looking at my face for ten minutes as though I were a column.

"*Daily, district notices received.* Four lines a week, and a printed head that does not say what office the man who wrote it was in, and eleven years of it in a copy clerk's hand and four before hers. I have read that book. I did not keep it and I have read it, because I was a custodian in that building and a custodian has the run of a floor, and the day book was on that floor, and the basket was in a corridor off it, and the rule book was at the end of a counter in it.

"And I am going to tell you two things about that column where it says where a thing was put, and then you will go down the hill, and I am not going to be asked a third thing, because I do not have a third thing.

"**The first is that the column is the only place in that building that could ever have said where a received paper went, and it is a good column, and the man who wrote it filled it in more often than he had to, and he was not careless.**

"**And the second is that on one line in four years he did not.**"

"He knew where it went."

"He knew where it went," said Ione Sere.

"You know where it went."

"I know a room and a press in it, and the press has been cleared twice since, and the room has had its shelves moved, and I am not going to give you the room, and I will tell you why, and it is not a protection of anybody, and it is not modesty, and it is the only free thing I am going to give you this month."

---

**"Because a man comes up here once a month and asks me for a record, and if I give him a room he will go and stand in it, and there is a difference between a record and a place. A record can be copied. A place has to be entered. And a boy of nineteen from a firm has been in that building twice this month and got further than anybody has got in a hundred and forty years, and he did it with a district's refusal as a warrant, and he lied about it, and he did not get caught, and I know that because I have read his slip.**

"So no. I know the room. I have known for years. It is the only thing I know that would be worth having and I am not giving it to anybody, and if you build the standing day and the trays and the box, then in a year there will be a young person with a form who does not have to lie, and that is how it will be found, and it will be found by somebody who was told no politely by me and who will not know that they were the eleventh."

---

"And I am not asking you not to build it," she said, and stopped, and then said it again because the first one had come out wrong.

"I am telling you what it is. It is not a plot and there is nobody in it. It is a building that has decided it would like to know, and a printed head, and a standing day, and a woman of twenty-seven who puts the fee at the top of the head where a district can see it, and a man of nineteen who cannot say what he holds, and a book in the west end with one empty cell in it that four hundred people could find in a week.

"And every one of those is correct and every one of them is the right thing, and I have watched it in three cities, and it never once costs anybody anything, and it costs everything by about forty years, and by then it is a different building and the people who did it are dead and it is all just how we do things here."

She put a plate into a rack and took a plate out and did not look up again.

"You are going to build it anyway," said Ione Sere. "I have known you for twenty minutes and that is not a reading, it is a fact about a man holding a piece of paper he did not write on. **And I am not the woman on your ridge with the parcel. She gave you a page and kept a book and told you her terms before you asked. I have given you a Friday and kept a room and told you neither, and there is a woman in this basin who is owed an apology and I am not going to be one, and if the day comes when you want me to be one, you will find me in this room, and I will not refuse, and it will not help.**"

She turned the lamp down at his end, which is a courtesy, and went back to the plates, and the reading room was very quiet, and there is a bench in it and a lamp at each end and a public entrance and a man of thirty-two sitting in it with two pounds and sixpence in a coat and three blocks of pencil in a cuff and no room for a fourth.
