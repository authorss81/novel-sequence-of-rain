# Chapter 116 — The Reach At The Datum

The ninth ward asked for it on the Monday of the tenth, in a room over a locker's yard, and the asking took twenty minutes, and it was the first thing the ninth ward had ever asked anybody for that was not refused.

The reach is off for about a fortnight a year. The clearing is two hours, Tuesdays and Thursdays, in the wet, on the low side and the two rain gardens and the back lanes, and it has been six men and a water steward twice a week since before Marda Loon's father had the pump house, and the whole of it is done in whatever hour the water happens to be out of the way.

"If a crew can be told," said Marda Loon, "it can be one crew on one morning instead of six men four times. And there is no way on this earth to tell a crew, and I have had a look at our own glass and it is a thing that cannot show a state, and I have asked a man at the bottom of the hill what it would take and he has told me what it would take, and it is two years and a glasswork line."

"You want a claim," said Neris Kade.

"I want a morning," said Marda Loon. "I do not care what they call it."

---

**The claim was entered on the Wednesday of the twelfth at about four o'clock, and it is the first binding entered in this basin since the ninth of the Bellweather month, and every part of it is on the page because a claim is a document, and documents in this basin are the only things anybody can be produced out of.**

*Place: the ninth ward's reach from the stone marker to the third bend; the low side; the two rain gardens; the back lanes.*
*Trigger: the district's own man reads the gauge in the stilling well at or below the glass datum at 05:30 on the sixteenth, which is a Sunday, and the district reads a gauge on a Sunday, and always has.*
*Effect, as entered: the reach holds at or below the flat from 05:30 on the sixteenth until 02:00 on the eighteenth; the low side, the two rain gardens and the back lanes clear throughout.*
*Listed alternative, as required: if the gauge is not read at 05:30 by the district's own man, no flat is claimed; the crew goes on the ordinary reading; and if the reach is up, the crew stands down and the clearing is done on the next ordinary Tuesday, which is a week later and in the wet, and the work is lost to the season and the six men are paid for nothing out of the district's own money.*
*Expiry: 02:00 on the eighteenth, which is the hour the effect ends, so that no period exists in which the claim's authority to direct anybody outlasts its reason for sending them.*
*Sponsor: M. Loon, water steward, ninth ward.*
*Attestation: a reader's attestation, of the order only, and of nothing else — M. Vale, reader, not on field duty, not in the service of this claim. The sponsor is on the record as answerable. The reader is on the record as a man who has read the order of the lines, and cannot be asked what they mean, and would not answer if he could.*

And in the resources, which is the third line of the entry and is the whole of the cost:

*Resource relied on: the district's own clearing rota, in the water steward's hand, kept at the pump house since her father's time, and in no other copy.*

**Orle certified it at about five and made no condition of it, and the certification fee of two shillings was paid at the counting floor out of the district's money, and the district book has no column for a fee, and the district clerk wrote *claim fee, 2s* on the back of a form and put the form in the drawer because there was nowhere else to put it.**

She did not go up the ridge about it. She went up that ridge once, in the rain, and had her coat put on her by a glass foreman, and asked him a question as a person and not as an office, and was told that this Office has never in fifty years sent a district a book, a form, a plate or a rule for keeping a record of its own water, and wrote *no* in her own minute. She did not do any of that this time.

What she wrote instead went in her own minute, in a drawer of her own, because nobody has ever asked her for a minute in eleven years:

*Certified. The sponsor is a water steward and not an officer of this Council and cannot be indexed. The resource relied on is a book in her own hand kept at a pump house, of which this office has held a copy since the fourteenth. This office has never held a district paper. It now holds one, of which no duplicate exists anywhere, of a district's own capacity, and the district did not know that it was a record until this claim.*

*No action. I have been an assessor for eleven years and I do not know what to do with that and I have not found anybody in this building to ask.*

There is a third thing in that drawer and it is why there is a fourth. In Drownmonth the Hearthward assembly submitted this Council a standing requirement on its own paper, separately, and asked for it to be read, and it says that no claim certified under the Charter be entered without the cost of that claim being published at the point of entry, or the sponsor recording in writing that the cost cannot be made public and why. No minute of this Council has adopted it. Nobody in this building has ever been told that it binds anybody. It is the most quoted sentence in the west end and it is not a rule.

And this claim entered no cost at the point of entry.

*Third. The standing requirement of the Hearthward assembly, of Drownmonth. Not adopted, not binding, and not enforced by this office. This claim entered no cost at the point of entry. The resource line is the cost and the resource line is not a column. The sponsor has not recorded in writing that it cannot be published, and I have not asked her to, and there was nobody in this room to ask me whether I was permitted to. The assembly that submitted it refused a claim in front of nine witnesses a season ago because of it and got nothing, and was right, and I have entered one against it this afternoon and said nothing to anybody about it. Noted. Not enforced. Nobody has asked me to enforce it.*

---

**He read the order of the lines on the Wednesday morning at a bench on a ridge with a tube in his coat, and the sentence he said out loud in an empty shop is the sentence he said in front of a minute-taker a season ago and has not stopped hearing since.**

"The sponsor is answerable. I am a man who read the order. If this is wrong, the woman who put her name at the top of it is the one who has to stand in a room, and I am not permitted to be in that room, which is the correct arrangement and I would not want it otherwise."

He is thirty-two. He had a pound eighteen and three in a coat on the Monday morning, and there was a brewer's float on the bench that wanted cutting by the Wednesday and it was two and three, and he did it in an hour and told the man it would still want doing Wednesday, and the man paid anyway, and it is a bench, and a bench is what he has got, and it is two pounds and sixpence now, which is in a coat, and none of it is anybody's. He may work a bench and take private commissions at his own price; he may not sign a reading for the Office, may not attest in its service, and may not be named in a sponsor's line of a claim. He attested the order of the lines of somebody else's claim on a Wednesday morning and went home.

**The plate said it at about four o'clock and he opened the tube at about six, and by then it was a two-hour-old object and not a warning of anything, because there is no instrument in this city that says such a thing out loud unless a claim has been entered, and the entry is the whole of the event.**

```
CLAIM: the reach holds at or below the flat from 05:30 on the sixteenth until 02:00 on the eighteenth; the low side, the two rain gardens and the back lanes clear.
ONE POSSIBLE TURN WILL CLOSE.
```

It does not say what the turn is. It has never said what the turn is, on any plate, in any year, and the man who reads it has never once been told and has never once been able to ask.

He put the tube back in his coat and wrote nothing on anything, and the inside of his left cuff has three blocks of pencil in it and no room in that cuff for a fourth.

He asked the district's man to repeat the datum figure twice at the bench before he entered it, and the man repeated it twice, patiently, the way a foreman repeats a rack count, and Marek said thank you, and nobody remarked on it, and there is nobody in this basin who has ever noticed that he does it.

---

**On the Friday morning the fourteenth, at about ten, Marda Loon was asked to come to the district office, and the young man with the fair hand who asked her was sorry about it and she has never told anybody what was in his face, because there was nothing in it.**

He put the claim in front of her. She cannot read a glass and has never pretended to. She read the resource line, because it is her own hand described, and the line said that the rota is in her hand at the pump house and that there is no other copy of it in the world.

"How long have you kept it?"

"Since my father had the room. Nineteen years."

"Is it a fair copy of the work?"

"It is the work. That is the same as a fair copy. There is nothing to copy it from."

"Was it ever a Council form?" said the district clerk, who had gone grey. "Marda. Was it ever a Council anything?"

"It was never a Council anything. It was my father's, and it is mine, and it is a book with the days in it, and there is no name on it and there was no name on his, and no paper ever came for it and nobody asked for it, and I have not been asked for it in my life, and I am not going to be asked for it in a hurry. And I will copy it out if a claim has to be able to point at something, and I would be obliged if nobody made a speech about it in the meantime."

They copied it out. Two hours, in her hand, on district paper, into a book with a spine on it, and the spine has the ward's name on it now and not a date, and the book will live on a shelf in a room over a locker's yard, and the district clerk wrote on the flyleaf at her request, in his fair hand: *Copied from the water steward's own book, in her hand, at her request, the fourteenth.*

"There," said Marda Loon. "Now if anybody ever asks anybody, somebody can be produced out of it, and I have not lost a thing, and there are two of them, and that is one more than there was."

**The six men and the water steward cleared the low side and the two rain gardens and the back lanes in one morning, on the sixteenth, from half past five, and it was the best morning's work the ward has done in nine years, and the reach came back at about ten past two on the eighteenth and went over the flat at half past six, and the market's low yard has been clear every day since because there has been no rain in it.**

The work is done for the season. It was four Tuesdays and four Thursdays and it is one morning, and it will not be four Tuesdays and four Thursdays next season unless somebody writes the days back into a book, and there is a book, and it is a copy, and nobody has asked what happens in a season in which nobody writes them down.
