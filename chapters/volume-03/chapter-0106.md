# Chapter 106 — Two Names And A Friday

Kestrel Yards on the morning of the twenty-eighth of Miremonth has a launch on the chocks with a foreman in her bottom planking, and the boards are up with all five conditions in chalk, and the receipt for two shillings dated on it, and Tarin Senn has added a sixth line in a different chalk and it is not a condition, it is a quotation.

*A man may read this board and act on it. A man may read this board and go and find the district that owns the figures and ask them. He may not be told the figures by this yard.*

**It is there because of the ninth ward, and it is there because on Friday the twenty-third a carrier at the slip road asked a boy at the gate what the half past one was, and the boy said it was a good forty minutes on the reach, and the carrier wrote *good* down, and went away with a word the yard had never put there.**

"I did not ask for the sixth line," says Marek. "You did it in nine minutes on a Friday."

"I did it in nine minutes on a Friday because a man put the word *good* in a boy's mouth and then wrote it down, and *good* is not a level." She squared the slate. "And now the argument, and it is the argument and not the ear, and I have said that twice this month already and I am not going to be a woman who keeps saying it."

"The argument."

"**Put the ward's hours on this board.**" She put her hand flat on it, the way she does. "Not my hours, theirs. Half past one and eleven past four, four hours and ten apart, and the nine minutes, both readings standing, neither withdrawn. There is a plate glass case at a school gate in the ninth ward with the same figures in it and a woman of fifty-eight copies them out for nothing, and a warden cannot read a notice of her own, and the notice the low blocks get is a lamp that a man trims."

"And the lamp's out."

"The lamp's out since the half hour last night. You'll have walked past it." She did not look up from the slate. "So write it on the board. The whole thing. Both names."

**"No."**

"No?"

"**No, and I want it minuted on this board that I said no and that you were right to press it.** A yard board is a gate. You've said so yourself and you've said it to a carrier at a slip road and he believed you inside a minute. If the ninth ward's hours go on this board they go on a gate, and a gate is the cheapest kind of court there is, and in about a fortnight somebody will come off a barge and read half past one off a gate and act on it, and the ward will have published its own reach hours in a commercial place and been paid nothing for it, because the sixth ward is the only thing this yard has ever refused to price and it is not going to start with somebody else's water."

"**So it is a commercial place.**"

"It is a public place where a man who wants a thing can have it for nothing, and that is a market, and you have spent a year and a wage teaching me what happens when the two of those are the same room." He put his hand on the board, on the other side of the receipt, so the two of them were not touching it at the same point. "It goes on a shutter on Tannery Walk, and a shutter is a thing on a wall that a man in a cart cannot read from the road, and then Ivy Tranter copies it out on four shutters for the low blocks and the school case, and there is a lit plate glass case at a school gate holding a hand-copied notice, and the lamp is out, and that is the whole of the district's public weather and it is in the right hands, which are the hands of a district."

"And in nine months a firm will print it with the ward's name on it at threepence a week and there will be a receipt."

"**Yes. And it will be on a firm's list before it is on a firm's board, and a firm's list is the cheapest notice in this city, and a district that cannot read its own glass has a private one twice a day. I have not slept properly about that since the autumn and I have done one single thing about it.**"

---

**He got the miller's money on the Friday and the brewer's on the Tuesday and neither conversation was about any of this.**

Two shillings and sixpence for a level read off a wall at Ferrygate that a miller has had repainted, and three and nine for a brewer's cellar float that wants cutting before Thursday, and the second one he did in a morning and told the man it wanted doing on a Tuesday, and the man said it wanted doing now, and Marek said *it will still want doing Tuesday*, and got paid anyway, and the two of them together are six and three, and he has one pound eighteen and three in a coat.

**And he asked Tarin to repeat the sixth line twice, in the yard, on the Monday, with a foreman's hammer going four streets off, and she repeated it twice and did not remark on it, and Ilva Sorn called a launch hour out loud at that moment for no reason anybody has ever established, and it is the third time in nine weeks that a woman in a yard has said a thing out loud at exactly the moment he needed it said.**

---

**On the Wednesday afternoon he walked down the hill to the ninth ward tower at about four o'clock and the glass was dark and he knew it would be and he went anyway, because the tower is four miles off the shop and the shop shuts at ten and there is nowhere else in this city he can stand and be angry.**

Gant Ollis was on the third landing with the book open at the fifth column. The pane in the frame is a bubbled temporary that a foreman bedded properly on a Wednesday a fortnight before the Bellweather month, and it cannot show a state, and behind it on the wiring is a lamp that a district pays for a week at a time, and on the twenty-seventh the district could not pay for the week beginning that day.

"The oil," said Marek.

"The oil's not paid. It's not paid for this week. It'll be paid for one week and then there was fourpence."

**And a man of sixty-three or sixty-four said the whole of it in about nine sentences, and it is the ninth ward's entire public weather service in nine sentences, and Marek stood on the third landing of a tower he has never been to the top of and listened to it with a pound eighteen and three in a coat pocket on a landing three flights below a glass he has never been to the top of either.**

*The seven and six came on the eighteenth and a shilling of it went on my evening hours and three and six on the oil, and that left three shillings. The firm pays a shilling on a Tuesday and the second of them was on the twentieth, so that's four shillings in the tin. Oil's three and six and she bought it on the Saturday, and the tin's at fourpence, and the shilling came in on the Tuesday and it sits there, because my hours for this week are a shilling, and the can's been done since Tuesday night.*

*And there's no column. That's the thing you'd want. There's no column in her book for it and there's none in the Council's and there's none in the firm's, because the firm doesn't know there's a lamp. So it's a Tuesday, she does it, and she doesn't write it down, and next Tuesday she'll do it again and be out again.*

**He did not offer. He had a pound eighteen and three in a coat and a district below him with a dark tower and he did not offer, and he knows precisely why and the reason is not a good one.**

**If Marek Vale pays for the ninth ward's lamp, the ninth ward's lamp is Marek Vale's lamp.** And a censured private reader who funds a district's public notice is a party to that district's public notice, and the whole of the argument of two seasons — the column with no sponsor field, the yard's five conditions, the seven boxes, the ward's seventh box saying the cost is not money — is that a notice must belong to a district and to nobody else, and there is no mechanism in this city for a thing to belong to a district, and he had a pound in his coat and a real reason and both of those were true and neither of them was the reason he did not do it, and the reason he did not do it was that he has been the party in the middle of somebody else's thing twice this year and had got it very badly wrong both times, and a third of them would have been a third, and he was thirty-two, and there is nothing in this basin he is allowed to give away.

He said instead: "Then a warden can see the notice on a shutter and not on a glass, and that's the whole of the service, and I can't make it better than that from a landing."

"That's correct," said Gant Ollis. "You could put a pane in it. Two years and a glasswork line."

"Yes."

**And then he went back up the hill, and the tower stayed dark, and the district's notice stayed in a plate glass case at a school gate with two hundred and forty children on the roll and forty of them who walk up from the low blocks, and the man who could have paid for a fortnight of oil in that tower did not, and nobody was told that he had not, and he did not tell.**

---

**He put the shutter up himself at about ten to eight on the Thursday morning, and it is the seventh public disagreement between two named readers, and it is the first one that asks anybody for anything.**

*The low ground, Friday's morning. Ours: the release holds to two o'clock and the flat is clear by nine. Hers: it turns at the first hour and the flat is clear by half past six. Nine minutes' disagreement in the third week of last month; this one is about the hour.*

*The ninth ward has a flat, a gang, and a window on Friday. The district has to decide by noon on Friday whether a crew goes on.*

*We are both right and we do not know which of us is, and the difference between us is where a crew stands at eight o'clock, and we are not in a position to say which way that is.*

*Both readings stand. Neither is withdrawn. Nobody must do anything because of this except decide.*

*— M. Vale, reader, not on field duty. S. Orin, counter-reader.*

**Seven lines, and he wrote it in pencil first and then in paint with a brush Ilse Doff lent him and had to be talked out of, and it is the first thing either of them has ever put up with a deadline in it, and she was not consulted about the deadline, and he had decided the deadline in about eleven seconds on a bench on the ridge, because a reading with no date on it is a reading nobody can refuse, and he wanted a thing that could be refused.**

She came at half past four and read it standing in the road, and then she read it a second time, which is the thing she does.

"Friday's noon."

"Friday's noon."

"You have put a decision on a wall in a district that has a dark tower and a plate glass case and forty children who walk up, and asked a warden to make it by noon tomorrow, and you cannot tell her what happens if she gets it wrong."

"No."

"**And you have printed it anyway, and you have printed my name under yours, and you have not asked me about the deadline.**"

"No."

She looked at him for a while. And then she took the brush off him and put the lid on the paint and gave the lid back.

"That is the first time you have printed something you were frightened of," said Sable Orin, "and you have done it without paying me for it, and I would like to be extremely clear that I noticed the fee."

"Noted."

---

**And at about six, on the ridge, with the working copy of the tower's feed under a glass weight at the near end and a wire going four miles off to a tower where the far end was held by Sable Orin, who had not stopped talking about Friday, he saw a movement in the middle of the strip that is in neither end of it. It is a fifth of a second long. It came before the movement it belongs to and it went when that movement went.**

**He lifted the weight off the end of the strip without breaking the hold and looked at the section under the lamp and put the weight back, and he did not tell her, and he did not put it on anything, and nobody remarked on it.**

He asked her, at about half past eight, to repeat the hour of the flat coming off, and she repeated it, and he wrote it down, and he asked her to repeat it a second time, and she repeated it a second time, patiently, down a wire, the way a foreman repeats a rack count, and he said thank you.

**And at about nine, a runner came up the hill from the ninth ward with a pencil note from a warden's office, and it is four words long, and it is the only sentence in it, and nobody signed it.**

***WHO DECIDES. N. K.***
