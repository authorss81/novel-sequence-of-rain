# Chapter 101 — Wrong Street, Wrong Day

The district sent a woman at about ten on the Friday morning, and she was not a woman who had come to be difficult, and that made it four times worse.

Verna Sly is thirty-eight and has been the fourth ward's clerk for eleven years and has the particular face of a person who has read every minute ever issued about her own district and can recite the dates. She stood in the yard in her coat and looked at the shutter for about half a minute before she said anything at all, and what she said first was:

"**I'll say to my face what I am here to say, Mr. Shaw, and then I'll go, and there's no form, and I'm not going to pretend there's a form.**"

"That's all right."

"I have told the district that the district does not recognise that shutter. Not the words on it, which are another woman's and are hers to publish, and not the second line, which is a copy of the first with a thing painted under it. The district does not recognise the *directive*. Because a district cannot recognise a direction to empty two buildings given by a private person on a private board, and if the district recognised it, that would be the district acting on a reading, and a district acting on a reading is a claim, and this is not a claim, it is a piece of painted wood."

"Ma'am, it kept four people dry for a bit and got a crew stood down for six hours for nothing, and I'd rather that be on the wall than be in a cupboard."

"I know you would," said Verna Sly, "and I have written down in the district book that the district was asked and the district declined to recognise, and the date and the hour, and that is the whole of what the district has done about it, and I would be obliged if you would let that be the whole."

---

**And then she said the thing that made the fortnight, and she said it fast, and she said it once.**

"**And I am not going to ask you to take it down, and I have thought about it for a day and a half and I have not come here to ask you to.**"

Shaw stood in the yard with a trowel in his hand.

"Because the fourth ward has been asked for a schedule three times in twenty years and has refused three times, and all three of those refusals were mine, and there is a box in this city where a district's answer to a question is supposed to be put, and there is not one line in it for the ninth ward and there are eight dates in it for the other eight and none of the eight dates is a date anybody said anything." She had the sheet out now, folded in four, in her glove. "**And three days ago a clerk of another district posted a letter to nine clerks asking one question, and I have had it, and I have answered it, and my answer went back in the ordinary post on the Thursday, and I did not tell the district what I had said, and I am telling you why I did not tell it.**"

"Ma'am—"

"Because the answer is a man's name and the man's name is a person in this ward who is not an officer of anything and has not been for a long time, and a district does not hand a name to a Council because a Council wrote to a clerk, and if I had told the district, the district would have held a meeting about it, and the district does not hold meetings about a man's name."

**And she put the folded sheet back in her glove and said, "That's the whole of the two things I've come with, and one of them's about you and one's about me, and neither of them's the district's, and I'd thank you not to put either of them on a shutter."**

---

**He took it down at about half past five the same afternoon, which took eleven minutes and a ladder and a man of sixty coming across the yard to hold it who wanted to help and did not.**

**And when it was off the wall and leaning in the yard he stood in front of it for a while, and it was a plank about two feet by three, painted in three hands, and two of them were hers and one of them was his, and his was the broadest and the stupidest-looking one, and it read FOUR TO SEVEN, TAKE THE SCHOOL AND THE CHURCH ROW OUT FIRST, and there was no place on it that was correct and there had never been any.**

He did not paint over it. That is what he wants said, because the fourth ward will hear about it inside a fortnight whatever he does or does not do, and it was his hand and his street and his two buildings and there is no version of this in which the street is not his.

What he did was turn it round.

**On the back of it, in the same broad white hand and the same paint out of the same tin, he wrote four lines, and the four lines are the only thing he has ever written that was not for a customer:**

*21st. Painting. Shutter for a woman's weather sheet, chalk line, four words large. No customer. Not for a district. No one to pay me.*

*I put the street on it. Not her. Wrong street, wrong side of the wall, came at ten like she said.*

*It cost a crew six hours in a wet field and a school a wasted afternoon and four people at number eleven a man of seventy-nine on a board in the dark.*

*She never named a street. I did. That's the whole of what's wrong with it and it's mine.*

**The district said it does not recognise it. I am writing that here because the district is right and because in nine days neither of us will remember which of us said it first.**

Then he put the plank flat on two battens against the inside wall of his yard, under a tarpaulin, with the writing face up, where it will be under a roof through the winter, and he went and got the day book off the shelf over the bench, which is a foolscap book with a printed head and four columns — *day, room, work, hours* — and which every plasterer in this city keeps and every plasterer in this city is owed for.

**He entered it as work.** One day, one room, no name on the job, three and four for the paint out of his own pocket, four hours, and against the hours column a figure that is not hours, and in the small print at the foot of a day-book column, where a man puts what he was paid and what it was for, he wrote *no customer*.

**And that is a fourth kind of book in this city and he has never heard of the other three and has never asked, and it is the only one of the four a man can be made to produce, and he has put a day's work in it that is not work, and if a man came with a warrant for that book in five years they would find it and it would be his own hand and there is nothing in it that a court could say was untrue.**

---

He was on the boards over the plainer's shop on the Saturday morning when a lad he knows came past and said the district office wanted to know if he was doing any more reading, and Shaw said no, and the lad said the district had said something about a form.

"I know what form is," said Shaw. "There's a form. It's got a box on it and there's a Council's man at the bottom of it and the box says *state in writing what your district is able to do in a flood* and there's about four inches of it and none of those four inches are yours."

"What are you going to do?"

**"I'm going to paint," said Perrin Shaw, "and I'm going to be seventy-two in this yard in about six weeks, and I'll tell you what the fourth ward is going to do about that form, and I'll tell you now so you can say you heard it here: nothing. The fourth ward is going to write one page, once, in its own hand, and it is going to be the best page anybody writes this year, and it is going to say no, and it is going to say why, and it is not going to be a schedule and it is not going to be in your form."**

**And it is not going to be sent to a Council, and it is not going to be printed on a shutter, and the fourth ward's clerk asked him as a favour and as nothing else, and he has agreed to keep it off his wall, and that is a promise between two people and not a resolution of anything, and he expects to be asked again in a year.**
