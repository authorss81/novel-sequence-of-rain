# Chapter 113 — The Second Time

The second time came on the Friday the fourteenth, and it was correct, and nobody was hurt, and Sable Orin was told about it by a man with a barrow who had come to the office on Tannery Walk to have a go at her about the third notebook, which he had.

"It was Fen Row again, miss. Upper this time, and it was the right one of the two."

"That is the right one."

"So they say. They went up at about three and they came down at about ten and there was water in the low doors to the sill and not over it, and the church row school had the whole of the lower room out of it and back by eight, and they had a man up the ladder with a line and a boy with a lamp, and it was not a mess and it was not nothing." He shifted the barrow. "And they will tell you they had it off a wall, because that is what they say when anybody asks them, and I said, whose wall, and they said, a painted one, in a yard, on the north side."

"Upper Fen Row," said Sable Orin. "Off the north wall. It came down the lower side. **It was my sheet from the fourteenth of Drownmonth and it is the only sheet I have ever had that was used properly, and it has now been used twice, and the man who put the place on it has only done it once, and the district has done it once without him.**"

---

**She went and looked at the fourth ward, which takes nine minutes each way, and the office was shut, and there was a queue on the step with a man from a firm on it, and she stood at the back of it for about four minutes and listened, and then she went home and wrote it in the fourth book, which is the only place in this basin where anybody keeps a record of what a reader was told.**

The entry is in four columns under the head that has not changed in two years.

*15th. Fourth ward, low side, by a man with a barrow, having gone there on the Friday the fourteenth, and confirmed at the counter the same afternoon. The sheet of the 14th of Drownmonth, corridor below the north wall, fourth to seventh hour, used again. The district placed it at the upper Fen Row, which is the correct one of the two. It was right. Water to the sill in the low doors, not over it. The church row school cleared its lower room and was back by eight. Nobody hurt, nothing lost that anybody has said out loud.*

*What it cost, in their words, which were not given to me and which I am writing in my own because I am the one who will be asked: a wall got taken down off a yard, an hour of a Thursday, and the district's standing order got used, and the district did all of that off a thing a man painted in his own hand on a plank he took off his own wall.*

Then, under it, in the same hand, smaller:

*And the thing under it. The district has now acted twice on a reading it cannot check and has been right twice, and a district that has been right twice on a thing it cannot check has stopped checking, and there is nobody in that office who will ever know that they have stopped, because nobody in that office ever started.*

---

**What she could not do was the obvious thing, and not doing it is the only refusal in four notebooks that is a refusal of her own act.**

She had a brush. She had paint. She had a hand four feet wide and she had corrected one man's shutter once already, over his line, without asking him, and it is still on the wall.

Perrin Shaw's shutter is face up on two battens against the inside wall of his yard, writing side up, under a tarpaulin, and there is room on the back of it. The four lines he wrote on it are there, and the district's answer is there, and the room is there.

She stood in the yard with the brush in her hand for about six minutes and did not paint on the back of another man's own work, and then she put the brush in her satchel and wrote it in the book as a decline, because the head of the book is a head for declines and there was nowhere else to put it and she was not going to invent a second head at the age of thirty-four.

*15th, evening. Declined: to correct the back of P. Shaw's shutter, which has room on it, and which carries the district's answer and his own four lines, and which is his work and was taken down at my asking and put under a roof for the winter. He would not let me pay for the paint the first time. I will not put my hand on the back of it. R. Shaw does not know I stood in his yard.*

**And the entry underneath it is the one that took her the evening, and it is the first entry in four notebooks that is not somebody else's words.**

*The district cannot use him. The district was right not to recognise that shutter and it is right still, because a district that recognises a direction to empty two buildings given by a private man on a private plank is acting on a reading, and acting on a reading is a claim. And now the district has had to use him, twice, and the only reason it can is that there is nobody to say it cannot, and in about a year there will be a bad week and there will be four people on the low side and there will be no line in any book in this basin that says where the advice came from except a plasterer's day book in a yard, and a man can be produced out of that book, and he is seventy-one, and he is not the district, and the district did not ask him, and the district cannot.*

---

The letter from the third ward came on the same afternoon, three days in the post, and it went in after the other two, and it is the first letter anybody in this basin has ever written to her about a sheet she gave away, and it is four lines and it is the correctest thing that has come through that door in a year.

*To the reader who came to this office on the fourteenth of Drownmonth. The district has your sheet and a copy of it and has had it since that day. The district placed it at the coal steps and the water came at twenty minutes to five, as you said. Nobody was hurt.*

*The district wishes to ask one question and it is not a complaint. May the district put your name on its copy, so that the next time a sheet comes into this office there is a person in it whom the district can go and ask whether it is still standing there?*

She read it standing up, and then she read it a second time, which is the thing she does, and then she sat down, which she had not expected to do.

**Because the third ward is right, entirely right, and it is right in a way she has spent two years constructing an argument against, and the argument does not work.**

A name on a sheet makes it a notice. A notice can be refused. A reading that can be refused is the only kind anybody in this basin has ever had the right to say no to, and the fourth ward has proved it this month in eight lines on a board in a goods yard. And if her name goes on a sheet then the third ward can refuse her, and the fourth ward can refuse her, and a reader's sheet becomes a thing that districts vote on, and the corridor goes back into being a corridor, and the third ward's wall is four hundred yards and nine doorways and a man of forty-one who can be produced out of it, and the corridor would have to be found again by a district at a bad hour in the dark.

And if it can be refused, it will be. Not out of malice. Out of an ordinary Wednesday, in a room, by nine people who have a wall and a market and a gauge, who have no reason to be hostile to her and no reason to be careful with her either.

**She took out a sheet and wrote the answer on the Monday, and it took her three attempts and about an hour, and the thing she finally wrote is not a refusal of the third ward and is not a yes, and she sent it on the Tuesday at a cost of twopence out of her own hand.**

*To the clerk of the third ward. I cannot put my name on it, and I am going to give you the whole of the reason and not a part of it, because you have given me your whole reason and the fourth ward is right about the practice and I am not going to be the reader who only gives half.*

*A name on a sheet is a notice. A notice can be refused. A reading that can be refused is the only kind anybody in this basin has ever been entitled to say no to, and the fourth ward has proved that this month in eight lines on its own board, and it is right, and I put the objection in my book at the time. So if my name goes on your copy then your council can refuse me in an ordinary week with no reason, and the sheet goes back into being a corridor, and your wall is the only reason you could use it, and the wall will not be there in eighty years.*

*So no. And I am not going to pretend that the practice is a good one, because it is not, and I have said so on a shutter in public with my name at the bottom of it, and I meant every word and I would write it again.*

*And here is the one sentence I will give you, and it is not a rule, because I do not keep rules and you should not write it as one. Write down, in the district's file, that you were told by nobody you can go and ask. If the next one is wrong then you can look at the last one and see that you were told by nothing, and that is a fact about a person you cannot name, and it is a better thing to have on paper than a fact about a weather.*

*— S. Orin, counter-reader.*

And then, at the foot of the fourth book, in the same hand, after four entries, she wrote a line that is not in any column the head provides for, and she ruled a line under the head and wrote it, and it is the fifth thing in four notebooks and the only one that is about her:

*The good case is a district that did it right and can prove it and cannot say who told it, and the cost case is a district that did it right twice and cannot say who told it either, and the difference between the two districts is that one of them has nine men in a room and the other one has a wall. The reader is the same in both. That is the whole of the book and it is a book of things I did not do, and the day I write a line in it about something I did, this is a different book, and I do not yet know what I would put in it.*
