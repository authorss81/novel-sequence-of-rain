# Chapter 98 — The Drawer Copy

There are four requests for the same page of the same book in three weeks, and the second floor's rule is that a request for a page of a Council book is answered by asking a licensing clerk, and the licensing clerk said no twice, and on the third occasion the second floor sent the boy upstairs himself.

Wend Lyle is nineteen. He has been on the second floor for eleven months and he counts the week's sheets on a Monday and copies out anything a partner wants kept, in his own hand, dated and initialled, into a drawer on a middle desk, because he was warned in his first week that a paragraph somebody can unpublish is not a record and a paragraph in a nineteen-year-old's hand is.

**The page is the available responses page.** One sheet, a printed head, a date against it, nothing under the date. He had seen it once, in a proof of the withdrawal paragraph, and he had read the head and understood it and thought it was a printer's error.

He goes up at about a quarter past four on the Wednesday of the twenty-first of Miremonth with a satchel and a card and the four requests, three of them from the firm's own district desk and one from a counting-floor clerk who wanted it for a customer.

The basin house press room is on the ground floor and there is a man in it who does not look up, because press rooms are where men go to be left alone with paper. Lyle asks for the page. The man asks him which page. Lyle says the available responses page of the Standing Response Register, fourth season column, and gives the minute number he has copied out in pencil on the back of a form.

**"There is nothing on the page," says the man.**

"I know there is nothing on the page. That's why the request is the same four times, is it — everybody wants to see that it's empty. It's the only page in that book anybody's asked for twice and I want to be able to say I saw it."

The man looked at him for a moment. Then he took the book off the shelf, opened it, put a flat brass weight on the page, and turned it round to face the boy, and did not let go of it.

Lyle read it. A printed head. A date in the right-hand column. Nothing under it. And in the left-hand column, a season number, and in the middle column, a district.

**The district was the sixth.**

He did not ask about that and he copied the date and the season and the district name into his own book and thanked the man and went back down, because he is nineteen and he has learned in eleven months that a man in a press room who turns a book round for you and keeps his hand on the page has told you everything he intends to.

---

**Then he did the thing, and he did it because he could not sleep on the Wednesday.**

On the second floor there is a board of live requests, and every request on it has a column for *held by us*, and about two-thirds of the entries in that column are a firm's own, and the rest are a district's, or a carrier's, or an insurer's, and there is a fourth column that the form does not have, which he wrote on the back of a form in pencil in his first month, headed *where is it actually*, and nobody has ever taken the form off him.

**He filled it in for the week, and then he went back two months and filled it in for two months, and then he found he could not stop, and at about eleven at night on the Wednesday he had nine items and then eleven, and by Thursday night he had twenty-nine, and it took him about four hours and a half in pencil on the back of two Council forms, and the entries are these:**

*1. Receipt for a firm paragraph: we hold it. Fine.*
*2. The Bellweather reach line, withdrawn in public, price withdrawn with it: we hold our own copy, the firms' sheet does not, and the two have never been set side by side. Fine, because we hold ours.*
*3. The ninth ward's return, eighteen pages, sold to us on the 2nd of Sleetmonth for 11s. We hold it. **We are the only people in this basin who hold it and it is a district's schedule of what it can do.** Fine.*
*4. The sixth ward's what-the-ward-can-do: asked for twice, not held by us, not held by anybody. **Never existed.***
*5. The available responses page: four requests in three weeks, and the answer is a date and nothing, and the date is the day the Council wrote it and not the day a district said anything.*

**And then, at the bottom, on a third form, because the first two were nearly full, the two that stopped him:**

*6. The ninth ward's reach hours, the half past one and the eleven past four, and the nine minutes. We print the ward's own name and the hours once a week at 3d. **We do not hold the reading. We hold the ward's permission.** The ward sold us the right to print. The ward did not write the hours and the ward could not check them and nobody asked the two people whose names are on the shutter.*

*7. A reading, fourth ward, a corridor and an hour, copied out on a shutter by a plasterer, with a street added in his own hand that is not in the reading. Not held by us. Not held by the district. Not held by anybody. **It went out on a shutter and it was wrong and there is no copy of it except the shutter.***

**He wrote both of those out in full, and dated them, and initialled them, and put them in the drawer on the middle desk under glass with a lehr log's worth of other paper, and he did it without being asked by anybody, and it is the first thing he has ever done in his working life that was not in a procedure.**

He lay awake about it and got up at five and came in early and checked the drawer, and it was there, and the date was on it, and the initials were on it, and the glass weight was on it.

**Then he did the second thing, which was the one that made it a list and not a complaint, and it took him eleven minutes.**

He wrote at the head of the form, in pencil, in nineteen-year-old handwriting, the sentence he had been turning over since the Wednesday:

*This is not a list of things people have kept. This is a list of things the basin has been asked for and cannot produce. Both of those look identical from the outside and one of them is a record and the other one is a hole, and I can only tell them apart by asking somebody, and asking somebody is the entire cost of this.*

And under it, in the same pencil and the same hand, an eighth item, which he added on the Friday morning because it was the one that had been bothering him since the Wednesday and it took him three attempts to word:

*8. The question item four of the minute of the 20th asks the office to which a district's statement of what it can do about its water is addressed. **Nobody knows. It is not a book question. Somebody's job was it, and that job is not in any index, because a person employed by a ward is not an officer of the Council and the Council's index lists officers of the Council.***

**He read item eight twice and thought it was the best thing on the form and thought also that a partner would find it interesting, and then he thought about what his foreman had actually said to him one autumn, which was that a thing the firm cannot produce is worth more to it than a thing it can, and about how that is true, and about how it stops being true at about the size of a person.**

He put the third form in the drawer under the glass and shut the drawer and counted the week's sheets, which is what he does on a Monday, and it was not Monday.

---

**On the Friday the twenty-third a licensing clerk came up the stairs and asked him for the drawer.**

He had been expecting somebody for about a week, and the asking went the way it goes: the clerk wanted a firm's withdrawal paragraph, the one that had been struck through twice in public with no price on it, and there is a firm's copy in the press on the second floor, and there is the counter clerk's own copy in a press, and there is Lyle's in a drawer on a middle desk, and the licensing clerk wanted the one nobody else knew about.

"Which of the three," said Lyle.

"The one with a hand on it," said the licensing clerk, who is a licensing clerk and not the one who made the nine telephone calls in the autumn, and has a different job altogether.

Lyle gave it to him, in the drawer, in the flat, and watched him read it, and the licensing clerk said the sentence that clerks say when they have been shown something they should have been shown six months earlier.

**"Right," said the licensing clerk. "That's a record. Ours isn't, because ours is in a press."** And then he said, without any particular emphasis, and put it back: "What else is in that drawer, Lyle."

"Nothing," said Wend Lyle.

And then, about four seconds later, and he heard himself do it, and he did not have a better answer by the time the four seconds were up: "The list of what we can't produce, which I keep for myself, because we can't produce it, and a list of what we can't produce isn't a document, it's the other thing, and the other thing is a nineteen-year-old's opinion with a date on it."

**The licensing clerk wrote nothing down. He said, "That's right," and went down the stairs, and Lyle listened to him go, and then he took the form out of the drawer and looked at the eighth item and did not alter a word of it, which is the only act of institutional discipline this firm has produced in nineteen years and it was done by accident by a nineteen-year-old who was not defending anything.**

**Nobody has asked him for it, and he has not offered it to anybody, and there is a drawer on a middle desk with three forms in it and a glass weight on top and no index anywhere.**
