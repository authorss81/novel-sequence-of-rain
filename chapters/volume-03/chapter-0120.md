# Chapter 120 — Read Back

The envelope from Hearthward came on the Monday morning of the twenty-fourth with a minute number on the covering line and four questions in it, one of which was the one that mattered, and it was dated the nineteenth, and on the covering line, in a different pen, in a hand that had been in a hurry, there were five words.

*Sent once before. Not answered. Not blaming you.*

There is no answer in the district's file to the first one, because the first one is not in the district's file, because the first one came on the twentieth of last month into an office that has had a queue on the step since the thirtieth of last month, and a woman with a fair hand in Hearthward waited four weeks and then wrote the whole of it again and posted it, and the fourpence a letter costs is the only money Kell Bray has ever spent on this and she has not mentioned it to anybody.

*So that a minute may be answered from a record and not from a memory, I ask one question of your district: when a reader or a keeper or any person at all brings a reading into the district, whose hand copies it into a book, what is that person's title in the district's own words, and on what date did they last do it.*

*The district may refuse to answer, or may answer in part. A refusal will be entered as a refusal and will not be held against the ward in any transaction. If your district keeps no such book, the answer is that it keeps no such book, and that is a complete answer and I would rather have it than none.*

**Nobody in the fourth ward had answered it, and the reason is not carelessness, it is that in twenty years this office has been sent four requests for a schedule and has refused four, and a fifth one arrives with a minute number on it and looks like the other four, and a woman who refuses four requests is not going to open the fifth on the strength of a covering line.**

"There is no such person," she said, to the room. "There has not been one in this ward for nine years. The last one to do it was the district clerk, in the ordinary way, in his own hand, on the days it fell to him, and he has been dead eleven years, and there is no name on him anywhere except the flyleaf of a book in a drawer, and that is the only book this ward has ever had and it is nine years of a man writing down the day and the week."

She took the drawer out. It is the drawer of papers nobody can produce, and it has a label on it that she has not touched in eleven years, because the label is on the other drawer.

"And the honest answer to her question is the one I put in our own minute nine days ago, and it is that anybody may come to this office and look at it. You may look at it now. And there is nobody in this ward who has ever asked, and I would be obliged if you would let me read it out in front of a witness, because I have been a clerk eleven years and I have never once read a line of it back."

---

**She read it out at about half past ten on the twenty-fourth, in her own office, with the door shut and four people in it, and it took her under a minute, and the four people were a district clerk, a woman from the parish, the man from the firm with the card, who had come back a second time about something else entirely and had stayed, and a boy of nineteen off a firm's second floor who had come to say that the district's page was being copied onto a shutter and was not going to say anything else.**

*11th Sleetmonth. Low side to the ninth ward, three trips, one boat, a man and a lad, lamp. Eleven. Sheet to follow.*

*25th Sleetmonth. Ninth ward's sheet not come. Wrote. No reply.*

"Eleven people," said Verna Sly. "Off the low side of this ward to the ninth, in one boat, with a lamp, at night, in a freeze, two winters back, and the reason is not in this file and the reason was not given to this ward, and the paper that had their names on it was to follow and did not follow, and this ward wrote about it and got nothing back, and that is the end of the file and it has been the end of the file for two winters and two weeks and nobody has ever come and asked me about it."

"And you cannot tell us who they are."

"**I cannot tell you the name of one of them, and I have been the clerk of this ward for eleven years, and I found out on Monday because a boy of nineteen read the drawer standing up, and I did not know and I have not been able to put it down since.**"

The boy of nineteen said nothing at all. He had a notebook in his coat and he did not take it out, and afterwards he told a colleague in a stairwell that he had wanted to write something down and had not been able to think what the thing he had wanted to write down was.

---

And then she did the thing that changes a position, and it is small, and it took four minutes, and it is the second time in twenty years that the fourth ward has entered a name in a book of its own on purpose.

She went to the counter and got the column book, which is the book with three headings and no fourth, and the third of which is *what he did not say*, and she had ruled the twelfth of this month into it and written nothing, and she wrote:

*Twenty-fourth. The dead clerk's file read out in this office, at the warden's request, in front of four witnesses, being a district clerk, a woman of the parish, a young man of a firm and a boy of the same firm. The line of the eleventh of Sleetmonth is the only paper in this ward that says eleven people were put off our low side and went to the ninth, and it is the only paper in the world, and there is no sheet, and the ward cannot name one of them.*

*Twenty-fourth. Entered here on purpose: the firm of the young man who read the file in this office on the third, the name of the firm, and the hour, and the fact that I did not ask who he was working for. This is the first time in eleven years that this office has written down the name of a party in respect of an enquiry, and it is written in order that this ward can complain, and a complaint requires an addressee, and I am aware of what it is to write a party's name in a book about a thing that happened to us, and I have thought about it for a day and a half and I am doing it anyway.*

The fourth ward has refused a Council, a firm, a form and a list in one document, on a board, at its own cost, and it has never once in twenty years entered a firm anywhere, and it entered one on the twenty-fourth because a boy read a drawer standing up and told it nothing and it wanted somebody it could write to.

---

And then she posted the answer, and it is the first thing this ward has ever sent anybody, and it went in the ordinary post on the Tuesday at a cost of twopence out of the district's money, and it is four lines, and it is a copy.

*To the clerk of the Hearthward district office. The fourth ward's answer, and it is the whole of it.*

*There is no such person and there has not been one in this ward for nine years. The work was done by a district clerk, in the ordinary way, and he is dead, and the book is a dead man's and it is in this office and anybody may come and look at it.*

*We have no book of readings in this ward. We had none for nine years and we have none now, and the last reading this ward was given that it acted on was a corridor and an hour and no place, written on a plank by a private man in a yard, and the district did not recognise it, and the district was right, and the reading was right, and there is no line in this office that says who told us, and we can produce the two things we have and we cannot produce a third thing that nobody in the basin has ever been able to produce.*

*A clerk of this ward, on the twenty-fourth day of this month, read the file line of the eleventh of Sleetmonth out loud in this office, in front of four witnesses, at the ward's own request, and it is the first time in nine years that anybody has read it back, and it is a line of a dead man's hand about eleven people who went off our low side to the ninth ward in one boat in the dark, and their names are on a sheet that this ward put into the post two winters ago and that has never been produced by anybody, and there is not one person alive in this ward who can say what any of them were called.*

**— V. Sly, clerk, fourth ward.**

She did not put it on the board. The board is four feet by three on the outside wall of that office and it carries one page of foolscap in four clips and a heading in a clerk's hand, and it says what the fourth ward declines and why, and it has not been improved, corrected off the face, or taken down, and it did not say this, and she did not add to it.

"That is deliberate," she said, when the district clerk asked her about it, standing in the office at about half past two on the Tuesday. "**The wall is for the things we decide. This is a thing that happened to us.** And a thing that happened to us does not get a heading in a clerk's hand and four clips, and if I put it up there in a fortnight somebody will read it as the ward having a position about it, and we have got one position on that wall and it is worth a shilling a week to us and I am not spending it on eleven people I cannot name."

She put the file back in the drawer, and the drawer is not locked and has never been locked, and the two lines are in it, and they have now been read out loud once, in a room, to four people, and the four people are a district clerk, a woman of the parish, a man from a firm and a boy of nineteen, and one of the four wrote nothing down and one of the four is going to write it down.

**The boy of nineteen stopped at the door on his way out and said the thing he had come to say, which was that the fourth ward's page was being copied onto a shutter, and then he said the other thing, unasked, to a clerk, in about nine seconds, and it was the first time in eleven months he had said a thing out loud that he had not initialled.**

"The firm has a drawer with things in it that we cannot produce. There is a Council standing day on the fourth Thursday with a printed head and a fee. I have worked out what the two of those are for, and I have not told anybody, and I have not told you, and I would like you to consider not asking me about it in a room."

"I'm not going to ask you about it in a room," said Verna Sly.

"No," he said. "I did not think you would. That is why I said it."

**And at about four o'clock on the Tuesday a boy came into the office with a bicycle and an envelope, and asked for the ninth ward, which is four miles and a lock downriver and not this office, and Verna Sly told him the road and he went out into the rain with it. The envelope was from Hearthward and it was five weeks old and it was a minute number and a letter, and the covering line said, in a fair hand, *I wrote this on the twentieth of last month and I did not post it until today, and I have not told the district, and I would be obliged if you would not tell it either.* There were eleven people at the other end of it who could not be named, and a ward with a district book of five columns and no column for a person, and nobody in the fourth ward's office said so, and the boy went up the hill road at the speed of a boy with a bicycle in the wet.**
