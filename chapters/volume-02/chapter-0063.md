# Chapter 63 — The Carrier's Man

Tobias Mear was not authorised to do what he did on the Wednesday morning of the ninth day of Drownmonth and he did it anyway, and the reason he did it anyway was that he had a memorandum in his own handwriting in a drawer in his own desk that told him the whole of what he had to find out and told him he was not permitted to print it, and there is a particular kind of man who cannot leave a piece of paper alone once he has understood it, and Mear was that man, and it had cost him his sleep on the Sunday.

The memorandum said: *7/6 against 1/4, five and a half times. These are not the same object. Publish the first. Hold this.*

**Publish the first.** That half had been done by somebody else without being asked, on a wall in a public room, in pencil, and it had been done twice since the storm, both times in public, on a wall, in pencil, and the price of doing it was that there was now a published number for forty minutes of the Bellweather reach and it was wrong.

**Hold this.** He had held it for four weeks and it had not improved.

On the Wednesday morning he went and tried to hold the two halves against each other one more time, and it failed, and then it failed a second way, and then he went and found out why a morning's worth of a market's own book can be a worse thing to look at than a number.

---

He came in at a quarter to eight, before the floor, and asked for the first day's book for the seventeenth of Drownmonth, and got it, because the forward book is not a private book; it is open on the counter from seven until the bells, and anybody in the building may read it, and one of the reasons the firm has never had a scandal is that it publishes its own entries to its own staff.

He read one morning. The seventeenth. Six lines.

1. Bellweather reach, low water, 7/6, refund 3/9. Sold the 4th, 11:40. Party: **a salvage interest, freeholder of two hundred yards on the ninth ward's low ground.** Use: barge, gang of four, strip and cartage. On the ground since the 3rd. Advance lodged, received.
2. Bellweather reach, low water, 7/6, refund 3/9. Sold the 4th, 11:52. Party: **a firm.** Use: *the firm's own onward sale, disclosed.* On the ground since the 3rd. Advance lodged, received.

Twelve minutes apart.

He read line two four times and then went and found the rule that permitted it, which took him nine minutes in a shelf of bound volumes, and it is one sentence and it is on the form and it is correct:

*A window is sold to a party that can show a use for the hours.*

Not *a use in the water.* A use.

**And a firm that has itself sold the hour on again can show a use, and it is a perfectly good use, and the use is not a hull and it is not a gang of eleven and it is not anybody who will ever be standing in four inches of water at seven in the morning with a chain in their hands.**

He sat on the edge of a counter on the second floor of a building with a brass plate on it whose date had gone soft in the middle, and worked out, in about four minutes, the whole of what the refusal rule was worth in the trade, and he had been an underwriter for six years and had made his living out of finding the moment two documents stopped agreeing.

He believed the rule excluded a district, and he had eight months of standing in the trade to know better than that and did not check, and the sentence is in his note and it is wrong. He had it in his head that the rule turns anybody out whose use is a pump, a timber stack, a rota and a bad back, anybody whose product is a thing they have already paid for, anybody whose marginal cost of doing the work the timber was bought for is the cost of turning up. It is a *good* rule and a *necessary* rule, and the reason it is necessary is the reason the ward is not in it, and a purchaser who cannot use an hour cannot be refunded in any way that means anything, and a market that sells hours to parties that cannot use them is a market that will have to be shut by somebody, eventually, in a room, with a finding. **Six days later a counter clerk of the same firm stood in a room over a locker's yard and took the first half of that sentence apart in about ninety seconds, and he was not in the room, and he has not taken it apart yet.**

**And it excludes nothing else at all, and a party that resells the hour is not a party that cannot use it. A party that resells the hour has made the hour into a thing with two uses, and one of the two uses is money, and money is a use.**

So: the seventeenth of Drownmonth had been sold twice before its sales closed at four o'clock on the Saturday, in the same twelve minutes, by the same firm, to two parties, and one of those parties was going to take copper, kerb and a foot of good gravel out of two hundred yards of the ninth ward's lowest ground, and the other one was not going to be in the reach at all, and neither of them knew the other existed, and neither of them had any remedy, because there is no form on which a purchaser of a window can complain that a purchaser of the same window has been sold the same window.

He went and wrote it down. He wrote it once, and legibly, and read it back, which he did not do.

*17th Drownmonth, forward book, lines 1 and 2. One window, two purchasers, twelve minutes, both advance lodged. Refusal rule satisfied in both: a salvage interest shows a hull and a gang; a firm shows its own onward sale, disclosed. The rule excludes a ward. It does not exclude a purchaser, and a purchaser is not a party to anything.*

*This is not a complaint. There is no rule broken and I am not going to write a letter about it. A market that will sell the same hour to a man and to a shop is a market that has learned to sell its own product, and the man and the shop do not know each other, and neither of them is owed anything by the other, and that is a market working.*

*And it will do it again on the biggest window of the season, and it will do it in public, on a wall, with both prices, because the refund rule publishes the withdrawn price and the publication is the only honesty in this building.*

---

The instrument was on the south-bank freight apron at about ten and it was Renn Sallow's, and Mear had walked down the hill road for it, and Sallow was in the well with a shackle in his hand.

"I want a second one."

Sallow came up out of the well and looked at him for a while.

"You have one."

"I have one. It's at the south end of this apron, in this wall, and it reads the bottom of the reach at one point." Mear put his hand flat on the coping. "A reach is not a bucket. There is a slope in it, and the slope is the entire question, and with one float in one wall I know how deep it is here and nothing whatever about whether a hull can lie in the middle of it, and every quotation we publish for that reach is a quotation about *this* point, and everybody in the basin is now quoting *this* point, including a man with a barge who is going to be forty yards from it in the middle of the ebb."

"How much."

"Two hundred and forty pounds for the instrument, and about forty for the wall, and a fortnight."

Sallow looked at him for another while.

"You are the second one of you," he said.

"The second one of whom?"

"The second man in a coat to come up that hill road in a fortnight and tell me what to make." Sallow put the shackle in his pocket. "The other one was a maintenance hand off the Weatherglass ridge and he did not tell me what to make. He told me what he would not do, and he would not anneal the glass, and he signed the docket in the box that says what the work was, and I have thought about that more than I have thought about anything this year." He wiped his hands. "Make your two. I will cut the datum to the same length as this one and I will number it in the same hand and I will send you the docket saying who annealed it, which will be me, because it will be me."

---

**Tarin Senn gave him the yard's slip at about one o'clock on the Wednesday and made him pay for the privilege in public.**

The Line already had an instrument — the one the firm had brought in a crate in the middle of Sleetmonth, free, on a retainer, whether the yard bought a window or not, fitted to the yard's own slip in about forty minutes by a maintenance hand off this ridge, in a good coat, at his own hour. What Mear wanted was the second one, and what he was asking for was a hole cut in a yard's slip at a cost of a day and a half of a yard's time and a plate off a hull, and he was asking it on the Exchange's money, and he had no standing whatever.

"You want to cut a second hole in my slip."

"I want to put a second float in the water beside the first one, in the same slip, ninety feet apart, so that there are two readings on the same reach at the same time. Then a buyer of a window on the seventeenth can be told the slope and not only the depth."

"And who publishes it?"

"You do."

"I'll not." She said it immediately and without any heat. "The yard's instruments are published under the fifth condition and they are published as the yard's, and if this one is the yard's it goes on the yard's board with the yard's name on it and the hour and the date, and if you have a second one on my ground it goes on my board the same way or it goes back down the hill road in the crate it came in."

"Then it is not the Exchange's instrument," said Mear.

"It is a float in my water," said Tarin Senn, "and the fifth line does not say *and not in a public slip*, and I said the fifth line out loud in a yard in the autumn and you lot gave it me in a quarter of an hour for nothing, and do not now come and tell me what the fifth line means while I have a hole cut in my slip."

He looked at her for a moment and then he said, "That is fair and I should have known it."

"You should have known it in about four minutes and you did not, and it has taken you since the twenty-third to work out that the firm cannot be the only party who holds a reading of the basin." She was not smiling. "Now write it down. Get Ilva to write it down. *Yard instrument, second float, Kestrel slip, installed the 9th of Drownmonth, published by the yard under the fifth condition, the Exchange has no exclusive use of it and did not pay for it and may not cut a third.*"

"Mear is not an agent, Miss Senn."

"Then Mear cannot use it either," said Tarin Senn, "and that is a better sentence than the one I gave you."

---

**And then he went down to the low ground at about four in the afternoon and was refused, in four minutes, by a woman with a barrow, and paid the price of the refusal without arguing about it.**

He had done it properly. He had written, and had it initialled, and had put it in an ordinary envelope, and the envelope said *the Cloud Exchange* because he was an employee of the Cloud Exchange and could not put anything else on it.

What he asked for was not the ward's schedule. He had learned that lesson on the Thursday before last, from a boy with a flat sheet of Council paper, and it was the most useful thing that had happened to him all quarter. What he asked for was a **reading**: the ninth ward's own level, at a stated hour, on a stated day, at the corner of the fourth block, from whatever the ward used.

Neris Kade read it standing in the doorway of the district office with a barrow handle in her hand.

"Mr. Mear."

"Ma'am. I have not got a glass and I have got to know what your low side does on the seventeenth, and I would pay the ordinary price and I would put it in the file under the date and the hour and I would publish whatever you told me, in your words, which is a thing my firm has never done with a district's word in nineteen years."

"You have a float."

"I have a float."

"So you have the only instrument in the basin that can tell you what that water is doing, and it is in a wall at the south end of a freight apron, and it reads a quarter of an inch, and a man with a fifth column on a tower a hundred and forty miles the other way has compared it with the district's own for three days and got twelve out of twelve." She put the barrow down. "That is worth saying out loud to you, and I am saying it out loud to you, and now I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to my pumps."

"Ma'am—"

"You have come to me for a reading and there is nothing in this ward that gives a reading, and there is a thing in that wall that does, and the reason the two of us cannot settle it is that you are a firm and I am a district, and five days ago you said in a letter that the district may refuse, and I am refusing."

"I understand."

"You do not understand at all," said Neris Kade, "because you have just spent the whole of a morning getting two instruments put into the water on a yard slip at a yard's price, and you have understood that perfectly, and it took you eleven days and a refusal." She picked the barrow up. "You have been up that hill road once this morning buying an instrument off a man in his trade, and you came down here to buy a second one off a woman with a barrow, and both of them were the wrong question. The difference is not the money. The man will read it back to you in a fortnight when he has cut it, and the ward will not read it back to you at all, because nobody has ever asked the ward anything and if you had asked her she would have said no and been right."

---

He wrote the letter at about seven in the evening, at the corner of the second floor's counter desk, in his own hand, and it is the only thing he ever sent to anybody in the Council in six years, and he sent it for the wrong reason and it was still the right thing.

*Assessor Orle, technical cell, Council's basin house.*

*I am an underwriter of the Cloud Exchange and I am not authorised to write to this Council and I am writing to you anyway because I have a memorandum that cannot be used by anybody and I have worked out that the only use a number like mine has is in a claim that cannot be brought.*

*I enclose a copy of a memorandum of the seventeenth of Sleetmonth in my own hand, which is not evidence of anything and is not offered as evidence. It compares two prices for the same forty minutes. The Exchange's price is 7/6, to a barge interest, and my own figure for the same forty minutes to a district holding the load already bought and paid is 1/4. Five and a half times, and every figure on both sides is true, and no person in either transaction said anything untrue, and I have instructed that it never be printed and I am now sending it to you, which is a different thing and is not what I instructed.*

*The reason is this. There is no form in the Charter of Public Notice, or in this building, or in this basin, on which a district can be shown what a claim was worth to it. I have spent four weeks looking for one and I have established that the reason is a form, and that the form is honest, and that the form is empty, and that the Council's own record of what each ward is able to do about its water has a heading and nothing under it for the ninth ward, and that the party selling windows has a form with nothing in it as well.*

*Two institutions, one street, and the same shape. I do not know what to do about it and I am not going to pretend to. I am sending you a number with nobody's schedule attached to it, which I understand is precisely the thing a review is for.*

*T. Mear, underwriter. This is not a claim, not a complaint, and not on the firm's paper.*

He read it back, and put it in an envelope with no firm mark on it, and took it down to the box on the ground floor himself rather than give it to the boy, because it was not the boy's errand and the boy's already had one.

**It arrived in the Council's basin house on the Thursday morning of the tenth of Drownmonth, in an envelope with nothing on it, in a hand nobody in that building had seen before, from a man who had never been inside the gate.**
