# Chapter 87 — The Miremonth Window

The ninth ward binds its own window on the seventh day of Miremonth in the district office over the locker's yard, with nine people in it and the rain coming off the roof in the way it does about four times a season.

Neris Kade reads the cost out loud before one word of the claim is written down, because the assessor did it in front of nine people in the Bellweather month and she has apparently got a habit, and there is a clerk in the room who will minute whatever she says and she would rather be corrected here than there.

"The ward can give you six men at the district rate for a day, and a gang for the pump and its gear, and nothing for the timber and nothing for the pump, because the timber and the pump are bought and paid. That is one and four. What is between them is the wall. A day of that is worth nothing and I can prove that, because it is worth nothing all the time and nobody has ever been asked to pay for it. There is no third figure and I cannot make one and I will not sign one if you put it in front of me."

She sits down. Nobody corrects her.

**A loss which is a period cannot be entered as a sum, and the Council may not supply a figure, and that is the ruling of the twentieth, and it stands for the season and not for each event.**

The claim goes on the rail at about four o'clock in a fair hand, on a sheet of foolscap, and the sixth box is the sixth box and the seventh box is the fifth time in this city that it has been filled in by anybody.

*Place: the Bellweather reach at the south end of the freight apron, the low flat, the ninth ward's ground from the N end, and the third block's drains.*
*Trigger: the ward's clearing gang stood to the flat on the second day of Miremonth and the pump gang stood by.*
*Effect: the reach comes off at about half past one on the low ground and at about eleven minutes past four at the south end, four hours and ten minutes apart on the water, and the flat takes a gang for forty minutes.*
*Expiry: the end of that morning, on the same line as the effect, at the assessor's instruction.*
*Sponsor: N. Kade, warden, ninth ward.*
*Concurring seat: the ninth ward, at four o'clock, nine present, in about two minutes.*
*Attestation: a reader's attestation of the order of the movements only — S. Orin, reader, counter, not in this office's service.*

**Marek Vale is not in the room. He has not been asked to attest, and he has not offered, and the trace hours on the sheet are his and hers together, and the attestation is hers alone.**

And the seventh box, which is not read out loud, because the seventh box is not part of the claim:

*The six lines above are what the ninth ward can do about its water. The bend is at half past one and eleven past four and the nine minutes is on the shutter. This claim is not a refusal. I want the gang on the flat for forty minutes on the second day of the new month and I have waited since the autumn for an hour with a name on it.*
*What it costs: the morning. Six men and a water steward and the pump gang and the gear. The timber stays in the shed and the wall waits and the table on the wall says two years and about nine months and nobody can produce the line between the pegs.*
*Route doubled or single, nine minutes apart, both readings standing, neither withdrawn. I cannot tell you what a morning is worth and I will not sign a figure.*

The concurrence takes two minutes, because a concurrence is the only part of a claim anybody gets to watch being bought, and the vote is eight to one, and the one is a man who says he has a cart on the reach and wants it minuted, and it is minuted.

**The bell is struck at four for the ninth ward, once, and the ninth ward's head above the rail does nothing at all. It has not lit since the middle of the Bellweather month, because the frame is a bubbled temporary in a brass surround bedded by hand, over a permanent that came out in the autumn and was never replaced, ordered and paid for and never collected by a district whose glasswork allocation does not cover a man and a crate sent forty miles.**

Nobody looks up. That is the whole of the ward-mismatch argument, and it takes about four seconds, and nobody says anything.

It comes in the middle of the sixth box, in the fair hand of the district clerk, and it comes to a sheet of foolscap pinned to a rail in a district office with nine people in it and a reader in it and a warden at the front with her cap still on, and nobody in that room knows what it is made of except the two people who have seen one before, and one of them is not there.

**A tide is arithmetic and needs no reader for its effect, because a tide is a clock with water on it. A reach still needs one, and the ward has got one, and she is not him, and the sixth ward has spent its spring.**

---

At about six the assessor writes a line into the Office's weekly sheet, in the cold version, in the ordinary hand, for the Monday's printing — a new line, and the column is still headed CLAIM — DISPLACED TURNS and still has no sponsor field and still carries no disclaimer:

*Window, ninth ward, the reach off at half past one and eleven past four, morning of the second of Miremonth. Basis: a claim entered by a district. No branch of this office was closed.*

**It is the first line anybody in this basin has ever entered about a window that a district bound for itself, and it names what closed and does not name who closed it.**

She reads it in the proof at about half past six and does not strike it, and says out loud to an empty room: "There is still no column in the world for a window, and the column is called dry lines, and now there are two of them in it."

On the ground floor of the basin house, in the bound book about two inches thick, there is still exactly one line, nineteen words long, saying the figure is unknown, and the fourth party has still not been asked.

**Nothing is refunded. Nothing reopens.**
