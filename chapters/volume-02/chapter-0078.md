# Chapter 78 — The Long Afternoon

The bench is in the outer room of the Weatherglass maintenance shop, under the north window, and it is the only bench on the ridge a censured reader is allowed to work at, because it is not in the Office and it is not a bench of the Office's and nobody upstairs has ever asked the foreman to move it, and Ilse Doff moved a lamp onto it in the autumn and has not moved it off.

He came in at about a quarter to six on the Monday morning of the twenty-eighth day of Drownmonth with a case of plate, a lamp, a cover, his own glasses, and a length of pencil that had no place in a cuff.

**A trace is not a promotion.** It is a stage of method and there is no rank in it and no warrant and no entry, and there is no such thing as a man who is a Tracer, and the only honest description of what he was about to do is that he had worked out how to hold a sequence continuously from one tower to another with somebody else holding the near end, and that it had taken four months, a fee, and a ladder he had not gone up.

She went up at about ten past six. He took the ridge end at about twenty past, with a plate off the tower's own feed written out in the register of the technical cell — the working copy, cut and ground for the purpose and stamped, and not fitted anywhere and not going to be fitted anywhere, because a fitted plate is a spent plate.

**A two-ended read gives you a bend. A trace gives you the hour the bend arrives at the far place**, because the sequence is then held from one end to the other and the hour is in the movement's own order and not in either party's clock. That is the entire difference, and it is worth a great deal more than a bend, and it is the difference between knowing that the water will turn and knowing when to stand your crew on a flat.

---

**It worked at about eleven minutes past four in the afternoon, and it took four attempts, and the thing that made it work was that she stopped helping him.**

The first attempt failed at about half past eight because the near end was not anchored; she was reading and reacting, and a reader who is reacting is not holding an end, she is watching one. He said so, once, in about nine words, and she said *go on, then* and went back to the glass.

The second failed at about eleven because he was doing the thing he has done since the spring, which is to lean on the level. The Exchange's reach gauge is a hundred and forty miles down the hill road in a wall at the south end of a freight apron and it is a good instrument and it is repeatable to a quarter of an inch at an interval by two different people with two different chains, and **a quarter of an inch is a magnificent fact about depth and it has never once been a fact about an hour.**

The third failed at about two in the afternoon, sideways, the way a failed trace fails, and it came apart in about eleven minutes and he felt it go and had nothing to do about it.

**Between the second and the third she did something at about half past one that neither of them mentioned afterwards and that is the whole of why the fourth one worked, and it is this: she stopped reading the sequence and started reading him.**

Not the glass. Him. She was on the far end of a wire four miles from a man on a bench, and for about forty minutes she did not look at her strip at all; she looked at the way he was breathing and she counted, and she put a mark on her own paper every time his count went off, and at the end of it she had eleven marks and every one of them was a place where he had decided something.

"You've been deciding," she said. "You've been deciding since about ten and you've decided nine times and I can see all of them from here, and every one of them was a decision not to ask me something."

"There was nothing to ask you."

"There was something to ask me nine times." She put the pen down. "**You do not get to be a private man in the middle of somebody's trace. That is the whole of the second reader and nobody has ever told you because you have never had one before.**"

The fourth held from about ten past four until about half past six and then let go, and in between it gave him the hour.

**The bend on the low ground on the second day of next month is at about half past one, and it arrives at the south end of the freight apron at about eleven minutes past four, and the water is off the flat at the top of it and the water is on the flat at the bottom of it, and the two hours are four hours and ten minutes apart on the actual water, and no instrument in this basin had ever known that before Monday.**

---

**And it was not clean, and the part of it that was not clean is the part that goes on a shutter.**

They had the Release. They had the bend. They had the hour at both ends and the order in between.

And they did not agree on **Route**, and this is the sixth public disagreement between two named readers on a shutter on the tannery side of Tannery Walk, and it is the first one about a movement that has a number of minutes in it.

She had it single. He had it doubled — a short hook under a long one, which is the ordinary shape of a clearing in this basin and has been for as long as anybody has kept a record of one. **The difference was nine minutes on the hour the reach comes off, and nine minutes is the difference between a flatbed being in the water and a flatbed being on the edge of it with a chain still hooked.**

Both readings stand. Neither has been withdrawn. And they went up on the shutter on the Tuesday morning at about ten past seven, in a fair hand, on a plank two feet by three, painted in three hands, none of them his, because he was not asked to paint and did not offer and had no right to:

*The reading above was wrong. It placed the bend four hours after the drop. The bend is in the third watch. The low ground, the second day of next month, the bend at about half past one. — S. Orin, second reading, the twenty-ninth of Drownmonth, about ten at night.*

And under it, in the same hand:

*The reading above disagreed on a movement. Route, doubled or single. Ours and hers differ by nine minutes on the hour. Both readings stand. Neither has been withdrawn. Nobody must do anything because of this.*

And underneath both, in a smaller hand, which is his:

*Both of the above are correct. The disagreement has not been resolved. This board is not a notice.*

**And a district read a disagreement with a number of minutes in it, and that had never happened in the history of the ninth ward, and it is the first thing anybody anywhere has published that is not a reading at all but the size of a difference between two readers.**

---

**The strain came on at about six o'clock and it was the third time this month and it was the worst of the three.**

There is a metallic taste at the back of the tongue that lasts three days. There is a pressure in the left ear about half a tone above the right which does not go away and has been there since the thirtieth of the Bellweather month and will be there for the rest of his life, and after about an hour and a half of a two-ended read on the Saturday of the twelfth it moved, and it moved the wrong way, and he did not test it and did not tell anybody and has not tested it since.

On Monday it moved again, and it moved further, and about seven o'clock in the evening he noticed that the room had changed.

**That is the whole of how he knows.** Not by measuring. By noticing that a shelf which has been in a certain place relative to a conversation in that room for four months is not in it any more. It is the way a man knows a thing about a room he has been in every working day since the thirtieth of the Bellweather month, and it is the only method he has, and a method like that gets worse the less you use it.

At about half past seven he asked the foreman to repeat a figure.

"Mr. Doff, what was the count in the rack on Sunday."

"Thirty-one. And the book says thirty-one."

"Thank you." He wrote it down, and then he said it again — "Mr. Doff, what was the count in the rack on Sunday" and the foreman said it again, patiently, and Ilse Doff looked at him for a second longer than a foreman looks at a man who has asked a question twice, and then she went back to her lehr log and did not remark on it.

**Nobody in this basin knows it exists. There are four people in this city who could say that a man lost the fine range in one ear in the Bellweather month and there is no one of them who would say it to him, and he has not told one of them, and there is a reason and the reason is that he worked it out about a minute after he first noticed it, standing up with a glass in his hand, in his own rooms, in the Bellweather month, and the argument he arrived at was that a man in his trade cannot afford to test a thing like that every week, because a man who tests it every week stops being able to do the work.**

**And then he noticed himself arriving at the argument, which is the part he does not write down either.**

---

**At about half past ten on the Monday night, with the best part of a day in him and the tower light on and her voice coming down a wire from four miles away, he nearly said a thing.**

It is not in a file and it is not in a minute and it is not on the shutter, and the reason he did not say it is not given either, because he did not have one, and because a man who stops to find a reason for a thing he is about to say has already decided not to say it.

What he said instead, at about a quarter to eleven, with his hand on a plate and nine hours of a held sequence going out of him at both ends, was:

"Miss Orin. Two channels and somebody else's competence at one of them. I have been trying to do that since the spring and the only reason it has taken until the twenty-eighth is that I have been trying to do it with my hands."

"Write that on the shutter," said Sable Orin, "and I will put a fee on it."

"That is the fourth joke you have made to me and I have written down every one of them and I intend to keep them."

"You have not written down one of them. You have written down the fee."

And the sequence went, at about a quarter to midnight, sideways, and both of them felt it go at the same instant, which they agreed afterwards was the correct sign and which is also the only reason it is worth doing it with somebody.

**And then they did the thing that is going to be on a shutter on the Tuesday morning and that is the whole of what the ninth ward gets out of it, which is that they wrote it down before they knew it was true.**

That was the term, and it was her term and not his and she had adopted it off a warden's letter in the middle of January and had said she would be insufferable about it, and it is the reason the board exists and it is the reason there is anything to argue about on the thirty-first of this month:

*The reading goes on a board a warden is welcome to read, free, and I do not pass it to anybody who has to pay for it, and the reading goes up before the claim.*

He had fought that term for four months on the ground that a reading published at the moment of seeing is a reading he has not yet checked, and he had been right about that, and on the Monday night at about a quarter to midnight he wrote it up anyway and by five to twelve he had the sheet in the satchel and had asked the foreman to leave the light on and went down the hill to walk, **because a term is not an argument and he had run out of the other kind.** And the shutter on the tannery side of Tannery Walk was shuttered for the night and its painter was at home, and it went up at ten past seven the next morning with the hour and the date and the place and both names on it, which is the whole of what his third condition has ever asked for.

**And the trace sheet that night is the longest thing he has written in his working life and it is four hundred words and it is on the back of a Director's circular and it says, in the middle, in his own hand: *the reading goes up first. That is her condition and I have stopped arguing with it and I have not stopped believing it is wrong, and both of those are on this sheet, and a form that says both is worth more than a form that says one.***

**And on the Wednesday morning he wrote the rest of it up, in pencil, in his own small hand, on the back of his own copy of a printed sheet with four paragraphs on it, which the foreman had let him take off the pin on the Tuesday, and which had been pinned to that bench since the Sunday morning and copied entire into a foreman's day book at about half past eight on the Sunday morning.**

*Trace, N9 tower local end anchored by S. Orin, this office's working copy of the tower feed, ridge end M.V., not on field duty, not in this office's service. Held 16:10 to 18:30 and 21:40 to 23:00. Nailed twice.*

*It is not a promotion and it is not a rank. It is two channels and somebody else at one end of them. It will not survive being done by one man and I have proved that in public for the third time.*

*Cost: metallic, third day running. Left pressure moved the wrong way again and further than the 12th. Left side quieter afterwards. Not tested. Not told.*

*Nine hours of holding and about eighty minutes of it a room two rooms and a bench where a shelf used to be. Do not test it. That is the argument I have been using for four months and I know now that it is the argument a man tells himself when he does not want to know, and I have written it down, and writing it down is not the same as doing anything about it, and I am not going to do anything about it.*

*Third block of the cuff is full since the thirtieth of the Bellweather month. This is the paper in front of me.*

He signed it. He did not date the fourth line and he did not notice that he had not.
