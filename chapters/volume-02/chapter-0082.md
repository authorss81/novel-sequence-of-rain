# Chapter 82 — The Number On The Board

The tide came up to six foot seven at the low side of the sixth ward at about four minutes past seven on the morning of the thirtieth day of Drownmonth and it stopped, and it went back down, and it never came above six foot nine at any point between the stone berth and the second bollard, and the chain barge lay outside the Nacre bar with nine foot three in the channel and eight and a half on the bar and could not get in and was not going to try, because the man who owns her said in pencil three weeks ago that he would not.

Forty tons of stone did not go on the low side of the sixth ward.

The masons were in the yard at seven, as they were every morning, and were released at seven, and were paid for the morning. The lighters were hired and the hire was paid and they went down the reach at the turn empty, four of them, and a lighterman and two men, and the crew of a stone barge stood on a quay for three hours doing nothing and were paid for the day.

**And at about ten o'clock, with the tide turning and the chain barge still outside the bar, a stone merchant's clerk came down the quay at a fast walk and asked whether there was any water in the channel, and Warrick told him, in about nine words, exactly how much there was and exactly what it was going to do, and the clerk wrote it down and gave it back to him and went away without a word about money, which is what Warrick has said he expected, and it is the only time in thirty-one years that a merchant has taken his tide off him without arguing about it first.**

The sixth ward's quay wall waits a month. There is one more tide before the end of the month and it is the small one and it takes the lighters at half, and Warrick has said in writing that if it is a bad month he will get three days at half and will take that as it comes, and it is a good month and it is a small one, and it will take forty tons in about nine hours instead of six.

**Nothing is refunded. Nothing reopens. Nobody is paid twice for the same morning, and the crew of a stone barge stood on a quay for three hours being paid for it, which is the same money twice and nobody's fault.**

---

**At about eleven o'clock on the same morning, in a counting floor in the west end of the city, the forward board went up, and there was no Bellweather reach line on it again, and this time the space where the line had been was not a nail and a rectangle of paint.**

It was a paragraph and a figure, and the figure was **one and four**, and underneath it, in the firm's own hand and smaller, it said where the firm had got it.

*THE BELLWEATHER REACH IS NOT QUOTED BY THIS FIRM THIS SEASON.*

*This firm has struck this line through twice in one quarter, at one price, in public, under its own refund rule. A market which has struck a line through twice cannot go on selling the thing behind it. We are not going to try.*

*We are therefore obliged to say what we know about the reach. We have a float, a chain, a drum and a staff at the south end of the freight apron, and a glass datum cut and seated by a maintenance hand who is not an officer of the Weather Office and who declared on the docket that he did not anneal it. It is good to a quarter of an inch at an interval, by two people with two chains. It is a level. A level is not a sequence and this firm has said so in public on the 21st.*

*The one figure we hold for this reach that is not our own is the following. It was computed by this firm on the seventeenth of Sleetmonth out of a return of the ninth ward's clearing rota, shelter rota and pump order. Eighteen pages. Furnished to this firm on the second of Sleetmonth, for eleven shillings, by a councillor of the ninth ward, out of a book, in a coat, on a vote taken at four o'clock on a Wednesday.*

*NINTH WARD, BELLWEATHER REACH, FORTY MINUTES: 1/4.*

*This is the cost of six men at the district rate for a day and a gang for the pump and its gear and nothing at all for the timber and nothing at all for the pump, because the timber and the pump are bought and paid. It is not our price and it is not for our object and it is not comparable with our price and we do not compare it. We print it because we have no other figure, and because a market which cannot sell a reach has to admit that a number exists, and the only number in this basin that it owns is one it bought for eleven shillings.*

*We are out of the reach line. — for the firm, M. Hesk, in his own hand.*

---

Edda Tarn wrote it out in the firm's hand and she did not enjoy it, and she had done the printing and the proof and the plates herself, and it went up in three places — the counting floor, and the quotation hall on a plate, and the free district sheet twice a day at a counter in a shop on the tannery side of Tannery Walk, on a press nobody in that building is allowed to inspect.

**And the ninth ward was not in the room and was not told and was not paid anything, and the twelfth person in this city to find out was a woman standing in the rain four streets away about forty minutes later.**

And on the second line of it, under the paragraph, in a smaller hand, which is a counter clerk's hand and is nineteen years old and has never once been asked to do this:

*This figure was furnished to this firm for money by a district that has not been paid it, has not been asked whether it may be printed, and has not been asked whether the firm still holds it. The firm holds it.*

---

Maro Hesk signed it himself, on the plate, in ink, at about a quarter to eleven, and he did it in the counting floor with about twenty people in it, and he did not make a speech, and afterwards a man from the works asked him whether the firm had been *pressured* into it, and he said no, and then he said one more sentence that he had clearly composed some time in the previous fortnight.

"It has not been exposed. Nobody stopped it and it was not exposed. **A market will not be the last party in a basin to admit a number exists, and we had a number, and it was somebody else's, and we had it for eleven shillings, and I would like everybody in this room to sit with how that happened for about four minutes before the board goes up.**"

Then the board went up, and it was the first withdrawal of a product that firm had ever made in public, and the reason was printed in full, and the price of the withdrawn line was not printed at all, because **there was no price. That is a first. A window not opened is refunded at the quoted price and the price is published. A line withdrawn with nothing against it has no price, and the refund rule has nothing to say about it, and the firm wrote a sentence about that on the plate as well, at about two o'clock, when a partner pointed at the hole and asked what was going in it.**

*We withdraw this line. There is no quoted price for it and so there is nothing to refund and nothing to publish. The rule does not reach this. — E. T.*

And Wend Lyle, who was nineteen and who read the board every morning because it was the only thing in the building that changed in a way he could be blamed for not knowing, stood in front of it at about ten past twelve and read all of it twice, and then went and got a sheet of paper and copied the whole paragraph out in his own hand, and dated it, and initialled it, and put it in the middle desk.

"That's the second one this month I have had to copy out entire," he said, to nobody, "and the first one was a circular off the Office on Sunday, four paragraphs, and the partner would not have it on a wall, and so it is in my hand in a drawer, and I have read it twice and I am not sure what I think about it and I would rather I had a copy of a thing I do not have to argue about."

"Would you," said Edda Tarn, from the other end of the floor. "Wend. Come here a minute."

He came.

"You have just made the second uncompellable record this month and the better of the two, and it is in a drawer on a middle desk in a nineteen-year-old's handwriting." She did not smile, and she did not manage not to smile either. "**That is the whole of what I have to teach you this year and I have been trying for nine.** Put it in the file. And then take a sheet and write on it, and pin it over there, what the rule is and what it just did not reach, because a hole in a rule is the only thing anybody in this trade is ever going to be given for free."

---

**And three feet from that paragraph, on the same wall, in the same room, on the same morning, was a printed sheet that nobody in that building had printed.**

It is the Office's weekly sheet. It is printed by the Office on the Office's press, and it goes out to every ward and every district office and every subscriber in the basin, and it is the second week running that it has been pinned to that wall by a tobacconist who sells the Exchange's free sheet four doors away and is not able to read either of them and pins both.

It prints four lines under that heading this week. It printed the same four the week before, and the week before that, and it has printed some of them every week for a season, and the column does not number them and nobody has ever asked it to.

*Claim, Hearthward terraces, the low row, week of the second. Basis: a claim entered by a district. No branch of this office closed.*
*Claim, ninth ward, the reach and the wall, week of the seventeenth. Basis: a claim entered by a district. No branch of this office closed.*
*Window, Bellweather reach, quoted and not opened, the morning of the thirtieth, forty minutes. Basis: a window quoted against an exchange instrument. No claim of this office was made. No branch of this office was closed.*
*Tide, sixth ward, low side, lower than quoted, forty tons of stone not carried, morning of the thirtieth. Basis: a claim entered by a district. No branch of this office was closed.*

**The fourth of those is the first line in a hundred and forty years of that column that is not about weather at all, and the column is still headed CLAIM — DISPLACED TURNS and the column still has no sponsor field and the heading still carries no disclaimer, and the second of those four is the ninth ward's and it does not say the reach, it says what the reach was, and nobody can tell from the sheet which morning is meant.**

Three feet away, on a wall in a public room in a counting floor in the west end of the city, there was a firm's printed paragraph about a reach, a reach, an instrument, a glass datum cut by a maintenance hand, and **a figure of one and four that a district wrote in a fair hand in a book about its own reach in the middle of a Wednesday in the autumn, and the column three feet away says a window on that same reach was quoted on the morning of the thirtieth and was not opened, and it does not say why, and there is no instrument in the Office that could say why, and there never has been one, and a Director has written down in print this week that he will not build one and does not consider the absence a defect in his work.**

Nobody joined them.

**Not because they are unrelated. Because joining them takes one sentence and the sentence takes a reader, and the reader is a censured man on a bench in a shop on the ridge who cannot be put in a sponsor's line and who has said, in about four rooms this quarter, that he cannot hold a trace alone, and who has held one, on Monday, for nine hours, with a woman in a dark tower at the other end of it, and who is not in this building and has not been asked.**

And nobody joined them, and it was not anybody's doing, and the two documents will be pinned three feet apart on that wall for the rest of the season because a tobacconist pins whatever is handed to him.

---

**And there is one more thing on the thirtieth, and it took forty minutes, and it is the only part of the day that is about a person rather than about a price.**

At about eleven o'clock, on the tannery side of Tannery Walk, four doors from a tobacconist's, a woman of thirty-seven stood in front of a shutter two feet by three in the rain and read a paragraph she had not seen before, and a figure.

**NINTH WARD, BELLWEATHER REACH, FORTY MINUTES: 1/4.**

That is her district's figure. That is the figure off page four of a return that her own water steward keeps, that was sold on the second of Sleetmonth for eleven shillings on a vote taken at four o'clock on a Wednesday by the five members of her council while she was on a pump, and that she has never been able to invoice against, and that a solicitor on the Nacre Gate has told her in a room is a list and not evidence of the party.

And it is on a market's board. In a fair hand. With the receipt underneath it saying eleven shillings, and the date, and the name of the district's clerk on the sale, and not one word of consent on the face of it.

She read it four times. She put her hand out and then took it off the shutter, because of the rule, and then she stood there for another minute and a half with her hands in her pockets, and a woman she knows came out of the chapel and stood beside her and did not say anything for a while, and then said:

"That'll be you, then."

"It will be me," said Neris Kade. "In a fair hand, off a book, on a Wednesday, for eleven shillings, and it is on a firm's wall and a shop's counter and a plate in a hall, and there is nobody in this city who can say who decided that, and there is no column in the world that says a figure is only yours if you say so."

"Do you want to go and say so?"

**"No," said Neris Kade. "Not today. Today I have the reach coming off the flat at half past one and at the south end at ten past four, and a ward that has been told its own hours for the first time in its life, and I have got nine men to a flat by nine in the morning and not by six, and I am going to go and write all of it on a shutter and get it to the school before eight, because the children walk up."**

She turned round and went up the lane. And the figure stayed on the wall, and nobody struck it out, and the ward was not paid again, and it was the best thing that had happened to that district in eleven years, and about a third of the people in the basin never heard about it, and the receipt for eleven shillings sat under it in a fair hand with a date on it.

**And on the ground floor of the Council's basin house, in a bound book about two inches thick with a printed head, on a ruled page, on the Wednesday afternoon, there is still exactly one line in it, and it is nineteen words long, and it is the first line anybody in this basin ever wrote about what a reach was worth, and it says that the figure is unknown.**

**And the fourth party has still not been asked.**
