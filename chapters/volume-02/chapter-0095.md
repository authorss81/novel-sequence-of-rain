# Chapter 95 — Dark for Money

The ninth ward tower's signal glass is dark on the eighteenth of Miremonth, and the head above the ward ledger rail in the Weatherglass is dark with it, and the reason is the district's glasswork line and not a hand.

**The glass has not lit since the middle of the Bellweather month. The bubbled temporary in the frame has been in it since the autumn before last. Two objects with two histories, never merged, never swapped, and no chapter in this volume may print a number of months for the dark.**

Gant Ollis sits in the tower with his book open at the fifth column — *Exchange sheet, four times a day, compared with this column* — and twelve agreements in it, and a two-line note to an instrument maker a hundred and forty miles down the hill road with the answer *it is the chain* folded into the back.

He has never looked at the substrate of the plate in the seat and does not look at it. He showed the run sheets and the fifth column to a counter-reader and did not show her the holder, and she did not ask, and neither of them went near the plate. The spare is still boxed on the shelf under the seat, dated *spare*, not fitted, because a spare plate that is fitted has been spent.

**He has never once been asked for anything out of that chair in eleven years, and if it was a record he would have to have somebody come and take it out one day, and then they would be here every season, and then he would be a public officer instead of a man in a tower.**

The lamp is trimmed. The oil is short. The keeper's evening hours are not on anybody's schedule and neither is the nine-inch plate in a coat in a maintenance shop on the ridge, and neither of them is in a book.

Down the hill the district tin is short. The ledger has paid the ward seven and six for the window it gave up, at the Exchange's price, with the ward's one and four refused beside it, and the money has not been released, because a ledger is not a fund and a payment has to be entered and countersigned and carried down in the ordinary post, and the ordinary post takes days.

Neris counts the tin twice. The shillings for the shelter fund are in a different tin. The district book has no column for a shilling a week and no column for seven and six, and she writes nothing in it, because there is nowhere to write it.

**He did not darken the frame and there is no hand in it anywhere. The paid-for pane sits uncollected in a shop forty miles away with a piece of its crown cut out of it and in a coat, and nobody knows. He still has not been up the ninth ward ladder.**

Marek works the bench in the outer room with the lamp Ilse Doff moved onto it in the autumn, and asks her to repeat a count, and she repeats it, and he asks again, and she tells him a second time without remarking on it.

The shop's day book holds Circular 15 entire, copied on the Sunday after it was pinned, the twenty-seventh, with the seal and the date, under glass with the lehr log. The bench sheet, the maintenance report of four hundred and eleven words, the two unfiled tags — all in the drawers, none of them touched, none of them fileable.

**The three uncompellable books — the shed foreman's level book, the tower keeper's tower book, the maintenance foreman's day book — have never been asked for, and nobody asks for any of them today.**

Tovan locks the press at five, counts the trays, puts the key in her pocket with the other key, and walks home past the dark tower. Her box holds three things: the thirtieth's day sheet before the classification, the ninth ward's claim with the seventh box at 10:09, the sixth ward's claim with the seventh box in pencil. Nobody knows how many are in there, including her. The first person to ask her about any of them was not Tovan.

Neris sits at her desk with the seven-line page locked in the drawer and the shelter card with nine names under it, unwritten, unmanageable, and closes the drawer on both, because managing it would mean writing it down, and writing it down would mean being asked about it.

The tower stays dark. It is dark for money, and the money is coming, and it is not here yet, and that is the whole of this chapter.
