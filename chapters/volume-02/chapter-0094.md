# Chapter 94 — Partnership

Kestrel Yards on the seventeenth of Miremonth smells of tar and wet rope, and the boards are up with all five conditions on them in chalk, and the receipt for two shillings under them with the hour and the date on it, and the whole morning shift is in earshot, because a yard is not a room and there is no door to close.

Tarin Senn and Marek Vale stand in front of the boards with the disagreement between them, and the whole morning shift is in earshot, and neither of them lowers her voice for it.

The disagreement is about the shilling. Not the two shillings — those are settled, invoiced on the Friday the twenty-fifth, posted on the Saturday the twenty-sixth, receipted on the Wednesday the thirtieth, chalked and dated and done. The disagreement is about the shilling a week the firm pays the ninth ward for the right to print its own reach hours, and about the threepence a week on the firm's list for the shutter with both readers' names on it, which is thirteen shillings a year and one pound six for two years.

"You sold them the hours," says Marek. "Not you. The ward sold them. Ninety seconds, no vote, no council, and nobody asked either named reader whether the reading might be printed. The board carries my name and hers and the words this board is not a notice, and the ward is not a party to the arrangement at all."

"I know," says Tarin. "I have got opinions about that and this board is not the place for them, and that is not the disagreement."

"What is the disagreement?"

"The disagreement is that you want the yard to refuse the firm's money on the ward's behalf, and I will not." She puts her hand flat on the board, the way she puts it on a hull she is arguing about, in front of everybody. "Two shillings for the use of published figures, at a rate the yard named itself, with the board extract and the hour and the date and a time sheet enclosed. A party may name its own figure. The condition is about the yard's name and not about payment. Both of those are on this board because they are the same board."

"It is the same money," says Marek. "A district is asked for a form with one box on it and answers it in a fair hand and eleven shillings buys the answer twice. The exchange of a good reading for a good price is the easiest transaction in the basin."

"It is," says Tarin Senn. "And a yard which sells dependability sells its crew the right to be unlucky, and I refused a contract that would have fixed a boom cable and a wrist and two carriers' discount, and paid four hundred and six pounds for that, and took the firm's instrument anyway and installed it on my own slip. I know what money does. That is not the disagreement either."

She looks at him, and at the board, and at the yard, and says the thing she has not said in either volume, once, late, in a yard, describing an arrangement between two working people:

"If we keep doing this — the yard bearing its errors in public, a reader putting both readings up before the claim with both names on it — then it is a partnership. Yours and mine. Not the firm's. Not the Office's. A yard and a reader, publishing before anybody asks."

**The word has not been said before. It is said once, in a yard.**

He does not answer it. He stands there with his hands in his pockets and about one pound twelve in his coat and a left ear that is quieter than it was, and looks at the board with the five conditions and the receipt, and does not answer.

**He does not answer it. He stands there with his hands in his pockets and about one pound twelve in his coat and a left ear that is quieter than it was, and looks at the board with the five conditions and the receipt, and does not answer, and the yard goes back to work around them.**

Nora Bragg squares a time sheet under the board and says nothing. Ilva Sorn signs a proviso on the slate about the yard bearing its own errors in public. The morning shift goes back to work.

They stand there for a while afterwards, either side of the board, not touching it, with everything said that can be said in a yard, and the rain coming down, and the disagreement standing, and both readings standing, and neither withdrawn.
