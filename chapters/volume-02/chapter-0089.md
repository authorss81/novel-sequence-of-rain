# Chapter 89 — The Two Thirtieths

Corrin Malle is in the maintenance shop on the ridge on the twelfth day of Miremonth because there is nowhere else in this building where a Deputy can stand and watch a method being staffed without having to sign anything.

He is fifty-four, Deputy for Field Forecasts, managing a method that has been recommended for continued trial, and a firm has built a product around it, and he is beginning to be frightened of what he is staffing. He signs nothing in the shop. He watches the rack count and goes back upstairs to staff it.

Ilse Doff has the kettle on and Circular 15 on the pin, copied entire into the shop's day book on the Sunday after it was pinned, the twenty-seventh, at about half past eight, with the seal and the date, under glass with the lehr log. Three lines about glass that are the only lines in that book in forty years that are not about glass, and a fourth about writing to the ward, struck out with the crossing-out left in.

"Third one's cheap," she says, nodding at the circular, the way she said it on the Sunday. "Closing, not a rule. And the fourth one's about the ninth ward, and the trouble's where it's been since the autumn, which is somebody else decide."

Malle reads the four paragraphs standing up, with his cap in his hand, and says the one true sentence he has said in this volume:

"Published uncertainty costs a yard contracts it cannot afford to lose. That is correct, and it is why a market can sell what an office cannot publish, and I am staffing the office that cannot publish it."

He puts his cap on and goes back upstairs to staff it.

---

The Office's position does not move. Circular 14 is unchanged and Circular 15 does not withdraw it and nothing in it is contradicted.

**Rook is in print, four paragraphs, every one of them true, and the third of them is a closing.**

A clerk pins a notice beside the circulars: the hearing on the fourteenth, two o'clock, basin house, the ninth ward's window, the sixth ward's tide, the temporary ledger. No new instrument. No new form. No new rule beyond the three the volume names: a window is not a claim; a refund is the price of not selling; a claim is not weather and breaches rather than voids.

Marek Vale reads the notice twice off the board, the way he reads everything off a board now, and writes the date down, and asks the foreman to repeat the hour.

Ilse Doff repeats it. He writes it down. Then he asks her to repeat it a second time, and she looks at him for a second longer than a foreman looks at a man who has asked a question twice, and repeats it, and goes back to her lehr log and does not remark on it.

**The hearing loss is permanent, worse on the left since the twenty-eighth, known only by a shelf not being where it was, never the ground of anything.**

He works the rest of the morning from the wrong side of the bench and does not say why.

---

In the afternoon he and Sable Orin hold the trace for an hour to check the Miremonth hours, ridge end to tower end, working copy to strip, her at the local end up the ladder he has still not gone up.

It holds. The bend is where it was: half past one, eleven past four, nine minutes apart on Route, both readings standing, neither withdrawn.

She is better at the order than he is, and says so, and charges him nothing extra inside the week because the retainer covers it, and restates the term: the reading goes on a board a warden is welcome to read, free, and she will not pass it to anybody who has to pay for it.

**Her terms are better than anybody's and are still not consent, and she does not know the shutter is on the firm's list.**

"You do not get to be a private man in the middle of somebody's trace," she says, the way she always says it now, and he nods, because he has run out of the other kind of answer.

They let it go at about six, sideways, the way a held trace goes, both feeling it at the same instant, which they agree afterwards is the correct sign.

On the way down she asks him the second question she asks in every tower, and it is the argument the next volume is built out of: a record of what was written down is not a record of what was available, and who wrote it down, and that person is a person who could be named.

He does not answer it. She writes it in her tower book with the date, because a record is a thing somebody can be asked for, and goes back to the glass.

---

In the evening Garrick Dellow sits in his kitchen with the school exercise book with the sailing ship inside the front cover, forty-one entries of what the clearing cost him after every strip in eleven years, to the penny, and the two sheets of good paper folded inside the back.

He does not send them anywhere. He puts the book back in the box under the bed and the two sheets back on the paint drum under the chain in the shed, and oils the chain, because the chain is a thing he can oil. Nobody has come up the hill road about them.
