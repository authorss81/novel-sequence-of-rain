# Chapter 96 — Lit for Money

The hearing room in the basin house is empty on the nineteenth of Miremonth except for the clerk, and the ledger is open on the trestle, and the rain is coming down the hill outside.

On the trestle there are three things. One is the bound book about two inches thick with a printed head, open at the payment page, with the date against it. The second, beside it, is the Standing Response Register itself, open at a page headed *available responses* with a date against it and nothing under the date. The third, beside that, is a salvage man's schedule on two sheets of ordinary paper, in a fair round hand, priced: forty-one entries to the penny, a hundred and sixty yards on a good forty minutes and a hundred and twenty on a short one, the clearing at 3/-, 3/6 and 4/1, the crew of four and a foreman, the tackle, the two carts, the N end first.

**The temporary compensation ledger expires at the end of the financial year. It is a ledger and not a fund. It pays documented loss only. It has paid one claimant and refused another in the same afternoon.**

On the wall is the returned ledger with the column, and beneath the column, in a clerk's fair hand, a new line for the season that is the first line anybody in this basin has ever entered about a window:

*Window, ninth ward, the reach off at half past one and eleven past four, morning of the second of Miremonth, gang on the flat forty minutes. Basis: a claim entered by a district. No branch of this office was closed.*

It names what closed and does not name who closed it.

**And the word at the head of the column has been changed by a clerk completing a form and nobody costed it.** The returned ledger's ruled form carries two heads, CLAIM and LOSS, and the clerk, finding no claim number for a window because a window is not a claim, completes the form on the LOSS head, striking CLAIM and writing LOSS in a fair hand with the date. The column now reads LOSS — DISPLACED TURNS. Nobody costed the change. Nobody struck it out.

The clerk dates the page, initials it, and leaves the room, because there is nothing else to enter, and the ledger sits open on the trestle with the date and nothing under it and the priced schedule beside it.

---

Down the hill, in the rain, the ninth ward tower's signal glass is lit.

It is lit for the first time since the middle of the Bellweather month. The head above the ward ledger rail is lit with it. The light is a plain lamp light behind a bubbled temporary in a hand-bedded brass surround, and it carries no legible state, and it is not a restoration.

**No pane has been fitted and nothing has been repaired.**

It is lit because a ward paid the keeper's evening hours and the oil, seven and six entered and countersigned and carried down in the ordinary post, out of the payment for the window it gave up at somebody else's price. A pane nobody ever collected sits forty miles away with a piece of its crown cut out of it and in a coat.

It was dark for money yesterday. It is lit for money today.

**Nobody learns why it took so long. Nobody puts a hand in anything.**

Gant Ollis trims the lamp and writes nothing in the tower's book about it, because a lamp is not a record. Marek Vale has still not been up the ladder. Sable Orin counts a shilling on the top step in the morning and does not say thank you. Tovan Iles copies minutes in the west end and is not thanked. Neris Kade stands at the school gate with her hands in her pockets and watches the light from four streets away, and does not go and say so, because the children walk up and the hours are on the shutter and that is enough for today.

The tower burns in the rain with its one colour.
