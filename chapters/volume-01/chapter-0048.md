# Chapter 48 — The Public Dry Line

The public forecast hall of the Veyran Weather Office is on the ground floor of the Weatherglass, down four steps off the yard, and it is a long low room with a barrel roof and eleven lamps on a rail and a row of benches, and it was built in about the eighteen-forty for forty people to stand in front of a glass and be told what the weather was going to do.

There were more than two hundred people in it on the evening of the forty-eighth day of the Bellweather autumn, and there were about forty more in the yard, and the doors were open, because it had never once been hot in this building and it was not hot tonight, it was simply full.

Nobody rang a bell. What happened was that the report on the Bellweather method was laid before a public table on the Monday, the column had been in the public ledger since the Monday, and a reader who had been censured on Sunday and dismissed from field duty, and who was still a maintenance hand in a shop on the ridge, wrote a document and lodged it in the ordinary way — which is that it goes on a table in a hall and a member of the public may read it out.

There is no instrument in this basin for a member of the public to read a document out, and nobody invented one, and it happened anyway, because Anwen Sarr arrived at about twenty past six with the sheet in her hand and asked for the room.

---

The document is nine hundred and forty words and it is the fourth thing Marek Vale has written in six weeks and the first one he has not put in a drawer.

It is headed:

*ACCOUNT OF ONE CLAIM, ENTERED AT THE WARD LEDGER BENCH ON THE TWENTY-NINTH DAY OF THE BELLWEATHER MONTH, UNDER AN AUTHORIZATION OF THE DIRECTOR OF THE TWENTY-SEVENTH. NOT CERTIFIED. By M. Vale, reader, sponsor.*

And then it does, in order, and in the ordinary order of business, and it does not put anything first that is not first:

*Place: the Kestrel corridor, the western lane over the reach, from the point to the yards' mark. Trigger: the Kestrel Line's arrival at the yards' mark, accepted, at 23:11. Effect, as entered: the corridor remains passable to and through 04:00 on the thirtieth. Listed alternative: if the arrival is not accepted, no corridor is claimed, the draw backs at the western end, the return lane closes on the published band, the four carriers' window is not met, and the low wards' floodplain takes the draw for the same hours. Expiry: 04:00 on the thirtieth, on the same line as the effect, at the assesser's instruction of the twenty-eighth. Sponsor: M. Vale, reader. Entered under the Director's authorization of the twenty-seventh. Not certified.*

*What the claim was made of, and how: the corridor was timed off a copper box on a bracket in a yard, held in a hand, published on a board with the hour on it, on four conditions, in chalk, in front of nine people. The Line's reading went up before the entry. The Line is named in this document in this line and not in a footnote.*

*What the claim displaced: the reach-level window on the morning of the thirtieth, forty minutes wide, on the bottom of the Bellweather reach, being the ebb under a long band in the western draw. A claim that holds an hour makes the hour dependable, and a dependable hour is not a variable one, and the sequence does not distinguish between probable and fixed, and a fixed turn has no bend in it, and the bend was the ebb. That is the whole of the mechanism and there is no weather in it.*

*What that cost: a banded stack of shoring timber and a replacement dewatering pump on a pallet, loaded since the sixth, nine crew assigned, and about forty feet of a low flat. It is not rebuilt. The wall it was for is in the reach.*

*And then the sentence that is the reason this document exists and the reason it is worse than what was promised:*

*This office has no instrument which looks at a reach. It has never had one. There is therefore no figure in this office, or in this basin, for what a reach-level window is or was worth to a district, and none can be had. The author of the claim cannot supply one, because the plate behind the reading has no chain behind it: a nine-inch square of sequence glass, cut by the author out of an object belonging to somebody else, run in a seat in a public building for one night and taken out at ten past six in the morning by a keeper who was told to ask nothing, entered in no register, and producing a reading with no registered provenance whatever. The claim stands. Its sponsor can produce nothing. Both sentences are true and neither can be got round.*

It is signed and dated and it has his name at the top of it and there is no reference in it to a document he could not file and no reference to a plate he could not use.

**He read it four times and he cut three things out of it, and all three cuts are on the record because he told Anwen Sarr about them on the Monday afternoon and she wrote them down and asked him to initial it and he did.**

*First cut: nothing. The twelve-year-old date is not in the document. He did not write it, and when asked why, said that a date in a working plate in a poor ward's tower would put that tower and that ward in front of a Council and the ward has four hundred and forty people on its low ground and a public notice glass that cannot be read, and he would not do that, and there is no rule that stops him and he did not want a rule to stop him.*

*Second cut: nothing. The chart margin on the north wall of this building is not in the document. When asked why, he said that his mother's name is on it, and that he has been reading it since the ninth of the month, and that a document read out in a public hall with his name at the top of it is a document that people will look for the rest of, and that is a different job and he is not going to do it in a hall built for forty with two hundred people in it.*

*Third cut: nothing. The sentence about the frame — the brass surround, the temporary, the paid-for pane that was never sent — is not in the document, and he has never said out loud why, and Anwen Sarr has a reason written down in her own hand in a room above a market hall, and the reason is not about a man at all. It is that *a district cannot read its own notice and cannot pay for the one it has paid for, and the day that goes in a document somebody will spend four years looking for a hand, and there is no hand, and the reason there is no hand is that nobody has any money.* That is the whole of it, and he has believed it for about a month, and the person who wrote it down in Hearthward is a woman who has spent six years asking an office for a document and has never once got the one she asked for, and she believes it, and he is not sure and neither is she.*"

That is the first thing and it is the thing Anwen Sarr fought him about for about four minutes, and she lost, and the reason she lost is that she was asked to read it out from the front of the room and she said no to that, and the reason she said no to that is that it is his document and it is his name at the top of it, and she is a district chair and she has spent six years asking an office for a document and she is not going to be the office.

So he stood at the front of a room built for forty, with more than two hundred people in it, and read nine hundred and forty words out loud in the ordinary order of business, once, in about eleven minutes, and there was no performance in it at all, which is why nobody could look at him.

The argument got to the back of the room in pieces, shouted by about nine people repeating what they had been told, and the pieces arrived late and out of order, and by twenty to seven somebody at the back was saying *a plate with no chain behind it* to somebody near the door who did not know what that meant, and somebody near the door said *it means he did it with a bit of glass out of a window frame* — which was wrong, in the way a thing is wrong when nineteen people repeat it in a room, and Marek Vale stood at the front and did not correct it, because the correction is on the paper and the room is not a room that can be corrected into.

---

Anwen Sarr stood up at about a quarter to eight and got to the front, and the room did not stop for her, and she waited, and then she stopped it, and what she said was this and there is a good deal of it in the minute of the public table and none of it in any minute anywhere else.

"I have been given a document," she said. "I asked for one on the ninth day of the month, in a corridor, and I was told it was not ready, and it was not, and I have waited thirty-nine days for it, and I have had it for two of those days.

"It is not what I asked for. I want to say that in this room, in front of two hundred people, and I want to say it about a document with the sponsor's name at the top of it, and I want to say it in the first two minutes so that nobody in the fourth row thinks I have been bought.

"What I asked for was the cost of a claim, with the turns it displaced and the preparations that stopped being available, in a form my assembly can hold.

"What I have been given is the cost of a claim, with the turns it displaced and the preparations that stopped being available, in a form my assembly can hold, and the man who wrote it has put at the end of it, in his own hand, the sentence that he cannot produce anything else and there is no instrument in this city that could have let him.

"That is *more* than I asked for and it is *less* than I asked for, and the difference between those two sentences is about three hundred words, and those three hundred words are the twenty-first of the month.

"Those three hundred words are a claim of mine — a claim I did not make, on a district that is not mine, about two people I have never met, in a street I have never walked down, and there is nobody in this hall who knows their names, because the sheet that said them was never sent anywhere and was never a notice, and there is no box in this basin for a district's own arrangements and there never has been, and the district that wrote it was thanked in four lines in the middle of a claim that was working.

"And I am not going to say their names, and I am not going to say the street, and I am not going to stand here and make a widow of an arrangement. I would like that understood, because in about four months somebody in this basin is going to want to use this hall for that, and I am telling them now in this room that a district chair who has spent six years demanding a document does not get to spend it on a piece of paper she has never seen.

"So I am going to say three things and then I am going to sit down and I am going to let this room do what it has been wanting to do for four months.

"One. **The method is sound.** The Council has said so and I am not going to quarrel with it in a hall, and the corridor held, and the yard is whole, and a mooring hand of nineteen has a bad wrist which the yard published against its own interest in chalk with the hour on it, and if this document is right then this is the best month this basin has had in nine years, and I believe it is right, and I have believed it since the nineteenth of the month in a chamber where a man stood up and told the truth and could not support one syllable of it.

"Two. **The method's success is measured in options removed and it has now printed that in a column on a public table**, and the column has six items and the sixth of them says what a claim closed and does not say who closed it, and the woman who drafted that clause is forty-six years old and she came into that chamber and gave away the one thing she wanted in the whole of her life in exchange for a column, and she did it in one sentence, and I was the one who said the sentence and I did it on purpose and I have written down that I did it on purpose.

"Three." And here her voice went, and only for about two seconds, and two hundred people in a low room with a barrel roof do not miss two seconds. "Three. **There is a man standing at the front of this hall who is the only person in this city who knows what that claim cost, and he has read it out, and there is not one document in the world that can prove a word of it, and every person in this room knows that, including him, and there is nothing whatever any of us can do about it, and that is not a failure of this hall. **That is the hall.** That is what a public record is. It is a record that the public can see and cannot change, and what it has shown tonight is a loss at the Lower Sill with no name on it, and that is the first time in the history of this basin that a loss has been printed where anybody could read it, and I am going to be asked for the rest of my life why it has no name on it, and the answer is in the fourth clause of my standing requirement, and I wrote it, and I would write it again."

And then, at about a quarter to eight, before she sat down, she walked to the table where the document was lying under a weight, and picked it up, and read the last three lines of it out loud from the front of a room built for forty.

"*Turns displaced: a reach-level window on the morning of the thirtieth, forty minutes, not established in any instrument of this office.*"

"That is my clause four," she said. "Word for word, except that the Council's own assessor improved the wording in the drafting, and I have decided that I am going to be ungracious about that for the rest of my life.

"I want the room to hear the whole of it, because there is a thing about that line nobody in this room has understood yet, and I understood it in a records room on a Saturday morning, and I have been waiting a fortnight to say so and I have been waiting because I was the woman who gave the name away in a chamber a fortnight ago in exchange for a column, and I was not going to put a man who argued against himself in the same breath as a thing I had done on purpose.

"He is not mine. I have never claimed him and I am not going to. That is the fourth clause, and it is the only one of the four that has cost me anything."

She sat down. The room did not make a sound for about four seconds and then it made an enormous one, and about nine people were shouting at once, and the clerk of the Ferrygate district was standing at the back with a ledger shut in front of her and had not heard a word of any of it, and the woman from the fourth block had gone up to the trestle table at the left-hand side, put a finger on the sixth item, and not sat down since.

---

By eight o'clock the hall was arguing about the column.

The public ledger was on a trestle table at the left-hand side, open, with the column at the top of the return and six items in it, and there were about thirty people round that table, and the two at the front of it were arguing about the morning of the thirtieth, and neither of them had been in the reach at half past three that morning and neither of them had been in any room at all, and both of them were right.

The younger one was a carrier's underwriter and he had come down from the north on the night coach and he had the yard's framed column memorised and the Office's ledger open in front of him, and he kept pointing at the third item.

"Trigger, line's arrival at the yards' mark, 23:11. That's the trigger. It's on the sheet. And the effect is corridor passable to 04:00. So he held the corridor to four o'clock on a night when the wind was steady, and the wind being steady is *why* the corridor is dependable, and the reason it's dependable is a claim. That's not an opinion. If there'd been no claim there'd have been gusts and a gust takes the lane."

"But that's not the forty minutes."

"Which forty minutes?"

"The forty minutes in the sixth item. That's the one that's got a district in it. Forty minutes on the morning of the thirtieth at the bottom of a reach. That's the *cost*. Trigger and effect is the good news. Item six is the bad news. And item six says nobody in this building has ever looked at a reach, which means nobody in this building can tell you what forty minutes on a reach is worth, which means that if you are an underwriter, you have got a loss of unknown size, on a district that did not complain and could not have complained, in a category that does not exist."

"That is the most frightening thing anybody has said to me in nine years," said the underwriter, "and you have said it about a published column, and I have got a price on that column by Thursday, and it is not a good price, and I am going to have to go and stand in front of a man in a yard on Wednesday and tell him that the yard's own honesty is now the yard's exposure, and he will say *good*."

And the other was Ida Crome of the fourth block, who had been in the queue at the public table on the Monday and had come back on the same bus on the strength of it, and she had the ledger in front of her and a finger on the sixth item and she had been saying the same four words for about ten minutes to anybody who stood near her.

"Forty minutes," she said. "Forty minutes. That's all that is. That's a dry line, that. My side of the river's got a dry line on it and that's what it is, forty minutes, and there's a shed on the south bank with a board on it and a pump on a pallet and four bands on a stack of timber, and the foreman painted it himself, and he put the sixth of the month on it and not the thirtieth, and there is not one name on that board, and I've been down there and looked at it and there's not one name on it.

"Forty minutes is a dry line. That's all a dry line is. And they can't price it and they can't say whose it is and they won't be able to say whose it is in a hundred years, and that's the first time anybody in this city has printed one, and I'd like somebody to write that down somewhere and I don't suppose anybody's going to."

Nobody wrote it down. There is no minute of the public forecast hall of the forty-eighth day.

---

**Above the hall, through the high windows on the north side, there is the ninth ward tower's signal glass, and it is unreadable.**

It has been unreadable since before the weather. There is a half-size bubbled temporary in it with a flaw in it the size of a thumbnail, and around the temporary a permanent brass surround, cut and threaded and bedded by hand in a cold week in the middle of a wet autumn by a maintenance hand in a work coat, and it is very good work, and it is a permanent frame around a temporary, and there is a town of a hundred and forty thousand people on the other side of that reach and four hundred and forty of them on the low ground, and the one piece of good glass that ward was ever going to be given is in a repairman's coat, and the frame is dark because nobody has got any money. The pane the district paid for two years ago and never got is in a glassblower's shop at Kestrel Yards, and the district's glasswork allocation has never once in two years covered a man and a crate sent forty miles, and is not going to for as long as there is a district and a glasswork line. Nobody in this building has ever been told otherwise.

And two rows back from the front, at a copy desk that has been set up against the north wall because there was nowhere else in that hall for a desk to go, Tovan Iles was copying the attendance sheet of a public meeting into the minutes of it, in one hand, without lifting the pen, at about nine o'clock on the Wednesday evening, with the best part of two hundred names on the sheet and about ninety minutes to go.

She is not permitted to keep a copy. That is the rule and it is a rule about minutes and it has never been a rule about anything else and she has never once in five years asked anybody to break it. The original goes up. The minute goes in the tray. The copy stays with her for about four minutes in a fair hand and then goes into the tray on top of the other copies, and she is a copy clerk and she is at a copy desk two rows back from a board in a room where the whole argument of four months is being had in public for the first time.

Her hand did not shake. Her jaw was set, in the way that a jaw is set in a person who is doing about two hundred and forty names in one hand without lifting the pen and is not going to let two hundred and forty people in a barrel-roofed hall see her do it.

There was a thing in her box on the ground floor, in her own hand, dated and timed and initialled, a true copy of the thirtieth's day sheet as it stood before the classification was changed. It is the only object in Veyra that says a corridor was held and a wall was not shored and that the two facts were on the same morning. It is worth nothing, it cannot be produced, no court in this basin would compel it, and there is no category in the Charter of Public Notice under which a copy clerk's private copy of a day sheet is a document anybody can be shown. Nobody in this building knows it exists. It was made on the third of the month and it is still in the box.

She is on a copy desk, and will be for the rest of this year and for nine more years after it. The glass-tender's examination is in the spring, held by somebody else, and she does not sit it. A twenty-seven-year-old woman did the correct thing on the third day of the month and lost a glass-tender's post for it, permanently, on a form whose ground is one line and true, and it is not going to be overturned, and nobody in that building thanked her, and nobody in that hall knew her name, and she did not put it on the sheet.

---

It was a wet evening in a basin that had been wet for a month. It came out of the west at about half past ten and reached the low end of the city six hours after the front of the ninth of the month went over the point above the Bellweather reach.

Six hours is the lag. Six hours is what it has always been, on every rain this basin has ever had, and for nine years that lag was the width of a headwall, and the reach was a bucket with a wall in it and the water stayed on the far side of the wall for the whole of the six hours and then went down the culvert under the apron and out.

The reach took it, because the reach is a bucket with no wall in it now. The lowest four blocks of the ninth ward took it the way a low ground takes an ordinary rain: slowly, about an inch an hour, which on a normal night is a job for two wardens and a barrow and a gutter.

Nell Cray was on the low side of Ferrygate at about eleven with a lamp and a pole. The water was in the channels, the rain gardens were taking it, the low lane was running, the cart marker was under nine inches, and the market was shut for a month with a season's seed stock in it. She got the low side clear before midnight and went home and slept. There is no column anywhere for a woman doing that on a Wednesday night and there never has been.

Sergeant Ampow's wardens were on the low ground of the ninth until about two in the morning, because that is what the ninth ward does, and there were no cartloads and no undercroft and nobody in a doorway, and the second pump house ran all night on its own pump, with the intake screen that had come away on its mountings on the thirtieth and had been lifted out of eight feet of reach water in a flat-bottomed lighter with a lifting boom, at the Line's own expense. It worked. The ward's own water went out of the ward's own drains and the ward slept.

And two thousand feet above the low end of the city, on the corner of Tannery Walk and Sump Lane, the ninth ward tower's signal glass was unreadable, and would be unreadable in the morning, and on the morning after that, and in three years, when a Bellweather of this order comes back to that reach under four years on a water office's own table on a wall at the Weatherglass, and a wall that is a gravel face will be shored a third time out of other timber or it will not be shored, and three years is a Friday and not a prophecy, and a mechanic of sixty-one has four columns of years in four different hands in a sheet in his inside pocket, and a repair that will be reversioned at the year end because the district's works panel schedules and does not cancel.

The lamps at the front of the hall were half out by eleven, because the building was never designed for an argument and never will be, and the man who came in to put them out found forty people still standing at a table with an open ledger on it, and left them alone, because he has been in this building for a good many years and he is a keeper of a sort, and he knows what a room is when it is doing something.

Marek Vale was at the board with the incomplete Bellweather trace pinned to it — nine inches of sequence glass in a wooden frame, with a card beside it in a fair hand that said what it was and what it was not, which is the only card in the history of this basin that describes a piece of evidence by listing everything it is not.

**A nine-inch square of sequence glass.** Cut by the writer of this account out of an object that is not his. It carries a day and a half of the second turn, from the eighth day of the month, and the approach to the collision as that stood at half past five on the evening of the ninth. **It does not carry the collision**, because the collision had not arrived when it was read. **It does not carry the thirtieth**, because the author was carrying it in a coat in a maintenance shop on the thirtieth, and everything anybody in this room is arguing about at eight o'clock tonight is a thing that happened on a reach, and this is not an instrument of a reach. It has no chain. It is here because it is true and because it cannot be used.

Cassian Rook was at the back, and had been at the back for four hours, and had spoken once, for about eleven seconds, to a woman who had asked him whether the Office intended to carry on certifying under a method that could not say what a claim cost, and had said, in a room of two hundred people, that the Office intended to carry on certifying, and that the column was in the public ledger, and that the Director's office had not been asked for an opinion and would not give one, and that anybody who wished to complain had the ordinary course, and that the ordinary course had been improved by three hundred words this week and he was glad of it.

Nobody who heard it thought it was a threat and nobody who heard it thought it was a promise, and Rook had said both sentences in the flattest voice he had and had then gone back to the wall.

**The method survives. The record is public and true and incomplete. A city of a hundred and forty thousand people can now see that something was spent at the Lower Sill on the morning of the thirtieth and cannot see the man who spent it, and the man who spent it is standing at the front of a room, and every person in that room knows it, and no document in the world can say so.**

Anwen Sarr got the hall and not the man, and she said so herself, in front of two hundred people, in the first two minutes.

Marek Vale was not forgiven and did not ask and has never asked, and no sentence ever forgives him, and nobody in this basin is going to tell him the corridor was worth it and nobody is going to tell him it was not, and there is a clerk at the water office's district crew who will come down that road one day a month to read a post with a chain and a staff and will write the window in the right column of a shed's own book and go off up the hill, and he has no idea that the man he is not writing about is standing in a public hall nine miles from the reach he was on.

That is the record of the Bellweather month, and it is honest, and it is the whole of it, and nobody in this city is going to be able to do anything about it.
