# Chapter 44 — The Column

The committee met on the Friday in the technical cell and again on the Saturday in the records room, and in eight days it had met eleven times and had produced a document that is four lines long and is the only institutional invention this basin has made in a year.

Marek Vale was on it. He was on it by the accident of being the only person left in the building who was both a reader and available, and he wrote the rules, and he argued his own name out of them, and he did not tell anybody why, and Anwen Sarr noticed and said nothing and used it four days later.

The three versions were all on the table by the Friday afternoon and they were all defensible and they were nearly three different objects.

The first was the strong one. It named the sponsor.

"Column headed *cost of claim*," said a man from the technical cell who had been in the building for twenty-two years and who had brought a pen out of habit rather than intention, "with the sponsor's name, the claim, the turn displaced, and the district. That is what anybody who reads a *column* expects. A column is a table. A table has names in it."

"That is a list of culprits with a heading," said Sabin Orle. "Call it what it is."

"It is the only version of this that anybody could act on."

"It is," said Sabin Orle. "And it is the one I am going to spend ninety seconds killing, and then we will have the other two, and one of them is going to be right, and I would like to do the ninety seconds now while we are all still in a mood."

---

"Assessor," said the man from the technical cell, "a cost nobody can be compensated for has been argued for in this building for eleven years by the Chair of the Hearthward assembly and by the reader and by the Chair of this Council, and the argument is that a cost you can document is a cost somebody can be compensated for and argued about. And I agree with all three of them, and it cuts the other way, and it cuts it this morning.

"If you name a sponsor, then the sponsor is somebody who can be compensated. And if the sponsor can be compensated then this Council has to have a compensation fund, and a compensation fund has to be administered, and administration has to have a schedule, and the schedule has to have an authority — and the authority is a Council assessor, and the assessor is me, and I have four certified trial claims in a locked drawer and eleven years and I have never had to say the word *compensation* out loud in a room.

"So the honest answer to Mr Vale's argument is that it does not defeat the naming. It **prices** the naming. And that is the thing nobody in this room wants to hear, and it is the thing that happened on the nineteenth, and it is in a report in nine seats' hands, and it says: *it is not accidental that a claim's cost cannot be documented; it is the design.* And the design does not go away because we are all feeling generous about a column."

There was a silence.

"Say the rest of it," said Marek Vale.

"There is no rest of it. That is the rest of it."

---

The second version was the empty one. It is worth ten minutes of the chapter because it is what an institution actually does when it cannot decide, and it is always worse than either of the others and it is always proposed by somebody with good intentions.

"Alternative, if I may," said Lior Venn. "A rule. *Claims certified or authorised under the Charter shall be published.* Place, effect, trigger, expiry. That is what is on every claim sheet already. The Office does it every day. It has a records stamp on it and it goes out with the returns.

"That is a real column, it requires nothing new, it will be honoured, and it will be read by nine seats and three regional seats and it will say exactly what a claim said and not one word about what a claim cost. And the assembly's second sentence — *or the sponsor recording in writing that the cost cannot be made public and why* — is then answered, because the sponsor publishes the cost, on the day, at the point of entry, exactly as asked."

"That satisfies clause four," said Anwen Sarr.

"It satisfies the first sentence of clause four completely, and forever, in about nine words, at no cost to anybody, and it is what the institution will do if we do not do something else first." Venn put the sheet down. "And I want to say the other thing about it, and I have said this once in a corridor in ninety seconds and I am not going to say it again, so I am going to say it here and nowhere else: **a rule that is honoured and a thing that is printed are not the same instrument, and a rule is the cheapest thing any building makes.**

"The fourth line of the Kestrel Line's conditions says *the claim names the Line, in the sponsor's own line and not a footnote.* That is a line of chalk in a yard and it is worth more than a rule, and it is worth more than a rule because you can break it. You can walk into a yard and stand in front of a board and point at it. You cannot walk into the Charter and point at anything."

---

The third version was the honest one and Marek Vale wrote it, and it took him the whole of the Friday night and most of the Saturday morning, and he argued for it against himself, in a room, out loud, correctly, and won.

Here is the argument. He made it to the committee on the second day, at about eleven in the morning, standing up, with a sheet in his hand, and it is the moment of the batch in which the protagonist builds the instrument of his own unprovability and uses it on himself, and it is entirely correct, and he had heard it before, and he had agreed with it before, in a shuttered room, in front of eighty people, and it had cost a district three preparations and a clerk's nine hours, and a woman standing in a foot of water in a ground-floor room on the Quay Steps at twenty-five to six in the morning on the twenty-first of the month.

"Three things, and then I will sit down, and I want them in the minute in my words because they are the only argument I have got and I would like it recorded that I made it and not that it occurred to somebody.

"The column does not name the sponsor. And I am the sponsor of the one claim that is going in the first entry, and I am arguing for that, and nobody in this room is going to think that is modesty, so I would like to say the reason out loud and then never again.

"**A cost anybody can name is a cost somebody can be compensated for and argued about.** A Council that had to compensate districts for every displaced preparation would not certify anything at all. And if it can be put a price on it then it was never a preparation — it was a plan with a budget. And a plan with a budget is not the thing this Council is trying to keep.

"That is the sentence. It is the best sentence anybody in this basin has produced in a year. It is in the report of the twenty-second and it will be in the spring report and it is going to protect this method for forty years.

"And I have agreed with it for six weeks and I want to say what agreeing with it cost, because I am the only person in this room who knows and I am not going to be asked and I am going to volunteer it, and then I am going to argue against my own argument, which is the only way I know to argue.

"It cost a district three preparations. Two of them went out in a courteous letter and one of them went out in silence, and nobody was ever told about the third, so nobody decided about it, so there was nothing to withdraw — and a thing that is nothing to withdraw is a thing that a claim does not take. It just stops being a plan, and the district does not find out, and there is no column anywhere for a district finding out.

"And nobody in this building can prove that, and everybody in this building has now spent three weeks proving that nobody in this building can prove that, and both of those are correct, and the sentence about compensation is the reason.

"So here is the argument for the column, and I am aware that I am using a sentence I disagree with, and I want the minute to say that too.

"That sentence is right about **compensation.** It is also right about something it does not know it is right about, and the something is this: the reason nobody can put a price on a displaced preparation is not that it is priceless. It is that it is **unowned.** Nobody ever told anybody it existed. The moment a preparation is on a schedule with a name against it, it has an owner, and an owner can be compensated and an owner can be invoiced and an owner's loss can be argued about, and a preparation that is an argument is a preparation that a claim can be made to have displaced and answered for.

"And the whole reason a cost cannot be documented is that a piece of paper in a book in a district that nobody asked is not a thing anybody can be shown. Not because it is secret. **Because nobody was ever told.**

"So if this Council prints what a claim closed, in the ordinary language of the district affected, and never prints who closed it — then the loss becomes public, and the district that had it can see that it had it, and a district that can see that it had a thing has got a document, and a document is not a promise, and it is not a person, and it cannot be priced by anybody, and it cannot be invoiced, and it cannot be argued about, and it cannot be bought out of a market.

"And that is the same protection as the sentence. It is the same protection, arrived at from the other end, and it costs this Council nothing whatever and it is the only version of this instrument that I have ever seen which does not put a price on a preparation.

"So I am arguing for the column and against my own name in it, and I am going to sit down, and I am not going to explain that again."

---

Nobody in the room asked him to. He gave a two-minute reason, in plain words, for keeping his own name out of a permanent public record of the one thing in his working life he knows he did, and eleven people in a records room heard it, and not one of them asked him a question, and every one of them heard what the question would have been.

And Anwen Sarr, who was sitting at the end of the table, and who had carried a clause in from Hearthward on the Thursday which said in its third sentence that the cost of a claim may be published as what the claim closed by no means naming who chose it, looked at the back of his head for about four seconds and understood precisely what had happened, and said nothing at all, and went out into the corridor at the recess and stood there on purpose.

---

"Sabin," said Marek Vale, in the corridor, at about twenty past twelve, on the day of the second sitting.

"Mr Vale."

"You are going to say no."

"I am going to say yes to somebody in about ninety seconds and then I am going to be asked why I did not say yes to the other one, and the reason I am going to give is a records reason and it will be correct, and you are going to hear it and it is going to be short, and I am telling you now so that you do not waste your afternoon on a conversation." She turned round. "The Office is not entitled to the sponsor's name in a column it will print."

"That is a good reason."

"It is a records reason, it is the correct one, and it is *mine*, and I have been carrying it since the Saturday, and I would like you to know that I did not arrive at it on my own. I arrived at it from the same place you arrived at yours. **A column with a name in it is a claim on a person. A column without a name in it is a record of a thing.** And this Council is an institution that keeps records of things and has never once in its history kept a record of a person against whom it intended to act. That is not a moral position. It is a *charter* position. It is why a reader's statement goes in the minutes as a statement and not as an objection. It is why the whole of the method works. And if we put your name in a printed column then in four years somebody will use the column, and the column will be the only document in this basin's history that names a person next to a loss, and it will be *ours*, and it will have been made in a room like that one by people who all thought they were being careful.

"I am not going to let this Council print a list of names. I have been in eleven years and I have watched a Council print a list of names once and I will tell you exactly what happened to it, which is nothing, for four years, and then everything."

"Right," said Marek Vale.

"That is the whole of it. Do not thank me, because I have also taken the name out of a column about a thing you did that I know about and that nobody else in this room knows about, and I did that on Saturday, and I am going to do it again, and I have not slept properly about it, and I would like you to go and have your lunch and to stop looking at me like a monument."

---

The rules were drafted on the Saturday and they are as follows, and they are the volume's last institutional invention, and they are ugly and correct and cheap and they took a full afternoon.

*Column to be added to the published ledger, headed **CLAIM — DISPLACED TURNS**, to be entered in the ordinary course in every return and to be read first by any person consulting the ledger for any purpose whatever.*

*Six items, in this order and no others, and a claim that will not fit in six items is not to be entered:*

1. **Place.**
2. **Stated effect, as entered.**
3. **Trigger, with the hour, and the hour at which it was satisfied.**
4. **Expiry, with the hour.**
5. **Condition, in the words on the sheet.** *Where the condition and the expiry are the same line, so state it, in those words, and the reason is that a reader of this column is entitled to know that a claim's authority to direct action did not outlast its stated reason for directing it.*
6. **Turns displaced.** *In this item is set out what the claim closed, in the ordinary language of the district affected, and where the district affected is not known the claimant's own words are to be used and the fact that the district is not known is to be stated. **By no means is the name of the person who closed it to be entered in this column, nor any word or mark from which the person might be identified.***

*And it was ruled, on the motion of the Chair of the Hearthward rain-garden assembly, carried without a division, that no other item is to be added to this column in any return, and that the column is not to be amended by a Deputy, and that any amendment to it requires the same quorum as a claim.*

And the first entry is the thirtieth, and it is entered in exactly those six items, and it reads, in full, as follows — and this is the whole of what the city will ever know:

> **KESTREL CORRIDOR, 29th–30th.**
> **Effect:** corridor passable to 04:00.
> **Trigger:** line's arrival at the yards' mark, 23:11.
> **Expiry:** 04:00, 30th.
> **Condition:** the same. *(Expiry and effect on one line; see note at item 5.)*
> **Turns displaced:** a reach-level window on the morning of the thirtieth, forty minutes, not established in any instrument of this office.

That is the whole of it. It is in a column and it is on a public table and it is true.

It is a corridor that a claim held, and an hour, and a place, and forty minutes on the bottom of a reach that no instrument in this city has ever looked at, and there is a district on the other end of it with four hundred and forty people on its low ground and a first pump house under eight feet of water until the first of the month, and the column says the reach-level window is not established in any instrument of this office, which is the truest sentence anybody has ever printed about a window in this basin, and it is true, and it will be read by nine seats and three regional seats and by four licensed carriers and by a market factor and by every district clerk in the basin who was ever sent a box that did not fit what she had, and it does not have his name on it, and he asked for that, in a corridor, on a Saturday, for reasons he gave once and has never repeated.

---

He read nothing all day on the Saturday and the reason was not a decision.

There was a figure in the file at about half past three — a level, from a district's own return, that somebody had queried, and it needed checking against a plate, and the plate was in the tube in his coat, and there is exactly one plate in this basin that holds it.

He got the tube out of the coat and set the glass on the frame in the records room with a lamp behind it, at about ten past four on a Saturday afternoon in a room with two windows, and he got the lamp up, and he moved it, because the rig is in the corner and the corner is on the right and the frame is on the left, and the lamp has to be behind the plate or it is not behind the plate.

And he stood there for about a minute and a half with the lamp in his right hand at the correct distance and a hand on the frame, and the rig is in the corner and the corner is on the right and he cannot get the lamp behind the plate and get his right hand on the frame at the same time without moving his head round the bench, which takes four seconds, and four seconds is not long, and a thing that takes four seconds is not a thing that stops a man reading a plate.

It takes about eleven minutes to read a plate properly. Lamp up and down the glass twice, then once more with the frame cold, then once with the frame tilted, because a tilt changes the incidence of the film and some things only show you at an angle. He had done that four hundred times. He had done it four hundred times with the lamp behind and the lamp not behind and the tilt, and the *lamp not behind* is the fine end, and the fine end is the part that tells a smear from a dropout, and the fine end on the left is a third of the way to gone.

He put the lamp down, and put the plate back in the tube, and put the tube in the coat, and read the figure off the return instead, in ink, and wrote the ink figure on the file, and the ink figure is the same figure to within an inch, because the return was read off a staff by a district crewman in the ordinary course.

He did not say why. He has never said why. He put the file in the tray and washed his hands and went and got his coat, and the whole of it took about ninety seconds, and there was nobody in the records room and there was no reason in the world for there to have been anybody in the records room, and if there had been, the man would have said that the lamp behind and the lamp not behind were close enough on a district's level, and that a third of the range was not worth a district's inch.

Which is true, and which is the sentence he has used every day since the seventh, and which is going to be true for the rest of his life, and which is the only lie he has left that costs nothing.

---

They sent it to the printer on the Saturday night and it was on the public table at the basin house on the Monday of the forty-sixth day, in the ordinary run, under the ordinary table, between a water office return and a notice of a boundary stone, and nobody rang a bell about it and there was no seal on it and no recommendation on it, and it was set in the ordinary way, and it is the single most consequential object produced by any institution in this basin in the whole of the year.

And that is what the column was for.
