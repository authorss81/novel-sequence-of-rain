# Chapter 47 — A Fragment Of An Index

The chart room is on the way in and there is a frame to do, and that is the whole of the reason he went up.

There were eleven frames on the north wall and nine of them were tower charts and they were all sweating, and the eastern ones had been gummed twice this autumn and the western ones had not, and the last one on the end was the ninth ward's, because the ninth ward was always the last one to be brought up to date and its margins were the dirtiest.

He had a block and a cloth and the block was warm and the room smelled of warm gum and old paper, and he had been doing this room since the sixth day of the month and it is the only work he has ever been completely sure of, and it is not a compliment to him and it never was.

He had done the other ten by the Tuesday. He had been up the ladder of the ninth ward tower eleven times in four years and had not been up it since the sixteenth, and there was a reason for not going up it and the reason was in a locked room a metre and a half above a public door on Tannery Walk, and the reason was that going up that ladder would mean being in that room in daylight with a lamp and a plate and no reason a keeper could not ask about, and a keeper who asked about it would get an answer, and the answer would be that the ninth ward's own feed plate has a complete instrumented Witness movement in its substrate dated twelve years back, arriving in the glass from below rather than out of the air, in the tower's own stamped margin, on the sixth night of the plate's legible run.

So he did the gum work, instead, with his hands, on a frame he could take down.

The margin was the same. That is the first thing. He had seen it on the ninth and he saw it on the forty-seventh and it was the same sheet, in the same frame, with the same brown cut edge and the same clean paper round it, and the trim was old and the sheet was old and the two were not contemporaneous, and that was as much as he had then and it was all he had now.

*CHART PROVENANCE — NINTH WARD TOWER. Sequence reference N9 — / lodged by — A. Vale / SEQ/N9/—*

Between the lodger's name and the sequence reference: a small violet square with a bell struck across it and a watch number beside it. That is a filed mark. A filed mark goes on a claim somebody rang a bell for, and it does not go on an observation, and he had been looking at one of those on a ward ledger bench on the nineteenth of a month in a room with the windows open.

And then the paper ended, and the inch and a half above the printed border was not there, and the cut was clean and brown and forty years old or more.

He put the frame back and the glass in and did the rebate and did the waxed paper behind the sheet against the damp, and then he went and got his appointment card out of his coat, because the archive is open to a reader for one afternoon a week and his was at two o'clock on the Tuesday, and he was forty minutes early, which is a thing he is always forty minutes early for.

---

The Weatherglass archive is under the ridge office and it is a cold room with a deal table down the middle and about nine hundred boxes in flat racks and a lamp over the table on a chain, and a rule on the door that says that a reader may consult the index and the catalogue and may not consult a box without the keeper's hand on it, and a second rule, which is older and is not on the door, that says a reader may not have a pen in the room.

Ione Sere was forty-six and was the keeper and had been the keeper for nineteen years and had a way of standing on the far side of a deal table that made the table seem to be evidence.

"Mr Vale."

"I have an appointment at two. I have come at twenty to two because I did not want to be early and be told I was early."

"You are early and I am going to say so, and you are going to have to sit on that stool for twenty minutes with nothing to do, and I am sorry about it and I am not going to change it." She had a card in her hand. "You have asked for the N9 sequence file."

"Yes."

"Which is a citation request," said Ione Sere. "I want to be precise about the form of that, because the form is the only part of this I have any authority over. A citation is a *reference*. It is a pointer. It is the number under which a thing is filed. Asking for a citation is asking me to tell you what number a thing has, and then you go away, and the box stays where it is.

"Ask me for the file and I will bring you the file. You have not asked me for the file. Everybody asks me for the file."

"Then I have the wrong form," said Marek Vale. "I want the file."

"You have the correct form for the thing you do not yet know you want," said Ione Sere, "and I have watched a great many people come into this room with it, and it is the form of a man who has a name and wants to know what is attached to it, and I am telling you that the number is under the chart margin, in the north wall, in this building, and you have just been standing in front of it."

He had not expected that and he did not show it, and she noticed that he did not show it, and something went across her face that he was not in a position to read.

"N9," he said. "N9 and the sequence reference with the other half of it gone."

"Yes."

"Give me the citation, then."

It took her about four minutes and she did it properly, in the way of a woman who has done this nineteen years: the ward, the tower, the class, the year of lodgement, the accession, and the box, and the shelf, and the sheet.

And then she put her hand flat on a card and said, "There is a second reference on the same sheet, in a different hand, and it is a routing reference, and I am obliged to tell you that routing references are not retrievable and have not been retrievable since I came here."

"Show me."

"I am not permitted to show you a box. There is a sheet of paper inside a box which is inside a rack which is behind a door, and the sheet is the index, and the index is not a document — it is a *finding aid*, and finding aids go in the catalogue and the catalogue is public and the catalogue is upstairs and anybody may read the catalogue this afternoon without asking me for anything at all."

"Then I shall read the catalogue."

"You may," said Ione Sere, "and I will tell you now that I am not going to help you find it and I am not going to stop you finding it, and I would like that distinction entered somewhere by you, because I would like there to be one witness to it and I do not expect it to be me."

---

The catalogue is a book on a table and it is in the hand of about four generations of keepers and it is the single most used object in this building after the benches, and Marek Vale read the N9 entries in about eleven minutes with a finger going down the column.

**N9 — TOWER SEQUENCE, NINTH WARD. Class: sequence, forecast. Lodged: witnessed.**

That is the entry for the chart provenance he had been looking at since the ninth of the month, and it is correct, and it is in the catalogue of the Weatherglass archive, and it has been in the catalogue for nineteen years of this keeper's tenure and rather longer than that.

And four lines below it, under a heading that is simply **WITHDRAWN OR MISSING**, which is a heading the catalogue has for a large number of things and is not a heading anybody reads:

**N9 — RECORD. Lodged as: witnessed forecast sequence. Status: WITHDRAWN OR MISSING.**

There is no shelf. There is no box. There is no accession and no year of withdrawal and no note and no stamp, and a catalogue entry with no shelf under it is an entry that a keeper cannot go and get, and a keeper who has been here nineteen years will have looked at it at least once a year, every year, in the ordinary way, and will have written *see —* next to it about four times and then stopped.

He put his finger on it and left it there for perhaps ten seconds, and Ione Sere, who had come and stood at the other end of the table about six feet away and had not said anything, said:

"That is the whole of the catalogue and I am not going to say anything else about it."

"Was it withdrawn?"

"It says withdrawn **or** missing. The catalogue has said *withdrawn or missing* since before I came and it is the correct form of words, because a keeper who writes *withdrawn* has asserted a fact and a keeper who writes *missing* has asserted a failure, and neither of those is a thing a keeper can assert about a file that was lodged as a witnessed record."

"Is it here?"

"No."

"Will it be?"

"No," said Ione Sere. "That is not a records answer and I am going to give you a real one, because you have come to this room four times in a fortnight on a different business and you have never once asked me for anything and I am going to answer this one properly and then I am going to go back to my desk.

"It is not here. It is not in this building. There are two rooms in this city where a record of that class can be and neither of them is this room, and I am not going to tell you which they are, and I am telling you that I am not going to tell you which they are as a **kindness** and not as a gate, and I would like you to have the distinction.

"Everything else in this catalogue is a thing I will help you find. That entry is a thing I will not help you find and would not find if I could."

She said all of that in a completely level voice and then she went back to her desk, and she did not look at him again for about eleven minutes.

---

He had about an hour and he used it, and the thing he used it for was the thing he had come for and the thing he had told himself for three weeks he was not going to do.

In a routing file the index is the only retrievable part. That is not a mystery and it is in the manual and it is why routing files are what they are: a routing file is a chain, and the chain is the *witness*, and the chain is made of routing entries, and a routing entry is made of a desk and a clerk and a hand and a date, and the boxes themselves are working papers and are not evidence of anything.

And an index of a routing file is not sealed. It is a sheet of paper with a column of references on it, and it is in a box on a shelf, and it comes out once and is looked at.

He asked for it. He was entitled to: a reader may consult the index, and he had a citation, and the citation was on a sheet in a chart margin in this building, and there is no rule anywhere that says a reader may not ask for the index of a routing file, and Ione Sere brought it out herself and put it on the deal table and stood at the other end of the table with her hands behind her back.

It is a sheet about two feet by fifteen, and it is a ruled sheet, and it is ruled in a hand, and it has been ruled the same way for nineteen years because it is a form.

The routing desk reference for that class of file is nine, and a routing entry is four or five words and a date, and there were about sixty of them on that sheet going back further than the retention period.

He found it at about the eleventh one from the bottom, and the reason he found it at the eleventh one from the bottom is that the entries go in date order and the bottom of the sheet is the old end.

And it is a hand-cut gap, about an inch long, on a rule, with a good blade.

He looked at the neighbouring entries for about four minutes and worked out what the two on either side of the gap were and what the whole of the line would have been, and then he put the sheet down and squared it, because he has never in his life moved a piece of paper that belongs to somebody else without putting it back exactly square to the edge, and then he stood at the table and did not pick it up again for about two minutes.

What is left of that line, and it is all that is left of it, and it is two words, an initial and a desk reference in a clerk's hand, is this:

**VASK, O., R-9.**

Nine is a desk. A routing entry is four or five words and a date, and the desk goes in the middle of it, and R-9 is routing desk nine, which exists, and which has men in it, and which has had men in it every year of this keeper's tenure.

That is the whole of the fragment, and it is a name, and it is on a piece of paper in an archive under a ridge, and he looked at it for about a second and a half and has not put a single syllable of it into his own head in a form he could repeat, and will not.

That is not a theory. It is arithmetic and it is the most ordinary fact in the whole of this business, and Marek Vale learned it at twenty-four, in a room that smelled of hot glass, from a man who taught him to read a wet cell: a routing entry is a witness, and a witness cannot be a person who was not there, and the date on a routing entry is the date the thing passed the desk, and if the person named on the entry was already in the ground when the thing passed, then the chain is a fiction, and a chain that is a fiction is not a chain, and a sequence lodged on a chain that is a fiction is not a witnessed claim, and every conclusion anybody ever drew from it is founded on a piece of paper that was not true.

Somebody had taken a routing entry off the end of a rule with a good blade and a steady hand, and had left the desk reference visible, because the desk reference is not checkable; the desk reference is a number that means routing desk nine, and routing desk nine existed, and it exists, and it has men in it.

The part that checks is the clerk's name, because a clerk's name is a person, and a person is a thing you can be shown a burial for.

---

Here is the whole of what Marek Vale established on the afternoon of the forty-seventh day, in a cold room under a ridge office, with no pen, with a keeper six feet away who had refused to help him and had not stopped him, and with a piece of paper in front of him that is the only object in the world that speaks to this:

He could establish three things and three things only.

**One.** The sequence reference *N9 —* was lodged. That is in the catalogue and it is in the chart margin and it is in his mother's hand and it has been there since he was twenty and it is not going anywhere.

**Two.** It was lodged as a **witnessed claim** and not as an observation. Because there is a bell struck across a filed mark beside the signature, and a bell does not get struck for an observation, and he has been looking at one of those since the nineteenth of a month on a ward ledger bench, and a claim gets a bell and an observation gets a rule and a stamp, and those are two different instruments in two different books and one of them has a bench and a brass arm and the other one has a counter and a queue.

**Three.** The citation beside the signature has been removed by hand, with a good blade and a steady hand, and the cut is old and the sheet is old and the two were not contemporaneous.

That is all. He named no document. He named no order. He named no year. He named no person.

---

Ione Sere came back to the table at about ten to three and looked at the sheet and said:

"You have not asked me where the sequence went and I would like it noted that you have not asked me that, because in nineteen years nobody has ever not asked me that."

"Because I do not want to know where the sequence went," said Marek Vale. "I want to know what the cut was. Those are different questions and if I had asked you the first one you would have told me the second one was off the table, and you were right to be going to."

She looked at him for a moment and something in her face did a thing that had nothing to do with records.

"Mr Vale. I am going to say one thing to you and I am going to say it in a records voice and I would like you to be aware, because I have thought about it for about nine minutes while you have been at my table, that I am saying it in a records voice **on purpose**, and I would like you to understand that about it, and I would not like you to remember the other thing and forget that.

"That routing entry is in an old hand. The desk, the name, the hand, the date, the set. That is what a routing entry is, and the hand is the important part of it, because the hand is how you know which generation of this office wrote it.

"And the routing entries in this office from the years before the disconnection were **renumbered**. There was a migration of the routing ledgers — I was not here for it, it was before I came, and it is a thing that everybody in this building who has been here longer than me knows and nobody has ever written down, because it is a filing matter and there was a filing instruction — and after the migration the *numbers* moved and the *desks* moved and the references in the older files do not agree with the references in the ledgers.

"So an old routing entry in an old hand is an old hand. That is all it is. It is not evidence of anything. It is a piece of paper from a filing system that was reorganised, and if you were to take that gap and carry it anywhere, the first person who looked at it would tell you — and they would say it in about nine seconds, and it is my duty to say it now rather than let it be said later by somebody who enjoys saying it."

"Right," said Marek Vale.

"That is the whole of it, and I have said it flatly, and I have said it in a records voice, and I have said it because it is in the manual."

"Yes," he said.

"Do you believe it?"

And that was the moment, and he heard it as a moment, and it was the only moment in this whole business in which anybody ever offered him an easy exit, and it was offered to him by a woman of forty-six who has nineteen years of a cold room under a ridge and a card in her hand, and who said it, as far as anybody in this basin could tell, in a records voice.

"Because if you do not believe it," said Ione Sere, "then I would like to know now, and I would like it to be now, and I would rather be asked by you in this room than be argued with in a corridor by a Deputy in a fortnight over a sheet with a violet square on it."

Marek Vale looked at her for about three seconds.

He had a plate in a tube in his coat forty feet above his head, nine inches square, cut from the crown of a pane the ninth ward had paid for and had never been sent, with a Witness movement in its substrate dated twelve years back, and he had been offered a date from a plate in a locked room in a poor ward's tower and a plate number that exists in no register in the world, and he had been censured on Sunday for an unproved assertion in a public forum and had been asked for a document and had not produced one.

If he had said *no* in that room, at ten to three on the afternoon of the forty-seventh day, to a keeper of archives who had offered him the one sentence that would have taken an inch of paper out of his life for ever —

he had about four seconds to understand that he would have become a man who says *I do not believe you* to a records keeper in a room with a rule in a drawer that he has never seen signed, and that in about a month somebody in this building would have to decide what to do about a reader who has accused the archive of lying, and that the answer would be a form.

"Mr Sere," he said. "I believe you. You have read me a manual and I have read the same manual, and you have said it in a records voice, and I have no evidence whatever to put against it and I have been looking for evidence for six weeks and I have found exactly one piece of paper with a cut on it, and a cut is not a witness."

"Thank you," said Ione Sere.

---

He took nothing.

That is the whole of the four refusals, and each of them cost something different, and he has never been able to do any of them again and has not tried.

**He did not take the sheet.** It is a routing index in a flat rack in a room with a rule on the door, and a sheet taken out of a routing index in a record of that class is the single most ordinary theft in the history of records, and it would be missed in about four years, and by then there would be a form and a form would be a reason.

**He did not transcribe it.** He has a folding rule in his pocket and it is nine inches long and it is the fastest way in the world to copy a sequence trace, and a routing index is not a trace, and the reason he did not transcribe is that a transcription of an index is not a copy of anything — it is a reading, and a reading with no place, no time, no effect and no witness is an unattached observation, and there is a counter upstairs with a queue at it and a rule about it in a small level hand in violet ink, and the rule is right, and Marek Vale has been the man on the wrong side of it since the sixth day of a month.

**He did not take a rubbing, a tracing, a photograph, or a note of the shape of it.** He stood at a deal table for about eleven minutes with a cold lamp over it and he wrote nothing, and the reason he wrote nothing is not virtue and he has never pretended it was: a note is a document, and a document is a claim, and he had been censured two days ago for a claim he could not support, and there is no version of a note of this that does not begin the same machinery again with his name at the top of it.

**And he did not ask for anything in writing.** Not a copy of the citation, not the accession, not the shelf, not the transfer history, not the year of the migration. He took the number that a public catalogue had given him and he left the room with it and he did not put a request in a tray, because a request in a tray is an event in a tray, and somebody has to answer it, and whoever answered it would have had to write the word *withdrawn* or the word *missing* on something with a date on it, and that is a thing that would then exist.

So the room gave him three things and the three things were the whole of what there is.

And he did not say the name on the index out loud in that room, and he did not write it, and he did not say it in the corridor afterwards, and he did not say it on the stairs.

He has it. It is in him in the ordinary way that a thing is in a person, and it is four letters, a comma, an initial and a desk number, and it is in a clerk's hand on a ruled sheet, and it is the only name in this city that anybody alive can be shown and cannot be, and he has not said it out loud once, and if you had asked him in a corridor on the Wednesday he would have told you the same thing he has told everybody all month, which is that there is a chart margin in a frame on the north wall of a building and a gap in it, and that is the whole of what he has, and that a gap is not a witness.

---

Ione Sere locked the room at four o'clock and he walked up the hill with no pen and nothing in his hands and the citation on a card in his coat, and the card says *N9 — TOWER SEQUENCE, NINTH WARD. Class: sequence, forecast. Lodged: witnessed.*

Three facts.

Marek Vale walked up the hill at four o'clock on the Tuesday of the forty-seventh day of a month in which nothing whatever was happening in the sky, past a district office in a poor ward with a chalk board outside it and four women's handwriting on it, and past a tower with a copper tank on it and a bubbled temporary in a brass surround he bedded himself in a cold week, and he did not go up, and he did not have a reason, and he went home and did not read a plate, which he had not done for three days and was not going to do that evening, because the rig is in the corner and the corner is on the right and it takes eleven minutes to read a plate properly and eleven minutes is four hundred seconds and about ninety of them are the fine end, and the fine end on the left is a third of the way to gone, and nobody in this building knows that, and nobody is ever going to know that, and it is not the ground of anything.
