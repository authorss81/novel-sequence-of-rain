# Chapter 27 — The Demand

The Council chamber on the second floor of the basin house has a table the length of the room and no ventilation worth the name, and on the morning of the twenty-seventh day of the Bellweather autumn there were fourteen people at it and three of the twelve seats had nobody in them, and one of the empty ones was Hearthward's, and had been since the eighteenth.

Marek Vale was not on the list of people the Council had asked. He was in the room because Cassian Rook had sent for him at eight in the morning, and the sending had come as a note, and the note had said, in a hand that was not in the technical cell's file and had never been in it before: *Bring the ninth of the month. I have read the annex. Bring the order and not the evidence, because I do not want the evidence and I want the order.*

He had read it four times and had decided, on the tram, that this was either the most insulting thing anybody had ever written to him or the first professional courtesy he had been shown in a month, and that he had no way at all of telling which, and that both of those were the same shape of thing and the shape was the shape of this building.

---

Cassian Rook was fifty-two and had been Director of the Veyran Weather Office for six years and before that had been the emergency warden of Low Spindle for nine, and he had a way of coming into a room that made about a third of the people in it straighten up without deciding to.

He was not a big man. He had a low-ward district's way of standing with his weight slightly forward and both hands on the table, and he was wearing a coat that had been worn on a wet night in a district and not put away.

"Marek Vale," he said. "Thank you for coming. Sit there, I want you beside me and not at the end, because I am going to use you for about eleven minutes and I would like you to be able to interrupt."

"That is an unusual thing to say to a man before you use him."

"It is an unusual thing to say to a man you have decided to use," said Rook. "Most of the men in this building will find out afterwards. You will have noticed by now that this office has a habit of doing the fair thing to you at the moment it stops being useful."

Nobody laughed. Marek, who had not expected the sentence, found that he wanted to.

"Before we start," said Rook. "You said in this chamber on the nineteenth that the method's success is measured in options removed, and that the good method is what is doing it, and that nobody is doing it on purpose. I have read the annex. I believe you. I want to say that in front of this room now, at the top, so that nobody spends the morning defending themselves against it, because you are right and I am not offended and there is no work for anybody to do about it."

"Sir," said Sabin Orle from the far end, in the tone of a woman checking a figure, "the Council has not ruled on the annex."

"The Council will rule on the annex," said Rook, "when the annex is a thing the Council can rule on, and at present it is four lines and a records stamp and a reader's word, and a Council that rules on a reader's word is a Council that has stopped requiring evidence, and I am not going to be the man who recommends that." He straightened a sheet. "I have said the true thing at the top. Everything from here is arithmetic. Are we all ready to be dull?"

---

The arithmetic took about two hours and it was, as he had promised, dull, and Marek watched it happen the way you watch a thing you have read about being built.

Four licensed carriers, a grain factor and two insurers had paid against a corridor figure that the Office had sold on the fifth of the month under a standard secrecy clause. The figure was now short by an hour and a half and the Office's own bulletin had said so. Every one of those four carriers had a claim on the Exchange for the difference, and the Exchange's remedy was the only remedy anybody had, and the Exchange's remedy was an unarguable window in a market where there were not going to be any more windows for a season.

The yards' route captains had converged. Not on a demand — on a turn. Eleven route officers from four yards, and the Kestrel Line's, and two of the independents, and they had all independently arrived at the same afternoon in the same band, and the band was inside the window the Exchange had sold, and that agreement was not collusion; it was four yards doing arithmetic with the same instruments and getting the same answer, and the reason they got the same answer was that the answer was correct.

The low wards wanted the draw to ease. The low wards had said so in a four-line letter, and in a re-filed request, and in two hundred and twenty people on the Hearthward terraces and a market shut with a season's seed stock in it and four hundred and forty on the low ground of the ninth with two inches of water in it that would not go.

And the two of them were the same air.

"That is where we are," said Rook. "I am going to read you the authorization and I am going to read it exactly as it is written, and then I am going to explain what it requires, and then somebody is going to tell me why it cannot be done, and I would rather we all had it in the same words."

He read it.

*The Director's authorization, Bellweather. A single claim shall cover the yard side and the low side. The basin is to be placed under one answer. Any authorization given under this heading is given on the grounds that a basin of a hundred and forty thousand people cannot be instructed to disobey two notices.*

"Two notices," said Ilva Sorn, from the back, "is not what the law says."

"The law says one. I have read the law. I wrote a memorandum in the spring arguing that the law should say two and I was not successful and the reason I was not successful is that four districts in this basin once had a two-answer week in the sixties and I was in it." He put the sheet down. "I am not going to tell this room what I took out of that low ground, and I have not told nine watershed seats and three regional seats, and I have been asked three times. I am going to say one thing about it and then I am going to move on, and the thing is that a Council which issues two answers issues obedience to neither, and I would rather be resented for one answer than answerable for the arithmetic of two."

"Somebody draw it," said Tarin Senn, "because I have never heard it."

"Noted," said Rook, and meant it, and moved on, which was the single most frightening thing he did all morning.

---

The Exchange's window went into the room about twenty past ten and it went in as an admissible instrument, and the mechanism by which it went in was so correct that Marek had to sit with his hands flat.

*The Exchange window of the fifth day is admissible in the corridor decision, four licensed carriers having paid against it.*

Not preferred. Admissible. It sat in the room with a legal standing because four businesses had committed money to a number, and four businesses' money was a fact in the world, and the Office had a duty to a fact in the world. Ivo Slate produced it, courteously, within the terms of his licence, and read out the terms of his licence, and produced the four carrier agreements, and did it all correctly, and none of it was anybody's fault.

"Four payments," Slate said. "All of them on the fifth. All of them against a sealed Office figure. I am required to produce them and I am producing them, and I am required to say that my principal considers the figure understated, and my principal is not a party to this chamber."

"Thank you," said Rook.

---

At eleven o'clock Cassian Rook turned round in his chair and said Marek's name, and eleven people in the room sat up, because everybody in that building knew the minute of the nineteenth.

"Mr Vale. The order of the movements on the ninth of the month. Give it to the room."

And Marek gave it, because it was in three files on the ridge and it was the truth and Rook had asked for the order and not the evidence.

Gather alone and early. Weight not completing, with Break in on top of it, early and hard. Route twice, a short hook beneath a long one. Release beginning and completing. Witness arriving out of order, upon the Break and not upon the Release.

He gave it in ninety seconds, standing at the table, in plain words, to fourteen people, and it was the third time he had said it and by this time he could say it without hearing himself, and the room wrote it down.

"Right," said Rook. "Clerk, that is entered as a reader's attestation of the order only, and the reader is not a sponsor, and the attestation goes in the day file and not in the claim file, and we will not refer to it again this morning." He wrote something himself, in a small hand, on a slip no bigger than a stamp docket, and put it in a drawer on the table, and shut the drawer.

That was the whole of Marek's use. It took four minutes and it was over.

"It is the best reading I have had out of anybody in six years," Rook said, not unkindly. "It does not help me at all. Mr Vale, thank you, you may go or stay, and I would stay, because the rest of this is arithmetic about a basin and there is no reason for you to want to be in a room about a basin."

Marek stayed.

---

Rook asked him the question at about a quarter to twelve, in the middle of a silence, and it was the only question in two hours that was not about a figure.

"Mr Vale. On the nineteenth you told this room that a good claim is measured in options removed, and I have not been able to put that down since, and I am going to use it now, and I would like you to know that I am using it and that I am not asking you to defend it. Take the low wards. If this Council issues one claim and the claim is the corridor — which it must be, because the corridor is what four businesses have paid for and the low wards' floodplain is not — then what is removed is the low wards' floodplain preparations. Now. How many options is that?"

It was not a trap. That was what made it unbearable. It was a man asking a man for a number, in a room, in front of a clerk, because a number was what the Council required.

Marek thought about it properly, which took him about eight seconds and which was the only time all morning that he thought about anything other than how much of the room was watching.

"One," he said.

The room did not make a sound.

"You will want to be careful with that," said a man at the far end.

"It is one," said Marek Vale. "It is not four hundred and forty and it is not two hundred and twenty and it is not nine. It is one thing, and the thing is the ability of a district to keep its own water off its own ground, and every district in this basin has that ability and no district has ever been paid for it, and it is not on a schedule anywhere and it is not on a rota with names on it, and when a claim makes the draw definite then the ability to be clear in your own streets stops being compatible with any plan that was not going to be needed, and the network does not distinguish between an impossible outcome and an unmade preparation."

"Thank you," said Cassian Rook.

He said it the way a man says it when he has been handed exactly what he came in for, and he did not smile, and he did not press it, and he wrote one line on the front of a sheet, and Marek could not see what it was.

"That is the number the Council needs," Rook said, to the room. "Not four hundred and forty. One, uncosted, unowned, and not on anybody's return. Clerk, will you enter that."

"Enter what, sir?"

"*That the residual exposure of a prepared district is a single option and is not a compensable loss.*" Rook capped his pen. "That is a sentence and it is going into the minutes and I want it in the minutes, because in about four years somebody is going to stand in this room and say that the districts had nothing at stake in a claim, and I want it written down now, by me, on a day when I am the one making the claim."

---

"Stop," said Tarin Senn.

She had said nothing for two hours. She had a board's worth of column of differences in a satchel and she had watched a man file a reading in a drawer and had watched a captain get a sentence put in the minutes that would protect the Office for four years, and she had waited, and the whole of the room heard her wait, and the room was not stupid.

"Captain."

"Go on, Director. I am not stopping anything. I want it minuted that the residual exposure of a prepared district is a single option and is not a compensable loss, and that the Kestrel Line's position is that the residual exposure of a prepared district is a single option and is not a compensable loss *on the day*, and that the word on the day is doing the whole of the work in that sentence, and that the day is a variable, and that a variable nobody can price is not a loss, it is an unpriced exposure, and an unpriced exposure in this basin has historically been paid for by the district that had it."

Nobody in that room had ever heard an insurer's word said out loud at a Council table by somebody who was not an insurer, and Hesk, at the back, put his head slightly to one side.

"Ask for the alternative," said Rook, to Marek. "Mr Vale. You are the reader and you are on the roster for nothing and you are, as far as I am concerned, the only man in this building with nothing to protect. Give me the alternative to one answer that does not consist of two answers."

"There isn't one," said Marek.

"That is not an answer, that is a shrug with a hat on."

"Yes," said Marek. "That is exactly what it is, and I would rather say it than build you one."

---

The session closed at half past twelve, and the Bellweather notice went up on the ridge and down to the districts and into four carrier's offices on the same afternoon, and it was the Office's ordinary daily sheet with the body in it and the corridor band in it and the low-ground line in it, and it was entirely true, and it carried a seal, and in the bottom right-hand corner of it, in a small violet square, was the approval stamp of the Director of the Veyran Weather Office.

Cassian Rook's stamp.

Marek walked past the board on his way down to the shop and looked at it for about a second and a half, the way you look at a thing you have been looking for without knowing you were looking for it.

Then he went down the stairs and got his coat and went up to the bench in the shop, and put the private plate out of the tube on the frame, and set the lamp behind it, and read the western corridor turn off the afternoon's reading with his own hands, because a conditional claim needed a trigger and the trigger was a corridor and the corridor was a thing that could be read, and he was the only man in the basin who could read it, and he was not on a roster, and nobody had asked him for it, and he was going to do it anyway.

The corridor held. It was a three-day band and the band was a hand's breadth short at the western end and there was a repeat in it that a man with a copper box could see from a boat and that no plate on the ridge could put in a column with the hours on it.

It was, as always, the only instrument in Veyra capable of contradicting four businesses, and it had a hole in it the shape of a man who could not produce a sheet of paper.

He had about forty minutes of it before the light went off the frame, and then four days of weather, and then a decision that somebody was going to have to sign.
