# Characters

## Marek Vale — protagonist

**Age:** 32  
**Occupation:** Weather researcher, sequence reader, instrument repairer at the Veyran Weather Office  
**Starting location:** The Weatherglass, Veyra

Marek is calm under pressure when he has a working instrument in his hand. He can read a pressure trace, isolate a faulty glass seam, and explain a storm's physical limits to a skeptical airship crew. He is not a natural fighter, a wealthy prodigy, or a man who is secretly chosen to rule the weather. His first advantage is disciplined observation.

### Want

Marek wants to recover the **Ninth Ward Record**, the missing forecast sequence signed by his mother before the Meridian Cut, and prove that the Bellweather Front’s official account was deliberately narrowed rather than an unavoidable natural accident. He also wants the Weather Office to publish the real cost of official forecasts. In practical terms, he wants a system that can save people without making him the only person who knows what is coming.

### Need

Marek must learn that responsibility is not the right to decide alone. He needs to share raw readings, uncertainty, and consequences with the people affected by a claim. His final task is not to become a better solitary predictor; it is to help build a system in which no one can turn private certainty into public obedience.

### Flaw and fear

His flaw is **compulsive precision under moral pressure**. When he is frightened, Marek narrows the problem until it becomes a number, a route, or a repair he can personally control. He hides uncertainty because he believes a frightening truth delivered privately is kinder than an open argument. He also assumes that if he takes responsibility for a forecast, he has the right to determine who bears its cost.

His deepest fear is saying “I knew” after a death, or hearing himself say “I did not know” when the evidence was there and his judgment failed. He is afraid that uncertainty will make him useless to people who need him.

### Skills and limits

- Strong in instrument repair, field observation, atmospheric mathematics, and practical forecasting.
- Can work with laborers, clerks, wardens, and pilots when he admits what he does not know.
- Weak in public authority, emotional delegation, and accepting help that changes his plan.
- Cannot see intent, read minds, or receive a complete vision of the future. He sees order and effect, never motive.
- His first reliable power is reading a short local sequence. He cannot safely redirect a storm at the beginning of the story.

### Personal anchors and habits

Outside the Office, Marek keeps a small workbench in his rented room above a tram repair shop. He eats cold barley soup standing at the bench, repairs bells and weather seals for neighbors rather than charging them, and talks to instruments in plain sentences when he is worried. He does not drink, gamble, or seek dramatic refuges; his vice is overwork. When he cannot sleep, he copies the day’s pressure traces by hand until the lines stop feeling like evidence.

His oldest non-Office friend is **Pell Ardan**, a glassblower and bicycle courier who worked beside him during the rain-maintenance apprenticeships. Pell is not a reader, but he knows which glass seams hold under pressure and which people can be trusted with an unfinished report. Their friendship gives Marek a place where being useful does not require being right. Pell’s blunt question—“Who did you ask to carry this?”—recurs when Marek starts making decisions alone.

Marek and Tarin first met during a Kestrel Line rescue in Hearthward, three years before the opening. Marek had a public forecast that was technically accurate but omitted a rooftop landing option; Tarin ignored it, saved her crew, and then made him explain the omission to a room of angry passengers. Their later trust begins from that disagreement, not from a rescue he performs to win her affection.

### Character arc

Marek moves from “if I can predict it correctly, I can protect them” to “if I share the uncertainty, they can decide together what protection means.” His failures must be decisions, not simple incompetence: he withholds a branch cost, uses a forecast without consent, or tries to carry a burden alone. By the ending he is one steward among many and can no longer operate the sequence alone.

## Tarin Senn — slow-burn romantic partner

**Age:** 30  
**Occupation:** Airship navigator, storm-ward mechanic, and captain of the independent Kestrel Line  
**Starting location:** Kestrel Yards

Tarin distrusts official forecasts because a route notice once sent her crew through a closing front. She knows that a technically correct forecast can still be a betrayal when it is issued by someone who will not share the danger. She runs her ships with crew consent and publishes the limits of her routes, even when that costs her contracts.

### Want and need

Tarin wants to keep the Kestrel Line independent and prove that experienced crews can navigate with local instruments and mutual trust. She needs to accept that institutions can be rebuilt from inside instead of treated as permanent storms. Her deeper need is to stop treating every form of cooperation as a trap and to let someone help without surrendering her judgment.

### Flaw and conflict with Marek

She leaves before repairs are finished and assumes that dependence is the beginning of control. Marek hides risk; Tarin hides exhaustion. Their relationship is a slow argument about who gets to carry a dangerous decision. Attraction is not the first or most important thing they build.

### Independent arc

Tarin converts the Kestrel Line from a single captain’s route into a worker-owned weather cooperative. She remains a demanding partner to Marek and does not give up her career to join the Weather Office. Their final life together is a chosen partnership, not a reward for leaving her identity behind.

## Neris Kade — Storm Warden

**Age:** 37  
**Occupation:** District warden for Low Spindle and the eastern storm drains

Neris is the person who arrives when a forecast’s paper consequence becomes a collapsed culvert or a trapped child. She believes emergency crews need authority to act quickly, but she has watched local officials use “urgent” to avoid asking residents what they are risking. Her independent goal is to make the Wardens answerable to the neighborhoods they protect without making every rescue wait for a committee.

Neris is blunt with Marek and refuses to let him treat a district as a test case. She is the first major ally who supports his practical work and the first to demand that he publish a spent possibility when he knows one exists. She survives the final storm with a permanent shoulder injury and becomes a leading organizer in the Weather Commons.

## Sable Orin — counter-reader and information broker

**Age:** 28  
**Occupation:** Former Weather Office clerk, now an independent reader who holds open readings in a back room above a bathhouse

Sable was trained in records and instruments, but she left after discovering that the Office had classified a real anomaly as a clerical error. She sells readings to people who cannot afford official forecasts. She wants the hidden data opened to everyone and believes secrecy is the Office’s real weapon.

Sable’s flaw is that she treats information as power and releases partial claims without the consent of the people exposed to them. She eventually becomes responsible for public records, but only after learning that disclosure without care is another kind of weather control. She is a rival and occasional ally, never a hidden romantic complication.

## Ione Sere — archivist and former custodian

**Age:** 46  
**Occupation:** Keeper of the Weather Office’s sequence archives

Ione is the first person who recognizes the pattern in Marek’s readings. She knows more about the Meridian Cut than she admits and once signed a report that omitted a dangerous branch. She is not a secret leader of a larger conspiracy; she is a frightened professional who preserved evidence while helping the institution that erased it.

Ione’s goal is to make the original Compact legible before the records decay. Her arc ends with a public confession and testimony. She does not receive a painless absolution, and she does not die merely to remove her difficult knowledge.

## Cassian Rook — central human antagonist

**Age:** 52  
**Occupation:** Director of the Veyran Weather Office; former emergency warden

Rook survived the first days of the Unweathered Year as a rescue captain in Low Spindle. He has spent his life believing that a city cannot afford a committee when a storm is moving. He protects Veyra from real disasters and uses the resulting trust to hide the cost of official forecasts. He is patient, persuasive, and capable of remembering every life saved while treating the lives he spends as an accounting error.

Rook is also the official who signed the Meridian Cut order twelve years before the opening. He believed that severing the Ninth Ward tower would prevent a basin-wide reversal, but he had not disclosed the low-ward branch Anja had identified. When the route failed, he ordered the supporting readings removed so the Council could not be held to a result it could not explain. Ione carried out the erasure, but Rook supplied the authority and later defended it as a maintenance accident. His final choice to release the core lock is not a conversion to Marek’s position. It is a recognition that a claim supported by public evidence is no longer his to enforce alone; he chooses the lesser remaining harm while remaining ideologically committed to central command.

Rook’s external goal is to prevent a basin-wide sequence collapse through the **Last Forecast**, a single binding claim that would close every competing local outcome. His internal flaw is that he has confused his responsibility for the city with ownership of its future. He is the principal opponent from the middle volumes onward and remains the final human antagonist. He is not a hidden god, an alternate personality, or an evil architect from outside the story.

Rook survives the ending. He loses his office, authority, and public claim to moral credibility, and he must live under the accountability system he tried to prevent.

## Lior Venn — records deputy and early obstacle

**Age:** 45  
**Occupation:** Deputy Director for Records and Public Notice

Lior is not a major villain. He suppresses anomalous readings because careers, budgets, and public trust depend on a clean record. He follows the safer path when the Office asks him to, then tries to justify each erased branch as an administrative correction. His early obstruction gives Marek a concrete opponent before the larger political machinery becomes visible.

## Maro Hesk — Cloud Exchange broker

**Age:** 41  
**Occupation:** Weather-window broker and insurer

Maro sells access to narrow forecasts and turns uncertainty into a price. He is greedy, but he also understands that an unreliable public forecast can ruin small businesses. His schemes expose how private markets profit from public weather dependency. He is pressured, exposed, and eventually forced to accept compensation and disclosure rules; he is not the final enemy.

## Oren Vale — Marek’s father

**Age:** 61  
**Occupation:** Retired culvert and pump mechanic in Low Spindle

Oren is blunt, tired, and protective of his son in ways that often feel like refusal. He blamed Marek for leaving Low Spindle for the Office and has never forgiven Anja for what he believes she did during the Meridian Cut. He wants Marek to stop treating the family as evidence. Their late reconciliation is partial: Oren accepts that Anja made a choice, but he does not pretend the loss no longer hurts.

## Anja Vale — Marek’s mother and the central historical witness

**Age:** At disappearance: 38  
**Occupation:** Sequence cartographer, formerly of the Weather Office

Anja was the first researcher to argue that a forecast’s sacrificed branch was a real civic possibility rather than a statistical nuisance. She disappeared during the Meridian Cut while trying to preserve a low-ward evacuation route. Her final records show that she deliberately closed one branch to stop a larger collapse and then failed to save the other route she had hoped to keep. She is dead; she is not resurrected or replaced by a secret surviving personality. Her legacy is a method, a warning, and the moral question Marek must answer.

## Relationship progression

- **Volume 1:** Marek and Tarin are forced to cooperate during the Bellweather Front. She distrusts his certainty; he distrusts her refusal of official data. They respect each other’s competence before they become close.
- **Volume 3:** Tarin makes him share a spent branch with a crew that has to live with it. This is the first real trust milestone.
- **Volume 6:** Their professional bond becomes vulnerable to a political marriage between public forecasts and private routes. They separate for a volume without disappearing from each other’s decisions.
- **Volume 7:** Marek hides the cost of an unauthorized intervention. The breach damages more than his career; it costs Tarin’s crew a safe route and tests whether she can still trust his judgment.
- **Volume 8:** He repairs the breach by publishing uncertainty before acting. They choose a shared work and private life, slowly and explicitly.
- **Volumes 10–12:** Their relationship is tested by different roles during the Last Forecast. Neither becomes the other’s magical solution. In the ending they share a home and workshop while keeping separate careers, finances, public responsibilities, and opinions.

The relationship is the only romantic relationship in the series. Other intimacy comes through friendship, family, professional respect, and civic trust.
