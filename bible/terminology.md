# Terminology

Use these terms consistently in prose and outlines. The vocabulary should sound like the work of weather researchers, wardens, clerks, pilots, and local assemblies, not like a fantasy game interface.

## Places and institutions

- **Veyra** — the rainward city-state where the series takes place.
- **the Sill** — the basin and floodplain around Veyra.
- **the Lower Sill** — the southern floodplain and drainage basin; the Lower Sill culvert is a concrete Bellweather-era landmark.
- **Lake Orra** — the western lake and the center of airship transport.
- **the Nacre Range** — the northern mountain boundary.
- **the Ninefold Basin** — the regional basin containing Veyra and its neighboring polities; the weather system is larger than the city.
- **Morrow Fen** — northern peat and reed country; dependent on basin rain capture.
- **Nacre Holds** — mountain settlements that supply glass, copper, and ore; distinct from the Nacre Range.
- **Red Sill** — southern fruit-and-olive farms on the basin’s dry split.
- **Ternway** — western airship passages and route markets built around fast pressure turns.
- **The Weatherglass** — the Weather Office campus and public forecast hall on the eastern ridge.
- **Low Spindle** — low industrial and residential district, vulnerable to floods and unreliable public forecasts.
- **Kestrel Yards** — western airship docks and independent route workshops.
- **Hearthward** — terraced residential, market, and rooftop-farming district.
- **Crown Basin** — dry upper basin containing the sealed upper chambers of the Meridian.
- **the Nine Ward Towers** — one tower for each of the original nine watersheds; they connect local readings to the Meridian.
- **ward** — one of the original nine watersheds of Veyra, and by extension its tower, its assembly, and its seat on the Weather Council. A ward is the unit of responsibility and of representation. It is not a district in any other sense, and the nine wards do not match the city’s neighborhoods.
- **signal glass** — the colored pane in a ward tower that shows a district the state of its published notice: clear for ordinary, amber for disputed, red for closed route, dark for voided. It is the ordinary citizen’s interface with the weather office, and it lies as readily as a printed notice does.
- **Veyran Weather Office** — the official forecasting service. “Weather Office” is the ordinary public term.
- **Weather Council** — the regional body that sets binding-weather policy and maintains the Meridian’s central mandate; it is distinct from the Weather Office, though the two overlap in personnel and authority. Its twelve votes are nine Veyran watershed seats and three rotating regional seats. A basin-wide claim requires eight votes, a named sponsor, and an expiry; an emergency mandate can be withdrawn by the same quorum, including at least one regional seat.
- **Storm Wardens** — field crews that maintain towers, drainage, rescue routes, and emergency closures.
- **Cloud Exchange** — private market for forecast windows, airship passage, rain credit, and weather insurance.
- **local assembly** — a neighborhood or watershed body with authority over local notices, shelters, and emergency plans.
- **the Quiet Ledger** — a dispersed preservation network for suppressed forecast records; not a single secret order.

## Weather and history

- **the Meridian** — the buried civic weather engine beneath Crown Basin. “First Weather Engine” is the formal historical name.
- **the core lock** — the mechanism at Crown Basin that recognizes a single approved claim and routes the basin’s sequence through it. It is the physical form of the locked grammar: whatever the lock recognizes as the only stable route becomes the route. It is also the one place a director can overrule a recognized Council quorum with a physical key, and doing so produces a counterturn rather than a clean authorization.
- **Bellweather Front** — the storm at the center of the opening arc, known for a rare collision of sequence movements.
- **the Unweathered Year** — the eleven-day rain failure that preceded the Meridian’s construction.
- **the First Compact** — the original civic agreement to share readings and show competing weather turns.
- **the Meridian Cut** — the failed attempt twelve years before the opening to disconnect a failing ward tower.
- **Ninth Ward Record** — Anja Vale’s missing sequence record and the central historical document Marek seeks; it contains a preserved sequence witness and evidence of a route that was physically sealed and erased from public record, not a secret prophecy.
- **the White Noon** — a catastrophic atmospheric state in which the movements fail to release rain and the sky becomes bright, dry, and falsely calm. It is a physical failure, not a separate entity.
- **White Noon Protocol** — the named emergency procedure by which the Director proposes the Last Forecast. The protocol is lawful. That is the point: the worst act in the series is committed through a valid process.
- **Last Forecast** — Rook’s proposed single basin-wide binding claim, intended to stop the White Noon by closing the compound branch of coordinated local alternatives.
- **forecast debt** — the public or hidden cost left behind when a binding forecast shifts a branch, including unpaid compensation and unresolved repairs.
- **Weather Charter** — the interim civic rules for evidence, alternatives, appeals, consent, expiry, and compensation; the institutional predecessor to the Weather Commons. It is interim, patchy, and hated by people who want a single answer.
- **Weather Commons** — the final network of nine watershed forecast cells, with local and regional seats; no permanent single-answer authority.
- **forecast cell** — one local unit of shared forecasting, built around a single ward tower. A cell can hold several conditional claims at once and is required to show its disagreements.
- **No-Forecast Assembly** — a Hearthward movement that rejects all binding claims and accepts the practical risks of leaving weather unwritten.
- **weather window** — a period in which a route, market, or field is considered safe enough to operate.
- **cycle** — one six-hour local watch period. It is the working unit of weather duty, staffing, and expiry. A private binding that affects a public route, water system, hospital, school, or market must be entered in the emergency ledger within one cycle.
- **emergency ledger** — the mandatory short record of a binding that touches public life, whether or not the binder holds an office. Failure to enter it is not a magical violation; it is a civic and legal one, and it is what allows an unknown binder to be investigated at all.
- **expiry** — the published end of a claim’s authority to direct action. An expiry never reopens a spent branch; it only ends the claim’s power to send people.
- **counter-memory** — used in the historical sense for the record left by the Meridian Cut, and in the ordinary sense for the private unease residents feel around sealed streets and gaps in the archive.

## Anja Vale’s two documents

These are separate artifacts and must never be merged in prose.

- **Ninth Ward Record** — Anja Vale’s official signed forecast sequence for the Ninth Ward, lodged with the Weather Office before the Meridian Cut. It contains a preserved sequence witness and the evidence that a route was physically sealed and deleted from the public record rather than spent. It is missing at the opening of the series and is recovered in Volume 11.
- **Crown Basin sheet** — Anja’s last working readings, written by hand inside the sealed First Compact chamber during the Cut. It records the branch she deliberately closed to keep the collapse out of Low Spindle, the route she failed to save, and a reference to Rook’s signed order. It is found in Volume 12. It contains no formula, no secret technique, and no prophecy.
- **Ninth Ward corridor** — the maintenance passage and service route that Anja tried to preserve. It was physically sealed, marked unsafe, and administratively deleted. Its loss is a lie of record, not a spend, which is why the ending can confront it without violating the rule that spent branches never reopen.
- **maintenance seal** — the physical closure applied to the corridor. A seal can be broken by a person with a key; a spend cannot be undone by anyone.

## Sequence language

- **sequence** — the ordered movement of atmospheric forces through an atmospheric spell.
- **movement** — one of the six stages of a sequence.
- **turn** — a single change in a sequence. A turn can be observed, routed, or closed.
- **branch** — a real possibility still available under current conditions. Branches belong to this world; they are not separate timelines.
- **read** — perceive the likely order or available branches without making a claim.
- **binding** — the act of turning a precise observation into a claim someone can act on. Binding is the point at which a spend can occur; a read alone does not spend.
- **claim** — a forecast with a specific place, time, and effect.
- **spend** — the closure of one available branch when a claim is bound.
- **compound branch** — a linked set of alternatives that a basin-wide claim treats as one coordinated possibility, such as several districts’ local evacuation routes.
- **reserve** — the Meridian’s live record of currently available compound branches, not stored power and not a complete future.
- **spent turn** — the now-impossible possibility closed by a spend.
- **closed door** — a human description of a spent branch, especially when a route, plan, or choice can no longer be made.
- **dry line** — the physical or social trace left where a spent branch mattered. It is evidence of absence, not a recoverable alternate world.
- **counter-memory** — a vague memory, missing plan, or contradictory record left by a spend. Different people experience it differently.
- **counterturn** — a harmful rebound when a false claim, a broken movement, or conflicting binding destabilizes a sequence.
- **conditional forecast** — a claim that states what will happen if named conditions are met. It preserves more choices than an unconditional forecast but becomes binding when the condition is accepted and triggered.
- **locked grammar** — a legal and technical configuration in which the central lock treats one approved claim as the only route through a sequence. It is not a sentient spell or a separate language; it is the old system’s centralized rule made operational.
- **veil** — a deliberate, maintained barrier against a forecast binding. A veil is safer in some circumstances but cannot be used indefinitely without instrument work and local attention.
- **witness** — a person or instrument that accepts and records a claim. Witnessing is part of the spell, not a passive observation.
- **anchor** — a physical instrument, ward, person, or structure that gives a sequence a stable local path.
- **Six Movements** — Gather, Weight, Break, Route, Release, Witness.
- **sequence glass** — an instrument that displays the Six Movements as shifting traces in glass, vapor, and static.
- **open forecast** — the final political term for a network of local, conditional claims that displays disagreement instead of silently choosing one outcome.
- **first-use examples:** a *read* is “the eastern glass is warming before the instruments agree”; a *binding* is Tovan writing Marek’s claim into the bellweather ledger and the clerk’s bell ringing for the district; a *conditional forecast* is “if the lower gate remains open at dawn, the market can move its stores”; a *veil* is a maintained instrument barrier that keeps an unchosen turn visible but unbound; an *anchor* is the storm glass, tower, or witness that gives a claim a local path; *forecast debt* is the unpaid repair, compensation, or lost option left after a binding; a *cycle* is the six-hour bellweather watch period, and a *counter-memory* is a Low Spindle resident saying the footbridge “was always shut in autumn” with no memory of when that began.

## Weather roles

- **Listener** — a person who senses a pressure change but cannot yet bind a claim.
- **Reader** — a trained person who can read a local sequence and make a short binding forecast.
- **Tracer** — a reader who follows a sequence across connected towers or stations.
- **Conductor** — a person who redirects one movement of an active sequence.
- **Chorus** — a cooperating group of readers and local witnesses that issues a shared conditional forecast.
- **Forecast Warden** — a civic role that maintains a local forecast cell and answers to the people it affects; use the full title when it could be confused with field service.
- **Storm Warden** — a rescue and repair worker in the citywide field service; use this term for crews, not for the final forecast-power stage.
- **counter-reader** — an unlicensed or semi-public reader who works outside the Weather Office; Sable Orin is the principal example.
- **route captain** — the crew leader responsible for an airship’s local decisions; Tarin Senn’s title.
- **dry-line witness** — a person or community that documents a spend, its absence, and the repair still owed.

## Language rules in the prose

- Prefer “forecast,” “claim,” “sequence,” “branch,” “spend,” “Storm Warden,” and “Forecast Warden” over vague terms such as “energy,” “level,” or “system” when the scene is about weather.
- Do not call a branch a parallel universe, a save file, or a time line. It is an unspent possibility in the same world.
- Do not use exact percentages or a visible skill tree. Uncertainty is described through competing turns, windows, observed traces, and practical limits.
- Treat the sequence-glass notice as the story’s only recurring interface. It is not a separate sentient or game-like System; when it appears, keep it to one short panel and let the character act rather than obey it.
- Distinguish a **read** from a **binding**. A character can notice a dangerous order without spending anything simply by thinking about it.
- Use “spent possibility” in emotional or civic discussion; “spent turn” is the Weather Office’s colder technical term.
- Call a consequence that cannot be restored a “dry line” only when it is a visible trace. A person’s grief is not automatically evidence of a branch.
- Keep the weather physical. A spell may be astonishing, but it still has pressure, moisture, heat, charge, glass, metal, water, and a route through the city.
