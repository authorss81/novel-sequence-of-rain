# Power System

## Core rule

Atmospheric spells are not a separate language from weather. They are movements of pressure, moisture, charge, heat, and attention through the same air that people live in. A spell works by putting those forces into an ordered sequence. A weather reader sees the order; a weather conductor can change one movement of an active sequence.

The power is not a promise of perfect foresight. It is a method for making a limited claim against a changing system. A free read has no branch cost. A binding claim has a real cost, even when the claim is accurate.

### The observable mechanism

The Meridian’s **Witness** movement turns a claim into a local constraint. When a claim is precise enough to be acted upon, the network strengthens the conditions that support it and displaces the nearest still-possible turn that would contradict it. The displaced turn is a branch, not a duplicate world. The choice is mechanical rather than moral: the network follows the strongest pressure, anchor, and accepted route, not the outcome with the fewest human casualties.

This is why a forecast can be accurate and still be dangerous. The claim may increase the odds of the stated result while making the most direct alternative unavailable. A branch can close a repair, a road, a message, a harvest, a rescue window, or a person’s decision. It is not enough to know that a possibility vanished; the story must ask who had a right to spend it.

### Binding threshold

A claim becomes binding when all four parts are present:

1. a place or bounded route;
2. a time or trigger;
3. a stated weather effect;
4. a witness—an official ledger, sequence glass, authorized person, or action taken in reliance on it.

Writing, speaking, entering, or acting on the claim can serve as the witness. A vague impression does not bind merely because the reader feels it strongly. A claim can be privately bound, but private binding still changes the weather. Public authorization, consent, disclosure, and compensation are civic requirements, not additional magical switches.

A binding is successful when the claimed route remains coherent and the counterturn does not overwhelm it. A false binding is not harmless: it spends the branch it displaced, then rebounds as a counterturn that may reverse direction, open a gap, or move the effect somewhere else. Repeating an already-bound claim does not spend a second branch, but a new claim that contradicts it can create a counterturn.

The smallest practical example is: “The Lower Sill culvert will fail before 02:10.” Binding it displaces the available branch in which a repair convoy reaches the culvert in time. The spend is not the building’s failure; it is the repair’s possibility. If the claim is wrong, the convoy is still lost and the damaged culvert may fail elsewhere or later.

### Legal and magical authority

Magic does not know whether a government is right. A public mandate, local assembly, contract, or court order can authorize a claim, but it cannot make a false claim safe. Consent, disclosure, appeal, and compensation determine whether the binding is legitimate and whether the institution remains trusted. An official expiry ends a claim’s authority to direct action; it does not reopen a branch already spent. A new claim must either preserve the old conditions or risk a counterturn.

The Charter’s public rules govern **publicly authorized bindings**. A private person can still bind a claim without permission, but the result is not exempt: any claim that affects a public route, water system, hospital, school, or market must be entered in an emergency ledger within one cycle, defined as one six-hour local watch period. The author must be identified when possible; if not, the affected assembly can demand an investigation, independent witness, and compensation review. Known alternatives must be listed before a public claim. The exact branch the sequence spends is often unknowable, so no institution may pretend that a tidy disclosure equals certainty.

## The Six Movements

Every atmospheric spell, from a ward tower to a rainmaking working, passes through six movements when it is stable. The movements are not six separate spells. A working may skip, repeat, or reverse one, but the consequences become dangerous.

| Movement | Physical work | What fails when it is disturbed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gather** | Draws moisture, charge, pressure, or heat from the surrounding air and instruments. | The spell draws too much from a person, pipe, roof, or field. |
| **Weight** | Establishes force, scale, direction, and the limit of what the working can affect. | The effect is too large, too weak, or attached to the wrong place. |
| **Break** | Crosses the threshold where stored weather changes state: cloud, rain, wind, charge, or visibility. | A sudden counterturn, inverted weather, or localized disaster. |
| **Route** | Sends the changed effect through a chosen path, such as a wind corridor or drainage line. | The effect travels unpredictably or strikes an unintended person or structure. |
| **Release** | Makes the result visible and physically consequential. | A false release, a dry line, or an effect that remains trapped in the sequence. |
| **Witness** | Fixes the result into local attention, record, infrastructure, and memory. | The spell cannot stabilize, or it fixes a result in the wrong place. |

A forecast reads the likely order of these movements. It does not reveal why a spell was made or who benefits from it. A sequence reader can see that a rain line will turn east; they cannot see the hidden intention of the person who activated it.

## Branches and spending

A sequence contains one current arrangement and several **branches**: possibilities that are still realizable under the present weather, resources, and human choices. A branch is not a ghost world or a cinematic alternate timeline. It is a real option that could have occurred in this world, such as a repair arriving in time, a road remaining open, a message being sent, a harvest surviving, or a person making a different decision.

A **binding forecast** is a precise claim with a place, a time, and an effect:

> “Before 02:10, the lower culvert in Low Spindle will fail.”

To make the claim binding, the sequence closes the currently available branch that most directly contradicts it. That closed branch is the **spend**. The closest contradiction is chosen by physical sequence pressure, not by moral value. A forecast can spend an ordinary maintenance delay, a rare personal choice, or an entire route. The system does not charge according to who dies; it closes the option that the claim displaces.

The cost is not a percentage reduction and not a wish. It is a real possibility becoming impossible. Once a branch is spent, no later forecast can reopen it. People may retain a vague counter-memory, a missing plan, or an argument about what might have been, but the possibility itself is gone.

### When a spend happens

A reader may privately observe a sequence without spending anything. The spend begins when the reader **binds** the observation into a claim that can be relied upon or enacted:

- writing the exact claim in an official ledger;
- speaking it to a person who will act on it;
- using it to activate a spell, route permit, evacuation, or market order;
- attaching a public forecast mark to a district notice.

A vague observation such as “the pressure feels wrong” is not a binding forecast. A private exact prediction that is acted upon is. Repeated publication of the same claim does not spend another branch. A new contradictory claim spends its own branch only when it meets the binding threshold; even then it also creates a **counterturn** because it conflicts with an established witness.

### Scope and weight

A forecast’s practical weight increases when more people rely on it, when it covers a longer period, or when it redirects infrastructure. A basin-wide claim can close one **compound branch**—a coordinated set of linked alternatives that would all contradict the same central outcome—so the spend is still one possibility, but that possibility may contain many individual choices. Weight does not make the system choose a more valuable branch. It makes a failure more dangerous, increases strain on the reader, and makes a false claim produce a larger counterturn.

A small forecast can still be morally devastating. “The north gate will close at dawn” may spend the possibility of a child leaving through it. A broad forecast may be statistically reliable and still erase a community’s only remaining choice.

A **compound branch** is a set of linked possibilities that would all contradict the same basin-wide outcome: a ward’s independent warning, a family’s chosen evacuation, a crew’s alternate landing, and a local repair that keeps a shelter open. They are separate choices, but the central sequence sees them as one coordinated alternative because the same pressure turn supports all of them. Closing a compound branch does not cause every affected person to disappear; it removes the shared route through which those choices could have remained compatible.

The Meridian’s hidden **reserve** is a live record of currently available compound branches, not a supply of stored power and not a complete list of every future. It updates as local readings, contracts, and actions change the basin. Rook’s Last Forecast attempts to bind the single compound branch that contains every coordinated alternative to the official White Noon response. That is why the reserve count matters: it measures options, not power.

### Failure and recovery

- A **false binding** still spends the branch it closed. If the claim does not become true, the sequence rebounds as a counterturn. The rebound may reverse direction, displace the effect, or open a dangerous gap around the original claim.
- A **broken sequence** can be repaired by a conductor, but the repair creates a visible environmental or physical scar. The same effect cannot simply be erased.
- A **dry line** is the trace left where a spent branch mattered. It may appear as an impossible route, a missing record, a changed habit, a failed crop, or a person arriving with no explanation for why they came. Counter-memory is evidence, not proof that every absent possibility is recoverable.
- Rest reduces strain but never refunds a spend. Repeated binding can leave a reader unable to distinguish ordinary intuition from the pressure of a possible branch.
- Short-term strain has a recovery path: lower the claim’s scope, sleep, repair the anchor, and let another trained reader witness the return to ordinary observation. A person who ignores the symptoms can bind a claim they no longer understand, and a failed counterturn can damage an instrument even when the weather itself survives.

Cumulative damage is shown through attention, hearing, sleep, and manual skill rather than a hidden meter. Marek’s final sequence burn does not make him powerless; it removes his ability to read a sequence alone. He can still work with a shared Chorus, repair instruments, and teach, but any claim he makes must be checked by people and sensors outside his body.

## What the system cannot do

- It cannot show a complete future. It can only see branches supported by current conditions and linked instruments.
- A preserved sequence is a **witness**, not a stored future. It can anchor a claim under present conditions, but it cannot be replayed as proof of what will happen.
- It cannot read intent, morality, or the truth of a person’s private motive.
- It cannot bring back a spent branch, resurrect a closed route, or erase counter-memory.
- It cannot create weather from nothing. A conductor can redirect a break or route that has already gathered enough pressure.
- It cannot safely control a whole basin alone. The more centralized and binding the claim, the more the sequence narrows.
- It does not reward raw intensity. A precise, narrow, well-anchored working can be safer than a dramatic but poorly supported one.
- It cannot force another person to consent. Consent is a social requirement because a public claim can bind people even when they never speak.

## The reader’s interface

Trained readers use **sequence glass**, an instrument that turns pressure, static, and condensation into six moving traces. A reader may receive one short, plain-language warning when a claim is about to bind:

```text
CLAIM: the lower ward will pass under dry wind at 02:10.
ONE POSSIBLE TURN WILL CLOSE.
```

The warning does not identify the lost possibility. It does not display a skill list or a numerical probability. The reader must decide whether the practical advantage is worth a real unknown cost. This is the story’s only recurring interface: there is no separate sentient or game-like System. Its notices are rare, brief, and tied to a consequential binding choice.

## Power stages

The stages are earned capabilities, not automatic levels. A person can be competent in one and weak in another. Most citizens have the ordinary sensitivity to feel a major pressure change, but only trained readers can bind a claim.

### Advancement gates

| Stage | Entry test | First-use test | Failure and recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Listener** | Notice a pressure or moisture change before an instrument records it. | Warn a crew of a local change without naming a result. | False alarm, headache, or joint pain; rest, ground the observation in a physical gauge, and try again later. |
| **Reader** | Bind one short claim with a place, time, effect, and physical anchor. | Use the claim to protect a small group while accepting an unknown spend. | A false binding causes a counterturn; the reader must repair the anchor, reduce scope, and disclose the failure. |
| **Tracer** | Follow a sequence through two connected towers while a second reader holds the local anchor. | Warn that a storm will cross from a valley into an occupied corridor. | Sequence bleed or an inverted movement; isolate the trace, stop the link, and repair both towers before another attempt. |
| **Conductor** | Alter one movement in a live sequence while holding a physical route open. | Move a break or route without creating a false basin-wide claim. | Injury, equipment failure, and a lasting scar; the conductor must let the scar be documented rather than attempting to erase it. |
| **Chorus** | Coordinate independent readers and local witnesses around a conditional claim. | Publish disagreement and still leave people a workable next action. | No forced consensus; the conflict itself is the failure signal, and the cell must choose a safer bounded claim. |
| **Forecast Warden** | Hold a local mandate, maintain a forecast cell, and submit its records to review. | Authorize a claim that can be challenged, expired, and compensated. | Loss of mandate or a failed review; the office is suspended and the authority returns to the affected community. |

No stage is skipped by a dramatic shortcut. Marek reaches Reader before the opening, Tracer through distributed practice in Volumes 2–4, Conductor after a physical intervention in Volumes 5–7, Chorus through shared work in Volumes 8–10, and Forecast Warden only after the final volume proves that he can submit his judgment to people other than himself.

### 1. Listener

A Listener notices pressure, charge, moisture, and animal or plant behavior before instruments register a shift. This is the baseline of weather work and the first useful skill in the series.

**Cost:** joint pain, fatigue, headaches, and false alarms. A Listener cannot make a claim without becoming a Reader.

### 2. Reader

A Reader can identify the first turn or two of a local sequence and make a short, bound forecast. The reader can limit the scope of a claim, which makes the cost less likely to spread but does not make it harmless.

**Cost:** one real branch for each new binding claim; inner-ear pressure, metallic taste, temporary loss of fine hearing, and increasing difficulty ignoring the sequence after a binding.

**Marek’s starting stage:** He is already a Reader, with an unusually good eye for instruments but no stable ability to trace a full sequence.

### 3. Tracer

A Tracer follows a sequence through several linked towers or weather stations and can distinguish order from simple correlation. A Tracer can warn that a storm’s effect will travel from a valley to an airship corridor, or that a ward’s closure is likely to redirect water toward Low Spindle.

**Cost:** a wider branch is exposed, the reader carries more “sequence bleed” from distant nodes, and a failed trace can invert a movement. A Tracer needs a physical anchor and a second trained reader for long distances.

### 4. Conductor

A Conductor can change one movement in an active sequence: turn a wind, split a rain line, hold a charge above a roof, or delay a break long enough for people to leave. The Conductor must understand the existing sequence and work inside a live storm or connected ward.

**Cost:** physical injury, damaged equipment, and a lasting environmental scar. A conductor cannot undo the branch already spent by a forecast, and repeated work can make ordinary weather feel like a roar. A conductor’s success often changes what the next storm will do.

### 5. Chorus

A Chorus is a group of readers and local witnesses who issue a shared, conditional forecast. A Chorus can display disagreement instead of silently choosing one result. It is stronger in stability, not in coercive force.

**Cost:** consent, time, and political responsibility. A Chorus cannot be forced to agree; conflicting claims can create a counterturn. The more people participate, the more visible the human cost of a binding claim becomes.

### 6. Forecast Warden

A Forecast Warden maintains a local forecast cell over time and can authorize a bounded public claim under a community mandate. A Warden’s power is institutional rather than personal. The authority can be reviewed, challenged, and withdrawn. This civic role is distinct from a **Storm Warden**, the field service that performs rescues and repairs.

**Cost:** the Warden gives up private certainty and becomes answerable to the people affected by the forecast. A Warden must keep sensors, records, appeals, and compensation working. The final stage is not a stronger version of prediction; it is the ability to coordinate without claiming sole ownership of the future.

## Marek’s progression

Marek begins as a competent Reader. He becomes a Tracer through instrument practice, failed field work, and cooperation with Tarin’s crews. He becomes a Conductor only after accepting that intervention leaves a scar and cannot undo a prediction. He learns the Chorus method through relationship and local politics rather than a solitary breakthrough. He becomes a Forecast Warden in the final volume by giving up his right to make one city-wide claim.

His final sequence burn is permanent and specific: without at least two independent witnesses and a physical anchor, Marek can no longer distinguish a live sequence from the pressure of his own past bindings. He can still make a claim within a Chorus, but the network can challenge his contribution. That limitation is the practical proof of his choice, not a hidden final upgrade.

Every stage costs something physical, social, or civic. None of the costs can be paid with a larger number or a hidden special ability.
