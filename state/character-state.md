# Character State — end of Volume 02, Batch 0003 (Chapters 71–82)

**No character has aged. Batch 0003 runs the twentieth to the thirtieth day of Drownmonth, eleven days, and it is inside one month. Nothing below this line is a plan; everything below this line happened.**

## Marek Vale

**Stage: Tracer, and it is a method and not a rank, and the authority is unchanged.** Dismissed from field duty, censured in the Office's own record, off every watch list and roster, may not sign a reading for that office, may not attest in its service, and may not be named in a sponsor's line **of a claim**. May work a bench, may be on the ridge, may take private commissions at his own hours and his own price. **He has lost the field differential and has drawn no field hour since the Bellweather month, and he has about one pound twelve.** He has paid eleven shillings and a shilling in coin in this batch out of his own pocket, on the top step of a ladder, and she declined a second payment inside the same week because the retainer covers it.

**What he did in this batch, and the shape of it.** He **entered the Tracer stage on the twenty-eighth of Drownmonth**, the only way the gate allows: a second reader anchoring the local end. He did not go up the ladder. He took the far end at a bench in the maintenance shop's outer room on the ridge, on a working copy of the ninth ward's tower feed cut and ground for the purpose and **not fitted anywhere**. Four attempts, the fourth holding from ten past four to half past six and then again in the evening. **What he got that a two-ended read cannot is the hour the bend arrives at the far place**: half past one on the low ground, eleven minutes past four at the south end, four hours and ten minutes apart on the water. He published it at the moment of seeing, at five to twelve on the Monday night, **over his own standing objection that a reading published the moment it is seen is a reading he has not yet checked**, because it was her term, and then wrote down that he still believed he was wrong and that both of those being on the sheet is worth more than one being on it. He attested the order of the movements on the sixth ward's tide claim, outside the Office's service, and **that is the third time he has put a reader's hand to somebody else's spend in this volume.**

**Physical state: worse, permanently, in the body, and not otherwise changed.** A very high thin unbroken ringing in the left ear and roughly a third of the fine range lost on that side, **and on the twenty-eighth a further loss on the same side**, known only by noticing that a shelf which has been in a certain place relative to a conversation in that room for four months is not. **It is not a binding, it is not the Volume 12 sequence burn, nothing in the record will ever mention it, and nobody in this basin knows it exists — including the woman he paid on a ladder.** He asked a foreman to repeat a figure at about half past seven, was told, asked again, and **she looked at him a second longer than a foreman looks at a man who has asked a question twice, and then went back to her lehr log and did not remark on it; and on the thirtieth, in a room of eleven people, he asked an assessor to repeat a tide hour twice, and she noticed for about a second and looked back down because she had a claim to get through.** **He has now asked a question twice, in a room, in front of other people, four times in nine weeks, and he does not know he does it.** He wrote the reason down on the back of his own copy of Circular 15 — *a man who tests it every week stops being able to do the work, and I know now that it is the argument a man tells himself when he does not want to know* — and wrote down that writing it down is not the same as doing anything about it, and did not do anything about it. **Absolute guardrail for the rest of Volume 02, kept: used physically in Chapters 78 and 81 of this batch and 74 of the last, disclosed in none. Sable Orin did not learn it and must not.**

**The bleed is not dramatised in this batch and no character reasons about it**, per the standing open-thread guardrail. Its cost is carried in the strain. **There is one moment, at about half past ten on the Monday night, in which he opens his mouth to tell her a thing and does not, and the chapter gives no reason and no content, and he decided not to find a reason, and a reader who has been paying attention knows why.** He did not tell her on the Friday, and he did not tell her on the Saturday, and he did not tell her on the Tuesday.

**Flaw, as performed rather than described.** Three failures in ninety minutes were not reading errors; they were **nine decisions not to ask her something**, and she found all nine from four miles away by counting his breathing. He has spent his whole adult life narrowing uncertainty until it is a route or a number or a repair he can manage personally, and in this batch the thing he produced is a method he cannot use alone and a cost he has told nobody about and a reading he published before he had checked it because somebody else attached a condition to him. **He is better and further gone at the same time and he has not noticed either.**

**What he believes now, and should be frightened of.** That a reader who wants to be alone on a stage is a reader who has not met anybody. That a Director can close a trade in glass with a seal and a date and never send for a form, and there is no appeal and no counter-signature, and **the only reason the ninth ward has any reading of its own at all is a nine-inch plate in his coat and a bubbled temporary in a brass surround, and neither of those is on anybody's schedule and neither of them is in a book.** And he has no idea that a firm has put his district's own figure on a board with a receipt for eleven shillings under it, or that a district said yes to a shilling a week in ninety seconds, or that the Office's own line about the morning of the thirtieth is pinned six inches from it and nobody has joined them.

## Sable Orin

**Stage: counter-reader, and she is now the local end of a trace, and she is better at it than the man who needs her.** Thirty-four, four notebooks, a strip of sequence glass bought rather than cut, and about one pound two of the money he has paid her in eleven shillings and a shilling pieces in a satchel. **She goes up the ninth ward ladder, which Marek has still not gone up, on three mornings, and anchors the local end of a trace on the Monday and the Tuesday, and the tower's own plate stays in its seat and neither she nor Gant Ollis looks at the substrate of it and neither of them mentions it.** She was paid in coin on the top step, counted it faster than the time before, and did not say thank you, and **he noticed the speed of the counting.** She declined a second payment inside the same week, in one sentence, because the retainer covers the week.

**What she has now, and it is the volume's argument about her:** five public disagreements with a named reader, none withdrawn, two of them about movements and one of them about a number of minutes. The keepers' argument, in a tower, in her own hand at the top of a page: *a record is a thing somebody can be asked for.* And her own consent problem, said twice and worse the second time: *a reader is a person, I am a person, a person has an address, and now I have one at eight o'clock in the morning by an appointment, and there is no version of the eleventh of this month where the appointment is not in a book, and I have never in my life been in an office and been hard to find.* **She is paid a shilling a week to be findable and she does not know what that has made her.** **She does not know her shutter is on the firm's list at threepence a week and must not find out in this volume.** She is not in the Quiet Ledger and the Quiet Ledger is not a thing, and no chapter in Act III names either. **She must not learn that the district sold the right to print the ward's hours.**

## Neris Kade

**Where she is:** Low Spindle, on the evening of the thirtieth day of Drownmonth, with a shilling a week from a firm, a figure of her own on a market's board, a page of seven lines, and a card of nine names in a drawer.

**State: the district has been given the one thing it has been asking everybody for since the Bellweather month, and has sold it inside ninety seconds.** The ninth ward now knows when its own reach comes off — half past one, arriving at the south end at eleven minutes past four, four hours and ten minutes apart, with a published nine-minute disagreement about Route that she copied onto four shutters herself and put on the low blocks' shutters in four minutes on a woman's own initiative. **And on the Tuesday she said yes, in a room with a clerk in it, to a shilling a week for the right to print the ward's own hours with the ward's name on it, with no council, no vote, no minute, and nobody in the room having asked either named reader whether the reading might be printed.** She wrote nothing in the district book because the district book has no column for a shilling. On the Wednesday she read the district's own figure of 1/4 on a market's board with the receipt for eleven shillings under it and said no to going and saying so, and went and wrote the hours on a shutter and got them to the school before eight, because the children walk up. **Her page is still seven lines and the eighth is *it does not say who may have this, and nobody has asked, and I said yes*, and she cannot write it. Her shelter rota is still unwritten and is not going to be managed in Batch 0004 either.** She has never yet been told that a Council's own register contains a finding naming her district's silence since the forty-fifth.

## Sabin Orle

**State: has done the batch's hardest thing, which is rule against her own preference, twice, and got a better answer than the first time.** Forty-four, eleven years in the technical cell, assessor for water scheduling.
1. She told a clerk she will not adopt an interim figure twice in a season because that is an invention and not an adoption, **and said out loud that the reason she could do it so easily is not that it is unreasonable but that nobody would notice for about two years and she would have gone on doing it.**
2. She wrote a four-line letter asking a ward for a figure and got the best answer of her working life, and **ruled that a loss which is a period cannot be entered as a sum, that the Council may not supply a figure, and that the interim figure of two shillings and sixpence stands for the season and not for each event, and the ward is paid for a morning and not for a wall.** The 2/6 is not re-derived and is not altered.
3. **She struck a sentence out of her own minute in front of the clerk and initialled the strike and left it legible**, because *a public record is not a place for a reason the assessor has got.*
4. She copied her own nineteen-word flyleaf finding into a second minute, because a finding that lives three inches above a copperplate note can be removed by whoever removes the page.
5. She issued a four-paragraph direction on the Standing Response stub — the first thing this Council has sent out of that building in a generation — **and it will not work, and she does not yet know that it will not work.**
6. **She certified a claim on a tide and wrote her own box and said so before she wrote it, which is the second time in eleven years and the first time she has ever told anybody in advance.** *A tide is arithmetic.* And she read the cold version of it out of a proof at half past six on the evening of the thirtieth and did not strike it and said out loud to an empty room: *there is no column in the world for a tide, and the column is called dry lines.*

**Where her position is now, and it is a move and not a softening:** from *a claim's cost cannot be documented, and that is the design*, through *a document nobody can be produced is not thereby a loss nobody has had*, to ***a loss which is a period cannot be entered as a sum, and the Council's only instrument for a sum is a figure a claimant supplies, and the Council may not supply one and may not invent one.*** **She has stopped looking for the document and started looking for the claimant, and she does not yet know that there are four of them in this basin and one of them is a man with a school exercise book.**

## Tobias Mear

**State: twenty-nine, six years an underwriter, and he has done the volume's central finding in a shed on a paint drum and put it in a Council file for the second time in a month.** He read Petrie's level book for an hour and a half as he was told to, and established that **a level book has the water in it nine hundred times and the plan in it never once**, that the *work done, or reason no work* column is empty in the row for the thirtieth of the Bellweather month, and that a level book's whole function is to settle a row afterwards. Then he did the arithmetic on the back of a 1961 Council form used as a pallet cover: **a preparation is a plan, a plan has a name on it, so a preparation can only ever be priced by the party that intended it — a ward, a yard, a lighterman, a salvage man — and the two hundred and forty children at the Sump Lane school are in four plans and are named in none of them, and forty of them walk up.** He then wrote what a working regime would have to look like, in four points, and arrived at the same wall from the other side: the loser's own figure, or a plan, and a plan has no sum, and the season has no rate in this basin. **And he counted the forty three times in a book of his own because he did not believe the first figure.** Petrie let him take the book and refused him a name, and told him to keep doing it in the shed with him in the room. Orle read it, said he is the first person in this basin to have put a figure in a document that is not a price and not a claim, and wrote on the tab: ***the fourth party has not been asked.*** **He is owed nothing, is not a district, and has said so on the page in his own hand.**

## Maro Hesk

**State: forty-one, ten years in the trade, and he has withdrawn a product in public for the first time in his life and been right.** He read a four-paragraph notice his own counter clerk put on the wall and said, twice, *that is a good notice* — *I have been in this trade ten years and I have never once been able to say the price of a river, and there is now a piece of paper in a public room which says the number and says what it is a number of.* He signed the withdrawal of the Bellweather reach line in ink, in the counting floor, with about twenty people in it, and printed the reason in full: **not exposed, not stopped, a market will not be the last party in a basin to admit a number exists.** He paid a yard two shillings in four days without argument, refused to add a line to a schedule of charges, and said the reason for both out loud: *we have not been asked to and we do not know what the line would be for.* He has read a memorandum of the ninth of the month and still not learned that the use rule excludes nobody, and he has answered a refusal rule and still not learned that the closing did the work. **He is not a villain and has done nothing dishonest in this batch and is more frightened than he was at the start of it.**

## Edda Tarn

**State: thirty-eight, nine years a counter clerk, and she has done the two most expensive useful things in the batch and one of them was a hole in her employer's rules.** She printed **the record of a reach** on three public boards — the first public measurement of a river in this city — with a nineteen-year-old's line in pencil under it: *a level is not a sequence, and if we print the level and call it a reading, somebody will believe it, and then it is a lie with a date on it.* She got her own fourth line acted on by somebody other than herself and was not thanked for that either. She made the firm ask nine districts a question for the first time in nineteen years, one question each, each district told it could refuse before the question was asked, and **left a line in the record that the ninth ward was not telephoned because it has refused in writing, which a partner read and did not strike.** She paid a yard two shillings, refused to add a line to a schedule of charges, and wrote on the back of a receipt a description of the position to a stranger because the stranger would understand it. And she withdrew a product in public, with **no price on it**, and wrote the sentence that fills the hole: *we withdraw this line; there is no quoted price for it and so there is nothing to refund and nothing to publish; the rule does not reach this.* **She has just taught a nineteen-year-old that a copied paragraph in a drawer is a record, and it is the whole of what she has to teach him this year.**

## Garrick Dellow

**State: forty-four, and he has done the volume's expensive thing without knowing he was doing it.** He has a school exercise book with a sailing ship inside the front cover containing **forty-one entries of what the clearing of a ward's drains cost him after every strip he has taken in eleven years, to the penny** — the only cost record of its kind in this basin, voluntarily kept, never asked for. And on the thirtieth he added to it, in a fair round hand, on two sheets of good paper, **the only document in Volume 02 that prices a preparation**: crew of four and a foreman, tackle, two carts, the order of the ground from the N end, spoil into the third block's drains, the clearing at 3/-, 3/6 and 4/1, a hundred and sixty yards on a good forty minutes and a hundred and twenty on a short one, and at the foot: *forty minutes is worth to me a hundred and sixty yards of the lowest ground in the ninth ward, stripped and carted, with the drains clear by the following Tuesday, and every year, eleven times, no more.* **He has sent it to nobody and has said out loud why: because the day somebody asks me for it, it stops being mine. And he has worked out that there is no box in the Council's book for a fella who isn't a ward, and no form for it, and no bell for it, and no copy but the one.** He cannot be a claimant, cannot invoice the Council, and does not know a Council is paying anything at all. **The load in the shed is unchanged and is in a second season; the wall is in its third and the two are not the same counter.**

## Tarin Senn

**State: has issued the first invoice in the history of that firm and been paid, and has not gone and said thank you, and the reason is the best thing she says in the batch.** She found the firm's attribution line carrying her yard's name, the hour and the date of the line, four streets from a poor ward that cannot read the Office's bulletin — and worked out in about ninety seconds that **a condition about attribution is a condition about naming, and a name on a window is not a fee**, and that the fifth condition had been earning nothing for a hundred and forty working days. She invoiced for two shillings, being the cost of her clerk's time to publish the figures, with the board extract, the hour, the date and a time sheet enclosed, and put it in the ordinary post, **which is the sentence to hold on to, because four hundred and six pounds earlier in the year was spent on not sending one.** She was paid in four days on a stamped receipt with a refusal to add a line to a schedule of charges, and she put the receipt on her own board under her five conditions with the hour and the date on it, and she did not go down to the counting floor to say thank you, **because a yard that says thank you on a firm's own floor has told the firm that the yard has noticed it needs to, and by September there would be a man in that building once a quarter about a retainer.** She also added a fifth chalk line: *Fifth line: 2/- received. ## Kell Bray

**State: thirty-one, Hearthward assembly clerk, twenty years in a ledger, and she has done the most uncomfortable act of the batch and she did it on purpose.** The Board asked her, in four courteous lines, on what authority a Council's minute names the failure of a district to answer for forty-five years and whether the finding is proposed to be re-set. **She did not withdraw it. She made it worse, in a minute in a corridor that is in the district file and is not in a Council book: *the finding stands; it is not withdrawn and it is not re-set, and the district it names has not been told that it exists.*** The reason is on the page: the ninth ward is the poorest ward in the city, and in about nine weeks somebody is going to use a line in a Council's own book to tell that district it has been in the wrong for forty-five years, **and there is nobody who is going to have to stand in that room and answer for it, and a clerk of another district put the line in.** She worked out in four minutes that the assessor's new direction will not work, for four reasons, and then wrote **nine addresses out in a fair hand from a book in her own bottom drawer that nobody in Hearthward knows exists**, carried them in herself at about four o'clock on the Thursday, and was refused in one sentence: *this Council has no schedule for receiving a list of addresses.* Skell put it in the second drawer from the top and it is still in that drawer. She went four miles to Ferrygate and came away with a minute and a half in which nothing interesting happened at all, **and that is the best minute and a half of her four days and the only thing in the chapter she would have written down if anybody had asked her to.**
