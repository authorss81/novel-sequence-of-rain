# Character State

Where the cast stands at the end of the most recently completed batch. For planned arcs, see `bible/characters.md`. For who is *written* so far, see `state/chapter-summaries.md`.

## Marek Vale

**Stage:** Reader. Unchanged, and no advancement is earned in this volume. He has not followed a sequence across two towers and must not do so in Volume 01.

**Where he is at the end of Batch 0003:** in the maintenance shop on the ridge, on the evening of the third day of the month, with a tube in a coat on a hook, a folded true document in the second drawer of his bench that cannot be filed anywhere, half of his usable hearing gone from the left side, and a high thin ringing on that side that he does not intend to explain to anybody. He has not slept properly since the middle of the previous month and has not been on a watch, a roster, or a field list since the eleventh.

**What he has done:** put a hull inside a claim. He asked a captain for it, in a yard, at the boards, in front of nine people, on the four Kestrel conditions, and she gave him the hull and the hours and made the terms; and then he entered a conditional claim on the ward ledger bench under a Director's authorization, with a bell and a clerk and no certification, with an expiry equal to its own effect and a listed alternative that named what it took; and it held, and it saved the corridor, and it took a quarter of an hour of eased air out of the last evening of a month, which was the ebb, which was the convoy's window, which was forty minutes wide on the morning of the thirtieth. He knew. He went to the south bank to be near it with a docket that was a real errand and a lie about why he was there. He said *it was just the water* to a shed foreman and has not been able to put it down since. And then he went into four minutes of cold moving water after a boy of six and came out with about a third of his fine hearing gone from the left ear for the rest of his life.

**Physical state:** **damaged, permanently, in the body, and not otherwise changed.** A very high thin unbroken ringing in the left ear and roughly a third of the fine range lost on that side — the part that separates a smear from a dropout, the part that is the whole trade. It was four minutes of cold moving water on the thirtieth and it is not a binding and it is not the Volume 12 sequence burn and is never to be written as one, and nothing in the record will ever mention it. The pre-existing strain — the half-tone pressure, the metallic taste, the thin hearing — was **unchanged by the Chapter 29 binding and was not improved by it**; he noticed that he was relieved and that the relief was the wrong thing to notice. He works on the wrong side of a bench now and does not say why, and his father stands on his right without once having to move.

**Flaw, as performed rather than described:** **he went and got the thing instead of the question, and it worked, and the working is the problem.** He built a claim that was as honest as he could make it, with an alternative that named what it took, and asked the one person whose free act could make it true, and got a yes in chalk, and entered a true thing in a good hand with a Director's stamp on it, and felt the specific warmth of *this is now somebody else's to do* and identified it correctly and did it anyway. He is not corrupted and he is not broken. He is competent, and the competence is the problem, and he must not become a man who enjoys it.

**What he owes:** Anwen Sarr a document, now fourteen days older and structurally blocked, because the honest version requires an instrument with a chain behind it and there is exactly one and it is the plate in the ninth ward tower that also holds a twelve-year-old Witness; the ninth ward's public glass, which is dark for money and cannot be told about without a confession to a keeper who has never asked; a boy in the fourth block and a woman with a bed made up; a shed foreman the truth, which he cannot give; **the twelve-year-old date in a locked room in a poor ward's tower, which nobody knows he has, and which is now on the same plate as the corridor he spent, and which he cannot write about without burying the first thing under the second; and a plate of hearing that he will not get back and has told nobody about.**

**What he believes now, and should be frightened of:** **that a good claim in a good hand, with a listed alternative and a Director's stamp, is the correct instrument, and that he is the correct man to hold it, and that the fact that it cost a thing nobody can name is a property of the weather and not of him.** The method survived. The report is going to the Council in the spring. The record has been closed by a classification that is not a lie. And the reader should be more afraid of Cassian Rook at the end of this volume than at the start of it.

## Tarin Senn

**Where she is:** Kestrel Yards, on the morning of the thirtieth, on a yard apron in the sun with a launch book under her arm and a nineteen-year-old with his wrist in a splint arguing with a rigger about the price of canvas.

**State:** **has put a hull inside somebody else's claim and has published what it did.** She gave Marek the corridor publicly at the boards, on the four conditions, with the hours on a slate and her own hand under them, and she said out loud, a day before, that the four lines were about the yard's name and that not one of them was about her crew. Then the turn held and the lulls stopped and a mooring hand's wrist came off the coil at 23:11, and she went up the lift that should have been a junior's for four minutes and eleven seconds while Ilva Sorn took the *Marrow* in off the quarter, and she came down and put both of those facts on the board in chalk with the hour on them, under the third line, published as *not the yard's error*, because a column of differences that only reports good news is not a column of differences. **She also flew the reach on the thirtieth in a flat-bottomed lighter and lifted the second pump house's intake screen out of the water by boom from under the freight apron, and it saved a pump house, and it is on the Line's own account as an error of the yard, and she is the one who paid for it.** She has still not asked Marek the second question. The shape of the answer has changed again and she has not begun to construct it.

**What she has of Marek:** a hull, an hour, four minutes of her own hands on a loaded line, a plate in a coat, and the discovery that **a claim under an authorization does not tell you what it has done to the thing you are standing in.** Competence acknowledged on both sides, and a decision about who is the instrument, twice, in opposite directions. Neither of them has said the word partnership. Volume 03 still owns the first real trust milestone and this is not it.

## Neris Kade

**Where she is:** Low Spindle, on the evening of the thirty-first, having locked a drawer.

**State:** **has moved from a woman who collects omissions to the person holding the only document in the city.** Her ruled page has seven lines, is stamped and dated in her own hand, and its seventh line is the one that carries the volume's argument: the Office has no instrument that looks at a reach, therefore nothing lost on the reach can be shown to have been lost by anybody, therefore a window the city cannot see is not a window the city loses. She sent the list to Malle on the twenty-second, as she said on the ninth day she would, and his reply was correct and moved the document out of her hands and into a building she does not control. She got a second opinion on a line that was already full from a maintenance hand in a hole, for free, in ninety seconds, and it was the answer she already had, and she learned from it that two copies of a true thing in two buildings is still one assertion. Her own shelter rota, the ninth ward's, is still unwritten and is still the line she cannot put her name to. **She has not asked Marek for the plate and has told him so to his face, and he said no, and she accepted it, and she has not stopped asking anybody for anything else.**

## Corrin Malle

**State:** **has buried it.** He used Marek once in a certifying room on the nineteenth and filed the attestation in a drawer, and he refused to take a reading off him in a corridor on the twenty-sixth, and he has said out loud that a spend on his watch is a spend with his name under it and that a Deputy who cannot explain a thing is entitled to call it the thing whose name he does not have to explain, which is in the Charter, Section 9. He reclassified item (a) of the thirtieth as a maintenance failure, and **he said both sentences out loud to Tovan Iles — that it is a true statement about the culvert and it is not a true statement about the thirtieth** — and he did it without heat and without apology and partly right. He moved Tovan to a copy desk on a form whose ground is true, and told her the whole of his reasoning including the sentence about the reader, and said he had not stopped respecting him for a second and had been the man who took his name off a watch. **He is not caught. He is not a villain. He has a reader he cannot use and a clerk he has lost and a record he can defend.**

## Lior Venn

**State:** **his own problem, named once and only once, in a corridor, in ninety seconds.** Asked whether a yard board is a record, he answered that it is a record of what a yard chose to write, that it is not a reading and is not lodged and is not compellable, and that **an observation that nobody was obliged to act upon is not a promise, and a promise is the only thing in this building a person can be answerable to.** He has never lifted one, he does not want to, and he thinks about it. He has now reclassified five things in a month, correctly, including the ninth ward's own rain gauge, the Ferrygate district's rain-garden rota, and the thirtieth's headwall, and every one of them was right. He countersigned the thirtieth's reclassification in the Records day book at 09:10 on the third day, in a building two floors away from the bench where it was entered, and that gap is a clerk's post.

## Pell Ardan

**State:** Unchanged and offstage this batch. Holds the balance on a pane that is no longer a pane, Marek's two years of tower glasswork, and Marek's knowledge that Pell knew exactly what was being asked. **Not in Chapters 22–35 at all, and nobody has been near his shop.** His standing warning — a ward with no public glass finds out about a private reading from him, eventually — is now one event nearer to happening and he does not know it.

## Anwen Sarr

**State:** **owed, and owed more expensively.** Clause four of her refusal was satisfied on its first sentence by the Council's report of the twenty-second and has been left unanswered on its second, which is the shape she drafted it to be. Marek told her the document was *not ready* on the eighteenth and has said nothing since, and Chapter 23 put on the page that he cannot write the honest version without a plate that carries a twelve-year-old Witness in it, which means the document she wants is blocked by a thing nobody in her assembly knows exists. **She is owed the Chapter 48 public hall and nothing before it may pay her off, and she has a new sentence to use unfairly and intends to.** She is not softened and she is not bought off.

## Sabin Orle

**State:** Unchanged and offstage this batch, and structurally load-bearing: **she is the one who told Marek to put the expiry on the same line as the effect, and her reason — that an expiry later than the effect is a period in which your claim's authority to send people outlasts your claim's stated reason for sending them — is the best piece of instruction the reader has had in a month.** She is the method's champion and its honest one, and she said the sentence that beat him, *it is not accidental that a claim's cost cannot be documented; it is the design*, and she lost nothing for saying it.

## Nell Cray

**State:** **closed out.** Ferrygate district assembly clerk, nineteen years, whose stated ambition is a week in which nothing interesting happens. The certified claim was discharged at 04:40 on the twenty-first, entirely true, and the market's eleven carts and two barrows went down a certified road from first light, and two people of the Quay Steps were in nine inches of water from about 05:05 until about two in the afternoon because the sheet that would have moved them was a piece of paper in her own book that nobody had ever been told about. **She found it herself, in her own hand, and could not produce a copy because the copy is the original.** She did not make it a lament and she did not make it a rescue. She wrote the rota out again, copied it four times instead of once, sent one to the warden's council with a covering note that says it is a district record and not a public notice, and knows exactly what she is doing and cannot stop doing it. **She is not a reader and she has no instrument and she spent nothing, and her own correct paperwork was the mechanism, and she has said so in writing to nobody.**

## Sable Orin

**State:** **Unchanged and offstage this batch.** Right, published, and unread, and she has stopped finding that interesting and started finding it useful. She published a basis for the first time and it changed the appearance of the document and not its usability. She was refused four times by people who were correct and she did not retract the sheet. She told a stranger to her face that she cannot name her a street. She has a fourth notebook headed *Declined. Who. Why, in their words. Date.* and it is the first sign of an archive. **She is not sorry. She is reorganising**, and the consent problem is live and is not named. **She is not in the Quiet Ledger, the Quiet Ledger is not yet a thing, and no chapter in Batch 0003 names either.**

## Oren Vale

**State:** **Chapter 34 happened and it was a bracket.** He went down a river path to the south bank with his son, looked at a banded timber stack and a pump on a pallet and a painted date nobody had looked at since the autumn, and gave Marek in about ninety seconds the whole of what nine people carrying shoring timber to a cracked headwall is worth: forty minutes to get the gear onto the flat and a night to do the work, the timber first and the pump second because the pump's bed is the shoring, one man who has stood on a Bellweather wall before and is not on the crew, and a wall that is good for the next front. He gave his son the mechanism of his own life and did not learn the cause. He asked *what have you done this month*, was told *glass*, said *that is not an answer*, and did not press. He mended nothing and was not thanked and told him the door closes. **He knows nothing about the plate, the claim, the cuff, the annex, the twelve-year date, the hearing, or the copy desk, and Marek told him none of it, which is the fifth thing in six weeks he has come to that kitchen for and not said.** He has a sheet in his inside pocket with four columns of years, four words in four hands, a front in three years, and a line in his son's writing saying *no minute.* He has said *come on a Tuesday when it's a nuisance and nothing's happened* and Marek keeps not doing it.

## Ferren

**State:** **Unchanged, and named in Chapters 33 and 34 from a distance.** The Hearthward rain-garden foreman is nine years the standing order's length, and on the thirty-first he is told by a warden to clear the throats on the two rows above the wall and is told plainly that it is not a claim and that there is no more rain coming tonight. He has not been asked for his account a second time. **Nobody may make him the mechanism of the Hearthward spend, and nobody in Batch 0003 makes him the mechanism of the Lower Sill spend either.**

## Cassian Rook

**State:** **In prose for the first time in Chapter 27, and the reader should be more afraid of him now than of Malle.** Fifty-two, Director for six years, emergency warden of Low Spindle for nine before that. He sent for Marek at eight in the morning and wrote *bring the ninth of the month; bring the order and not the evidence, because I do not want the evidence and I want the order*, and he is the only man in the building who has treated Marek fairly and usefully at the same time. **He said the true thing at the top of the session so that nobody would spend the morning defending themselves against it.** He read his authorization aloud exactly as written and explained what it required. He took the four licensed carriers' money as a fact in the world rather than a problem. He used Marek for ninety seconds on the order, wrote a slip the size of a stamp docket, put it in a drawer, said *it does not help me at all*, and moved on. He **asked the one question nobody else asked** — how many options does a prepared district have, and accepted the answer *one* with thanks, and had it minuted in the words *that the residual exposure of a prepared district is a single option and is not a compensable loss*, on the day he is the one making the claim, because in four years somebody will say the districts had nothing at stake. He is never wrong about the weather, never lies, never monologues, and his approval stamp is on the Bellweather notice of the twenty-seventh and on the docket that carried the Chapter 29 claim to the ward ledger bench. **He is the reason the volume's method feels reasonable, and his authorization is the vehicle by which a claim with no concurring seat and no positive routing effect became a claim at all.**

## Kell Bray, Ott Belm, Gant Ollis, Maro Hesk, Ivo Slate, Petrie, Doak, W. Farrow, Wen Ardery, Aldis Prynne, Immy Vant

- **Gant Ollis:** keeper of the Ninth Ward tower, eleven years. **Marek has not been up the ladder in Batch 0003.** He still has a working feed, a brass surround, a boxed spare, a date in the margin of a plate, and about a decade of Oren Vale's arithmetic in the tower's book, and he has never asked.
- **Ott Belm:** the ninth ward's district copy, nineteen, in the service about three months. **His wet notebook now also has the thirtieth in it in Ampow's own words, and that line is the reason nobody in the fourth block died.** Nobody has tidied it up.
- **Kell Bray:** Hearthward assembly clerk, thirty-one, twenty years in a ledger. Offstage.
- **Maro Hesk:** forty-one. **In the yard on the twenty-fourth and correct about the only thing that matters in a carrier's market, which is that an underwriter reads a seal and not a column.** He did not profit from the thirtieth and did not pretend to have been asked to save it. He is the customer of the pressure, not its author.
- **Ivo Slate:** courteous, licensed, and admitted the Exchange's fifth-day window into a Council chamber because four carriers had paid against it, which is the whole volume's method working through a records counter. He wrote his agency report on the Wednesday of the sixth and it follows the board and precedes the collision.
- **Petrie:** **south-bank shed foreman, about thirty years in the shed, and now one of the volume's two real witnesses.** He remembers the levels, the hour, the weather, his crew, the man who came (W. Farrow, Friday, 16:40, with a chain and a staff and the right column) and the man who did not come, and he has no account of why nine men stood in a shed, and he says so four times in one morning. He keeps a level book with a column he has never filled. He told a lantern man that there is a front in three years and it is not a threat. **He is not a plot device, he is not the villain, he suspects nobody, and he was lied to exactly once by a man who was standing next to him in the mud.**
- **Doak:** municipal apron man at the Bellweather freight apron, on the apron since five in the afternoon on the thirtieth, who told the water office in the spring that the wall had gone to gravel a foot above the water line, and who went into the water to his knees at half past three and came back up on his hands and was not hurt. He and Tarin Senn between them kept the road and the culvert in two different columns.
- **W. Farrow:** water office district crew, a decent man who does the job the way it is written down, who did the monthly levels check on the twenty-ninth at 16:40, wrote the window into a shed's own book in the right column, told the shed to inspect the flatbed's ground first, and went off up the hill. **He is the last person in the causal chain to have done everything right.**
- **Wen Ardery:** nineteen, four months in the Kestrel yard, a mooring hand. **A bad wrench of the right wrist at 23:11 on the twenty-ninth, not broken, and the yard's own published account of exactly why.** He sat on a coil and said nothing about it, which is correct, and asked whether he could have his hours in the launch book credited, and they were.
- **Aldis Prynne:** the reader on the long Bellweather watch, the second roster, six days, from the twenty-sixth. He read the order in the Ternway reversal in the spring and got it wrong by about forty minutes and it cost a ferry. **Marek is not on that watch and there is no version of the sentence in which he is.**
- **Immy Vant:** six, of the fourth block, Low Spindle. Went back into nine inches of water at eight in the morning for a bird cage, was in a doorway in a foot and a half of moving water at about twenty past one in the afternoon, and was got out and was furious and unhurt, and his mother thanked Marek and he was grateful to be not thanked. **Four minutes of cold moving water and a third of his fine hearing, for a canary.**
