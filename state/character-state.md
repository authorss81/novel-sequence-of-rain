## Marek Vale

**Stage: Tracer, and it is a method and not a rank, and the authority is unchanged.** Dismissed from field duty, censured in the Office's own record, off every watch list and roster, may not sign a reading for that office, may not attest in its service, and may not be named in a sponsor's line **of a claim**. May work a bench, may be on the ridge, may take private commissions at his own hours and his own price. **He has lost the field differential and has drawn no field hour since the Bellweather month, and he has about one pound twelve.** He has paid eleven shillings and a shilling in coin in this batch out of his own pocket, on the top step of a ladder, and she declined a second payment inside the same week because the retainer covers it.

**What he did in this batch, and the shape of it.** He **entered the Tracer stage on the twenty-eighth of Drownmonth**, the only way the gate allows: a second reader anchoring the local end. He did not go up the ladder. He took the far end at a bench in the maintenance shop's outer room on the ridge, on a working copy of the ninth ward's tower feed cut and ground for the purpose and **not fitted anywhere**. Four attempts, the fourth holding from ten past four to half past six and then again in the evening. **What he got that a two-ended read cannot is the hour the bend arrives at the far place**: half past one on the low ground, eleven minutes past four at the south end, four hours and ten minutes apart on the water. He published it at the moment of seeing, at five to twelve on the Monday night, **over his own standing objection that a reading published the moment it is seen is a reading he has not yet checked**, because it was her term, and then wrote down that he still believed he was wrong and that both of those being on the sheet is worth more than one being on it. He attested the order of the movements on the sixth ward's tide claim, outside the Office's service, and **that is the third time he has put a reader's hand to somebody else's spend in this volume.**

**Physical state: worse, permanently, in the body, and not otherwise changed.** A very high thin unbroken ringing in the left ear and roughly a third of the fine range lost on that side, **and on the twenty-eighth a further loss on the same side**, known only by noticing that a shelf which has been in a certain place relative to a conversation in that room for four months is not. **It is not a binding, it is not the Volume 12 sequence burn, nothing in the record will ever mention it, and nobody in this basin knows it exists — including the woman he paid on a ladder.** He asked a foreman to repeat a figure at about half past seven, was told, asked again, and **she looked at him a second longer than a foreman looks at a man who has asked a question twice, and then went back to her lehr log and did not remark on it; and on the thirtieth, in a room of eleven people, he asked an assessor to repeat a tide hour twice, and she noticed for about a second and looked back down because she had a claim to get through.** **He has now asked a question twice, in a room, in front of other people, four times in nine weeks, and he does not know he does it.** He wrote the reason down on the back of his own copy of Circular 15 — *a man who tests it every week stops being able to do the work, and I know now that it is the argument a man tells himself when he does not want to know* — and wrote down that writing it down is not the same as doing anything about it, and did not do anything about it. **Absolute guardrail for the rest of Volume 02, kept: used physically in Chapters 78 and 81 of this batch and 74 of the last, disclosed in none. Sable Orin did not learn it and must not.**

**The bleed is not dramatised in this batch and no character reasons about it**, per the standing open-thread guardrail. Its cost is carried in the strain. **There is one moment, at about half past ten on the Monday night, in which he opens his mouth to tell her a thing and does not, and the chapter gives no reason and no content, and he decided not to find a reason, and a reader who has been paying attention knows why.** He did not tell her on the Friday, and he did not tell her on the Saturday, and he did not tell her on the Tuesday.

**Flaw, as performed rather than described.** Three failures in ninety minutes were not reading errors; they were **nine decisions not to ask her something**, and she found all nine from four miles away by counting his breathing. He has spent his whole adult life narrowing uncertainty until it is a route or a number or a repair he can manage personally, and in this batch the thing he produced is a method he cannot use alone and a cost he has told nobody about and a reading he published before he had checked it because somebody else attached a condition to him. **He is better and further gone at the same time and he has not noticed either.**

**What he believes now, and should be frightened of.** That a reader who wants to be alone on a stage is a reader who has not met anybody. That a Director can close a trade in glass with a seal and a date and never send for a form, and there is no appeal and no counter-signature, and **the only reason the ninth ward has any reading of its own at all is a nine-inch plate in his coat and a bubbled temporary in a brass surround, and neither of those is on anybody's schedule and neither of them is in a book.** And he has no idea that a firm has put his district's own figure on a board with a receipt for eleven shillings under it, or that a district said yes to a shilling a week in ninety seconds, or that the Office's own line about the morning of the thirtieth is pinned six inches from it and nobody has joined them.

**The bleed: seen once, physically, unreported, unremarked.** At about six on the Wednesday, the working copy of the tower's feed under a glass weight at the near end and a wire four miles to the tower end where Sable Orin held the far end and had not stopped talking about Friday. ## Hester Skeat — NEW, and she is a person and not a device

**Sixty-one. **What she has:** two copies, a dry one in the dresser and a wet one under the stairs, in a rainward city, and nine hundred entries between them, twenty-two years, every one of them right because she wrote every one of them herself off the sheet somebody brought her. She reads it about twice a year. She has found three things in it: the ward went quiet for the last eleven months of her time and she cannot say why; a young reader out of Hearthward came twice a week for two years and stopped on a Tuesday in the spring and **there is no line saying he stopped**; and **she was the place where a corridor became a street, for twenty-two years, without ever once asking, because a clerk asking a reader to be more particular is a clerk telling a reader it is not good enough, and nobody ever told her that was what she was doing and she would have asked the first week.** She is not sorry about it, and she is not proud of it, and she has said both out loud to a stranger on a step.

**Why she keeps it and will not give it: one of these exists in the world, and if it goes into a press there is one of it and no second, and a thing with one of it is a thing that can be altered, and there would be exactly one person alive who could say so, and she would be sixty-one and she would not be on a wage. She is not a person who can be asked about because she has never held an office of anything.** She has been thanked by nobody, has asked for nothing, is owed nothing, and **must not be softened, rescued, or made into the woman who tells the truth.** Her third term is the one she would have made last: she will not say which day she would give a great deal to see, and he is not going to ask.
