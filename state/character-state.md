# Character State

Where the cast stands at the end of the most recently completed batch. For planned arcs, see `bible/characters.md`. For who is *written* so far, see `state/chapter-summaries.md`.

## Marek Vale

**Stage:** Reader. Unchanged. Has not followed a sequence across two towers and must not do so in Volume 01.

**Where he is at the end of Batch 0002:** in the back of the Council's technical cell on the afternoon of the nineteenth day, in a room of eighty people applauding a method, having just said the true thing out loud and been unable to support one syllable of it. He has signed a three-line minute that puts his own name on the first line and a district's refusal underneath it in a footnote. The Bellweather watch was stood down on the eleventh and his name came off it and went onto nothing at all.

**What he has done:** cut a brass surround around a temporary, cut a spare plate and not fitted it, read the tower's own feed plate clean and learned that the feed is healthy and therefore that the Thursday-night gap was not a fault; **found a complete twelve-year-old Witness in the substrate of a working plate and refused three times to take, copy, or record it**; agreed a four-line method with a captain on a public board; attended a Council certification as the attesting reader; and **stood up in a room and told eighty people what a good claim cost, and could not prove one word of it.**

**Physical state:** **unchanged and not improved.** Inner-ear pressure half a tone up on the left, the metallic taste that began with the Chapter 5 binding, thin hearing in the left ear, a bent needle he does not remember picking up, and about a fortnight of not sleeping. **The Chapter 21 binding made none of this worse, and the batch was explicit that strain does not improve.** The permanent damage is Chapter 31 and is four minutes in cold moving water; it is not here and is not foreshadowed as a symptom.

**Flaw, as performed rather than described:** **it has now been performed in public and it cost him everything at once.** He narrowed a frightening problem until it became a speech, made a motion he could not support in the one room that was made for it, and understood in the middle of the applause that the method's success is measured in options removed. He also spent two whole evenings not saying the thing he came to say, to two different people, and the second of them has told him exactly what it would have cost and he still did not say it.

**What he owes:** Anwen Sarr a document — she has now made it an assembly standing requirement in writing, clause four, and he has told her the document is *not ready*; the ninth ward's public glass, which is dark for money and not for a hand, and which he cannot be told about without confessing to a keeper who has never asked; the district a spoken order he has no chain for, which is in a boy's notebook and four hundred and forty people's heads; Gant Ollis a tower that has a working feed and now, for the first time in two years, a spare; his father a Tuesday afternoon in which nothing has happened; and **the twelve-year-old date in a locked room in a poor ward's tower, which nobody knows he has.**

**What he believes now, and should be frightened of:** **that the problem is not that nobody will make the call.** For twenty chapters he believed that. He has watched a competent person make it correctly, cheaply, in nine minutes, in front of applauding people, and he was right about the method and it took him the Office and the assemblies in the same afternoon. There is no villain to object. The good method is what is doing it.

## Tarin Senn

**Where she is:** Kestrel Yards, on the nineteenth day, with a column of differences published on three boards and a sortie flown on the *Petrel* at twenty to two on the seventeenth to take an hour with two sets of eyes on it.

**State:** Has moved from a captain who paid for being right to a captain who has decided what the Line is worth and written it down in chalk in front of a carrier's man. **She has said in public that the Line is not a resource, it is a witness, and that if somebody's claim is made true by her hull being in the lane at an hour, the claim names her.** She set the terms and the yard's route officer signed a proviso on top of them. She has the humiliation of Chapter 5 inverted and priced. She has still not asked Marek the second question, and the shape of the answer has changed.

**What she has of Marek:** an hour agreed in public, a sortie flown together in which she held it and he said nothing at all, and a term sheet neither of them has called a partnership. Competence acknowledged on both sides. No warmth and no coldness, which is its own kind of attention.

## Neris Kade

**Where she is:** Low Spindle, the eleventh day, with a five-line page in a locked drawer, a wet notebook copied out in her own district's hand, a school day and a morning of wages paid for by the ward, and a letter outstanding at a yard.

**State:** **Anger has become a document.** She has written the fifth line and it is not the one anybody expected, and she knows she got away with something by writing it. She has asked the city for a *turn* rather than a window, in a district she can be answerable for, and has made herself a reader as well as a warden. She has told Sable Orin, in a drying room, exactly why she is not going to translate a corridor into four blocks a second time, and it was the correct reason.

**Open in her:** **her own shelter rota is still unwritten and is still the thing she cannot put her name to.** The list has still not gone to Malle. What she sent instead was a letter to a captain, and she cannot yet say whether that was courage or a smaller version of the same habit.

## Corrin Malle

**State:** Came up the hill on a Sunday coach, out of hours, to clap for a method he staffs, with a sandwich. **He read clause four on the nineteenth of the month in a bed in a rented room and told Marek the price of saying it in a certifying room, and he was right about the price, and Marek did it anyway.** He has now said aloud that the Office cannot use a man who has been shown to be unprovable in public, which is the decision he made in advance. He has not yet buried anything, because the spend he will bury has not happened. **He still holds the Bellweather entry.**

## Lior Venn

**State:** Reclassified Sable Orin's day-twelve sheet at 15:40 on the seventeenth under Rule 6, correctly, with a new sentence: *The publication of an instrument, a place, or an author does not convert an observation into a claim.* Two reclassifications, two level hands, no argument. He is the Office's **records** problem and has never watched a branch close, and what he did in Chapter 19 was close the door on a document that was already useless and made it look like principle.

## Pell Ardan

**State:** Unchanged and offstage this batch. Holds the balance on a pane that is no longer a pane, Marek's two years of tower glasswork, and Marek's knowledge that Pell knew exactly what was being asked. **Chapter 15 put Marek two streets from a kitchen instead of a glassblower's shop, and the reader now knows the two men who understand his work are the glassblower and the father.** Has not been told.

## Anwen Sarr

**State:** **Has refused, in writing, in front of her clerk, on grounds that are correct, and lost.** The refusal is filed, four clauses, with a standing requirement that no certified claim be entered without its cost published at entry. She is still owed a document and is no longer going to be polite about asking. **She has a new sentence to use on people** — *it is not ready* — and intends to use it unfairly for the rest of her life. She has told Marek, in the rain, that she did not refuse for him. Ferren stood by the door in his cap and said nothing. She is right and the volume must not soften her.

## Sabin Orle

**New in prose this batch.** Forty-four. Council assessor for water scheduling, eleven years in the technical cell, sponsor of the trial claim. Four certified claims behind her, all of them true, three of them true where an uncertified forecast would have been wrong. Drove up on a Sunday evening to hear a refusal. **Not a villain, not offended, and loses nothing for saying the sentence that is the thesis of the volume: it is not accidental that a claim's cost cannot be documented; it is the design.** She is the method's champion and its honest one, and the first person to be genuinely glad Marek is in the room and genuinely sorry afterwards.

## Nell Cray

**New in prose this batch.** Ferrygate district assembly clerk, nineteen years, whose stated ambition is a week in which nothing interesting happens. Keeps the district's own book and its returns and its day book. **Moves the shelter rota herself, in boots, every winter for six years, and twice in the wet.** Filed the rain-garden rota into an ordinary return in answer to a badly designed form, with a covering note that says there is no forecast in it and no claim. Will be the person who finds out what has happened to her district in the next ten days.

## Sable Orin

**State:** **Right, published, and unread, and she has stopped finding that interesting and started finding it useful.** She published a basis for the first time and it changed the appearance of the document and not its usability. She was refused four times by people who were correct and she did not retract the sheet. She told a stranger to her face that she cannot name her a street. She has a fourth notebook headed *Declined. Who. Why, in their words. Date.* and it is the first sign of an archive. **She is not sorry. She is reorganising**, and the consent problem is live and is not named.

## Oren Vale

**State:** **Present in prose for the first time in this batch, and not reconciled.** Sixty-one, retired culvert and pump mechanic, nine years in the ninth ward's outfall run and eleven years telling the ward that the ward runs on a rota and not on a glass. **He gave Marek the full-pipe mechanism, unprompted and better than the Weather Office has it, and Marek arrived already knowing it.** He knows nothing about the claim, the plate, the feed fault, the cuff, the private plate, the trace, or the trial. **He does not mention Anja and refuses in advance of the question.** There is a bent bracket off the third hook that has been bent for a year and neither of them touched it. He said: *come on a Tuesday when it's a nuisance and nothing's happened.* Chapter 34 is still the Oren chapter and it is not a chapter in which they are right with each other.

## Tarin, Kell Bray, Ott Belm, Gant Ollis, Maro Hesk, Ivo Slate, Nell Cray, Ferren

- **Gant Ollis:** keeper of the Ninth Ward tower, eleven years. Carried two plates down a ladder at first light and has never asked. Keeps everything, including about a decade of Oren Vale's arithmetic in the tower's outfall-run book. Has a brass surround now.
- **Ott Belm:** the ninth ward's district copy, nineteen, in the service nine weeks. Wrote the order down as it was said, with the times, and did not tidy it up. **His wet notebook is the only document in Veyra that says any of what happened that night.**
- **Kell Bray:** Hearthward assembly clerk, thirty-one, twenty years in a ledger, writes without lifting the pen. Filed a four-clause refusal as it was read aloud to him and was given a second page without being asked.
- **Maro Hesk:** forty-one, weather-window broker and insurer. In prose, with contracts, and **not an antagonist.** The Exchange is the customer of the pressure, not its author.
- **Ivo Slate:** courteous, licensed, and has now written the Kestrel Line's four conditions on his own Exchange board and agreed to read them out to anybody who asks.
- **Ferren:** present and silent in Chapter 20, in his cap, at the back. His account of Thursday is unchanged and nobody in this batch asked him for it.
