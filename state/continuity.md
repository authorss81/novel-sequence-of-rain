# Continuity State

## Canon status

Bootstrap design canon is established; chapter-level canon has not yet been dramatized. Treat the bible and series outline as the source of truth and reveal information gradually in prose.

## Fixed world rules

- Atmospheric spells move through the Six Movements: Gather, Weight, Break, Route, Release, Witness.
- A read observes without spending. A binding claim has a place, a time or trigger, an effect, and a witness; it can close one real branch in this world.
- A false binding still spends and causes a counterturn. A spent branch cannot be reopened, though a hidden or administratively sealed route may be distinct from a spent branch.
- Branches are not parallel universes. Counter-memory is evidence of absence, not a supernatural recovery mechanism.
- The Meridian began as a civic instrument for displaying disagreement. Later legal authority made one official claim binding across the basin.
- Rook signed the Meridian Cut order. Anja closed one branch to protect Low Spindle and died; the surviving Ninth Ward corridor was sealed and erased from the public record by Rook’s order, carried out by Ione Sere.
- The pre-Meridian origin of atmospheric sequence remains intentionally unanswered.
- A **ward** is one of the nine original Veyran watersheds and is the unit of civic identity, tower, assembly, and Council seat. The nine wards do not match neighborhoods or wealth. **The Ninth Ward is the poor ward of the low eastern ground and it contains Low Spindle**, which is how the ward-mismatch politics and the Meridian Cut are the same problem.
- A **cycle** is one six-hour local watch period. A private binding that affects a public route, water system, hospital, school, or market must enter the emergency ledger within one cycle.
- An **expiry** ends a claim’s authority to direct action. It never reopens a spent branch.
- The **core lock** at Crown Basin answers the physical key, not the Council quorum. Forcing it produces a counterturn.
- A **maintenance seal** is opened by a person with a key, under a public warrant, with a crew and tools. Restoring the Ninth Ward corridor is a maintenance act and spends nothing. No binding opens it, and no forecast is to blame for it having stayed shut.
- **The Last Forecast is attempted twice.** Volume 10 is a lawful quorum-certified activation under the White Noon Protocol, disrupted before the Witness movement completes, so it spends nothing and leaves the compound branch intact. Volume 12 is the identical claim put to the core lock without a quorum. Rook seats the key and releases it without turning it; the lock is never actually turned.
- **The twelve final deaths trace to one mechanism.** Low Spindle’s standing order summons the shoring crew on the strength of an early wind precursor whenever a published claim holds a turn past dawn. The bound claim is accurate, and holding the turn revokes the precursor’s authority to summon anyone. The crew never leaves the shed and a real, unclaimed reversal arrives at 05:10. The river gate failure and the fouled cisterns are storm damage, not spends.
- **Ottine Vask** was a routing clerk who died two years before the Cut. Her name on the Ninth Ward routing trail proves the witness chain was falsified. She is evidence, not a suspect, and is never resurrected or given a posthumous message. The index entry naming her was cut by **Ione Sere**, three years after the Cut, in a second act distinct from Rook’s deletion order.

## Canon artifacts that must not be merged

- **Ninth Ward Record** — Anja’s official signed forecast for the Ninth Ward, lodged before the Cut. Proves the corridor was sealed and deleted rather than spent. Missing at the opening; recovered in Volume 11.
- **Crown Basin sheet** — Anja’s final hand-written readings from inside the sealed chamber. Records the branch she closed, the route she failed to save, a maintenance seal tag number, and a reference to Rook’s signed order with the low-ward projection omitted. Found in Volume 12.
- Neither document contains a formula, a secret technique, or a solution to the White Noon.

## Starting continuity

- Marek Vale, 32, is a Veyran Weather Office researcher and instrument repairer. He is a Reader, not a Conductor, and begins without a bound Bellweather claim in prose.
- Tarin Senn, 30, captains the independent Kestrel Line. Marek and Tarin have prior professional friction from the Hearthward rescue and no settled romance.
- Neris Kade is a Low Spindle Storm Warden. Sable Orin is a counter-reader. Ione Sere is the archive custodian. Cassian Rook is the final human antagonist.
- Corrin Malle, 54, is Deputy for Field Forecasts and Marek’s direct supervisor. He suppresses the Bellweather entry and testifies against Rook in Volume 11. He is the Office’s **field** problem; Lior Venn is its **records** problem and the two must not be merged into one obstacle.
- Tovan Iles, 27, is the junior glass-tender who writes Marek’s claims into the ledger, keeps a private copy of the true Bellweather entry, is demoted to copy work in Volume 01, and becomes the first archivist of the Weather Commons by way of the Volume 11 public archive.
- Ottine Vask was a Weather Office routing clerk, dead two years before the Cut. Her name appears in Volume 01 on the hand-cut index entry to the missing Ninth Ward Record. The hand is identified in Volume 05; the reason is given in Ione’s Volume 11 testimony.
- Pell Ardan, 34, is Marek’s oldest friend, a glassblower and courier. He is not a reader. He is injured in the Volume 07 breach.
- Anwen Sarr, 46, chairs the Hearthward rain-garden assembly and defeats Marek’s first attempt to force a local claim in Volume 04.
- Juba Tren, 38, is a mason and organizer in the No-Forecast Assembly. His position receives a fair trial in Volume 09 and is confronted by a preventable disaster, not discredited by a speech.
- The first ten chapters must establish ordinary work, the Bellweather crisis, the read/binding distinction, a first spend, a first cost, and the Ninth Ward clue. The clue in Volume 01 is a **citation** to the missing record with its index entry cut out by hand, not the record itself.

## Ending constraints

- The final conflict is the White Noon and Rook’s Last Forecast, not a new enemy.
- Rook’s mandate is withdrawn in Volume 11; the Volume 12 climax is the physical core lock. He never regains recognized authority and keeps the key until he surrenders it.
- Marek refuses a single answer built on the Ninth Ward Record, accepts incomplete information, and loses the ability to read a sequence alone. Two separate things are refused and two separate things are done: the corridor is restored by physical labor at no cost, and the witness-anchored binding is refused because it would close the compound branch and make Anja the last authority.
- The final operation spends exactly two things: the branch in which Low Spindle’s early wind precursor summons the shoring crew, and Marek’s private read of the basin.
- Twelve people die in the final operation, including people who followed an official local notice correctly. The loss is visible and compensated where possible, not erased.
- Rook survives, loses authority, and faces public judgment. Anja remains dead. Marek and Tarin share a home and workshop while retaining independent careers and the right to disagree.
