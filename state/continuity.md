## Fixed world rules

- Atmospheric spells move through the Six Movements: Gather, Weight, Break, Route, Release, Witness.
- A read observes without spending. A binding claim has a place, a time or trigger, an effect, and a witness; it can close one real branch in this world.
- A false binding still spends and causes a counterturn. A spent branch cannot be reopened, though a hidden or administratively sealed route may be distinct from a spent branch.
- Branches are not parallel universes. Counter-memory is evidence of absence, not a supernatural recovery mechanism.
- The Meridian began as a civic instrument for displaying disagreement. Later legal authority made one official claim binding across the basin.
- Rook signed the Meridian Cut order. Anja closed one branch to protect Low Spindle and died; the surviving Ninth Ward corridor was sealed and erased from the public record by Rook’s order, carried out by Ione Sere.
- The pre-Meridian origin of atmospheric sequence remains intentionally unanswered.
- A **ward** is one of the nine original Veyran watersheds and is the unit of civic identity, tower, assembly, and Council seat. The nine wards do not match neighborhoods or wealth. **The Ninth Ward is the poor ward of the low eastern ground and it contains Low Spindle**, which is how the ward-mismatch politics and the Meridian Cut are the same problem.
- A **cycle** is one six-hour local watch period. A private binding that affects a public route, water system, hospital, school, or market must enter the emergency ledger within one cycle.
- An **expiry** ends a claim’s authority to direct action. It never reopens a spent branch.
- The **core lock** at Crown Basin answers the physical key, not the Council quorum. Forcing it produces a counterturn.
- A **maintenance seal** is opened by a person with a key, under a public warrant, with a crew and tools. Restoring the Ninth Ward corridor is a maintenance act and spends nothing. No binding opens it, and no forecast is to blame for it having stayed shut.
- **The Last Forecast is attempted twice.** Volume 10 is a lawful quorum-certified activation under the White Noon Protocol, disrupted before the Witness movement completes, so it spends nothing and leaves the compound branch intact. Volume 12 is the identical claim put to the core lock without a quorum. Rook seats the key and releases it without turning it; the lock is never actually turned.
- **The twelve final deaths trace to one mechanism.** Low Spindle’s standing order summons the shoring crew on the strength of an early wind precursor whenever a published claim holds a turn past dawn. The bound claim is accurate, and holding the turn revokes the precursor’s authority to summon anyone. The crew never leaves the shed and a real, unclaimed reversal arrives at 05:10. The river gate failure and the fouled cisterns are storm damage, not spends.
- **Ottine Vask** was a routing clerk who died two years before the Cut. Her name on the Ninth Ward routing trail proves the witness chain was falsified. She is evidence, not a suspect, and is never resurrected or given a posthumous message. The index entry naming her was cut by **Ione Sere**, three years after the Cut, in a second act distinct from Rook’s deletion order.

1. **A corridor is an hour you can put a hull in, and an hour is made of a pressure gradient standing between two airs** (Ch 26, Malle). A gradient with a *bend* in it is a good gradient; the bend is where the air eases and a ship can come down onto a mark.
2. **A claim that holds an hour does not improve it. It makes it dependable, and a dependable hour is not a variable one, and the sequence does not distinguish between probable and fixed, and a fixed turn has no bend in it** (Ch 29). The claim therefore takes the long band and the hook goes with it, because the hook is the turn that most directly contradicts a long band held to certainty.
3. **The reach comes down when the basin's draw is on it** (Ch 13, restated Ch 29 and Ch 34 by Oren). **The hook under the long band is the ebb** — fifteen minutes of eased air in which the basin's outflow runs free and the bottom of the reach drops to the lowest point of the month. **The ebb is the convoy's window.** About forty minutes of usable flat between roughly 07:20 and 08:10 on the morning of the thirtieth.
4. **And the sentence that is the whole of Batch 0003: a reach-level window is not a weather window.** There is no instrument in the Weather Office of this city that has ever looked at a reach, so no amount of rain changes anything, and there is not one word in the Charter, or in the rules of any ward, or in the vocabulary of any instrument on the ridge, or in the mouth of any Deputy, that would be caused to move by the fact that fifteen minutes of air has stopped being available on the last morning of a month. **This is why nobody ever had to decide anything, and it is the institutional shape of Chapter 21's Hearthward spend repeated in a category nobody had named.**

**The Kestrel corridor claim (Ch 29), as entered. The volume's third of four bindings, and Batch 0003's only one.**
*Place:* the Kestrel corridor — the western lane over the reach, from the point to the yards' mark. *Trigger:* the Kestrel Line's arrival at the yards' mark, accepted. *Effect, as entered:* the Kestrel corridor remains passable to and through 04:00 on the thirtieth. *Listed alternative, as required:* if the Line's arrival at the yards' mark is not accepted, no corridor is claimed; the draw backs at the western end, the return lane closes on the published band, the four carriers' window is not met, and the low wards' floodplain takes the draw for the same hours. *Expiry:* 04:00 on the thirtieth day — **deliberately equal to the effect, at Sabin Orle's instruction, so that no period exists in which the claim's authority to direct action outlasts its stated reason for sending anybody.** *Sponsor:* M. **Why it is not certified, and why that is the whole trap (Ch 28, Tovan Iles).** Certification under the Charter requires six things: a named sponsor, a published expiry, a listed alternative, a concurring seat, a reader's attestation, and **a positive routing effect at the yards within the expiry window.** Marek satisfies five. He cannot satisfy the sixth and cannot satisfy it *because* his claim is honest: a conditional claim's content is *if this is done*, and the Council's condition asks for an effect, and the Line cannot be compelled to arrive, and **if the Council could compel the Line the Line would stop being a witness and become a resource** — which is the Kestrel Line's third condition restated as the law. **A Director's authorization does not require any of the six.** It is three lines, it names no place, time, effect, alternative, sponsor or expiry, and it puts a claim on the ward ledger with a bell and no witness and no counter-signature. **Nothing compels anybody. It makes Marek answerable.**

**THE PUBLIC DRY LINE — EXACTLY WHAT IT IS. This is the batch's central civic object and it is three things, not one, and none of them is a monument.**
1. **A hand-lettered board at the Bellweather freight apron (Ch 43).** Painted by **Petrie**, south-bank shed foreman, about thirty years in the shed, on a plank and in white, on the forty-second day, over two days. It reads: **SOUTH BANK DEPOT. / SHORING TIMBER AND REPLACEMENT DEWATERING PUMP, STORED AGAINST THE WATER OFFICE'S ORDER 114. / LOADED THE SIXTH. / NINE CREW ASSIGNED. / THE LOAD IS HERE. / PAINTED BY THE FOREMAN.** **It carries no seal, no person's name, no date on the thirtieth, and no accusation, and it is a load list.** The *load that never moved* is Tarin Senn's phrase and it is a description and not a name. **Petrie paints the sixth of the month and not the thirtieth, because the sixth is when the load went on and the thirtieth is when somebody decided it did not come off, and there was no such somebody, and a name on a board would send the whole city looking for a man and stop them looking at the load.** The district works office's printed enamel sign, **SOUTH BANK DEPOT — LOAD HELD. NO DISPOSAL**, is the shed's public sign, is refused by Petrie, and goes back in its box. **The board and the shed are still there at the end of the volume. The timber and the pump are still there. The culvert is not rebuilt.**
2. **The CLAIM — DISPLACED TURNS column in the published ledger (Ch 44).** Six items and no others: place; stated effect as entered; trigger with the hour and the hour it was satisfied; expiry with the hour; condition in the words on the sheet, and where condition and expiry are the same line the fact is to be stated in those words; and **turns displaced — what the claim closed, in the ordinary language of the district affected, and where the district is not known the claimant's own words with the fact that the district is not known stated, and by no means the name of the person who closed it, nor any word or mark from which the person might be identified.** No other item may be added in any return, and the column may not be amended by a Deputy, and any amendment requires the quorum of a claim. **The first entry, in full: KESTREL CORRIDOR, 29th–30th. Effect: corridor passable to 04:00. Trigger: line's arrival at the yards' mark, 23:11. Expiry: 04:00, 30th. Condition: the same. Turns displaced: a reach-level window on the morning of the thirtieth, forty minutes, not established in any instrument of this office.** **The column has no sponsor field at all. It does not name a sponsor. It is not a dry line and is not called one in Chapter 44.**
3. **The word, from a member of the public (Ch 46).** *My side of the river has got a dry line on it.* Spoken on the forty-sixth at about half past eleven, out of a queue of about sixty people at the public table, by a woman who has been standing in the wet for an hour. **Nobody in the building had used the phrase. The person who coined it is not the person who gets the credit.** **The shed's board and the column both exist by Chapter 46 and neither is called that by either.**
4. **The First Dry Line, in the hall on the forty-eighth (Ch 48):** a carrier's underwriter and **Ida Crome** of the ninth ward's fourth block — who was in the queue at the public table on the forty-sixth and came back on the same bus — put a shed's board and a ledger column together in public for the first time — *the ground's got a line drawn on it that isn't there, and the line's on a board at a shed and in a book in this building, and both of them are the same line and neither of them says who ruled it* — and a room of two hundred people that has been arguing for four months argues in public for the first time. **There is no minute of the public forecast hall of the forty-eighth day.**
