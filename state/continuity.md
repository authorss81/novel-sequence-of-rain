# Continuity State

## Canon status

Design canon is established. **Volume 01 Chapters 1–10 are now prose canon** and are recorded under *Prose canon from Volume 01, Batch 0001* below. Anything not listed there is still outline-level and must not be treated as having happened. Treat the bible and series outline as the source of truth for everything else, and reveal information gradually in prose.

## Fixed world rules

- Atmospheric spells move through the Six Movements: Gather, Weight, Break, Route, Release, Witness.
- A read observes without spending. A binding claim has a place, a time or trigger, an effect, and a witness; it can close one real branch in this world.
- A false binding still spends and causes a counterturn. A spent branch cannot be reopened, though a hidden or administratively sealed route may be distinct from a spent branch.
- Branches are not parallel universes. Counter-memory is evidence of absence, not a supernatural recovery mechanism.
- The Meridian began as a civic instrument for displaying disagreement. Later legal authority made one official claim binding across the basin.
- Rook signed the Meridian Cut order. Anja closed one branch to protect Low Spindle and died; the surviving Ninth Ward corridor was sealed and erased from the public record by Rook’s order, carried out by Ione Sere.
- The pre-Meridian origin of atmospheric sequence remains intentionally unanswered.
- A **ward** is one of the nine original Veyran watersheds and is the unit of civic identity, tower, assembly, and Council seat. The nine wards do not match neighborhoods or wealth. **The Ninth Ward is the poor ward of the low eastern ground and it contains Low Spindle**, which is how the ward-mismatch politics and the Meridian Cut are the same problem.
- A **cycle** is one six-hour local watch period. A private binding that affects a public route, water system, hospital, school, or market must enter the emergency ledger within one cycle.
- An **expiry** ends a claim’s authority to direct action. It never reopens a spent branch.
- The **core lock** at Crown Basin answers the physical key, not the Council quorum. Forcing it produces a counterturn.
- A **maintenance seal** is opened by a person with a key, under a public warrant, with a crew and tools. Restoring the Ninth Ward corridor is a maintenance act and spends nothing. No binding opens it, and no forecast is to blame for it having stayed shut.
- **The Last Forecast is attempted twice.** Volume 10 is a lawful quorum-certified activation under the White Noon Protocol, disrupted before the Witness movement completes, so it spends nothing and leaves the compound branch intact. Volume 12 is the identical claim put to the core lock without a quorum. Rook seats the key and releases it without turning it; the lock is never actually turned.
- **The twelve final deaths trace to one mechanism.** Low Spindle’s standing order summons the shoring crew on the strength of an early wind precursor whenever a published claim holds a turn past dawn. The bound claim is accurate, and holding the turn revokes the precursor’s authority to summon anyone. The crew never leaves the shed and a real, unclaimed reversal arrives at 05:10. The river gate failure and the fouled cisterns are storm damage, not spends.
- **Ottine Vask** was a routing clerk who died two years before the Cut. Her name on the Ninth Ward routing trail proves the witness chain was falsified. She is evidence, not a suspect, and is never resurrected or given a posthumous message. The index entry naming her was cut by **Ione Sere**, three years after the Cut, in a second act distinct from Rook’s deletion order.

## Prose canon from Volume 01, Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10)

**Calendar.** Day four = Monday, day five = Tuesday, day six = Wednesday, day seven = Thursday, day eight = Friday, day nine = Saturday. A **cycle** is the six-hour watch period; the **watch bell** marks the hour. Weather phases in this volume are the *leading rain*, the *second belt*, and the *collision*; *turn* remains the technical term for a single change in a sequence. **The front reaches Veyra at about 18:00 on day nine.** The Office's estimate moves all batch and is never quoted as a fixed number: nine hours at 10:00 on day nine, six at noon, one at 17:00; in Chapter 1 it is *four days*, with a six-hour disagreement between the ridge's six best instruments. **Do not re-derive an absolute hours-to-arrival figure from a chapter clock. Use 18:00 on day nine as the single anchor.**

**Glass chain at the Ninth Ward tower, now dramatized.**
- The tower's **feed seat** takes a working *sequence plate*; the tower's public frame takes a **signal pane**. Same bundle at T-4, four years before the opening: serial **4471** went into the sequence feed and **4470** into the public frame, both cut in one week by Pell Ardan, the last tower bundle the Office ordered.
- 4471 cracked. Marek rendered it, then cut and fitted a replacement on day five, which Malle inspected and passed on day six at fourteen hundred. It read clean.
- 4470 cracked later, in the autumn, on the frame side, and came out. Its replacement, serial **4486**, was quoted at the end of the year, paid for, and **never collected**; it has sat in Pell's shop for two years. A bubbled temporary went up on a Wednesday six weeks before the opening. **On day six Marek cut a nine-inch working plate out of the crown of 4486** and kept it, on Pell's terms, so the Ninth Ward's public glass is a temporary that cannot carry a legible state and has been unreadable for a fortnight. The ward has been living off handwritten copies.
- The cut is dramatized in Chapter 3: Marek scores two faces with a diamond scribe, breaks the pane along the line, and signs Pell's shop book *M. Vale, glasswork, unpaid*. The tower crew go away with an empty cart.
- Marek cut his private plate to the feed's thickness and ran it in the Ninth Ward seat in rotation, so his private plate and the tower's feed plate record the same thing. **This is why he has no independent witness.**
- On day eight at 06:10 Marek removed the feed plate to read it and took it down in a sailcloth under his glasswork docket. The Ninth Ward feed is on a dead seat with no spare, and has had no spare for two years.
- **The Ninth Ward's ward head at the Weatherglass does not light at all and has not for a fortnight.** It is not showing clouded amber. Nothing is coming down the wire from that tower, and the ward cannot publish a legible state even to itself.

**The Hearthward conditional claim (Ch 5), verbatim as entered.**
Place: the Hearthward terraces — upper spill gate, the upper and middle rows, the lowest row. Time/trigger: through the front's leading rain, to the third watch. Effect, as written: *the spill gate holds shut — stored water to the third watch, upper and middle rows dry, lower row drains clear.* Listed alternative: open the lower outfall in the second hour of the rain and take the market's ground floor. Expiry: the third watch. Sponsor: Marek Vale. Condition accepted by the Hearthward rain-garden assembly. Entered by Tovan Iles at bench four; the entry bell struck twice, once for the yards' ward head (the terraces' upper tanks feed the Kestrel freshwater header) and once for Hearthward. First line written in the emergency ledger in four years; that ledger already carried one older line, in different ink, about a ferry. **The claim is entirely true**, and it binds at the first hour of the rain, when the gate is shut and there is no third party left to refuse.

**Hearthward terrace drainage, now dramatized.** Three rows: upper, middle, lowest. The outfall runs under the market and discharges at a straight stone throat at the foot of the lowest row. Under a surcharge the throat cannot relieve itself; water rises on the retaining wall and the lowest row's ground floors go under. A pipe that is *already full* takes a terrace's rain and gives nothing back, so the wall keeps rising for as long as the terrace has water in it. Six ground-floor rooms on the lowest row sit below the surcharge line. A clean outfall clears the design load in about three hours. The rain-garden crew is Ferren's, five men plus the foreman, and it clears the drains Tuesdays and Thursdays; the standing order has run for nine years and before that was the water board's. The foreman is named **Ferren** and has no surname in prose. The water steward is a young woman who has been telling Ferren when to come for three years.

**The Hearthward flood (Ch 6), as cost.** Six ground-floor rooms on the lowest row wet from 23:40 to 08:00. The lowest row's drains filled at 23:40 and ran clear at 08:00. The row then drained in half an hour, and the throat ran free, which is the physical proof that the pipe was never the constriction and had already been full when the rain came. The seed vaults' lowest eighteen inches were ruined; a season's stock. Sixty-one people out of the market; a hundred and sixty went up the terraces before midnight. The market shuts for a month. **Nobody dies.** The claim's success closed the branch in which Ferren's crew cleared the drains that night; Ferren was never ordered to stand down, remembers the whole night clearly, and has no account of it, together with a specific feeling of relief that he cannot account for either.

**The sequence fault (Ch 7).** In the second turn, Release thins to nothing for two and a half hours and resumes forty minutes late. It is not a plate dropout: a logged March dropout in the same feed dropped hard and left a crescent. The only other plate showing the same absence is Marek's own, off the same feed, run by his own hand. The Ninth Ward feed has a documented dropout history, and Marek's own unfiled tag in the second drawer from the top records the March one correctly. The four times exist only in pencil on Marek's cuff and on a sheet of bench paper in a flat drawer. **Marek has told nobody. Not Tovan, not Tarin, not Neris, not Malle.** He has minuted it in front of Malle as an unexplained sequence fault and has told Malle he has spent two days carrying it.

**Sable Orin's number (Ch 8).** An unsigned broadside from the drying room above the Ninth Baths, with a number and an hour and no place: *the undercroft on Sump Lane goes before the low blocks, 01:20 to 04:40.* **Neris pinned the card to her office board and wrote the whole pump order off it** — low blocks first, the Sump Lane grate crew onto the terrace and into the school undercroft before midnight, the terrace-end drain crews standing by at the second pump house, the ward's line to the assembly rooms open by ten. That act is what made it a binding, and she is aware in Chapter 8 that a witness existed and that she would not be able to find it. It closed something small, and it is **never to be identified, in prose or in any state file.**
The same chapter carries a *different* and unrelated last-winter counter-memory, and the two must never be merged: **Neris's** shelter rota changed underneath her after she acted on one of Sable's numbers and the original sheet cannot now be found. Tovan's last-winter anecdote in Chapter 4 is a **refused** claim — a good claim on this bench, two winters ago, that the district read off the bell and turned down in favour of its own published plan, so that nothing happened and no record anywhere says so. That one is a plant for the district's right to refuse, and it is the moral seed of Anwen Sarr's Volume 04 win over Marek.

**Neris Kade's list (Ch 8).** A ruled page headed *NINTH WARD — WHAT THE NOTICE DID NOT SAY. One line per notice, in the order they came*, in her district office's records drawer. Four lines by the end of Chapter 8: the fifth day's bulletin, the seventh day's western draw, the eighth day's second belt, and then a fourth line with no date on it because it is not about a notice. The fourth names Marek: *It does not say why the ward's own glass cannot be read, and it does not say whose plate is in my coat.* The unwritten fifth is her own shelter rota. She has told Marek she will send the list to his Deputy.

**Records machinery, now dramatized.** An **unattached observation** is a reading with no place, time or effect; it is filed and stamped in the day's rack and is not compellable. **Rule 6 of the Charter of Public Notice** is the authority Lior Venn cites when reclassifying one. Venn's handwritten line on Sable's sheet: *The Exchange is not a party to any district's preparations and is not required to price them. If a number has not been lodged, no party is obliged to act on it, and no party may afterwards be blamed for not having done so.* **Venn is the records problem and has never watched a branch close. Malle is the field problem and has now been given one true answer and one circle.** The **Compact is not named in prose and must not be.**

**The yard boards (Ch 2 and Ch 10).** On day five Nessa Tarrow copied the Office window and Tarin's counter-reading onto one sheet with both hours, and at 11:10 Tarin put all three versions on one sheet. **Sable's corridor reservation (Ch 10), verbatim in substance.** *Verified: the return lane is short by an hour and a half; the yards must be under sail by four o'clock; the draw opens ahead of the published window.* Reclassified at 14:10 on day nine as an unattached observation. Vale / SEQ/N9/—* with a violet filed mark, a square with a bell struck across it and a watch number, sitting between the lodger's name and the sequence reference. The date and the citation were removed. The cut edge is brown with age and the paper around it is still clean, so the trim is old and the sheet is old and the two were not contemporaneous. **The reader has not been told what the removed piece covered. ## Ending constraints

- The final conflict is the White Noon and Rook’s Last Forecast, not a new enemy.
- Rook’s mandate is withdrawn in Volume 11; the Volume 12 climax is the physical core lock. He never regains recognized authority and keeps the key until he surrenders it.
- Marek refuses a single answer built on the Ninth Ward Record, accepts incomplete information, and loses the ability to read a sequence alone. Two separate things are refused and two separate things are done: the corridor is restored by physical labor at no cost, and the witness-anchored binding is refused because it would close the compound branch and make Anja the last authority.
- The final operation spends exactly two things: the branch in which Low Spindle’s early wind precursor summons the shoring crew, and Marek’s private read of the basin.
- Twelve people die in the final operation, including people who followed an official local notice correctly. The loss is visible and compensated where possible, not erased.
- Rook survives, loses authority, and faces public judgment. Anja remains dead. Marek and Tarin share a home and workshop while retaining independent careers and the right to disagree.
