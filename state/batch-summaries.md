# Batch Summaries

Rolling record of completed batches, newest first. Chapter-level detail lives in `chapter-summaries.md`.

## Volume 01 — Batch 0002 (Chapters 11–21)

**Clock covered:** day nine from about ten past six in the evening to day nineteen in the late afternoon. Weekday map for the batch: day ten = Sunday, eleven = Monday, twelve = Tuesday, thirteen = Wednesday, fourteen = Thursday, fifteen = Friday, sixteen = Saturday, seventeen = Sunday, eighteen = Monday, nineteen = Tuesday. **The collision reached the basin at about 18:00 on day nine and is prose canon from Batch 0001; no chapter in this batch states an hours-to-arrival and none re-derives the arrival hour.** The front is behind them or overhead throughout; the batch ends on a Tuesday afternoon in a shuttered room, not on weather.

**Batch goal achieved.** Marek has gone from a maintenance hand with a private plate and a hidden spend to the attesting reader on a Council-certified claim who has said the true thing out loud in a room that was applauding and cannot produce a syllable of evidence. The Office has stopped using him and the assemblies have stopped listening to him, both in the same afternoon, both in one three-line minute.

**Batch beginning (Chs 11–12):** the Bellweather watch works, in public, for nothing. Nineteen strangers get the order of the six movements out loud on a stone floor, in a boy's wet notebook, and the Office's bulletin about the collision is entirely true and says nothing about the low ground. The morning after, the ninth ward discovers that it has a document-shaped hole in it exactly where it lives.

**Batch escalation (Chs 13–15):** the Exchange's purchased window, four carriers, Maro Hesk in person, the Lower Sill convoy loaded and not moved, the Kestrel Line's four conditions chalked on a board, a counter-reader whose number is published with its basis for the first time and acted on by nobody, and the first field trip up a ladder Marek owes two years of work on.

**Batch midpoint (Ch 16):** a retired culvert mechanic explains a *full* pipe as distinct from a *surcharged* pipe, in a kitchen, better than the Weather Office has — and Marek cannot say the thing he came to say. The discovery is of scale, not of history: the man who has the mechanism has been two streets away for twelve years and Marek's working life has been organised to avoid a kitchen.

**Batch external climax (Ch 20):** Anwen Sarr refuses to concur, in writing, in front of the clerk who has to file it, on grounds that are correct and that the room has already decided are obstructive. The trial goes ahead without Hearthward. Her clause four — that no certified claim be entered without its cost published at the point of entry, or the sponsor recording in writing that it cannot be — is the volume's demand and is answered by nobody.

**Batch reversal and cliff (Ch 21):** the Nacre Gate trial claim is certified, it is entirely true, the market books its carts, the convoy makes its window, the district withdraws its own preparations in a courteous four-line letter, the minutes praise the method, and Marek names in the middle of the applause what the method is made of. **Nobody can prove a syllable of it. That is the batch.**

**Rules taught without a lecture:**
- A read is free and says so out loud, in the first half of Chapter 11, before anything else happens.
- A binding needs a place, a time, an effect and a witness, and a *certified* binding needs a named sponsor, a published expiry, a listed alternative and a concurring seat. The trial claim is a complete, correct, well-made instrument.
- **A binding closes coordinated independent preparation, not only weather.** Three linked preparations go out in Chapter 21 and two of the three are reported aloud in the room, in a letter, as a courtesy.
- A certified claim's cost is **unprovable in principle, and the unprovability is the design.** Stated in the room by the assessor in Chapter 21 and it is the thesis of the volume.
- Caution is still free and still uncounted: a school closed on a warden's own authority, a morning of wages, a letter to a yard.
- A reading that nobody acts on is **not a lesser claim — it is not a claim at all**, and publishing a document that names its own basis does not make it compellable. Chapter 14 is the exact inverse of Chapter 8.
- An assembly's right to refuse is real, cheap, almost never exercised, and **correct** when exercised, and nobody in the room can see that it is correct.

**Bindings in this batch: one.**
1. **Ch 21 — the Nacre Gate trial claim.** Certified under the Charter on the nineteenth day. Sponsor Sabin Orle, assessor for water scheduling. Concurring seat Nacre Holds. Trigger 04:40 on the twenty-first; expiry the second watch of the twenty-first; listed alternative, the low side floods to the low marker and the autumn market closes. Attestation by a reader, of the order only. **Entirely accurate.** Cost: a compound branch of three linked preparations — the Ferrygate rain-garden clearing rota, the Ferrygate ferry fallback, and the Ferrygate low-ground shelter rota that has moved two elderly residents out of a ground-floor room every winter for six years. **The cost is unprovable to everyone but Marek, and it must remain so in every later batch; no state file may describe how anyone could prove it.**
**Chapters 11–20 spend nothing.** The Chapter 11 watch is a read. Sable Orin's Chapter 14 reading is published and nobody acts on it, which is the chapter's whole event.

**Power state at batch end:** Marek is still a Reader. **No advancement. He has not followed a sequence across two towers and does not do so in Volume 01.** Strain is unchanged by the Chapter 21 binding — inner-ear pressure half a tone up on the left, a metallic taste, and thin hearing in the left ear, all where they were on day seven. **The permanent hearing damage is Chapter 31 and is four minutes in cold moving water; it is not in this batch and is not foreshadowed as a symptom.**

**Money and hours spent:** a school day and a morning of wages out of four hundred and forty people; a day's crew-hours on the ninth ward's low ground; a morning of Marek's sleep every night for a fortnight; a joint sortie in the *Petrel*; a day of Anwen Sarr's and a night of Kell Bray's; a clause in the post; eleven men's time at the south-bank shed, loading and not leaving. **No deaths.**

**Standing debts created:** Anwen Sarr's published cost, now an assembly standing requirement in writing; Neris Kade's letter to a yard, which is a request made in public and can be refused; Tarin Senn's four conditions, which the yard can honour and the Office cannot; Sabin Orle's annex; **Marek's three refusals in Chapter 18**, and the fact that he is the only person alive who knows what is in that plate; a spare plate in a box in a tower; a bent bracket off a third hook; and a full pipe.

**Reconciliations made inside this batch, recorded here because they touch Chapters 1–10 without altering them.**
1. **No contradiction with Chapters 1–10 was found and nothing was restarted.** The two Chapter 8 dates — *unreadable for a fortnight* and the routing slip's *out since the sixth* — remain unresolved in prose, and **no character in Chapters 11–21 states which date is correct.** Gant Ollis states the money-shaped reason the frame is dark (a replacement paid for and never collected, and a district glasswork line that cannot cover a man and a crate sent forty miles) and nobody in the scene connects it to Marek, because the connection cannot be made without a confession that has not been earned. Marek's own private knowledge of the pane is in Chs 18 and 21 only, and he says nothing about it to anyone.
2. **The Office's published figure is now handled as the card required.** The Office's *collision* hour read one hour at 17:00 on day nine and the collision came at about six, so the published arrival was approximately right; the one figure that was flatly wrong by a measurable margin was the *corridor* window, short by an hour and a half, which is a yards' problem. Chapters 11, 12 and 19 keep those two things separate and use them separately. **No hours-to-arrival appears anywhere in the batch.**
3. **A plate's readable depth.** Chapters 15 and 18 need the ninth ward feed to hold a week of run, and the feed has only ever held about two days of legible trace. Resolved on the page and now canon: the fine trace runs about two days; beneath it the registration goes cold and coarse and holds longer, and it is only legible with the lamp oblique and the reader's eyes soft. **This is what makes the twelve-year Witness possible, and it is the load-bearing physical fact of the volume's Act IV clue.**
4. **The Hearthward spend is not reopened.** The six rooms stay ruined, the season's seed stays in the market hall, the market stays shut, Ferren still has no account of his Thursday, and the Hearthward claim is not discussed as a spend anywhere in this batch. Chapter 16 gives Marek a *mechanism* for what he already knew and does not touch the branch.
5. **Chapter 5's plant was not restated.** The dead stretch in the moisture trace is not re-observed and is not re-described in Chapter 11–21, and no character is given the Chapter 7 reading before Chapter 7. Chapter 21 uses Chapter 7's vocabulary — *a dropout drops hard and leaves a hard edge and a crescent; this thinned; a shallow slope* — because Chapter 7 has happened and Marek owns those words. Chapter 21's callback to the Chapter 5 panel is to the *claim and its six ruined rooms*, not to the gap on the bench.
6. **The two plates are still not independent evidence** and are never described as such. In Chapter 21 the evidence is a third thing entirely: a thinning in the Release on a plate that belongs to the Council and that Marek read on the morning it happened, which is why it cannot be produced.
7. **The relationship stayed professional.** Competence, one argument, one shared scare in the *Petrel*, a decision about who is the instrument, and a decision about terms. No attraction beat, no rescue, no jealousy.
8. **Sable Orin's numbers still name a corridor and a time and no place**, and Chapter 14's refusal turn is built on exactly that rule. Her published *basis* names her instruments and her author, and it changes the appearance of the document and not its usability. She names no district, street, school, or block.
9. **The Ninth Ward pane's three senses were kept apart** in every scene: the paid-for pane (4486, quoted, paid for, uncollected, and the crown of it now a plate in a tube), the public notice pane (the position in the frame, holding a bubbled temporary), and the ward's public glass (its unreadability). The frame is still unreadable at Chapter 21 and the cause is the ward's poverty.
10. **Wording note on the Council's seats.** The cards call Hearthward's signature a "regional seat." Hearthward is a Veyran district, not a regional polity, so Chapters 19–21 call it a **concurring seat** and explain why Hearthward is on the list — the terraces' upper tanks sit on the northern feeder and their steward has corrected the Council's water schedule twice in writing — and the fallback that the Council actually used is **the Nacre Holds regional seat**. The substance of the card is unchanged: one concurring seat, one signature asked for, one refusal, and the trial going ahead without Hearthward.
11. **Two paragraphs were cut before the batch was finalised for guardrail reasons, and neither is a hole.** Malle's anecdote in Chapter 21 originally ended in historical deaths; it now ends in a district superintendent who was right, lost his district's confidence and finished on a copy desk, because **Volume 01 contains no deaths.** The dry-line flash-forward in Chapter 11 was cut, because the dry line is Act IV.

**New canon proposed this batch (fold at the volume audit):** the full batch cast — **Ott Belm, Gant Ollis, Nell Cray, Kell Bray, Sabin Orle, Marda Loon, Petrie, Enid Fisk, Ottilie Fisk, Abel Fisk**; the **certification bell** against the entry bell; the **Council's basin house** and the technical cell's chamber as a working room with a plate rack; the **four Kestrel conditions** and Ilva Sorn's proviso; the **Ferrygate fallback set**; the **full pipe as distinct from a surcharged pipe**; the **substrate beneath the fine trace** as the only place a twelve-year-old Witness can live; **Ottilie and Abel Fisk, 4 Quay Steps**, and the reader's prior introduction to that household through Enid Fisk in Chapter 14; the **Office's morning bulletin** as a document-shaped hole; and **Marek's job as a job** — named, rostered, and paid.

**Not yet prose canon, and forbidden to appear as fact in any later summary:** the Kestrel corridor claim, the convoy that never leaving, the Lower Sill culvert's failure, the landslide, the dry line, the spent-turn column, Tovan's demotion, Ottine Vask, Ione Sere's lie, Marek's hearing loss, the Chapter 34 Oren chapter, and **the two Ferrygate residents' nine hours, which are Chapter 22 or later and are not dramatised anywhere in this batch.** Those are Chapters 22–48.

## Volume 01 — Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10)

**Clock covered:** day four 21:40 to day nine 18:00. Weekday map established: day four = Monday, five = Tuesday, six = Wednesday, seven = Thursday, eight = Friday, nine = Saturday. **The front reaches Veyra at about 18:00 on day nine**; no absolute hours-to-arrival is quoted anywhere in the batch, because the Office's figure moves all week, which is the point of Chapter 1.

**Batch goal achieved:** Marek has gone from a maintenance hand with a bench trace to the named reader on the Bellweather watch, having spent his first branch in public on Thursday and spent the two days after it concealing the cost. He holds the only reading of the collision's order, an unprovable sequence fault, and **three blocks of pencil on the inside of his left coat cuff: two readings and one filing mark.**

**Batch external climax (Ch 6):** the leading rain floods six ground-floor rooms on Hearthward's lowest row and ruins a season of seed stock. Nobody dies. The claim was true.

**Batch moral climax (Ch 7):** Marek reconstructs the spend, understands that his own binding closed the garden crew's Thursday clearing, decides not to file it, and gives Malle a technically responsive and false account of what the claim displaced.

**Rules taught without a lecture:**
- A read is free. Stated outright by Marek in Ch 1 and again in Ch 7, with visible relief both times.
- A binding needs a place, a time or trigger, an effect, and a witness. The witness chain is pencil, entry sheet, and bell, shown as a job in Ch 4.
- A conditional forecast binds when its condition is accepted and triggered, not when it is written. Shown in Ch 5, where the Hearthward assembly accepts and the bell makes it real.
- A binding closes the still-available turn that would have prevented its outcome, and four people with shovels and a two-day window is such a turn. Shown in Ch 7.
- Caution is free. Neris closes a school on her own authority in Ch 8, spends nothing, and is never credited.
- The Office's defence against inconvenient evidence is reclassification. Established as doctrine by Tovan in Ch 4, demonstrated by Venn in Ch 10.
- A public claim touching public life must be entered in the emergency ledger inside one cycle. Named in Ch 4, used for the first time in four years in Ch 5.

**Bindings in this batch: two.**
1. **Ch 5 — the Hearthward conditional claim.** Sponsor Marek Vale. Condition accepted by the Hearthward assembly. Entered by Tovan Iles, bell struck for the yards' ward head and for Hearthward, first line written in the emergency ledger in four years, expiry the third watch. Entirely true. Cost: the garden crew's Thursday clearing of the lower row's drains, which can no longer happen and is never identified as having happened.
2. **Ch 8 — Sable Orin's unsigned number**, which became a binding only when Neris Kade pinned the card to her board and wrote the whole pump order off it. What it closed is small, deniable, and **deliberately unidentified in prose and must stay unidentified in all state files.**

**Power state at batch end:** Marek remains a Reader and has not followed a sequence across two towers. Strain has worsened permanently: inner-ear pressure, a metallic taste, and a loss of fine hearing in his left ear that is not yet explained and is **not** the Volume 12 sequence burn. He can no longer stop looking at a movement that has already stopped and does not yet know this is a symptom.

**Money spent:** six ground-floor rooms on Hearthward's lowest row, a season's seed stock in the vaults' lower six drawers, a market shut for a month, an hour of Marek's sleep and a bent needle, a night with a dead feed seat in the Ninth Ward tower, a morning of Low Spindle wages, a charter worth a month of the *Petrel*'s overhead, and a paid-for pane nineteen hundred across that a poor ward will now never be sent. **No deaths.**

**Standing debts created:** Pell's two years of unpaid tower glasswork; the Ninth Ward's unreadable public pane *and* the paid-for pane Marek destroyed, which the Office has already spent and the ward will now never be sent; Anwen Sarr's written demand that the cost of a claim be published; Malle's two asked questions and the second answer, which was a circle.

**Clock discipline established (binding on Batch 0002):** the Office's published figure to the front **moves all batch and is the only clock the prose is allowed to quote** — fifty hours on day seven, twenty at ten o'clock on the night of day eight, nine at 10:00 on day nine, six at noon, one at 17:00; days one to four carry a six-hour disagreement between the ridge's own instruments, and Chapter 1 says the front is *three days and a bit* out. **No chapter states an absolute hours-to-arrival and none may**, and no outline card may reintroduce one: the Batch 0001 cards carried an hours-before-the-front column that has been removed. **The figure must slide downward through the batch and must never put the collision in the past.** The single fixed anchor is **the collision reaches the basin at about 18:00 on day nine**, with the Bellweather watch covering it and the drainage after it. The three weather phases in this volume are the **leading rain** (Thursday night, day seven), the **second belt** (the small hours of day eight, 01:20–04:40), and the **collision** (about 18:00 on day nine).

**New canon proposed this batch (fold at the volume audit):** the Hearthward terrace drainage arrangement (upper spill gate, upper/middle/lowest rows, outfall under the market discharging at a straight stone throat, a pipe already full takes a terrace's rain and gives nothing back, Tuesdays-and-Thursdays rain-garden clearing by Ferren's crew of four and a foreman, four catch pits under iron grates at the foot of the lowest row); the serial chain 4470/4471/4486; **the physical arrangement that makes the Chapter 7 corroboration possible** — a plate ground to a feed's thickness seats behind the holder, face to face with the ward's own plate, and two plates in a holder take the same trace off the same feed; the Bellweather watch roster and its *reader* line; the Weatherglass ward heads as mirrored indicators wired to the entry bell, and the ninth ward's showing nothing; the violet filed mark (a square with a bell struck across it) for a lodged witnessed claim; the Charter line "The Exchange is not a party to any district's preparations and is not required to price them"; the tower crew docket that puts Marek's glasswork debt on the ward's account in his own name; Tovan's refused claim of two winters ago, which is a plant for the Volume 04 district refusal; Feth Raal's two ghost needles, left deliberately unexplained; the Weatherglass's nine ward charts held under glass because the north wall sweats; the Council's **technical cell** (a hundred and eighty people, headcounted by Malle in Ch 9 and named without one in Ch 7) and the **Charter review** (Ch 2) as ordinary machinery; and the physical property of sequence glass that **cracked glass keeps what sound glass throws away**, a plate under strain holding the strain in the fracture so that the crack renders what the sound pane would have lost (Ch 1). The last of these is load-bearing for the whole volume and has no home in `bible/terminology.md` yet; it belongs in the bible at the volume audit.

**Not yet prose canon, and forbidden to appear as fact in any later summary:** the Bellweather corridor claim, the Lower Sill convoy, the culvert, the flood at the Lower Sill, the Oren chapter, the Nacre Gate trial claim, the spent-turn column, Ottine Vask, and the date of the trace in the Ninth Ward tower's sequence plate. Those are Chapters 11–48.
