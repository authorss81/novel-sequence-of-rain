## Volume 02 — Batch 0001 (Chapters 49–58) — **ACT I, WHAT A WINDOW IS FOR**

**Clock covered:** the ninth of Sleetmonth to the second of Drownmonth. **No character has aged: nothing in this batch is more than eleven weeks after the forty-eighth day of the Bellweather month.** Chapter → day: 49 = 1 (Monday, 9th), 50 = 2 (Tuesday, 10th), 51 = 3 (Wednesday, 11th), 52 = 5 (Friday, 13th), 53 = 8 (Monday, 16th), 54 = 9 (Tuesday, 17th), 55 = 10 (Wednesday, 18th), 56 = 11 (Thursday, 19th), 57 = 12 (Friday, 20th), 58 = 13 onward (Saturday 21st through the 2nd of Drownmonth). **The table in `state/continuity.md` is the authority and Chapter 58 deliberately runs past the batch's nominal span to the second of Drownmonth, because the notice and the register cannot happen inside a fortnight and the chapter says so.**
**Ordinary clock hours are quoted freely in this volume.** Volume 01's no-hours-to-arrival rule belonged to a single front with a sliding published figure and is **not restated**. There is no front in this batch.

**Batch goal achieved.** A censured man is employed by a market to cut its glass; a window is defined, priced and published; a district finds its own quarterly return in a commercial schedule; a reach is measured for the first time in the city's history and given a number; and the ninth ward, in order to stop its weather being sold twice, gives up its own reach window in a witnessed claim that is entirely true, correctly certified, deliberately chosen, and costs it a wall. **The batch ends on a register with a heading and nothing under it.**

**Batch opening (Chs 49–50):** a maintenance hand who cannot be put on a watch takes private work from the Cloud Exchange, and the work is a glass datum for a tide gauge in a wall at the south end of the freight apron — **an instrument the Weather Office has never built because it has never wanted to look at a river.** In a yard three hundred yards from that shed, a table outside a chandler's is selling weather windows, and the yard discovers that its own published column of differences is the raw material. **A window is not a claim, spends nothing, guarantees nothing, and is therefore outside the Office's authority and inside everybody's life.** Tarin Senn adds a fifth condition requiring attribution and the market gives it to her for nothing in a quarter of an hour, and nobody yet knows what that is worth.

**Batch escalation (Chs 51–54):** the ninth ward's quarterly return — the sheet Neris Kade got carried in four minutes in the Bellweather month — is bought by the Exchange's district research desk for eleven shillings in the ordinary course, and the ward discovers that a schedule of what it will do about rain is the cheapest possible form of exclusion and that a district's right to be asked is also a district's right to be resold. **Maro Hesk's counting floor defines the refund rule, which is the volume's engine: a quoted window that does not open is refunded, and the price of the withdrawn window is published, and a price is a measurement, and there has never been a measurement of a reach.** The only reach figure in the history of the city sits struck through in pencil in a margin — two shillings, put there in a hurry by a partner in the days after the storm. Tarin refuses a forward window contract on the grounds that a yard which sells dependability is selling its crew the right to be unlucky, takes a dull coal charter instead for four hundred and six pounds, and then takes the firm's instrument anyway. **And a twenty-nine-year-old underwriter does the arithmetic in a shed and finds that the same forty minutes is worth 7/6 to a barge interest and 1/4 to a ward, five and a half times, with every figure true and nobody lying, and writes a memorandum in pencil instructing that it never be printed.**

**Batch midpoint (Ch 57):** the ninth ward assembly sits for four hours with nine people in it and a freeholder who is not a villain and a reach window that has already been sold. **The Exchange will not sell the ward a window because the ward cannot show a use, and the reason is that the ward's use is a pump and a timber stack it has already paid for, and so the ward's only asset is the thing it is being exploited for.** Dellow will not withdraw and will not be a bad customer. The ward's lever is a concurring seat and a conditional claim, and a Council assessor who has never had to do this reads the sponsor's cost out loud in a room before certifying it, and the cost is the ward's own repair window and a second consecutive season for the Lower Sill wall and three years becoming two years and about eleven months. **Six for, one against, one abstained, and the ninth ward votes to make itself poorer and safer, correctly, with the price stated before the vote.** Marek says in a lane, unasked, that he cannot hold a trace across two towers, and the assessor writes it down as a limitation.

**Batch external climax and cliff (Ch 58):** the claim goes on the rail in a fair hand and **the panel comes on the sheet for the first time in the series** — the Chapter 29 notice came to a plate in a coat, this one comes to paper. The bell is struck and he cannot hear it and the ninth ward's head does not light and the claim binds anyway, because a ward cannot be bound by a thing it is told. A copy clerk two hundred feet away copies the seventh box in full and nobody in the building knows. A counter-reader prices the second reader at eleven shillings and attaches a term she did not write. **The back-cut comes on the twenty-third and the reach does not come down, the claim is entirely true, the ward has its best Monday morning in four years, and about nine hundred pounds of timber in four iron bands stays in a shed under a painted board for a second season.** The firm strikes the line through in public under its own rule, pays its own buyer thirteen shillings, and declines in writing to claim anything against the ward — **because the ward never bought anything, and there is no form for a thing you did not buy.** And the volume's question opens on a bound volume in a press on the ground floor of the technical cell, in which the ninth ward's line has nothing under it and has had for longer than anyone looking has been alive, and the clerk who produces it is not hiding anything and does not know what the page is.

**Rules taught without a lecture:**
- **A window is not a claim.** No place, no trigger, no witness, no entry, no spend and no guarantee. Stated in full by the man who invented it, and it is the reason the market is legal and the reason it cannot be regulated by the one body with instruments. (Ch 52, 50.)
- **A refund is the price of not selling.** It is not the price of what the buyer would have done, and a firm that never asks cannot pay a second claimant. (Ch 52.)
- **A price is a measurement, and a measurement of a preparation is somebody's opinion of what the preparation was for.** 7/6 and 1/4, five and a half times, both true. (Ch 54.)
- **A market that has struck a line through twice in public cannot go on selling the thing behind it**, and that is the only reason this volume has a chance. (Ch 58.)
- **A claim is not weather, so a claim does not void a window contract — it breaches it,** and the breach falls on whoever entered it. A contract fact, never to be generalised into a magic rule. (Ch 57, 58.)
- **A district cannot show what a claim was worth to it either, and the reason is a form, and the form is honest, and the form is empty.** (Ch 58.)
- **A notice is a thing a ward is told, and a ward cannot be bound by a thing it is told.** (Ch 58.)
- **A condition about attribution is free to obtain and is not consent.** (Ch 50.)
- **A crew is not made safer by a clause about who is a party to a contract.** (Ch 53.)
