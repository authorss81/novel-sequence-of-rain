## Volume 01 — Batch 0004 (Chapters 36–48) — **ACT IV, THE PUBLIC DRY LINE, and the end of the volume**

**Clock covered:** day thirty-four from about 08:15 to day forty-eight at about half past ten at night. Weekday map, locked and matching `state/current.md`: **day 34 = Wednesday, 35 Thursday, 36 Friday, 37 Saturday, 38 Sunday, 39 Monday, 40 Tuesday, 41 Wednesday, 42 Thursday, 43 Friday, 44 Saturday, 45 Sunday, 46 Monday, 47 Tuesday, 48 Wednesday.** Chapter → day: 36 = 34, 37 = 35, 38 = 36, 39 = 37, 40 = 38, 41 = 39, 42 = 40, 43 = 42, 44 = 44, 45 = 45, 46 = 46, 47 = 47, 48 = 48. **The collision reached the basin at about 18:00 on day nine and is not re-derived. No chapter in this batch states an hours-to-arrival, the Office's sliding published figure is not used once, and there is no front, no belt, no collision and no body anywhere in Chapters 36–48.** The only weather in the batch is an ordinary wet evening on the forty-eighth day. **The storm is over. Nothing in this batch is a weather event.**

**Batch goal achieved.** The method survived and the record did not, and a room had to be told about both. Five days of evidence, a finding entered in writing on the first day of district evidence that the effect of a claim on a prepared district **cannot be demonstrated from the record**, one column invented to print what closed without printing who closed it, a hand-lettered load list at a freight apron, a censure, a document read out in a public hall, a public coinage, a routing fragment, and a dark window. **Nothing is spent, nothing advances, and no panel is produced anywhere in this batch.**

**Batch opening (Chs 36–37):** a Bellweather review is called under an ordinary Charter power with eight citations, and the second name on the list is the reader who spent the corridor. The chair is Cassian Rook and he declares his own interest **at 11:04, four minutes after the review began, in front of a clerk**, and then does the fairest available job for five days. The terms of reference carry a second half — *and if it cannot, to report that it cannot* — which is the first sentence in this basin that agrees with Anwen Sarr and agrees with her by conceding everything. **Clause four is examined by the woman who wrote it, in front of the clerk who wrote it down, and the second sentence of it turns out to be unanswerable, because the honest answer to *why can this not be published* is *because there is no instrument that sees it*.** Anwen Sarr is offered a softer form of the standing requirement, refuses it, and substitutes a stronger one — place, effect, trigger, condition, expiry, and a line headed *turns displaced* in which is written what closed **and by no means who closed it** — and gives the name away on purpose, in a room, on the record, in exchange for a column.

**Batch middle (Chs 38–42):** the Kestrel Line's file goes in — nineteen days of column, eight of them with a difference on them, the fifth-of-the-month sheet, the four conditions (never paraphrased by anybody who was not in the yard), Ilva Sorn's proviso, and a mooring hand's wrist printed in chalk with the hour on it under the heading *not the yard's error*. **The only cost in the entire record is on that sheet, and it is a wrist, and a wrist is not a category.** In the corridor afterwards, Maro Hesk reports that two carriers have priced the yard's honesty as an exposure. Then the reader gives the same answer he gave on the nineteenth, names the instrument out loud — *a plate with no chain behind it in my coat* — and **three people in the room decline to ask the next question, and the assessor minutes the number three.** Aldis Prynne is called and explains, for free and correctly, that the Office's whole record of the ninth of the month is a copy of a voice in a room, and cannot say where any of it came from, and has been in the building thirty-one years. **In Ferrygate a clerk produces a document and it proves something smaller than anybody wanted: a preparation existed, and nothing whatever about what happened to it. The nearest miss in the series stops, visibly, at an ordinary and completely impassable procedural fact and not at a refusal.** In Low Spindle a warden hands over seven lines, cannot answer the ninth-hour question, discovers she has been a clerk and not a warden for a fortnight, and cannot write the eighth line of her page. At the midpoint, **Corrin Malle answers the question nobody has ever asked him — *what did the classification do?* — and finds that what it did was remove a thing from the categories that can be complained about, and that the design is older than him and he cannot find the author, and that a classification is not a name and is a better hiding place than one.** He is recommended nothing. He says so himself, in one flat sentence, in a corridor.

**Batch external climax (Chs 43–44):** at the Bellweather freight apron a shed is kept. A water-office form is refused, a district printed sign is refused, and on the second day a shed foreman paints his own board with the load and **the sixth of the month and no name**, because the sixth is when the load went on and the thirtieth is when somebody decided it did not come off, and there was no such somebody, and a name on a board would send the whole city looking for a man and stop them looking at the load. Then the column is invented, contested for two days, and nearly three things, and **Marek argues against the strong version using an argument Sabin Orle beat him with on the nineteenth, correctly, and out loud, to keep his own name out of a permanent public record of the one thing in his life he knows he did.** Sabin Orle decides it in ninety seconds: *the Office is not entitled to the sponsor's name in a column it will print.* The first entry is the thirtieth and it says forty minutes, and no instrument, and no name.

**Batch moral climax and volume cliff (Chs 45–48):** **dismissed from field duty and censured, correctly, in eleven minutes, on a ground every clause of which is true, by people who are each individually right.** He does not produce the twelve-year date, the plate, or a document of any kind, and the volume neither rewards nor punishes him for it. The report is laid before a public table and the method is praised and the two reclassifications are set out and nobody objects, and **a woman in a queue who has been standing in the wet for an hour supplies the name: *my side of the river has got a dry line on it.*** In the archive, the routing fragment reads **VASK, O., R-9** and three things are proved and no more, and Ione Sere tells him about a ledger migration with total conviction. In the public forecast hall, built for forty, with more than two hundred in it, he reads out nine hundred and forty words that are not the document he promised, Anwen Sarr reads it out from the front of the room, says in front of two hundred people that it is not what she asked for, and by eight o'clock two people who were not in the reach at half past three that morning are arguing about the thirtieth with a ledger between them, and they are both right. **The volume ends in a barrel-roofed hall with half the lamps out, a piece of glass pinned to a board with a card listing everything it is not, a column being argued over, a copy clerk two rows back copying a meeting she is not permitted to keep, and a poor ward's tower with a dark window above the low end of the city.**

**Rules taught or completed without a lecture:**
- **A review of a method is not an inquiry into a claim.** The terms of reference say *the districts affected*, and a district that can be shown to have been affected is by definition the thing being tested, so the review is convened with the two districts that lost something absent from its own citation list, and the reason is arithmetic. (Ch 36.)
- **A Director's authorization is not a certification and the consequence of it is that the sponsor is the only answerable name in the sheet.** Rook puts the sentence in the minutes himself, on the record, at the end, while he still can. (Ch 45.)
- **A reader's attestation is a fact about the reader, and this office has not in the history of the instrument asked a reader from which instrument a reading was taken.** Established on the record, by a witness, by accident, and minuted with the number of people who declined to ask the next question. (Ch 39.)
- **A preparation that was not lodged cannot be shown to have been displaced and cannot be shown not to have been displaced, and that is the ordinary operation of the method and not a defect in it.** (Ch 40.)
- **A classification removes a thing from the categories that can be complained about, and a classification is not a name.** Stated by Malle at the midpoint and by Marek in the same room, and neither of them is sure who is right and the chapter is. (Ch 42.)
- **The only honest public record of a cost is one that names what closed and refuses to name who closed it, and a rule that is honoured and a thing that is printed are not the same instrument.** (Ch 44.)
- **Right out loud is a procedural offence.** The censure is correct and every clause of it is true and the institution has found that a man who says a true thing he cannot support has done something procedurally wrong. (Ch 45.)
- **A name on a board is a sentence with a person at the end of it, and a load is a sentence with a thing at the end of it, and only one of the two can be repainted every nine years by the man who wrote it.** (Ch 43.)
- **A public coinage is adopted by a clerk completing a form, on a Thursday, in the ordinary course, and the person who coined it is not the person who gets the credit.** (Ch 46.)
- **Caution is still free and still uncounted.** A warden carrying a quarterly return in four minutes, a shed foreman keeping a shed, two carriers pricing a yard's honesty, and a woman in a queue are all in no column anywhere. (Chs 41, 43, 38, 46.)

The trial claim's cost remains unprovable, and Chapter 40 is the proof that it is unprovable **in principle rather than in luck**.

**Reconciliations made inside this batch, recorded here because they touch Chapters 1–35 without altering them. Nothing in Chapters 1–35 was restarted, rewritten or renumbered.**

**Batch 0004 review repairs, all made inside Chapters 36–48 and recorded here because they touch the batch's own guardrails.**
1. **Chapter 44 no longer names the Chapter 21 cost.** The first draft had Marek, arguing in a records room, describe the trial claim's cost as *a two-line sheet in a book with two names on it* and *two people in a ground-floor room at the bottom of some steps in Ferrygate*. ## Volume 01 — Batch 0003 (Chapters 22–35)

**Clock covered:** day twenty-one from 03:26 to day thirty-three in the late afternoon. Weekday map: day twenty = Wednesday, twenty-one Thursday, twenty-two Friday, twenty-three Saturday, twenty-four Sunday, twenty-five Monday, twenty-six Tuesday, twenty-seven Wednesday, twenty-eight Thursday, twenty-nine Friday, thirty Saturday, thirty-one Sunday, thirty-two Monday, thirty-three Tuesday. **The collision reached the basin at about 18:00 on day nine and is prose canon from Batch 0001; no chapter in this batch states an hours-to-arrival and none re-derives the arrival hour, and the Office's sliding published figure is not used once.** The batch introduces a fourth weather phase, **the body** — the sustained western draw and the peak, arriving off the lake at about 16:00 on the twenty-fourth, with the peak rain 02:00–05:00 on the thirtieth. **The batch ends in a copy room, not in weather.**

**Batch goal achieved.** The storm's worst is over by Chapter 32. What is left is a record. Marek has gone from a man who lost both audiences in one afternoon to a man who was handed a good instrument and a good crew and used them, and the using is the volume's last look at his flaw. The Office has stopped using him, the field list has a hole where he was, the long watch has somebody else's reader on it, the Council's method has been reported on and praised, the districts were told to prepare and to return prepared, and a claim of his is in the world with a Director's stamp on it and nobody can be shown what it took. **He is a Reader. He has a third of his fine hearing in one ear and a plate in a coat and a document in a drawer he cannot file, and by the end of the batch there are three people in Veyra holding the only true record of what happened on the thirtieth and all three are in a position to do nothing with it.**

**Batch beginning (Chs 22–23):** the trial claim is discharged, entirely and correctly, and the cost of it arrives at ten past five in the morning in a ground-floor room on the Quay Steps and is covered by nothing and by no notice, because the thing that would have covered it is a piece of paper nobody was told about. Then the Council's report is very good, and the man who made it true in public is not sent for, and the reason is not spite — the Office uses the instrument that works, and the instrument that worked on the nineteenth was a man who could produce paper. **He writes four hundred and eleven words of an instrument's account of itself, containing the collision's order, and it cannot be filed anywhere.**

**Batch escalation (Chs 24–26):** the body arrives and the joint request turns out to be a bucket. A yard publishes a column of differences for eight days and accidentally builds the Office's alibi. A ward runs nine days of rain on a full pipe and pays for it in a morning of wages nobody will ever price. The districts re-file the request and the Council returns an answer nobody can argue with — *prepare in the ordinary course and to return prepared* — which takes the deadline off the request without answering it, in eleven correct words. **The long Bellweather watch goes up and Marek's name is not on it, and there is no version of the sentence in which it is.**

**Batch midpoint (Ch 28):** the Council's own conditions refuse an honest claim, and the reason is the best reason in the volume: *if the Council could certify a number that depends on a private person doing a thing, the Line would stop being a witness and become a resource.* So he goes to a yard and asks for a hull, and gets one, publicly, in chalk, on four conditions — and Tarin Senn discovers, out loud, at the boards, that all four of her conditions are about the yard's name and not one of them is about her crew.

**Batch external climax (Ch 32):** the peak. The headwall goes at about 03:30 and nine days of the ward's own rain comes back up out of the ward's own drains into the ground floors it came out of, and the south-bank road is cut at 05:00, and **the two are in two different columns with two different hours and the public account is correct about both.** Nobody dies, because the wardens had the lowest four blocks clear five hours earlier in Ampow's own hand. And a captain flies a flat-bottomed lighter under a freight apron and lifts a pump house's intake screen out of the water, and pays for it in public, on the column.

**Batch reversal and cliff (Ch 35):** the record. The spend is filed as maintenance failure by a Deputy who says both sentences out loud — a true statement about the culvert and not a true statement about the thirtieth. A clerk of twenty-seven enters a Records act in a field book and loses a glass-tender's examination, permanently, on a form whose ground is true, and nobody thanks her, and she keeps a copy in her own box. **Rook's authorization stands. The claim stands. The method survives. The record is closed.**

**Rules taught or completed without a lecture:**
- **A read is free and an authorization is not a certification.** A Director's authorization requires no sponsor, no expiry, no alternative, no concurring seat, no attestation and no routing effect. It gets a claim on the ward ledger and a bell. It compels nobody. It only makes the sponsor answerable. (Ch 29.)
- **The Kestrel Line's four conditions restated as the law.** *If the Council can certify a number that depends on a private person doing a thing, the Line is a resource, not a witness.* The yard's term sheet is why the claim cannot be certified, and the yard's term sheet is why it can be true. (Ch 28.)
- **A corridor is an hour. A claim that holds an hour makes it dependable. A dependable hour has no bend in it.** The mechanics of this volume's spend are the mechanics of the ending's, at the scale of nine men and a stack of timber, and there is no magic in either. (Chs 26, 29, 32.)
- **A binding closes preparations and not only weather, and it closes the preparation nobody was told about as readily as the one somebody cancelled.** Two of the Ferrygate preparations went out in a courteous letter and one went out in silence, and the silent one is the one that put two people in a wet room. (Ch 22.)
- **A second effect is not a hidden cost.** The wrist is on a board, in chalk, with the hour, published by the yard, and it is not on the claim's sheet and could not be, because the claim says *corridor*. (Ch 30.)
- **A classification is not a lie and is not a defence.** Stated in Chapter 35 by a Deputy about a wall and by a warden about a page and by a clerk about a rule, and it is the volume's institutional answer in three voices. (Ch 35.)
- **The record's protection is a classification, and a classification does not have to be a lie in order to be one.** (Ch 35.)
- **A copy clerk's copy is worth nothing and is the only true record in the city.** (Ch 35.)
- **Caution is still free and still uncounted.** A district's whole population moved up four floors in a day by thirty people with handbells, and a captain's four minutes on a lift, and a maintenance hand's second opinion in ninety seconds, and nine days of wages for an ear, are all in no column anywhere. (Chs 32, 30, 25, 31.)
