# Chapter Summaries

Rolling window of the most recent twenty to thirty chapters, in full detail, for voice and continuity. Older entries are archived by volume.

## Volume 01 — *The Bellweather Forecast* (Chapters 1–48)

### Chapter 0001 — The Shuffled Plate

Day four, 21:40, the Weatherglass maintenance shop. Marek renders the Ninth Ward tower's cracked feed plate (serial 4471, from Pell's four-year-old T-4 bundle) and finds the Six Movements arriving out of order: Gather and Break where Weight belongs, a doubled Route, Release unfinished, Witness in the last third. He ruins one plate, is floored by the bench, gets a bent needle and a metallic taste, and works out that three air masses are arriving inside one cycle, which is why the ridge's instruments disagree by six hours. He does not write it into the chart-room register. He writes time, plate number, tower and the movement order in pencil on the inside of his left cuff and promises himself, twice, that if he is wrong he will say it out loud to a person. He clears the backlog — a fatigued aneroid, a slug-eaten rain-gauge float, Feth Raal's barometer for free — and finds two faint ghost needles standing off her true needle. Ends with Feth, at her stall, reporting men in good coats asking after narrow forecasts and a second version of any figure. **Read only; nothing spent. The cuff note is the first thing Marek has ever kept off the record.**

### Chapter 0002 — Two Versions of One Notice

Day five, 06:00, Kestrel Yards, Tarin's POV. Ivo Slate of the Cloud Exchange offers a sealed, licensed, insured Office window for the same passage at half her rate, backed by a Bellweather figure the Office sold four days ago under a standard secrecy clause to four carriers, a grain factor and two insurers — and the Office does not publish. Ilva Sorn makes the true arithmetic: the Line's published uncertainty is its only currency and the yard cannot keep spending it. Nessa Tarrow, the night watch, copies the Office window and Tarin's counter-reading onto one sheet with both hours. Tarin declines the window, flies the *Petrel* on her own nine-year-old box and her own reading, gets the passage right four hours early, and loses a month of the *Petrel*'s overhead because the Nacre factor took the Exchange window at 06:40 for its insurance. She posts all three versions on the Line's board. Slate closes his little book and thanks her for publishing both, because his agency report will cite the yard board as evidence that the Office's window was understated at the time of sale.

### Chapter 0003 — What a Ward Cannot Read

Day six, 10:00 to 18:00, the Weatherglass and Pell's glassworks. The new Ninth Ward feed plate Marek fitted reads clean, so the shuffle is in the air and only cracked glass had been positioned to show it. He has no standing and Malle's day is a solid block of watch assignments; Malle's only requirement is "bring me something with an instrument behind it." The only good glass in the district is the Ninth Ward's permanent signal pane, two years unfunded and sitting in Pell's shop. Pell refuses, then names the price: Marek does every piece of glass work that tower has coming for two years, at his own hand, no charge to the ward, and Pell keeps the balance. The tower crew come at half past midday and Marek tells them in the doorway what he has done and why; the crewman enters it on a docket as glasswork owed to the Ninth Ward. On the stairs, Malle asks the first question nobody has ever asked him — *if you are right, what would I see that would prove it* — and Marek answers truthfully and reveals nothing: Release before Witness in the second turn, visible on the ward's own glass, which is why he needs to be on the tower. At six he reaches the ward ledger bench, and Tovan Iles tells him to come at nine, and not with a tube.

### Chapter 0004 — The Bell and the Pencil

Day seven, 09:15, the ward ledger bench, Tovan's POV. Fourteen unattached observations on the rack and the watch bell an hour off. Marek brings an order of movements with no place, time or effect, so the bench has no drawer for it. Tovan teaches the rule as a job: an observation is not a promise; an observation nobody acted on cannot later be compelled to mean anything. She names the emergency-ledger rule — a public claim touching a route, water system, hospital, school or market must be entered inside one cycle — and reveals the ledger has one line in it from before her time, about a ferry. She tells Marek that last winter she acted on an unsigned counter-reader's number with no name on it, it was right, and the ward's shelter rota changed underneath her and nobody can reconstruct why. She stamps his plate as an unattached observation and writes his name on the source tab, which nobody ever wants. Off the record she asks whether a reading nobody acted on counts as having predicted anything; he answers that a reading never acted on is a reading never tested, and she hears the evasion and lets it stand. The Hearthward assembly arrives and the chapter ends with Tovan seeing that only a claim will get them anything.

### Chapter 0005 — The Third Watch

Day seven, 11:00, ward ledger bench then the public forecast hall. Marek's structural turn. An unconditional claim needs an instrument chain and he has none on Hearthward, so the only shape available is a conditional one, which puts the decision with the ward. He states the claim's whole reach, including the part he has never seen: the lower row drains clear. The water steward tells him the rain-garden crew clear the drains Tuesdays and Thursdays. Ferren is standing in the room and Marek does not turn round, does not ask where he went, and does not ask what the last time looked like; he tells Ferren to clear them tonight and Ferren says he will. The alternative is written into the sheet (open the lower outfall in the second hour and take the market's ground floor), the expiry is the third watch, the sponsor is Marek. Malle allows it and says the name on it will be Marek's and not the ward's. The single sequence-glass panel appears immediately before the entry, quoting the claim's own effect and warning that one possible turn will close. Tovan enters it, strikes the bell twice — once for the yards' ward head, because the terraces' upper tanks feed the Kestrel freshwater header, once for Hearthward — and writes the first entry in the emergency ledger in four years. The ninth ward's head stays cloudy amber, because its pane is a bubbled temporary. Marek reads the claim out twice to a full hall and then fails to go and check his own arithmetic, and feels an unfamiliar relief at being relieved of it. First binding of the batch: inner-ear pressure, a metallic taste, and the beginning of an inability to look away from a movement that has already stopped.

### Chapter 0006 — The Lowest Row

Day seven 23:00 to day eight 08:20, Hearthward terraces, Tarin's POV. The leading rain arrives with no wind in front of it and Tarin moors the *Wren*, *Sparrow* and the barge *Penny* on the grass above the market in a crosswind coming off the reservoir. The claim is true and visible: the gate holds, the upper rows are dry. At 23:40 the lower row's drains fill and the surcharge line climbs the retaining wall, and nine hours later the row drains in half an hour, which proves there was nothing behind the pipe. Six ground-floor rooms flood; the seed vaults' lowest eighteen inches are ruined; nobody dies. Ovett counts sixty-one people out of the market. Marek has been on the terrace with the ward glass since eight the previous evening and leaves at first light. Tarin asks him one question — who stood the garden crew down, and when — and he gives a list of the questions he did not ask, and says there is no more, and does not say the sentence that would have explained it. At eight, Ferren tells a Kestrel glazier that he has no memory of the night he did not work, that nobody stood him down, that he remembers the whole night clearly and the drains being clear, and that what he cannot get past is that there was nothing wrong and that he was glad. He is not lying.

### Chapter 0007 — The Gap in Release

Day eight, 08:30, the Weatherglass, Marek's POV. He has brought the Ninth Ward feed plate down at 06:10 in a sailcloth on the strength of his own glasswork docket, leaving the tower's feed on a dead seat with no spare. The plate is instrumented and dull everywhere except the second turn, where Release thins to nothing for two and a half hours and returns forty minutes late. He compares it against a logged March dropout in the same feed: that one dropped hard and left a crescent. This one is not a fault. His private plate, cut from the ward's signal pane and run in the same feed seat in rotation, shows the same absence — two plates, one feed, one man, no independent witness. He reconstructs it physically: the row drained in half an hour, so the pipes were already empty, so the crew's Thursday clearing cannot have happened, and the crew cannot be sent, because the branch in which they went is the branch in which the row needed them. He states plainly in his own reasoning that looking cost him nothing and that the water in those rooms was the cost of making. He puts the four times in pencil on his cuff, keeps the bench sheet in a drawer, and decides not to file. Malle asks what the claim displaced; Marek gives him the mechanism and then a false rainfall answer; Malle lets it go because he has a front to staff, and tells him to bring somebody else's plate. Marek minuted the sequence fault and asked for it on the record. The fine hearing in his left ear is thin and unexplained.

### Chapter 0008 — What the Notice Did Not Say

Day eight, 16:00, Low Spindle, Neris's POV. The published Bellweather notice is technically accurate and useless: Sergeant Ampow says it is the truest notice he has read in the district and it does not tell him when to start the pumps, whether to close the school, or what to do with the ninety people on the ground floors. Pet the runner returns from the ridge with a routing slip: the ninth ward's pane is out for replacement, replacement delayed, and a tower clerk told him that somebody cut a working plate out of it and told the crew himself. Neris writes the name and the question *did he know* in her own notebook and gives it no answer. In the drying room above the Ninth Baths, Sable Orin releases an unsigned number with an hour on it — *the undercroft on Sump Lane goes before the low blocks, 01:20 to 04:40* — on the ground that the Office is withholding the shape of the danger. She refuses to have sent it to Neris in particular; she put it out where anyone might read it. Neris names the shelter rota that changed underneath her last winter and that nobody can now reconstruct, mentions it once, and moves on. She takes the number, and offers to have it publicly attributed. She empties the Sump Lane school at 18:00 on her own warden authority, costs the ward a morning of wages, and spends nothing at all. She starts a ruled page: *What the notice did not say.* Four lines. She declines to write a fifth, which would be about her own rota.

### Chapter 0009 — The Arithmetic of a Correct Claim

Day nine, 10:30, the Weatherglass, Marek's POV. Second low point. Neris corners him in a corridor and asks for a time for the low blocks; he has the plate that would give her one and cannot give it to her because it has no chain, and when she presses he describes his whole career back to her as the reason. Juba Tren has come up the hill to see the Deputy, is told to wait, and gets on his knees to clear a blocked step on the Weatherglass front, saying almost nothing, which is the only mercy Marek gets all morning. Anwen Sarr brings the ledger clerk and Ferren into the public hall and reads five items: the claim said the row drains clear and it did not for nine hours; the claim was true and saved two hundred and forty people; the drains were cleared on Tuesday and not on Thursday and Marek knew the rota and never asked; a surcharged outfall does not drain in half an hour, so either the crew went out and two witnesses are wrong or they could not; and what did the claim require the district to give up. Marek answers with a sentence about records procedure, and she walks out carrying it, having told him she will hold him to publishing the cost in writing. The low point is not shame: it is recognising that his father's prediction about him was right and that he had been counting on not finding out. Malle summons him, shows him the joint request — one claim to cover both the Kestrel corridor and the low wards, which are the same air and cannot both be had — and tells him the only reading of the collision's order in the building belongs to a maintenance hand who has already given him a true answer that was not an account. The collision is confirmed at six hours. Marek takes the Bellweather watch: low ground, ninth ward, second belt to fourth watch, one reader, and his own name.

### Chapter 0010 — A Steady Knife

Day nine, 17:00, the Weatherglass, Marek's POV. The watch roster is up with his name on it as *reader*, and the joint request pinned beneath it states in two correct lines that the western draw can either hold the Kestrel corridor open or ease the lower basin and not both, with an Office note that the Exchange's Bellweather window is *admissible* in the corridor decision because four licensed carriers have paid against it. The front has gone quiet and is now an hour out instead of six. The Line's board carries one sheet: an instruction has come down to copy the Office window only, and Nessa Tarrow has rubbed out the two hours of Tarin's counter-reading, carefully, so the paper could be reused. At the records counter, Sable Orin's broadside — *verified, the yards must be under sail by four o'clock* — has been reclassified at 14:10 under Rule 6 of the Charter of Public Notice as an unattached observation: not lodged, not compellable, not producible, with a handwritten line from Deputy for Records and Public Notice Lior Venn stating that the Exchange is not a party to any district's preparations and is not required to price them. Marek checks his own plate against it and it agrees exactly, and he feels relief, because the same machinery that buries her number has buried his. In the chart room, reglazing the Ninth Ward tower's framed chart, he finds that the chart's upper margin has been trimmed off with a knife: the cut edge is brown with age while the paper around it is still clean, so the trim is old and the sheet is old and the two were not contemporaneous. What survives of the provenance line reads *SEQ/N9/ — lodged by — A. Vale*, with a violet filed mark of a bell struck across it and a watch number, and then the paper ends, because the citation and the date were taken off. He writes the time, plate reference and filing mark in pencil on the inside of his left cuff — three readings and a filing mark — and tells nobody. He goes down the hill to the low ground with a claim he has not written, a collision nobody else can see the order of, and a piece of paper upstairs with a hole cut in it.
