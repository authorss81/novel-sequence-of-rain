# Open Threads

Planned promises, marked with how far each has actually been opened in prose. A thread is only *promised* to the reader once it has been dramatized; until then it is design.

## Mystery threads

- **Bellweather spend:** **open in prose, and the reader has it.** Chapter 29 is the claim and its cost; Chapter 30 is its arrival and its second effect on a yard; Chapter 31 is the convoy's window coming and going; Chapter 33 is the absence and the one clerk's handwriting in the level book; Chapter 34 is a mechanic's ninety seconds on what it was worth; Chapter 35 is the classification that closed it. **The spend is the missing repair, not the landslide and not the headwall's failure, and the public account attributes all three correctly and separately.** What the reader has and no character has is the whole chain: Marek entered the claim, the claim held a long band, a held band has no hook, the hook is the ebb, the ebb is the window, and the window was forty minutes wide on the morning of the thirtieth. **The one document that joins the two ends is a warden's ruled page in a locked drawer, and it is not compellable, and the other is a copy clerk's private copy in a box, and nobody knows about either.** The dry line, the spent-turn column, the shed's public sign, the hand-lettered board at the freight apron, and the First Dry Line are **Chapters 36–48 and are not prose canon.**
- **The cut index:** **opened as a hole only, and untouched in Batch 0003.** Chapter 21 has Marek refusing to equate a hand-trimmed chart margin with a room full of people and a bell, and that is the only place the two are allowed to touch. The reader has not been told what the removed piece covered, who cut it, or that a routing clerk was named. The remaining fragment reading **VASK, O., R-9** belongs to Chapter 47 and appears nowhere in Chapters 22–35, and neither the Meridian, the Compact, Ione Sere, the Ninth Ward Record, nor the Crown Basin sheet is named anywhere in Chapters 22–35. The hand is identified in **Volume 05**; the reason is withheld until **Ione's Volume 11 testimony**. Do not invent a separate culprit, and do not let the Volume 01 hook resolve to Rook alone.
- **The unrepaired trace:** **still open, and now load-bearing for a different reason.** Chapter 23 established on the page that **the honest version of Anwen Sarr's document cannot be written**, because it needs an instrument with a chain behind it, and there is exactly one in the city, and it is the Ninth Ward tower's own plate, and that plate is also the only instrument in the basin carrying a **complete, instrumented Witness movement dated twelve years back**, arriving in the glass from below. Writing about either is a different and worse project than the one Anwen asked for, and it would put a poor ward's tower in front of a Council inquiry. **Marek is now the man who spent a corridor, and he is the only person alive who knows about the date, and the two things he cannot prove are on his own plate in the same room.** Chapter 18's three refusals stand; he has not been up the ladder in Batch 0003. **Do not let it be resolved before the archive, and do not let anybody learn of it by accident of narration.**
- **Ninth Ward Record:** **not mentioned in prose and must not be.** Anja's signed forecast. Proves the surviving corridor was sealed and hidden rather than spent. Missing until Volume 11.
- **Crown Basin sheet:** not mentioned. A separate document. Records the branch she closed, the route she failed to save, a seal tag number, and Rook's signed order with the low-ward projection omitted. Found in Volume 12.
- **Meridian Cut:** **not named in prose and must not be** until its planned volume. Rook's signed order and Ione's deletion surface gradually. **Rook is in prose from Chapter 27 and the reader knows only that he is competent, useful, and the man who stamps things.**
- **Original Compact:** not named. Nobody has gone near Crown Basin, and the volume does not go there.

## Civic and political threads

- **The city working system for not knowing, now dramatized three times and then used against a clerk.** Tovan Iles's unattached-observation drawer, the entry bell, the violet filed mark, the emergency ledger, the certification bell, Lior Venn's Rule 6 reclassifications (now five of them across the volume), the **countersignature line in the Records day book**, and the **classification column in the field day book that is not the entering clerk's to fill.** **Chapters 22–35 end with that machinery costing a twenty-seven-year-old woman her glass-tender's examination and putting her on a copy desk, and nobody in the building thanking her.**
- **The trial claim's cost:** **opened in Chapter 21, dramatised once as a consequence in Chapter 22, and it is unprovable.** A compound branch of three linked preparations — the Ferrygate rain-garden clearing rota, the ferry fallback, and the two-name low-ground shelter rota — closed by a Council-certified claim that was entirely true. Ottilie and Abel Fisk were in a ground-floor room for nine hours on the twenty-first and nobody was hurt and no notice covers it and **it is not mentioned again as an event.** **Nothing may ever be produced that proves it, and no state file may describe how it could be proved.** The institution's answer is already on the page: *it is not accidental that a claim's cost cannot be documented; it is the design.* The dry-line register that would eventually exist is not in this volume.
- **Anwen's written demand:** **escalated and now structurally blocked.** It is clause four of a filed refusal, and **Chapter 23 established that the Council can satisfy the first sentence of it and has, and cannot satisfy the second, and will not answer the second.** Marek told her the document was *not ready* on the eighteenth and has said nothing since. **She is owed the Chapter 48 public hall and nothing before it may pay her off.**
- **Malle's compromise:** **completed.** He used Marek once in a certifying room on the nineteenth and filed the attestation in a drawer; he refused to take a reading off him in a corridor on the twenty-sixth; and he buried the thirtieth's entry in Chapter 35, and he said out loud, to the only person who writes things down for a living, that a spend on his watch is a spend with his name under it and that a Deputy who cannot explain a thing is entitled to call it the thing whose name he does not have to explain, which is in the Charter, Section 9. **He is not caught, he is not a villain, and he is partly right, and the day sheet that went out is correct in every particular.**
- **Tovan's demotion:** **open in prose and permanent.** It follows the refusal to enter a false classification, it costs her the glass-tender's examination, it is not a favour, and it is not overturned in this volume. She has now been the clerk on the bench for three claims (Hearthward, the Kestrel corridor, and the third — Sable's is hers) and has been asked to keep none of them.
- **Tovan's copy:** **open in prose and nobody knows.** A true copy of the thirtieth's day sheet as it stood before the reclassification, in her own hand, dated, timed, initialled, in her own box. **The first person to ask her about it is an open question for Batch 0004 or later, and it is not Tovan who goes looking.**
- **The no-forecast trial:** not started. Juba Tren has appeared once, in a corridor, and said almost nothing.
- **The ward mismatch:** **not yet named as politics, and the Chapter 29 night is now its strongest physical instance.** The ninth ward's head showing nothing at all, on the night a claim touching that ward's own floodplain went into the world, with a permanent frame paid for two years ago and never sent and a temporary bedded in a brass surround.
- **Weather Commons:** not started.
- **Ferrygate as a district that prepared:** **established, spent, and closed out in prose.** The three fallbacks went out under a claim they never knew about; the nine hours are done; **Nell Cray has copied the rota four times and sent one to the warden's council and knows exactly what she is doing and why it is worth nothing.**
- **The Lower Sill convoy:** **open in prose and closed as a fact.** Loaded since the sixth, nine crew, timber banded, pump on a pallet, a painted date nobody looked at, a window written into the shed's own level book by a water office clerk on the Friday, a mark that came off the post at 07:20 on the thirtieth, forty feet of flat gained, and the long band back at 08:10. **It did not move, and it was not stood down, and no document anywhere in Veyra says it will not go, and the only person who knows the whole of it is a maintenance hand who is also the man who spent it, and he told a shed foreman that it was just the water.** Petrie is a shed foreman, not a plot device, and he is not the villain and suspects nobody.

## Character threads

- **Marek's flaw:** **performed four times, and the fourth is the volume's.** Chapter 7: withheld a branch cost and answered Malle with procedure. Chapter 9: gave Anwen a records sentence. Chapter 18: three conscious refusals to take, copy, or record a thing he was entitled to look at. Chapter 21: stood up in a room and made a motion he could not support. **Chapters 28–29 are the fifth and sixth: he goes and asks a competent adult for the *thing* instead of the question, and gets a good instrument and a good crew, and enters a true claim with a Director's stamp on it, and the warmth he feels is *this is now somebody else's to do* and there is nobody left to do it.** He does not enjoy it and he does examine it, and he went to the south bank on the thirtieth to be near a thing he spent. **The later public failure in Volume 07, in which hiding a branch costs Tarin's crew a safe route, is still ahead and must not be anticipated — but the shape of it is now on the page in a yard, in chalk, under the third line, and it is the yard's own account of it.**
- **Tarin's question:** she asked who stood the garden crew down, and Chapter 7 answered it. **What she has not been told is why, and she now has a second instance of it and still has not been told why.** She has flown a hull inside somebody else's claim, has published the claim's second effect on her own yard in chalk with the hour on it under a heading that says *not the yard's error*, has taken a lift that should have been a junior's for four minutes and eleven seconds, and has found out that **a term sheet protects a yard's reputation and not a crew's body** — which she said out loud in the yard a day before it happened, and knew, and did not act on. **She has still not asked him the second question and the shape of the answer has changed again.**
- **Sable's correction:** **live and unnamed.** She published a basis, was right, was read by nobody, kept the sheet up, and started a fourth notebook headed *Declined. Who. Why, in their words. Date.* She has not appeared in Batch 0003. She knows she cannot remember asking the people she numbers. She is not sorry and she is reorganising. Do not let Volume 03 name this before it happens. **She is not in the Quiet Ledger, the Quiet Ledger is not yet a thing, and no chapter in Batch 0003 names either.**
- **The district's right to refuse:** **planted in Chapter 4 in Tovan's mouth, paid once in Chapter 20, and now supported by a second district.** Anwen's refusal is correct, filed, and looks like a defeat from inside the building. Nell Cray's district withdrew two preparations and had no third to withdraw, and the third went because nobody was told about it. **Anwen Sarr is not softened and is not bought off.**
- **Neris's list:** **seven lines, stamped, locked, and the seventh is the one that carries the volume's argument.** Sent to Malle on the twenty-second, correctly reclassified on the twenty-fifth, and the cost of the list has been paid in the way she expected and not the way she wanted. **Her own shelter rota is still unwritten and is still the line she cannot put her name to.** What she has learned is not to ask a second man whether a true thing is more true when two men say it: he said no, and the answer was the answer she already had.
- **Pell's warning:** he told Marek that a ward with no public glass finds out about a private reading from him, eventually. **Chapter 29 is the first time the claim he is warned about is in a room with the bell on it, and Chapter 35 is the first time the ward's unreadable frame is a reason a district cannot be told something it needs.** He has not been told anything and Marek has not gone to Kestrel Yards' workshops.
- **Oren's reconciliation:** **Chapter 34 is it, and it is a repaired bracket.** Four minutes, done well, nobody thanked him, the first thing he and his father have repaired together in six years. **He gave Marek the mechanism of his own life in ninety seconds and did not learn the cause, and Marek did not tell him, and the chapter does not reward him for coming.** *Come on a Tuesday when it's a nuisance and nothing's happened* is still standing and Marek keeps not doing it. **What Oren now has that nobody has is a sheet of paper in his inside pocket: four columns of years, four words in four hands, a front in three years, and the note that the money will be reversioned at the year end because the works panel schedules and does not cancel.** Chapter 36 or later has to decide whether it is ever more than a bracket.
- **Tovan's two claims, and the glass-tender's examination:** **closed as a prospect.** The examination is held in the spring by somebody else and she does not sit it. She is on a copy desk for the rest of this volume and for Volumes 2–10.
- **Anja stays dead:** unchanged. The handwriting in Chapter 10's chart margin, and the twelve-year date in Chapter 18, are two facts the reader can put side by side, and **Chapter 23 added a third thing the reader can put beside them: the reason he cannot write the document is on the same plate.** **No character has put any two of them side by side, and nobody may until the archive.**
