## Mystery threads

**The thread that moved, and the thread that did not.**

- **The Standing Response Register — NEW, opened in prose at the end of Chapter 58, and it is the volume's question.** A bound volume in a press on the ground floor of the Council's technical cell, filled in by hand once a season for two hundred and forty years, a column for each of the nine wards, a line for each season, headed *available responses to a season's water*. **The ninth ward's line has nothing under it, and has had nothing under it for longer than anyone looking at it has been alive; the fourth ward's the same and the sixth has a run of about nine.** The clerk did not go and count along: he put two fingers on a line and said that's the ninth, that's ours, and there's nothing under it, and there's been nothing under it a long while, and then he said the sentence the whole of Volume 03 is built on: *A response that isn't recorded wasn't available, miss. That's what the form's for. If it wasn't written down, it wasn't there to use, and I'm not saying it wasn't. I'm saying nobody wrote it, and I wasn't asked, and the fella as left it wasn't asked either.* **He is not hiding anything and does not know what the page is. The reason nothing is under it is that somebody stopped asking the ninth ward, and nobody wrote down that they had stopped.** Three people have seen it: Neris Kade, who asked; Marek Vale, who was in the corridor; and the clerk. **No member of the Council has seen it. Nobody has asked who stopped filling it in, and no character has yet noticed that they should.** **Do not resolve this in Volume 02. It is handed to Volume 03.**

- **The Bellweather dry line:** unchanged and still open. The claim of the twenty-ninth stands; the column has a **second entry** in Volume 02, recording a window quoted and not opened on the morning of the thirtieth with no reason in it, and the heading carries no disclaimer and the column still has no sponsor field. **The second entry says what was lost and not why, exactly like the first, and the reason is that the Office has no instrument which looks at a reach. ## Civic and political threads

- **A window is not a claim.** The volume's ruling, the market's product, and the reason a firm may sell hundreds of them: no place, no trigger, no witness, no entry, **no spend and no guarantee.** It is outside the Weather Office's authority by construction, and the Office's own Circular 14 says so and is entirely correct.
- **The refund rule, and the publication of a withdrawn price.** A window not opened is refunded at the quoted price within the week, and **the price of the withdrawn window is published**, and it is the only reason this volume has a chance: a market that has struck a line through twice in public cannot go on selling the thing behind it. It is also the reason the Exchange cannot conceal anything and the reason it cannot pay anybody: **a refund is the price of not selling and is not the price of what the buyer would have done, and the firm has a rule against asking which is now the reason it cannot pay a second claimant.**
- **A claim is not weather, so a claim breaches a contract rather than voiding it.** Clause 9 of the Exchange's own form defines force majeure as an act of God, the King's enemy, riot, **or the direction of a public authority**, and the firm has declined in writing to say which of the last two it considers a ward's certified claim to be. **The ward has no answer, has a solicitor on the Nacre Gate, and has been told not to spend anything on it.** Open.
- **The exchange of a good reading for a good price is the easiest transaction in the basin, and that is the sentence Volume 03 inherits.** A district is asked, correctly, for a form with one box on it, and answers it in a fair hand, and eleven shillings buys the answer twice. The Exchange will print the district's own figure. The Exchange will not put it beside its own, because **two prices for two different objects cannot be compared and a market that pretended they were would be lying.** The ward's marginal cost for a reach window is 1/4 and the Exchange's price is 7/6 and the difference is the whole of what the Exchange knows about its own buyers, and Mear's memorandum says so in pencil and instructs that it never be printed.
- **A district's right to be asked is a district's right to be resold.** No form, no notice, no hearing, no duty anywhere in the building about a price. **Anwen Sarr's fourth clause now exists in two forms: the DISPLACED TURNS column, which she got, and a side-by-side publication demand, which was refused within the hour by a printed sheet whose fourth paragraph is a good argument.** She is owed nothing, gets nothing, is not bought off, and will defend the column for the rest of her life.
- **The ninth ward's quarterly return.** Eighteen pages. Sold to the Exchange's district research desk for eleven shillings on the second of Sleetmonth, on a vote of the warden's council taken at four o'clock on a Wednesday while the warden was on a pump, and the ward has no purchase in it. **It is the only schedule of the ninth ward in the world, and the reason is a form with one box on it in a press downstairs where it is not.** Do not let anyone recover it as a document in this volume.
- **The Exchange's free district sheet, twice a day, at a counter in a shop.** More legible than the Office's own bulletin, prints its own basis, and prints *the Office's* on the one line that is not its own. **It is not kindness: a district that cannot read its notice cannot buy a window, and a district that cannot buy a window is a district nobody has to compensate.** A ward with a dark public glass is a ward with a private one, twice a day, and the ward has to decide about it, and it has not been asked to yet in a room.
- **Tovan's demotion:** open, permanent, and dramatised in its consequence. **She has made a second true copy, on her own initiative, of a claim sheet containing the fullest statement of a claim's cost in the city.** Nobody in the building knows the box is not empty, and nobody has asked her about anything.
- **Tovan's box — now the volume's most dangerous object, and it holds two things.** (1) A true copy of the thirtieth's day sheet as it stood before the classification was changed, made on the third of the Bellweather month. (2) A true copy of the ninth ward's entered claim sheet **including the whole of the seventh box**, made on the twenty-first at 11:26, and recopied from the rail on the twenty-fourth. **They are the only two documents in the basin that say, on the same two months, that a cost can be written down in a fair hand and be worth nothing to anybody.** **The plan reserves a third object for later in the volume. The first person to ask her about any of them is not Tovan. Nobody knows how many are in there, including her.**
- **The Lower Sill wall:** now on its **second consecutive** deferred season. **Three years is two years and about eleven months, which is a Friday.** The money will be reversioned at the year end because the works panel schedules and does not cancel. **Petrie's *no work, reason* column, empty for thirty years, has now been written in for the first time, by Neris Kade, in a different book, and it is the first line in the basin that says the work was not done and why — and it is not compellable and it is worth nothing and it is correct.**
- **Malle's compromise:** completed in Volume 01 and he does not obstruct in Volume 02. One offstage sentence of his is reported — that published uncertainty costs a yard contracts it cannot afford to lose — and it is correct.
- **Anwen Sarr's standing requirement:** paid once in Volume 01 in the form of a column, and asked for a second time in Volume 02 in a worse room, and refused within the hour. **Not softened, not bought off, owed nothing, and now two clauses deep in the same direction.**
- **The ward's right to refuse:** real, cheap, correct, and **shown to be insufficient** for the first time. A ward can refuse a claim and cannot refuse a schedule. A ward can refuse a contract and cannot refuse a contract's counterparty. A ward can refuse a price and cannot refuse being priced.
- **The no-forecast trial:** not started. Juba Tren has not appeared in Volume 02.
- **Weather Commons:** not started.
- **The ward mismatch:** named as politics for the first time in Volume 02, and not by anybody with a title. **A district whose ground is worth more as a resource than as a neighbourhood, whose only asset is the thing it is being exploited for, and whose weather is now the product of a firm that has read its own quarterly return.**

## Character threads

- **Marek's flaw, now performed in its commercial form, and performed at its worst and most ordinary.** Nine weeks of being the only person in the basin who knows what a claim cost, and no forum, and no document, and no standing; and then a forum appears, and a document appears, and a market appears — **and what he does with it is cut a glass datum.** The Volume 01 pattern of going and getting the *thing* instead of the *question* has become a job, and the job pays for it, and the job is for the party his warning hurts. **He also did the Volume 01 trick in the lane in Chapter 57, unprompted, to a Council assessor with a page: he said out loud, before he was asked, that he cannot hold a trace across two towers and will not put the ward's window in the middle of it — which was true, and cost him the hour, and was a man performing honesty at a moment when honesty was cheap and visibly so.** Nobody in the room noticed. **He is not redeemed and he is not a martyr and he has not noticed either.**
- **Marek's hearing:** permanent, never repaired, **never explained to anybody in this basin and never going to be**, not the ground of anything, and used physically and never as a plot — Ch 50, a figure asked twice; Ch 53, the same, and Tarin noticed and did not ask; Ch 58, the entry bell at four feet above a stone rail, unheard, with the sound arriving late in other people's shoulders. **The one disclosure in the first draft was reversed at the batch review and this is now an absolute guardrail for the rest of Volume 02.**
- **The Tracer gate:** identified, named, priced and unentered. *I can't. I want a second reader at the far end and I can't do it alone. I did it once, in the spring, off the ninth ward's own feed, with one man at one end of it and me at the other, and it came apart in about forty minutes and it came apart sideways.* **The practical cost is on the page: because he cannot place the bend, the ward's claim is drawn to the Exchange's quoted hours rather than to the weather's, and a market's timetable is now the timing of a district's protection.** Entering the stage requires a second reader and he has one, and she charges eleven shillings and attaches a term.
- **Sable's correction:** live, and now for the first time about money. Eleven shillings in coin before she starts, and a term that is not hers: *the reading goes on a board a warden is welcome to read, free, and I do not pass it to anybody who has to pay for it.* **She has been free with no conditions for two years and it got her four refusals and a fourth notebook, and she has decided that being paid with a term is better, and the reason is honest and the result is not consent.** She is not sorry, she is reorganising, and she was waiting at the end of a corridor on a bench when the batch ended. **She is not in the Quiet Ledger and the Quiet Ledger is not a thing.**
- **Tarin's question:** the second question is still unasked. She has now refused a contract that would have fixed a boom cable and a wrist and two carriers' discount, on the grounds that a yard which sells dependability sells its crew the right to be unlucky; she has paid four hundred and six pounds for that; she has taken the firm's instrument anyway and installed it on her own slip; and she has added a fifth condition to her own board about attribution. **She discovered in Chapter 50 that the yard's own public honesty is somebody else's raw material, and she could not stop it, and the thing she did about it is a line of print that a market put on a list for free.** **The word partnership is not said and no romance has entered either volume. Volume 03 still owns the first real trust milestone and Volume 08 owns the deliberate choice.**
- **Neris's list:** seven lines, stamped, locked, and **quoted whole in this batch including the two sentences the Volume 01 record left between its halves.** There is no eighth line and she cannot write one. **What she did write this batch is a line in a shed level book, and it is the first line in the basin that says a repair was not done and why, and nobody can be shown it.** Her own shelter rota is unwritten and is still the line she cannot put her name to.
- **Gant Ollis:** has a chapter, and has never once asked Marek about the plates, and works out that a maintenance hand bedded the brass and thinks the better of him for it and stops there. **He has made the first independent check on a private weather product anybody in this basin has ever made — twelve comparisons, twelve agreements, written in his own book's fifth column, sent to nobody — and he has sent a two-line note to an instrument maker a hundred and forty miles down the hill road and got the sentence *it is the chain.* He is a keeper, not a public officer, and no council has ever asked him for his book and he is not compellable and he is right.**
- **Maro Hesk:** the reader likes him and is frightened of him and both are earned. He has never done anything dishonest and applies to himself a test no hostile reader could fail; his product is legal because the Office will not touch it; **his argument for the whole thing is that a guarantee requires spending a possibility and a market cannot run on a quorum, and he says that in an empty room to nobody and he is right;** and the thing he is frightened of is his own memorandum. **He has struck a line through twice in public and has said out loud that his firm would rather print the hole than print a lie, and he has declined in writing to bring any claim against a district it has just ruined, after taking advice it says it was not told to take, and the reason is that a hearing produces a finding and a finding is citable.**
- **Garrick Dellow:** not a villain, twenty years of asking a ward for nothing except the water to go off its land, and he said the thing that the whole volume turns on without knowing it: *when I took eleven of them off this ground in eleven years nobody ever came to the door to ask me for one.* He has paid seven and six for the window of the twenty-third and will not be a bad customer, and he will not be a fuss in a market on the ward's account either, and he wants the ward to come and tell him what it is going to do instead. **He is the only person in the basin who has voluntarily said that he would rather the ground were a place than a resource, and he is a man who makes his living from the other thing.**
- **Rook: on stage once, in print, and it is the scariest document in the volume.** Circular 14. Four paragraphs. A window is not a claim; the Office will not certify, endorse, price, regulate or advise upon one; **the Office has no instrument which looks at a river or a reach, has never had one, will not build one, and does not consider the absence a defect in its work**; and any claim for the value of a reach is a claim against a party that sold a window and not against the Office. **Anwen Sarr read it forty times looking for a lie and there is not one. He is correct, he is never wrong about the weather, and he has just told a ward in print to go and sue a shop.**
- **Anwen Sarr:** owed, paid once, refused twice, and not softened. **Her standing requirement now has a column and a refused demand, and the refused demand's own language is better than the column's and she knows it.**
- **Tovan's two claims and the examination:** closed as a prospect. The examination is in the spring, held by somebody else, she does not sit it, and she is a copy clerk for the rest of this volume and for Volumes 3 through 10.
- **Anja stays dead:** unchanged. **Nothing in this batch touches the chart margin, the twelve-year date or the routing fragment, and the reader has no new reason and no new clue.** Nobody may put any two of those facts side by side until the archive.

## Ending threads

**Untouched. Nothing in Chapters 49–58 anticipates the ending.** The ending's mechanism is already visible in this batch at a smaller scale, and it is the same mechanism as the Low Spindle gate claim in Volume 12: **a claim that holds a turn revokes the authority of a preparation, and the ward closed its own window on purpose and paid for it in a wall.** The two Last Forecasts, the corridor repair, the core key, the private read, the nine towers and the Chorus are all as recorded in `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md` and none of it is anticipated, referenced or prepared for here. **In particular: the compensation ledger is not opened in this batch and must not be opened before Act III; the district cannot be compensated for anything because it never bought anything, and that is the reason the ledger has to be built out of somebody else's arithmetic later.**
